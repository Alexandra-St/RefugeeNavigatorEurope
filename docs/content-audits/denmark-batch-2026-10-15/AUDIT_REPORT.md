## 3. Official-source audit

### Method and limits

Each of the 85 unique catalog URLs was checked on 15 October 2026 using an online page reader; all 98 source records were mapped to their exact stored URL, then assessed against the relevant article passage. The reader does not expose reliable HTTP status codes; therefore this report does **not** claim an HTTP 200 response. A readable page is evidence that page text was available to the reader, not proof that every claim is legally correct. Redirect status/destination could not be reliably distinguished in every reader result. For seven distinct Retsinformation statute/regulation URLs and the L 43 proposal record, the exact page rendered only a JavaScript shell; their full operative text could not be read directly in that tool. Official indexed text was available for the narrowly described BEK 828 and L 43 facts below, but this is a weaker check than reading the full operative source. PDFs for Act 501/2023, Act 667/2026 and LBK 995 were readable. No LastCheckedAt values were altered.

The source records comprise 85 unique URLs and 11 repeated-URL groups (13 records beyond one record per URL). Duplicates are linked source records reused by articles, not 13 extra unique web pages. The full row-level register follows. “Readable text” means reader text was available; it is not an HTTP-status assertion. “Limited” records state the direct-access limitation. Claim area identifies the article-specific purpose for which the source is cited; the article review and issue register explain whether that use is supported and any qualification needed.

## 4. Legal findings

### BLOCKER — BLK-01: 17 March 2027 / 17 March 2028 conflict remains unresolved

**Affected:** B (`2d106fd3-e981-4ef9-9733-568b654dfca5`) and G (`afc14824-4035-4ca7-9662-6d5352ba63cb`); all languages discuss this issue.
**Verified evidence:** the official [BEK 828/2026](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2026/828) indexed official text showed the regulation as current at the check, effective 1 October 2026. Its operative §1 allows the covered permits to be extended until 17 March 2028 subject to the regulation’s conditions and withdrawal rule; §3 addresses new permits after commencement; §4 sets commencement and repeals BEK 1095/2025. The full HTML itself could not be read directly in the audit reader, which limits verification of every operative qualification. The Immigration Service’s [Ukraine information page](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB/You-want-to-apply/Ukraine/Displaced-from-Ukraine) and [Section 1 application page](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB/You-want-to-apply/Ukraine/Displaced-from-Ukraine) still state 17 March 2027 in the rendered agency guidance reviewed. The conflicting statements cannot be reconciled from the pages reviewed.

**Consequence:** do not infer that every individual permit is automatically valid to 2028. B and G currently attribute the discrepancy rather than promise individual validity; both articles must remain consistent and the article’s explicit attribution should not be silently removed. **Publication is blocked** unless the Danish Immigration Service gives a clarifying current answer or a human editor explicitly accepts publishing an attributed discrepancy. The full regulation and any later agency clarification must be checked immediately before release.

### BLOCKER — BLK-02: L 43 is not enacted at the audit snapshot; legislative gate remains open

**Verified status at 15 October 2026:** the [Folketing L 43 page](https://www.ft.dk/samling/20261/lovforslag/l43/index.htm) recorded the proposal as introduced (`Fremsat`) on 8 October 2026, with first reading scheduled for 13 November 2026. It had not been adopted, promulgated or entered into force at this snapshot. Official proposal material at [Retsinformation](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/ft/202612L00043) describes proposed changes and proposed commencement/transition dates; these are not current law. The direct Retsinformation page itself was not readable as full text through the audit reader. No enacted successor was identified in the official parliamentary status reviewed.

**Potential scope:** proposed changes concern the Ukraine Special Act and may affect eligibility/application groups and transition rules; the project's existing legislative watch specifically calls out employment obligations (`arbejdspligt`) and other Special Act rules. Because the proposal had not completed Parliament, there is no current enacted text, promulgation date, or operative commencement to apply. The exact final impact on residence permits, pending applications, employment, assistance, education and other articles is therefore unresolved, not a fact to publish as law.

**Action:** before any batch deployment, recheck the Folketing status, amendments, final enacted text if any, promulgation, provision-by-provision effective dates, transitional clauses, and effects on all eight articles. The gate is not waived by this audit or by earlier article approvals.
