### EXT-01 — current framework and public guidance mismatch

**Status: PARTIALLY CONFIRMED; source statements remain CONFLICTED.** The official [BEK no. 828/2026](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2026/828) record identifies the regulation as `GÆLDENDE` (in force), BEK no. 828 of 23 September 2026. The direct HTML endpoint renders a JavaScript shell and its `/pdf` endpoint did not return a readable document. Retsinformation’s official indexed statutory text supplied the complete operative §§1–4; this is full official indexed text, not a direct HTML/PDF read.

- **§1(1):** residence permits granted under §§1 or 2 of the Ukraine Special Act can be extended until 17 March 2028, unless there are grounds for revocation under Special Act §10.
- **§1(2):** the Danish Immigration Service (DIS) decides extensions on its own initiative; extension cases have suspensive effect while DIS processes them.
- **§2:** extension decisions may be appealed to the Immigration Appeals Board; Special Act §28(3)–(5) applies.
- **§3:** permits newly granted after the regulation enters into force are time-limited until 17 March 2028.
- **§4:** the regulation entered into force on 1 October 2026 and repealed BEK no. 1095/2025.

The current DIS pages for [Section 1 applicants](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB/You-want-to-apply/Ukraine/Displaced-from-Ukraine) and [family members under Section 2](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB/You-want-to-apply/Ukraine/Family-to-displaced-from-ukraine) still state that the resulting permit is valid until 17 March 2027. Section 2 page text was directly readable and showed an update date of 3 September 2026; the English Section 1 page text continued to show the 2027 date, but no later explanation of BEK 828 was found. The guidance and regulation therefore remain inconsistent in what a reader sees; the newer regulation establishes the extension framework, but the agency pages do not explain each person's decision or current expiry date.

**Important limits and cases:**

- A permit granted before 1 October 2026 is not shown by this regulation to be automatically extended without the DIS decision/process in §1(2). The owner should use the individual permit and any DIS extension communication to confirm the person's current date.
- A permit granted on or after 1 October 2026 falls under the distinct issuance rule in §3 and is limited to 17 March 2028, subject to other statutory conditions.
- A pending extension case has suspensive effect during DIS processing under §1(2); this procedural protection is not an individualized confirmation of a final extension date.
- Revocation grounds under Special Act §10 remain relevant. In addition, Special Act §3(4), inserted by Act no. 725/2026, restricts extension beyond the point at which a qualifying person becomes subject to Ukraine's mobilisation rules. The restriction concerns the statutory group and application date in that provision; it must not be generalized to all permit holders.
- The regulation does not state that every permit holder's own permit card or written decision already shows 2028.

**Editorial recommendation:** B and G may retain a transparent attribution of the two official statements, but future copy should describe 17 March 2028 as the regulation's conditional extension endpoint and 17 March 2027 as the date still displayed on the named DIS guidance pages. It should say that DIS handles extension cases on its own initiative and that an individual’s current status/date must be confirmed from their own written decision or DIS. Do not call the matter resolved for each individual, remove attribution, or imply automatic extension. A human editor must decide whether the attributed mismatch is suitable to publish while DIS guidance remains unchanged.

### HOUS-01 — municipal placement scope

**Status: CONFLICTED.** The article's broad wording suggests that a Ukraine Special Act residence-permit holder is assigned to a municipality and is covered by Integration Act §12 temporary-placement rules. The directly readable official [Act no. 546/2022 PDF](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2022/546/pdf), §4 no. 5 inserting Integration Act §57(5), says a person granted a permit under Ukraine Special Act §2(1) is not to be assigned a temporary place or housing under Integration Act §12. It allows the Immigration Service, where very special personal circumstances exist, to decide that §12 placement should be made. This is a specific statutory exception, not a general right to housing.

However, the current SIRI [Ukraine FAQ](https://integrationogjob.dk/nyankomne/faq-ukraine/) describes §2 permit holders more broadly as covered by the housing-placement rules. The applicable consolidated Integration Act and the apparent difference between the statute and agency FAQ were not reconciled in the sources available. Accordingly, I do not treat either the article's broad generalization or a blanket exclusion as fully settled for every current case.

**Recommendation:** hold this housing statement for a separate authorized editorial correction. Before changing it, read the current consolidated Integration Act and ask a qualified Danish legal reviewer or competent authority to reconcile §57(5) with current SIRI guidance and the relevant §2 subgroup. Any future text must state the exact covered route and the exceptional-discretion condition, and must not promise an available home.

### HOUS-02 — allocation is not an individual housing guarantee

**Status: CONFIRMED with scope limits.** BEK 339/2026 §§1–4 sets proportional municipal figures for distribution of covered persons during 2026. Official [DIS “yes to a residence permit” guidance](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB/You-have-received-an-answer/Ukraine/Ja-Ukraine) says the Immigration Service decides municipal assignment and municipalities have a role in integration and finding accommodation; requests cannot always be met. The source page shows update date 18 August 2022, so current administrative details remain time-sensitive. The official current [SIRI Ukraine FAQ](https://integrationogjob.dk/nyankomne/faq-ukraine/) describes handover/responsibility and housing processes but has no page-level update date.

These materials distinguish the allocation authority, municipal task and practical placement; they do not establish an individual right to a chosen municipality, a particular permanent home, or guaranteed availability. Pending applicants' reception-centre/private-accommodation options are described separately by [DIS “waiting for an answer”](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB/You-are-waiting-for-an-answer/Fordreven-fra-Ukraine), updated 11 September 2026. Housing benefit and deposit-loan schemes are separate, subject to their own qualifying conditions and individual decisions; neither municipal distribution nor a residence permit alone guarantees payment.

**HIGH-02 reassessment:** the distribution regulation and current operational pages have now been checked, so the missing-source-access portion is partly resolved. The article remains **not ready for release** because HOUS-01 is conflicted and the key DIS assignment page is dated 2022.

## 7. Appeal deadlines — APPEAL-01

**Related audit finding:** HIGH-03. **Affected article:** G `afc14824-4035-4ca7-9662-6d5352ba63cb`, all languages. **Checked:** 15 October 2026, 20:34–20:37 UTC.

**Status: CONFIRMED for the statutory route, subject to exceptions.** The official PDF rendering of Ukraine Special Act no. 324/2022 ([Retsinformation API PDF](https://www.retsinformation.dk/api/pdf/229946)) was directly opened. Its §28(3) provides an eight-week appeal period from notification for covered DIS decisions, subject to §§6–9. §28(4) gives suspensive effect to an appeal filed within seven days. Under §28(5), the Board chair may in special cases allow a late appeal or grant suspensive effect after the relevant time limit. §28(6)–(9) assign certain decision types elsewhere or exclude them from the ordinary appeal route, including specified health/social expense, §14 and §15 decisions.

For **extension decisions**, BEK 828/2026 §2(2) expressly makes the decision appealable and applies Special Act §28(3)–(5). The same periods and late/suspensive-effect discretion therefore apply to the extension route, subject to the provisions incorporated.

For **initial refusals**, §28(3) supplies the ordinary appeal period subject to statutory exceptions in §§6–9 and the decision type. The seven-day suspensive-effect rule is conditional on filing within that period; it is not a universal promise of permission to remain in every case. The specific decision and appeal instructions remain controlling for the person.

No later reviewed amendment changed §28; however, the source is the original enactment, not a current consolidated version, and no full consolidated §28 PDF was retrieved. The current BEK's express incorporation independently confirms §28(3)–(5) for extensions. **Editorial recommendation:** keep the existing non-numeric wording in G. If numbers are proposed later, obtain a separate editorial/legal review preserving the exceptions and distinction between appeal deadline and suspensive effect.
