# Lithuania — Education: Sources and Claims

**Research checked:** 2026-11-09
**LastCheckedAt for every source:** 2026-11-09
**Scope:** `education-school-enrolment`, `education-pupil-language`, `education-higher-admission`
**Method:** Official Lithuanian and EU sources only. Research, not article drafting, legal advice, or a recognition/admission decision.

## Scope

This review covers access to Lithuanian pre-primary and compulsory general education for children with temporary protection (TP), enrolment and class placement, language/adaptation support, the relationship between Ukrainian online schooling and Lithuanian compulsory education, admission to Lithuanian higher education, recognition of prior learning, and education support for Ukrainian pupils/students. It distinguishes national law, ministry guidance, municipality/school procedures, and institution-specific offers.

## Research verdict

**READY FOR CONTENT DRAFTING WITH QUALIFICATIONS.** Lithuanian law gives people with a right to reside (including temporary residence) access to education and guarantees pre-primary, primary, basic and secondary education. Education is compulsory through age 16; pre-primary education is a separate programme and is compulsory for children in the statutory age cohort, generally from age six, while earlier entry is possible under conditions. Municipal education divisions and schools are the practical first contact. Current ministry guidance says school placement can proceed using documents if available, an interview, and other information; prior Ukrainian records are useful but are not described as a prerequisite to initial school enrolment. Grade assignment should reflect age, prior achievement, and school assessment, with the Ukraine-specific guidance allowing a higher or lower class with parental request/consent. A family has no established national right to a chosen school or specific seat.

Lithuanian-language instruction and school educational support are established. The ministry’s Ukraine FAQ describes individual learning plans, additional Lithuanian lessons, a tailored adaptation period and optional Ukrainian-language study, but it does not establish a current guaranteed duration, weekly hours, dedicated assistant, or uniform municipal package. Its five-lessons-per-week Ukrainian-language example is expressly conditional on a school being able to provide it, and the FAQ has no clear current update date; do not turn it into a universal 2026 entitlement. The 2026 ministry report confirms that eight schools currently run Ukrainian education programmes, which is evidence of active provision but not a right to attend a particular one.

The reviewed current law does not establish Ukrainian online schooling alone as satisfying Lithuanian compulsory-school attendance for a child living in Lithuania. A historical 2022–2023 arrangement allowed Ukrainian remote study only alongside registration at a Lithuanian school and Lithuanian-language learning; other official sources described a maximum one-school-year arrangement. That emergency arrangement is time-bound and does not prove a current exemption. Formal distance learning remains possible in defined Lithuanian school/programme contexts, but no general parent-selected Ukrainian online-school exemption was established. [LT-EDU-S01, S03, S09–S12]

A TP holder may apply to Lithuanian higher education, but TP does not itself guarantee admission, recognition, a state-funded place, fee waiver, or scholarship. A Ukrainian secondary qualification is assessed by the institution authorized to recognize it or through SKVC. Previous university study/credits are assessed separately by the receiving institution. For Ukrainian citizens seeking state-funded first-cycle, short-cycle or integrated places, official guidance limits the route to applicants who completed Lithuanian secondary education and meet the current admissions criteria; other applicants may apply to institution-funded/self-funded programmes. Ukrainian-specific higher-education aid remains targeted by cohort or particular annual calls, not a general TP benefit. [LT-EDU-S16–S25]

## Compulsory-school and enrolment boundaries

- **Compulsory general education:** compulsory schooling continues until age 16. The standard pathway includes primary and basic education; upper-secondary options may continue after compulsory schooling. Do not use “compulsory ages 6–18.” (S01, S06)
- **Early-childhood versus pre-primary:** early-childhood/pre-school education is a separate service with municipal admission and possible parental fees; pre-primary is compulsory for the applicable age cohort and prepares children for primary education. It is generally entered in the calendar year a child turns six; earlier entry from five is subject to the statutory assessment procedure. (S01, S05, S07)
- **Primary start:** ordinarily begins in the calendar year the child turns seven. Entry at six is possible in defined cases; the foreign-pupil case should be decided through the school’s assessment, not presumed from age alone. (S06)
- **TP/minor coverage:** temporary protection confers the relevant access for protected minors. Do not expand this to every person who lived in Ukraine or assume a child’s rights depend on a preferred municipal school. (S01–S03)
- **Where to apply:** for primary/basic/secondary school, contact the municipality’s education division/coordinator or the nearest/selected school. If a municipality uses CPIS, check that municipality’s 2026/27 local instructions; otherwise use its own process. The CPIS 24-municipality list is implementation only. (S03, S07–S09)
- **Enrolment in the school year:** ministry guidance states basic education can accept pupils during the academic year. Procedures, priorities, seat availability and first-choice school remain local. (S06–S09)
- **Seat:** legal access and a municipality coordinator do not guarantee the family’s first-choice school, a language-specific class or immediate placement at that site. The authority should advise an available route; a single nationwide overflow workflow was not established. (S03, S06–S09)
- **Fees and services:** general education at public/state/municipal schools is free; textbooks are funded. Personal supplies and optional services may cost money. Pre-primary meal support and means-tested meals/supplies have separate rules. (S01, S14–S15)
- **Transport:** the law provides transport in defined rural-distance and compulsory-programme circumstances; do not claim free transport for every pupil in every locality. Confirm the municipality/school route. (S01)

## Class placement / missing-document boundaries

The Ukraine FAQ says the school bases placement on records if available, a conversation/interview with the child and parents, and other information; the ordinary comparison point is learning with age peers. With parental request or consent, a pupil may be placed a form lower or higher. The learning-progression procedure allows attainment information to inform class selection; it does not create an automatic equivalence table for Ukrainian grades. (S03, S10)

For initial general-school enrolment, do not tell a family that a Ukrainian transcript, completed curriculum recognition or full file is mandatory before the child can start. Provide whatever identity and learning records are available and ask the school what local documents are needed. The evidence does not establish a universal 2026 list or universal rule about translating school records. This school-placement procedure is distinct from recognizing a secondary diploma for university admission. (S03, S06, S16–S18)

The general ministry information says a school health certificate is normally part of some annual/local school processes, particularly in pre-primary materials. The reviewed sources do not establish that a particular Ukrainian vaccination record is a universal condition for general-school admission, nor a national exemption from health checks. Ask the school/municipality for current health documentation; do not state that missing vaccination papers automatically deny a compulsory-school place. (S05–S07)

## Lithuanian-language and adaptation support

- Lithuanian is the state language of education, and people with a right to reside have statutory access to education and state-language learning. (S01)
- The Ministry’s Ukraine FAQ says a pupil’s inability to speak Lithuanian or speaking only Ukrainian/Russian is not an obstacle to enrolment. A school prepares an individual learning plan based on achievements/needs and may give additional Lithuanian instruction. (S03)
- The FAQ describes an individual adaptation/transition period and gradual integration; duration depends on the pupil. It does not establish legally fixed weeks, months, hours per week, or automatic exemption from the full curriculum. (S03)
- Schools/municipalities may form Lithuanian-only groups or combine language preparation with staged subject participation; availability is local and should be confirmed with the municipal education division. Older ministry pages that described set semester/year patterns should be treated as examples of past organization, not current entitlement. (S03–S04)
- Ukrainian can be recognized as a second foreign language on request where the relevant grade/programme permits it. The FAQ’s five lessons per week is expressly “if the school can ensure it”; do not publish it as a guaranteed 2026 allocation. (S03)
- Current ministry evidence confirms eight schools running Ukrainian curricula in June 2026. This supports the existence of active Ukrainian-curriculum programmes but not a right to a place or the continuation of the 2022 remote-school exception. (S13)
- State funding covers general educational provision and support services in public/municipal schools, but no per-pupil entitlement to a Ukrainian-language teacher, assistant, psychologist or a specified grant was established. (S14)

## Ukrainian online-school boundary

The current Law on Education makes education compulsory until 16 and guarantees school programmes for people with the right to live in Lithuania. No current 2026 provision located in this review creates a general exception for a child residing in Lithuania to satisfy compulsory education solely by attending a school online from Ukraine. (S01–S02)

A 2022–2023 ministry emergency memo described remote Ukrainian-curriculum learning for up to one school year. It required the pupil also to be assigned to and registered at a Lithuanian school; the Lithuania-based school needed written confirmation from the Ukrainian school and had to ensure Lithuanian-language learning, with peer/social activities available. A 2024 report also lists online study as a historic option. These are past emergency arrangements and cannot be extended beyond their documented period. (S11–S12)

Lithuania has formal distance-learning routes delivered by eligible Lithuanian schools under defined criteria. This is not equivalent to unrestricted home education or an automatic recognition of an online Ukrainian-school record. The evidence reviewed does not identify a current national authority that can approve a parent’s unilateral substitution of Ukrainian online attendance for Lithuanian compulsory-school registration. Ask the municipality/school before relying on any distance-learning arrangement. (S01, S03, S11–S12)

**Safe conclusion:** Ukrainian online schooling alone is **not established** as a substitute for Lithuanian compulsory education. A historic one-year concurrent-registration arrangement does not prove that a current exemption exists. Do not state a categorical “never” where the law provides recognized formal distance-education pathways; the safe boundary is that no current Ukraine-specific stand-alone exemption was found.

## Higher-education admission and recognition

- A person with TP may apply to a university or college. TP establishes neither admission nor a special admissions quota. The institution/programme determines application timing, entrance requirements and documents. (S01–S02, S16, S21)
- Ukrainian secondary-school diploma holders are directed to the chosen HEI. If it has authority to recognize foreign qualifications, it makes the individual academic-recognition decision; otherwise it may refer the applicant to SKVC. (S16–S18)
- Academic recognition of a school-leaving diploma for higher admission is different from recognition of completed university courses. The receiving HEI assesses prior credits/partial study according to its procedures. (S16, S18)
- SKVC’s public documentation page says it can carry out academic recognition for applicants holding a Lithuanian temporary or permanent residence permit, among other groups. Applicants without a complete file may apply under a special refugee/document-loss procedure if the absence has objective reasons and they currently live in Lithuania; indirect evidence and an interview/background report may be used. The procedure is not an automatic diploma reconstruction or a promise of university admission. (S17)
- Lithuanian higher-education institutions offer programmes in Lithuanian and English; language proof and thresholds are set by the programme/HEI. Check the current admission page. (S21–S22)
- A study admission decision does not itself replace immigration requirements for a person without another valid basis to stay. For a current TP holder, do not assert that they must automatically switch to a student permit; no such universal switch requirement was established in the education sources reviewed. (S01–S02)
- Professional recognition for a regulated occupation is separate from academic recognition/admission. This review does not assess the competent professional-recognition route. (S17–S18)

## Fees / scholarships / Ukraine-specific support

### School-age pupils

- State/municipal general education is free, and the government funds textbooks and core teaching/support needs. Private-school tuition and optional/additional services are distinct. Personal supplies are not all guaranteed free. (S01, S14)
- In 2026, free lunch is provided without an income test to pupils in pre-primary and grades 1–2; older pupils may receive free meals and school-supply support under means-tested criteria and municipality procedures. This is not an Ukraine-only benefit. (S15)
- Meal, transport, after-school and other service rules differ. Do not state that every pupil receives all meals, transport, books and supplies at no cost. (S01, S14–S15)
- Active Ukraine-specific pupil provision identified: eight schools running Ukraine’s curriculum in June 2026 and over 200 Ukrainian teachers working in those programmes. The source supplies national aggregate figures only. It does not establish individual eligibility, current capacity at any named school, universal teacher-assistant support, or a guaranteed Ukrainian-language class. (S13)

### Higher education

- Tuition varies by HEI, programme and study level; TP does not create a general tuition waiver. Current programme-specific costs must be checked with the institution. (S19, S21–S22)
- State-funded bachelor/short-cycle/integrated eligibility for foreign applicants is limited. For the route relevant to temporary residents, the applicant must have completed secondary education under the Lithuanian curriculum and meet current statutory/admission criteria. Applicants with Ukrainian secondary qualifications can still apply to self-funded/institution-funded programmes. (S19–S20)
- SMSM’s June 2026 update says state support is being provided to Ukrainian citizens who entered Lithuanian HEIs in 2022 and 2023, describing a €300/month stipend and 60% of study price funded by the state (the remainder by the HEI). The VSF current summary says its €300/month stipend runs through 2026-12-31, but the detailed eligibility page for the stipend says admission between 2022-02-24 and 2022-12-31; the tuition-support eligibility page says 2022 and 2023 entrants. This is a material scope/date discrepancy between current summary/ministry reporting and older detailed fund pages. Do not tell a new 2026/27 applicant that they qualify; seek current VSF confirmation for the individual cohort. (S13, S24)
- The 2026 full-time Master’s state scholarship call included Ukraine (20 awards within 70 total), with €592 monthly in 2026 plus a study-cost grant capped at national standard study cost. The published 2026 call was closed by 2026-11-09, competitive, and nationality/call-based; it was not a general TP entitlement or a newly open 2026/27 application. (S23)
- The Study in Lithuania catalogue listed 2026/27 institution-specific Ukrainian-citizen tuition discounts (including VILNIUS TECH and Vilnius Academy of Arts), with application deadlines in April–August/June 2026. Those deadlines had passed on the research date. These offers are not national waivers and may not apply to TP holders who do not meet the call’s citizenship/programme criteria. (S25)
- No current national guarantee of university dormitory placement, emergency housing or a new universal tuition exemption for Ukrainian TP holders in 2026/27 was established. Individual HEI accommodation and scholarship pages must be checked for live calls. (S21–S25)

## 2026 changes / stale guidance

- **Education-right amendment:** Law No. XV-953, adopted 2026-05-14 and published 2026-05-20, amended Education Law Art. 24 wording on who may study and the state-guaranteed programmes. Use the current consolidation; do not rely on older wording limited to residence-permit holders for the full statutory right. The TP-specific child rule is also in the Aliens Law. (S01–S02)
- **2026–2027 admission technology:** CPIS is used for general/pre-primary admissions in 24 municipalities from the 2026–27 intake. Other municipalities retain their own channel; newly arriving children should contact the local authority/school instead of assuming CPIS coverage nationwide. (S07–S08)
- **Current Ukrainian-curriculum schools:** the Ministry reported eight schools and approximately 3,300 pupils in June 2026. This is a current fact, not a national guarantee of placement or capacity. (S13)
- **No confirmed general Ukraine online exemption:** older arrangements for remote Ukrainian schooling were limited by time and required Lithuania-side registration/support. No extension to 2026 was established. (S11–S12)
- **Higher-education funding:** the 2026 ministry update confirms targeted support for 2022/2023 entrants; it does not create a blanket benefit for new entrants. Public VSF subpages have inconsistent cohort/end-date wording; validate with VSF before publishing applicant-specific eligibility. (S13, S24)
- **2026/27 Master’s call:** the Ukraine-eligible 2026 state Master’s scholarship call had closed by the research date. Do not recommend it as currently open. (S23)
- **2026/27 university discounts:** listed institution-specific Ukrainian-citizen discounts had application deadlines that passed earlier in 2026. Confirm whether a new call exists before directing an applicant to apply. (S25)
- **Older Ukraine FAQ:** its enrolment and learner-support descriptions remain useful as guidance, but it has no clear last-updated date and contains examples that may reflect emergency-era staffing. Treat as qualified administrative guidance and cross-check with the school/municipality; do not promote old fixed-duration or fixed-hour models as current law. (S03)

## Local / institution variation

- Municipalities set admission procedures and priorities for state/municipal general and pre-primary schools, and CPIS is not yet universal. School catchments, available places, document details, late-year intake and overflow arrangements are local. (S06–S09)
- Adaptation format, extra Lithuanian lessons, Ukrainian language provision, class grouping, psychological/social pedagogical support and subject-by-subject transition are organized according to the pupil’s needs and school capacity. The published national guidance does not guarantee a fixed package. (S03–S04, S14)
- The eight Ukrainian-curriculum schools are a distributed institutional offer. Confirm each school’s current programme, location, admissions and available seats directly with the school/municipality. (S13)
- Higher education institutions set programme-specific admission deadlines, language proof, fees and internal scholarships. Only the statutory admissions route is nationwide; a university’s scholarship is not. (S19, S21–S25)

## Unresolved issues

1. The Ukraine-specific Ministry FAQ does not show a clear current update date. Its general school-admission and individualized-support descriptions align with the statutory access framework, but exact 2026 school-level procedures and language-support capacity must be confirmed locally.
2. No current national publication reviewed here provides a uniform procedure for a compulsory-age child when the family’s preferred school has no seat. The municipality must advise the placement route; do not promise a specific school.
3. No current 2026 source establishes a universal identity/health/vaccination-document list for initial school enrolment, or a blanket rule that missing vaccination records prevent admission. Local health paperwork should be checked directly.
4. The exact 2026 operation of the historic one-year Ukrainian online-school arrangement was not established; the primary evidence documents 2022–2023 and does not authorize extension. No current Ukrainian online-only exemption was found.
5. The detailed VSF stipend eligibility page (updated 2024) and stipend summary (updated 2026) do not use consistent admission-cohort/end-date wording; the June 2026 ministry announcement describes 2022/2023 entrants. Exact eligibility/continuation must be confirmed with VSF.
6. A current national catalogue of active 2026/27 Ukrainian university scholarships, tuition exemptions and dormitory support was not established. The published calls found had passed deadlines by the research date; university-specific offers can change.
7. This review marks the boundary for regulated-profession recognition but does not map competent authorities or professional requirements.

## Drafting boundaries

### Safe to state

- Children with the relevant right to stay, including minors with TP, have access to Lithuanian education; compulsory education continues until age 16.
- Early-childhood/pre-school, compulsory pre-primary, primary, basic and secondary education are different programme stages; pre-primary is compulsory for the applicable age group.
- Contact the municipality education division/coordinator or nearest school. Local procedures and 2026 CPIS coverage vary.
- For a child from Ukraine, the school may use documents if available, interview and other information; ask for any available prior school records, but do not call a complete Ukrainian file a universal entry precondition.
- Class placement is individualized, informed by age and learning evidence, and may be adjusted with the parents’ request/consent; no automatic Ukrainian-grade equivalence is established.
- Lack of Lithuanian alone is not an enrolment barrier. Individual Lithuanian-language and educational support can be planned; exact hours and duration are not nationally guaranteed in the sources reviewed.
- Eight schools were reported to run Ukrainian curricula in June 2026; programme and seat availability must be confirmed directly.
- TP holders may apply to higher education; admission, recognition, language criteria, fees and funding depend on law and the selected HEI/programme.
- SKVC/authorized HEIs can assess foreign qualifications; prior university credits are a separate receiving-HEI decision. SKVC’s refugee procedure may assess incomplete records, but does not guarantee recognition/admission.

### Require qualification

- Any statement about mandatory pre-primary starting age, early primary entry, local priority/catchment or late-year intake: state the legal age rule and point to local procedure.
- Any language or adaptation support: describe individual/local organization; do not promise fixed hours, duration, class, teacher or Ukrainian support.
- Any claim about a current Ukrainian online option: distinguish formal Lithuania-based distance learning from the expired/dated emergency arrangement; require current written confirmation from school/municipality.
- Any higher-education state-funded eligibility or support: specify nationality/status, previous secondary curriculum, admission cohort, call year and programme. Verify the current 2026 call and VSF rules.
- Any specific scholarship, fee reduction or dorm room: identify the institution, call, eligibility and deadline; do not generalize it nationally.

### Do not assert

- That a preferred school must accept a child, that every municipality has the same process, or that a current CPIS portal covers all cities/late arrivals.
- That missing Ukrainian school records automatically block admission or that the child must obtain SKVC recognition before joining general school.
- That a pupil is automatically assigned to an exactly equivalent Ukrainian grade.
- That vaccination records are a national precondition to compulsory-school admission or, conversely, that no health documentation is ever needed.
- That a pupil with TP is guaranteed a specific language-support package, a fixed number of Lithuanian/Ukrainian lessons or a fixed adaptation period.
- That Ukrainian online schooling alone satisfies Lithuanian compulsory-school obligations, or that historic 2022–2023 emergency arrangements remain in force.
- That TP guarantees university admission, automatic academic recognition, transfer of credits, free tuition, state-funded place, scholarship or dorm accommodation.
- That a scholarship/fee waiver from one institution or closed 2026 call is a nationwide or currently open entitlement.
- That academic recognition for university admission also authorizes a regulated profession.
