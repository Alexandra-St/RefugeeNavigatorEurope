## Research verdict

**READY FOR CONTENT DRAFTING WITH QUALIFICATIONS.** Current Lithuanian law and Government Resolution No. 224 as amended by Resolution No. 742 establish the national scheme, bounded qualifying groups, Migration Department role, digital residence document, military-duty condition and exceptions, and national end date of 4 March 2028. The 2026 Government and Migration Department notices describe the current replacement process for existing digital permits. Initial application details are less fully established: the Migration Department’s detailed initial-process pages are dated 2023–2024 and include expired dates and locations. Use them only for the limited process distinctions they still evidence; do not copy their obsolete dates, fixed processing period, or branch-specific details as current nationwide instructions. Current initial biometric/photo steps, a complete current document checklist, processing time, and any fee for initial issuance were not established in the reviewed current official materials.

## Compulsory categories, exclusions, and terminology boundaries

### Lithuanian beneficiary scope

The safe description is limited to the groups in Resolution No. 224: (a) Ukrainian citizens resident in Ukraine before 2022-02-24 and displaced from that date because of Russia’s aggression; (b) stateless persons and non-Ukrainian third-country citizens who had international or equivalent national protection in Ukraine before that date; (c) their family members under Decision 2022/382 Art. 2(4); and (d) stateless persons and non-Ukrainian third-country citizens who had a valid Ukrainian permanent residence permit and lawful permanent residence before that date and cannot safely and durably return to their origin country or another receiving state. The national rule also addresses certain people temporarily outside Ukraine and qualifying children born after 2022-02-24. It does **not** establish general coverage for every former Ukrainian resident or adopt every EU optional category. (S01, S05)

Resolution No. 224 point 1¹ adds a 2026 condition when Ukrainian military obligations apply. Resolution No. 742’s national exceptions must be read with it: continuous temporary protection in any EU Member State across the national 2026-08-04 cutoff, and a Lithuanian temporary-protection application already pending at that cutoff. The latter is a national transitional rule. The EU corrigendum’s own exception refers to continuous status in the same Member State; do not collapse the two wordings. (S02, S07–S09)

Law Art. 93 sets individualized refusal grounds. Do not label the military condition an exclusion applying to every applicant without checking whether a Ukrainian military duty applies and whether a transitional exception controls. (S01–S03)

### Status versus document

`Laikinoji apsauga` is temporary protection. `Leidimas laikinai gyventi` is a temporary residence permit. Under the current Government resolution, the Migration Department issues a digital document giving temporary residence rights and confirming temporary protection. The document is not the legal scheme itself and is not a travel document; the Migration Department’s FAQ states that the digital permit does not replace a passport for travel, although the FAQ’s process details are dated. (S01–S03, S12)

## Initial registration and document boundaries

Resolution No. 224 assigns the Migration Department the role of registering eligible people and issuing a registration certificate (with ILTU and ESI identifiers); the resolution also permits certain other state or municipal bodies to register within their responsibilities. Law Art. 95 makes the Migration Department the issuing authority for a temporary residence permit or, under the Government resolution’s conditions, the digital document. A registration certificate and a residence document are distinct outputs. (S01–S03)

MIGRIS is the current administrative channel expressly named for replacing existing digital permits. Migration Department pages last updated in 2023–2024 also describe submitting an initial permit application through MIGRIS followed by one in-person visit, but list expired permit validity dates and office details that cannot safely be reused. The reviewed evidence therefore supports the historical/current platform distinction, but **does not establish a fully refreshed 2026 nationwide initial application workflow**. (S10–S13)

Law Art. 92(2) allows an eligible person to be admitted without a valid travel document, medical insurance, and certain other entry/stay documents where objective circumstances prevented satisfying those requirements. This concerns admission and cannot be paraphrased as “no identity evidence is required for a permit.” The current 2026 Department guidance says military-duty evidence may be shown by a passport exit stamp or other official proof, such as a Reserv+ document; these are examples, and the Resolution requires proof where the military obligation applies. (S01–S03, S09)

The 2026 appointment and original-document requirement, attendance with parent/guardian for children over two, photo option for under-two children, and guardian form for children under 16 all belong to the **permit replacement** process. The current reviewed sources do not establish the same requirements for initial application, nor a universal initial fingerprint or biometric procedure. (S10)

The digital document is issued to the person’s MIGRIS account under the 2026 replacement guidance. Replacement is free. Initial application fee, a current processing deadline, universal initial document list, current fingerprint/photo requirements, and any categorical address-declaration prerequisite are not established in the reviewed current official evidence. Do not copy 2023 FAQ processing time into current content. (S03, S10–S13)

## EU and Lithuanian date boundaries

1. **National start:** Resolution No. 224 was adopted on 2022-03-16 and applies protection from 2022-03-04. (S01, S06)
2. **Current national period:** Resolution No. 742 amended the Lithuanian rule to 2028-03-04 and took effect 2026-10-09. The Interior Ministry explicitly says Lithuania aligned its regulation to the EU extension. (S01–S02, S11)
3. **EU extension timing:** Decision 2026/1912 Art. 1 establishes protection through 2028-03-04 and applies the extension from 2027-03-05. (S07–S08)
4. **Military condition timing:** Art. 2 applies from 2026-08-05 after corrigendum; the corrected EU continuity cutoff is 2026-08-04. Lithuanian Resolution No. 742 also defines its national transition exceptions, including pending Lithuanian cases. (S02, S07–S09)
5. **Individual document:** the law distinguishes the protection status from the permit/digital document. Existing-document replacement through MIGRIS is a separate administrative action; the reviewed sources do not prove uniform automatic reissue or that all existing documents carry the same expiry date. (S01–S03, S10–S11)

Do not use the original, uncorrected EU military dates, the 2023 permit dates, or the EU decision alone to assert that Lithuania has changed an individual permit. (S07–S13)

## 2026 changes / unresolved issues

- **National legal amendment:** Resolution No. 742 was adopted 2026-10-07, published 2026-10-08 and effective 2026-10-09. It amends Resolution No. 224, sets the national date through 2028-03-04, adds the military-obligation condition and national transition clauses, and provides for certain digital documents to be valid through 2028-03-04. (S01–S02)
- **Military-duty condition:** from 2026-08-05, where Ukrainian military obligations apply, the national rule requires that they have been fulfilled and proof provided. The Migration Department gives examples of proof but does not make that example list exhaustive. (S01–S02, S07–S09)
- **Transition nuance:** the Migration Department’s 2026-08-13/14 guidance says the change will not affect requests submitted before 2026-08-05. Resolution No. 742 later specifies national exceptions using the 2026-08-04 date, covering continuous TP in any EU Member State and pending Lithuanian requests. For edge cases around the cutoff, follow the exact current legal text and the individual administrative decision; do not compress the guidance and legal exception into a new eligibility rule. (S02, S09)
- **Document replacement:** the Department began accepting replacement applications on 2026-10-05. The request is completed in MIGRIS, the applicant books and attends an office visit with originals, any Migration Department customer-service office may be selected regardless of residence, replacement is free, and the resulting digital document goes to the MIGRIS account. This is documented as replacement, not a full first-application workflow. (S10–S11)

## Drafting boundaries

### Safe to state

- Lithuania’s legal term is `laikinoji apsauga` (temporary protection); its main national instrument is Government Resolution No. 224 under the Law on the Legal Status of Foreigners.
- The bounded national beneficiary groups in Resolution No. 224 points 1.1–1.3, preserving each nationality/residence/displacement/family/arrival condition.
- The Migration Department registers eligible people, issues the registration certificate specified in the resolution, and is responsible for the residence document/digital document.
- The national protection and digital-document period was amended to 2028-03-04 by Resolution No. 742, effective 2026-10-09; this is independently confirmed by the Ministry of the Interior.
- Where Ukrainian military obligations apply, the 2026 national rule requires fulfillment and proof, subject to the exact national transition exceptions. Keep the “where applicable” condition and do not use an exhaustive evidence list unless a current official source establishes one.
- Existing digital-permit replacement applications are handled through MIGRIS with a booked in-person visit and originals; replacement is free and the new digital document is delivered to the MIGRIS account.
- Lack of a valid passport may not prevent admission in the objective circumstances specified by Law Art. 92(2); this does not waive all identity or eligibility evidence for an individual application.

### Require qualification

- EU-covered groups and family-member rules must be stated with the exact Lithuanian Resolution No. 224 limits; do not generalize from EU optional provisions.
- Distinguish temporary protection, the registration certificate, and the digital residence document.
- Explain that MIGRIS has been used for initial submissions, but the current 2026 evidence reviewed here gives detailed steps for replacement, not a fully refreshed initial-application workflow.
- Apply the national military transition exceptions in Resolution No. 742 precisely and distinguish them from the narrower same-Member-State exception in the EU corrigendum.
- Separate the EU extension date, Lithuania’s national amendment effective date, and a person’s document validity/replacement process.
- Age-related attendance and image instructions in the 2026 notice are for replacement of an existing document.

### Do not assert

- That every Ukrainian national, every former Ukrainian resident, or every non-Ukrainian third-country national qualifies.
- That Lithuania has adopted every optional EU category.
- That temporary protection in another EU country automatically creates Lithuanian protection, allows concurrent protection, or makes transfer automatic.
- That initial application is wholly online, or that an initial applicant universally must provide specified fingerprints/photo/biometrics based on the replacement notice.
