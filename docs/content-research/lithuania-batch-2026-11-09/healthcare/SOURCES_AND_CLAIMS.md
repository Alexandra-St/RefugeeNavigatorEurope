# Lithuania — Healthcare Access, Proof and Urgent Care

**Research checked:** 2026-11-09
**Baseline:** `08e210219af6d70bf8c6d7b2a4874a08cd709981`
**Scope:** exactly `healthcare-access`, `healthcare-proof`, and `healthcare-urgent`. Research only; this is a national evidence review, not a medical or individual insurance decision.

## Scope

This review distinguishes (1) state-funded emergency and necessary care for people entitled to or granted temporary protection (TP), (2) full compulsory health insurance (CHI/PSD), and (3) provider registration and proof. It does not provide a complete clinical service directory, individual reimbursement calculation, or local appointment checklist. No Italian SSN/ASL/STP terminology is applicable here.

## Research verdict

**READY FOR CONTENT DRAFTING WITH QUALIFICATIONS.** Lithuanian law provides a specific floor of state-funded emergency medical assistance and other necessary healthcare for people granted TP and for people entitled to TP while their application is pending, through the applicable TP period. “Necessary” services are those for which omission could worsen the patient’s condition enough to require emergency medical assistance. This is not the same as full CHI and does not mean every routine service is automatically covered. VLK’s current Ukraine/TP FAQ confirms the service boundary, identifies the primary-care institutions to approach, and says a family doctor, dentist, or psychiatrist assesses whether a necessary service is needed. [LT-HC-S01–S04]

Full CHI is available to TP beneficiaries who meet another legal insurance basis: notably employed people with the required Lithuanian residence/work basis and contributions, and TP holders within specified state-insured categories such as minors and defined pension/disability groups. It is not automatic for every adult upon TP grant. Employment-based CHI generally begins from the employment/contribution basis; the special TP state-insured category begins only when the person has been granted TP and meets the listed category. The precise register-effective date should be confirmed with VLK if not displayed. [LT-HC-S01, S05–S07]

Proof is also layered. A TP residence permit/digital document proves immigration status, not by itself active CHI. A Migration Department-issued registration/status paper has historically carried a personal ESI patient identifier used by care providers/pharmacies; ESI is distinct from the national personal code and from the CHI register. Current guidance confirms digital permits can be saved or printed, but the reviewed 2026 pages do not confirm whether every current digital permit displays ESI. Do not present an ESI number, digital permit or personal code alone as proof of full CHI. [LT-HC-S08–S11]

## Healthcare access and insurance boundaries

### Two separate entitlements

1. **Emergency and necessary care.** Law on Health Insurance Art. 6(5) provides budget-funded emergency medical care and “necessary services” for people granted TP and for those entitled to TP pending the decision. The statutory definition is care whose omission could worsen the patient’s health enough that emergency medical assistance would be required. This entitlement is not full CHI. [S01–S04]
2. **Full CHI (PSD).** A TP beneficiary qualifies through a separate basis, such as employment with the relevant Lithuanian residence/work status and contributions, or a listed state-funded category. VLK’s current TP-specific list includes under-18s and persons meeting defined Lithuanian/Ukrainian pension/disability criteria. An adult who is merely unemployed or a student is not thereby fully insured. [S01, S05–S07]
3. **Service versus register.** Necessary/emergency rights can exist without full CHI registration. For full CHI-financed planned care, the insurance status must be active in the VLK register and the person ordinarily registers with a contracted primary-care institution/family doctor. [S04–S08]

### When coverage starts and ends

- **TP applicant before decision:** state-funded emergency and necessary services apply to a person who is legally entitled to TP, pending grant/refusal decision and no longer than the relevant TP period. The date is not “arrival” alone; the legal entitlement/TP application facts matter. This is not a CHI insurance start date. [S01–S04]
- **TP state-insured category:** VLK says only the specified TP subgroups are state-funded CHI insured. The basis is TP granted plus the qualifying category; do not treat a pending TP applicant as already in that full-CHI TP subgroup. Confirm the exact register-effective date with VLK if delayed or disputed. [S01, S05]
- **Employment:** VLK states employee CHI normally begins from the day the employment basis for contributions arises. Employer/worker contributions and insurance-record status matter. VLK’s general materials also require the applicable Lithuanian residence status for third-country nationals; coverage for a person working under the TP application-stage work right before permit issuance is not fully reconciled in the public health pages reviewed. [S01, S05–S07]
- **Self-employment:** lawful individual activity can create a separate contribution basis; VLK says legally working self-employed residents pay CHI contributions. This package does not calculate contributions or determine whether an individual TP applicant’s document meets a self-employment registration rule. [S05–S07]
- **Unemployment:** a temporarily resident foreigner may qualify after at least six months’ work and registration with UŽT under the general CHI route; other state-funded criteria differ. TP status alone is not enough for every adult. [S01, S05]
- **Minors, dependants and pension/disability groups:** TP minors and the specified pension/disability categories have TP-specific state-funded CHI. Other dependants are not automatically covered merely because a parent/spouse has TP or CHI. General statutory family, pregnancy and care categories may separately apply when their conditions and documents are met. [S01, S05]
- **End:** full CHI ceases when its legal category/contribution conditions lapse under the Health Insurance Law; TP-based necessary-care eligibility is tied to TP entitlement/grant and applicable TP period. A TP extension to 2028-03-04 is not itself a substitute for checking active CHI status. [S01, S03, S06, S18]

### Routine care when fully insured

- Register with one chosen family-doctor/primary-care institution that has a VLK/territorial fund contract. The institution may be public, municipal or contracted private; a non-contracted provider may charge. Local patient lists and capacity matter. [S07–S08]
- Family-doctor services are the normal primary-care entry. The family doctor can deliver care and make referrals for specialist, diagnostic or hospital services as required. Covered outpatient specialist services normally require referral; emergency inpatient and specialist care does not. [S07]
- CHI covers the nationally defined package, including primary and specialist care, hospital services, preventive care, rehabilitation, mental-health pathways, dentistry, certain reimbursable medicines and medical aids. Individual items remain subject to referral, list, provider contract, clinical criteria and cost-sharing rules. [S04, S07, S09–S10]
- An ESI identifier can help the provider retrieve the record; it is not itself insurance. If VLK’s status check says uninsured despite an apparent employment/state category, contact the VLK/territorial fund and relevant contribution administrator rather than assuming the digital TP residence document automatically activates full CHI. [S06–S08, S13–S15]

## Primary / specialist / hospital care

- **Full CHI:** register with one contracted family-doctor clinic for routine primary care; obtain referral for ordinary specialist/hospital pathways where required. Emergency services may be accessed without a referral. Provider choice is subject to contract, local lists/capacity and service rules. [S07–S08]
- **TP holder without CHI:** request assessment at a primary-care institution (polyclinic, family clinic or family-doctor centre). VLK states a family doctor, dentist or psychiatrist assesses whether the statutory necessary-care threshold is met. If immediate emergency, go to emergency department/call 112. [S04, S11–S12]
- **Hospital:** CHI-funded inpatient care follows coverage/referral rules, with emergency exceptions. For uninsured TP holders, state funding attaches to emergency/qualifying necessary services, not every routine admission or test. [S01–S04, S07, S09]
- **Dental:** full CHI covers defined primary dental care through contracted providers, but patients may pay for materials/medicines and extras; children and certain qualifying groups have exceptions. For uninsured TP beneficiaries, the current special rule funds emergency/necessary care if criteria are met. Migration Department’s 2023 page mentions voluntary donated “free necessary dental care”; this programme is not evidence of a nationwide 2026 entitlement. [S04, S09, S16]
- **Medicines:** full CHI reimburses only medicines/medical aids in the approved lists and under prescribing criteria; outpatient co-payments may apply. Uninsured TP holders can receive selected prescription medicines for conditions linked to necessary healthcare under V-109, not all prescriptions free. [S04, S10, S17]
- **Mental health:** CHI beneficiaries use ordinary primary and specialist mental-health pathways through contracted providers; uninsured TP holder’s psychiatrist may assess whether requested care meets the necessary-service threshold. No universal TP-specific psychotherapy, language-access or waiting-time guarantee was established. [S04, S07]

## Costs and reimbursement boundaries

- **Insured care:** most covered care is paid by CHIF when delivered by a contracted provider and used according to referral/clinical rules. Some dental materials, outpatient medicine co-payments, non-reimbursed items, optional upgrades and non-contracted care may be charged. Ask for the reason and price before agreeing to a paid/part-paid service. [S07, S09–S10]
- **Uninsured TP:** state pays emergency medical assistance and services meeting the statutory necessary-care standard; specified prescriptions may be publicly reimbursed. This is not “all care free,” and clinicians assess whether the statutory threshold is met. [S01–S04, S17]
- **Emergency department:** no referral is needed for emergency care. A non-urgent case may be redirected to scheduled care or billed; the TP-specific state-funded standard still applies where the necessary-care threshold is met. Do not promise that every ED visit or ambulance transport is free to every uninsured foreigner. [S01–S04, S11]
- **No single co-payment amount:** cost depends on service, medicine, provider contract, reimbursement list, referral, materials and patient category. No universal fee or regional price was established for all TP scenarios. [S07, S09–S10]

## Urgent and emergency care

1. **Immediate emergency:** call **112**, free 24/7, for emergency medical service (ambulance) and other emergency responders. The call can be made without a SIM. Call-takers can communicate in Lithuanian, Russian, Polish or English. [S11–S12]
2. **Go to hospital emergency department** if there is a real threat to health/life and the person can travel safely. Emergency medical care does not require a family-doctor referral. [S07, S11]
3. **Urgent advice, not immediate emergency:** VLK identifies **113** as a medical advice number for when there is no immediate threat; dispatcher may send an ambulance if needed. It is not a replacement for 112 in a life-threatening emergency. After-hours urgent outpatient service locations differ by city/provider; VLK gives examples such as emergency medical offices in some cities and hospital emergency departments elsewhere. [S11–S12]
4. **TP-specific payment:** emergency and qualifying necessary care for an entitled/granted TP person has a state-budget basis. If the clinician concludes the visit is not urgent/necessary or the service is outside the TP uninsured package, payment may be due. Show the available TP/identity document and ESI if known, but do not delay urgent care to retrieve them. [S01–S04, S09, S11, S13]
5. **Follow-up:** once the emergency is stabilized, routine follow-up goes through the registered family doctor/contracted care route if CHI-insured. If not insured, seek primary-care assessment for whether continuing services meet the necessary-care definition; ask VLK/provider about the payer before accepting noncovered planned care. No automatic long-term follow-up coverage is established solely by having visited an emergency department. [S04, S07–S09]

## Children / pregnancy / mental-health boundaries

- **Children:** TP beneficiaries under 18 are an express state-CHI group when TP has been granted. Children entitled to TP pending a decision and other children covered by the statute receive emergency/necessary care, National Immunoprophylaxis Programme vaccination and preventive examinations. Keep medical/provider enrollment and exact document process distinct from the legal right. [S01–S02, S05]
- **Pregnancy and birth:** VLK’s current TP-specific page lists state-insured groups but does not list pregnancy alone as a TP-specific automatic full-CHI category. General state-insurance rules can cover certain non-working women from 28 weeks’ pregnancy through 56 days after delivery when the legal residence/category conditions are met. Uninsured TP people retain emergency/necessary-service rights. A Migration Department page last updated in 2023 lists pregnancy/post-natal care for uninsured Ukrainian refugees, but routine maternity scope in 2026 was not confirmed from a current detailed rule; verify with VLK or the provider. Pregnancy complications/childbirth onset warrant emergency contact; 112 guidance expressly includes those cases. [S01–S05, S11, S16]
- **Mental health:** full CHI follows standard family-doctor, primary mental-health and specialist referral/contract pathways. For non-CHI TP holders the primary psychiatrist can assess necessary-care eligibility. Do not promise that all counselling/psychotherapy or language support is funded. [S04, S07]
- **Vaccination:** children receive vaccines under the National Immunoprophylaxis Programme within the statutory TP child-care entitlement; routine clinic location, record reconciliation and vaccine schedule are provider/municipality matters. Do not generalize the old COVID-specific 2023 guidance to a current special Ukraine-only vaccination programme. [S02, S16]

## 2026 changes

- **TP duration:** Lithuania’s current TP scheme is extended through **2028-03-04**, subject to its eligibility and transition conditions. That affects the period during which TP-linked statutory rights may operate; it does not create full CHI for every holder or automatically renew a missing personal permit/record. [S03, S18]
- **Digital permits:** Migration Department’s 2026 guidance says current TP permits are digital and may be printed or saved on a device; failure to renew can affect access to services. The reviewed guidance does not characterize that digital file as an insurance card or confirm ESI placement. [S03, S15]
- **Insurance status:** Current VLK FAQ continues to distinguish full CHI categories from budget-funded necessary care for uninsured TP holders. No 2026 law change was found converting all TP holders into full-CHI insured people. [S01, S04–S07]
- **Medicines:** current VLK FAQ and Ministry V-109 materials preserve a specific selected-prescription pathway for uninsured TP holders; ordinary CHI reimbursable-medicine rules and co-payment mechanisms remain separate. No evidence supports a blanket 2026 free-prescription guarantee. [S04, S10, S17]
- **Mental health, routine maternity and local programmes:** no current nationwide TP-only 2026 expansion with universal access, cost or language guarantee was established. The older Migration Department health page is dated 2023 and is not treated as proof of current programme availability. [S04, S16]

## Regional / provider variation

- **Nationwide law:** TP emergency/necessary care, CHI categories, contribution basis, statutory service definition and children’s vaccination/preventive-check entitlements are national. [S01–S03]
- **Provider contract and registration:** CHI-financed routine care requires a provider with a territorial fund contract and primary-care registration. An institution outside the contracted network may charge. Patients’ lists, appointment process, specialist referral workflow, service availability and emergency outpatient organization vary by provider/locality. [S07–S09, S11]
- **No regional healthcare card equivalent to TP:** VLK’s insurance record is national; ESI identifies the patient. Local institution/provider records and capacity affect operational access but do not change the statutory coverage basis. [S06–S08, S13–S15]
- **No local example generalized:** older Migration Department listings of donated dental care, diabetes supplies or COVID vaccination are not treated as nationwide current public entitlements. [S16]

## Unresolved issues

1. **Full CHI for a TP applicant working before document issuance.** Work law and the Health Insurance Law use distinct status/insurance triggers; public VLK pages do not expressly reconcile a TP applicant’s permission to start work before the physical/digital permit is issued with the third-country resident CHI record. The person should check VLK/Sodra status; do not assume full CHI begins merely from the work-right date.
2. **Exact CHI registry-effective date for TP-specific state-funded categories.** Legal category and state-funded basis are clear; the reviewed English FAQ does not expose a precise date for every document/status combination. Confirm the insured-register date with VLK if not displayed.
3. **Current ESI display/issuance with 2026 digital permit formats.** 2024 guidance identifies a registration/status card carrying ESI; 2026 Migration Department material confirms digital permits, but does not say whether ESI is embedded in all digital formats or how to request a duplicate identifier.
4. **Routine maternity/post-natal scope for uninsured TP.** The current statute and VLK FAQ establish emergency/necessary services; the Migration Department’s Ukraine health page (last updated 2023) lists pregnancy/post-natal care more broadly. Current 2026 detailed scope and exact eligibility/payment for routine antenatal visits were not established. Check VLK/provider and avoid a blanket free-maternity claim.
5. **Service determination/payment for borderline urgent care.** “Necessary” is defined by likely deterioration to emergency level, with primary-care clinician assessment. The sources do not supply a single fee schedule for every rejected/nonqualifying case, provider, or ambulance-related service.
6. **Routine dental and mental-health access for uninsured TP.** Current sources support necessary-service assessment and special children’s rules; they do not establish universal free routine dentistry, psychotherapy, interpreter support, provider availability or wait times.
7. **Insurance for adult dependants and pregnancy category overlap.** TP-specific state-insured groups are defined, and some foreign residents may qualify through separate general state-funded categories. The reviewed material does not settle every spouse/dependant case; check their own status and documents.

## Drafting boundaries

### Safe to state

- Lithuania separates full CHI from state-funded emergency and qualifying necessary care for TP beneficiaries.
- A person entitled to TP can receive emergency and qualifying necessary healthcare while a TP decision is pending, subject to statutory eligibility and TP period.
- Uninsured TP holders do not automatically receive every routine service free; a clinician determines whether a requested service meets the necessary-care rule.
- Full CHI can apply through employment/contributions or specified state-insured categories; TP-specific state insurance includes under-18s and listed pension/disability categories.
- Full CHI requires checking the active VLK insurance record and, for routine family-doctor care, registration with a contracted primary-care institution.
- TP permit/digital document, ESI identifier, national personal code, CHI record and EHIC have different functions.
- For an immediate emergency call 112; 113 is advice where there is no immediate threat. Do not delay emergency help for a card.
- Routine insured care may involve referral, provider-contract rules, medicine co-payments, dental materials or non-covered charges.
- Children have specific national-programme vaccination and preventive-care protections under the statute; pregnant persons’ general insurance eligibility and care conditions depend on their specific category.

### Require qualification

- State that employee CHI generally begins when the employment/contribution basis arises, but verify the individual TP permit/record and do not equate work authorization with CHI activation.
- Explain selected prescription-medicine funding for uninsured TP holders only for the conditions and procedure in current VLK/V-109 guidance.
- Describe general pregnancy/state-insurance categories only with their residence, status, timing and documentary conditions; refer routine maternity scope to VLK/provider.
- Describe specialist, hospital, dental and mental-health services as available under full CHI or where necessary-care criteria apply, subject to referral/provider contracts and capacity.
- Explain ESI as the patient identifier described by official guidance; do not guarantee it is printed on every current digital permit.
- State emergency department/ambulance costs only according to the patient’s TP/CHI status and clinical classification; the 112 call itself is free.

### Do not assert

- That every TP holder is automatically fully insured, or that every adult’s CHI is state-paid.
- That TP alone gives blanket free routine primary care, all specialists, hospital stays, prescriptions, dental work, mental-health treatment or maternity care.
- That a TP digital residence permit is a CHI card, an ESI identifier, or necessarily displays ESI.
- That ESI, personal code, EHIC and VLK CHI status are interchangeable.
- That no personal/immigration evidence is ever needed operationally, or that a missing card removes the underlying statutory emergency/necessary right.
- That all emergency-department visits, ambulance transport, or care to all uninsured foreigners is free; distinguish the specific TP entitlement and clinical classification.
- That the EU TP extension by itself extends CHI or a specific medical-card validity period.
- That a 2023 volunteer dental, diabetes-supply or COVID offer remains an open national 2026 programme.
- That provider availability, waiting time, language access, co-payment or local registration process is identical nationwide.
