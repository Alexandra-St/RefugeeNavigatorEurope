# Lithuania — Initial Accommodation, Private Rental and Housing Support

**Research checked:** 2026-11-09
**Scope:** housing route during initial arrival/temporary protection, ordinary private rental, residence declaration, housing and social assistance. Research only; this is not an individual eligibility decision or a promise of accommodation or benefit.

## Scope and research verdict

This review distinguishes (1) short-term state/municipal accommodation while a temporary-protection (TP) residence-permit application is pending, (2) an ordinary private tenancy, (3) declaration of a place of residence, and (4) separate national and municipal housing/social-support schemes. These are related but legally distinct routes.

**READY WITH QUALIFICATION for content drafting across the housing aspects.** Current national law establishes a time-limited free accommodation route for eligible applicants awaiting a decision, and a separate right to seek housing support for people granted TP. It does not guarantee a bed, a particular location, a private host, or ongoing free accommodation. Ordinary tenancy rules can be stated at national level, but actual contract terms, deposit amount, landlord practice, utility arrangements, and municipal assistance vary. Residence declaration is not the lease and is not the same as registering a lease in the Real Estate Register. Multiple housing supports exist, but eligibility, amounts and application procedures differ; no general automatic rent payment or immediate social-housing placement is established.

The most operationally important limits are: free state/municipal accommodation pending a TP decision is limited to six months from registration of the residence-permit application; a fixed-term residential lease must be in writing; advance rent may not be demanded beyond the first month, while no general statutory residential-deposit cap was established; and ordinary rent compensation requires a qualifying registered lease and means test, whereas the TP-specific statutory route removes some of those conditions but still requires an application and local administration. ## Current program and stale-information boundaries

- **Current national accommodation rule:** free state/municipal accommodation for the statutory group pending a TP decision is capped at six months from registration of the permit application; after that, institutional accommodation may be available on paid provider terms. (S01)
- **Possible extension:** EUAA describes a narrow extra period after sudden involuntary homelessness, with a longer period for vulnerable people. The exact current primary order and procedure were not verified; present only as a lead for direct confirmation, not an assured entitlement. (S05)
- **RIA rather than old volunteer pages:** RIA’s current institutional role is evidenced. Older 2022 pages about volunteer hosts or placement within 72 hours are historical and must not be represented as a current guarantee. (S03–S06)
- **TP duration:** Lithuania’s TP period is extended through 2028-03-04. This does not itself extend free housing or any municipal rent-support period. (S07)
- **Host reimbursement:** Vilnius’s 2022 notice stated that compensation to hosts for free accommodation ended on 2023-12-31. Do not direct hosts or residents to this as a current programme. (S23)
- **One-time benefits:** Migration Department’s national overview was updated in 2023; current municipal pages show some schemes remain listed, but the older categories and lease conditions should be rechecked with the relevant municipality. The Vilnius 2026 amount is not national. (S17–S19)
- **Municipal lease-compensation renewal:** VMB’s 2025-12 notice shows period-specific renewals, not a permanent or currently open deadline. (S20–S21)
- **Historical Vilnius housing top-ups and pending-decision benefit:** 2022 municipal arrangements and the current service listing are local leads only; present funding/availability was not established in this review. (S22–S23)
- **2026 social-housing change:** current SADM information and legislative material reflect the waiting-time target and actual-rent compensation rule. Do not describe the three-year point as a guaranteed move-in date. (S15, S25)
- **2026 utility / low-income support:** current SADM information includes TP beneficiaries and 2026 amounts/formula context. Recheck annually updated thresholds and municipal application procedure before quoting amounts. (S16)

## Drafting boundaries

### Safe to state

- A statutory free accommodation route exists for the specified TP applicant group while the residence-permit application is pending, for up to six months from registration; placement, location, availability and any extension are not guaranteed by the sources reviewed.
- RIA is the current institutional reception/accommodation lead; an individual should confirm intake and availability with the competent authority.
- If the six-month period ends, institutional accommodation may continue only under the provider’s rules and on paid terms.
- Fixed-term residential leases must be written. A written contract is prudent and should identify occupants, rent, utilities, deposit, duration, termination and return conditions.
- Rent cannot be demanded more than one month in advance. No general statutory deposit cap was established in this review; do not quote a universal cap.
- Lease registration, residence declaration, tax reporting and immigration status are separate matters. Registration may be needed for third-party effects and some assistance routes; declaration procedures depend on residence evidence and municipality.
- People granted TP can apply for housing support, including social housing and specified rent compensation, under statutory and local conditions. TP is not automatic approval.
- Means-tested social/utility assistance and housing assistance are distinct; local authorities assess applications and municipality-specific thresholds/procedures apply.

### Require qualification

- Any statement about the narrow extra accommodation period, vulnerability test, or how a person is referred: seek current primary-order and RIA confirmation first.
- Any exact rent-compensation amount, settlement grant, income/property threshold, renewal date, document checklist or processing time: specify municipality and year and confirm the current service page.
- TP-specific rent compensation: explain the statutory exemption from specified ordinary means/term tests, while retaining application, agreement-evidence and local implementation conditions.
- Social housing: explicitly state the queue and that the statutory waiting-time target is not a guaranteed offer or immediate accommodation.
- Residence declaration: explain accepted evidence and owner-consent alternative only for the relevant municipality; do not make a nationwide claim based solely on Vilnius guidance.

### Do not assert

- That every displaced Ukrainian receives accommodation, a bed immediately, private-host placement, or free housing for the whole TP period.
- That historical 72-hour or volunteer placement offers remain a current national procedure.
- That a free accommodation extension is certain or that the extra one/three-month details are fully established by a current primary source in this review.
- That a private landlord must accept an applicant, may never refuse, or must accept a particular immigration document as a tenancy offer.
- That every lease must be registered for validity, or that every tenant must file the lease with VMI.
- That there is a nationally fixed deposit cap, standard deposit amount, or guaranteed return without contractual assessment.
- That a residence declaration is the same as a lease, lease registration, or a right to immigration status.
- That TP alone guarantees social housing, rent compensation, a one-off grant, or utility assistance.
- That a Vilnius programme, sum or checklist applies across Lithuania or remains funded/open merely because a web page remains accessible.
- That the 2028 TP end date extends housing or rent support automatically.

## Unresolved issues

1. The current primary legal order underlying the additional short accommodation period described by EUAA was not verified from an accessible authoritative text. Do not include its exact duration or eligibility as settled user-facing guidance until confirmed with RIA/municipality or the current order.
2. Current nationwide reception intake channel, capacity, waiting time and geographic placement are not published in the reviewed primary sources. An individual must confirm availability.
3. Current VMB page on TP-specific rent compensation was intermittently inaccessible during review. The statutory route is established, but the up-to-date Vilnius checklist, calculation and funding period require direct confirmation.
4. Municipal one-time settlement assistance is unevenly documented. A 2023 national summary and 2026 Vilnius service page do not establish a nationwide current amount or uniform eligibility checklist.
5. Residence-declaration public instructions are partly old or municipal. Verify the current route and required evidence with the local eldership/municipality before asserting a specific document list.
6. No general statutory residential-deposit cap was found in the reviewed Civil Code provisions. This is a bounded search finding, not proof that no other generally applicable rule or contract-specific restriction exists.

## Final topic verdict

**READY FOR CONTENT DRAFTING WITH QUALIFICATIONS.** The national accommodation limit, core rental-contract rules, distinction between declaration and tenancy, and statutory housing-support eligibility boundary are sufficiently established. Draft with clear language that accommodation and benefits require individual assessment and may depend on capacity, funding and municipality. Do not publish a guaranteed placement, a fixed nationwide benefit amount, a universal deposit cap, or the unverified extra-accommodation extension as fact. Recheck municipal procedures and annual thresholds before operational guidance.
