# Lithuania — Employment Rights During Temporary Protection

**Research checked:** 2026-11-09
**Baseline:** `f104a6011599562866ac4920c83e5fb13c4e561a`
**Scope:** exactly `work-permission`, `work-employer-duties`, and `work-minimum-pay`. Research only; this is not an individual immigration or employment decision.

## Scope

This review covers employed work and self-employment boundaries for people entitled to, or granted, temporary protection (TP) in Lithuania; baseline employer obligations; and statutory minimum pay current on the research date. It does not provide a complete tax, business-registration, professional-licensing, payroll calculation, or individual eligibility guide. Temporary protection, an ordinary employment-based temporary residence permit, an EU Blue Card, and VMI registration for individual activity are separate legal/administrative matters.

## Research verdict

**READY FOR CONTENT DRAFTING WITH QUALIFICATIONS.** Article 94 of Lithuania’s Law on the Legal Status of Foreigners gives people entitled to temporary protection the right to work and pursue self-employment while a decision on protection is pending, no longer than the applicable TP period; the Migration Department’s current public guidance states that work may start after applying for TP. The law exempts this group from obtaining a work permit. This is the TP-specific authorization basis, not a general rule that any pending residence application or receipt allows work. [LT-EMP-S01–S03]

Ordinary Lithuanian employment, prior Sodra registration, payroll/social-insurance, workplace safety and employment-information rules continue to apply. UŽT says TP-entitled people and TP holders with the relevant Lithuanian permit do not need an Employment Service document authorizing work. The reviewed material identifies no separate Ukraine-specific hiring procedure that replaces the ordinary employer process. The statute’s third-country-worker reporting mechanism and its precise application to each TP document/cohort should be handled conservatively; do not imply that the work-permit exemption waives Sodra or other required reporting. [LT-EMP-S04–S08]

As of 2026-11-09, the national minimum monthly wage (MMA) is **€1,153 gross** and the minimum hourly rate (MVA) is **€7.05**, effective 2026-01-01 under Government Resolution No. 700 of 2025-10-16. The monthly minimum may be paid only for unqualified work; the hourly floor remains relevant according to the applicable working-time/pay arrangement. A future enacted rate is already set by Government Resolution No. 509 of 2026-07-01: from 2027-01-01, €1,245 gross monthly and €7.61 hourly. Do not present either minimum as net pay, and do not say the monthly amount is a universal salary for every skilled full-time job. [LT-EMP-S09–S12]

## Work authorization boundaries

1. **Exact statutory basis and start point.** Under Law Art. 94(1), a foreigner who is entitled to TP has the listed rights until a decision granting/refusing TP, but no longer than the TP period. Point 4 expressly grants the right to work, exempts the person from a work permit, and permits self-employment. Art. 94(2) grants TP beneficiaries the corresponding listed rights during TP. The Migration Department states operationally: a person may start work after applying for TP. Therefore say **after applying for temporary protection**, subject to being within the group legally entitled to TP; do not say on arrival or on any registration receipt. [S01–S03]
2. **No separate work permit for TP right.** The statutory exemption is express. UŽT separately says there is no need for its work-authorization document for the covered Ukraine TP group. This differs from an ordinary employment-based TRP, a Blue Card, a permit/market test for other nationals, and an application for a new residence ground. [S01, S03–S04]
3. **Before physical document.** Current Migration Department guidance makes application the start point and does not condition work on first receiving the physical residence document. A person’s application must be for Lithuanian TP, not an unrelated residence application. The TP entitlement and exact case facts still matter. [S01–S02]
4. **Employed / seasonal work.** The statute’s TP work right is not stated as restricted to a single sector. Seasonal employee remains an employee under the applicable seasonal/contract and Sodra rules. No distinct seasonal TP work permit or special seasonal Ukraine hiring process was identified. Do not transfer the broader TP rule to someone who lacks TP entitlement. [S01, S03, S06–S07]
5. **Self-employment.** TP law includes self-employment. VMI registration is a separate tax/administrative step; VMI says third-country nationals operating through a permanent base must provide a Lithuanian temporary or permanent residence permit with the VMI request. VMI’s Ukrainian-language guidance says individual activity must be registered and income declared/taxed. The relationship between the statutory “after applying for TP” work start point and VMI’s permit-document requirement in a particular pending case is not fully explained in the reviewed public pages; verify that document edge case with VMI/Migration Department before operational advice. [S01–S03, S14–S15]
6. **Pending ordinary permit / 2026 rules.** Art. 94(1)(5) is a distinct and limited rule for an entitled person who files/renews a TRP on specified Art. 40(1)(4) or (10) grounds; it includes employment and self-employment pending decision and exemption from the ordinary work permit/Employment Service decision. Do not say every residence application, renewal, or receipt gives this right. The 2026 amendments effective 2026-05-22 introduced other foreign-worker and employer rules, which remain separate. [S01, S04, S08]
7. **End of authorization.** TP-based work access is bounded by TP entitlement/period. Do not advise continuing work on an expired/withdrawn TP document or pending unrelated application without identifying another valid legal work basis. The person/employer should confirm the legal position where TP is refused, withdrawn, expires, or another residence title is issued. [S01–S02]

## Employer duties

- **Verify the basis.** Confirm the worker is within the TP entitlement/grant and record the relevant evidence using the employer’s ordinary compliance process. The Migration Department decides TP entitlement; UŽT does not issue a TP decision. The no-work-permit rule does not authorize every Ukrainian citizen regardless of status. [S01–S03]
- **Employment contract.** For subordinate employment, VDI says execute the written contract before work, in two copies, in Lithuanian or Lithuanian plus another language acceptable to both parties and understood by both. State the function, remuneration and workplace. [S05]
- **Written conditions and workplace rules.** Provide the relevant written employment information and inform the worker about applicable workplace/labour and occupational-safety rules before they start. Use current Labour Code timing and exceptions for the particular contract. [S05]
- **Sodra before start.** File the electronic 1-SD at least one working day before the employee begins; a narrow one-working-hour rule applies to the company’s sole management-body case described by Sodra. Submit identity-document copy first if the person has no Lithuanian personal code. Sodra filing is not a work permit. [S06–S07]
- **Payroll and social insurance.** File monthly SAM data, transfer applicable contributions, and file 2-SD after termination within the stated period. Employed workers are listed under social insurance that includes accident-at-work and occupational-disease coverage; exact rates depend on the applicable 2026 classification and employer group. [S06–S07]
- **Ordinary labour protections.** Apply current wage, working/rest-time, equal-treatment and workplace safety law. Provide applicable safety instruction/training and safe equipment. Relevant details depend on the job, risk and contract. No TP-based waiver is established. [S05, S09, S16]
- **Foreign-worker reporting.** Art. 62(8) describes information about foreign workers being provided through the Sodra information system to VDI and UŽT under the prescribed procedure. Because the reviewed TP-focused guidance does not state a clear categorical TP exemption or list every electronic field for every TP cohort, do not state “no foreign-worker notification is ever required”; employers should use the current Sodra/UŽT procedure for the worker category. The materials do not establish a universal direct Migration Department notification for each TP hire. [S01, S03, S06–S08]
- **Employment Service / Migration Department applications.** The covered TP work right does not require an Employment Service work permit/authorization document. Specified post-TP employment-based TRP cases also have a labour-market decision exception, but that exception is route-specific. [S03–S04]
- **Subsidies.** A hire does not by itself create an employer entitlement to a wage subsidy. Any active labour-market measure is separate and eligibility- and intake-dependent; no automatic Ukraine/TP hiring subsidy was established in this review.

## Minimum-pay boundaries

- **Current at 2026-11-09:** €1,153 gross MMA per month and €7.05 gross MVA per hour, effective 2026-01-01 under Government Resolution No. 700 (2025-10-16). [S09–S10]
- **MMA limitation:** Labour Code Art. 141 allows the monthly minimum wage only for unqualified work (work without special qualification or professional-skill requirements). Do not describe €1,153 as a universal ceiling or the minimum lawful pay for skilled work. [S09, S12]
- **Hours:** the monthly figure concerns the relevant full-time monthly working norm; part-time/partial-period pay depends on the actual schedule and applicable agreement. The hourly floor is €7.05. Do not calculate net pay or apply one monthly figure to all shifts. [S05, S09]
- **Equal application:** no TP-specific lower rate was found. Ordinary Lithuanian pay rules apply to TP employment. Collective or contractual terms may provide more favourable pay; the national minimum is a floor, not a cap. [S01, S05, S09]
- **Enacted next rate:** from 2027-01-01, Resolution No. 509 (2026-07-01) sets €1,245 gross/month and €7.61/hour. Keep it clearly future-dated when describing the position on this review date. [S11–S12]
- **Supplements and public/private distinctions:** this review does not calculate overtime, night, holiday or sectoral supplements. They depend on current Labour Code, pay system, contract and any applicable collective agreement. Public-sector remuneration may follow its own statutory pay framework; do not present the private-sector minimum figures as a complete public-pay schedule.

## Self-employment boundary

The TP statute expressly includes self-employed activity for the covered TP entitlement/period; it is not the same as subordinate employment and does not create an employment contract or employer 1-SD/SAM duties for a client. VMI registration, income declaration/tax, social-insurance obligations and activity-specific licences are separate. VMI’s general page requires a third-country national operating through a permanent base to submit a Lithuanian temporary or permanent residence permit alongside an individual-activity registration request; its Ukrainian-language page describes individual activity for Ukrainians and VMI registration. The exact document accepted by VMI in a pending TP-application case is unresolved in the reviewed public sources. Do not tell users that TP alone completes tax registration, or that registration itself is immigration work authorization. [S01–S03, S14–S15]

## Regulated-profession boundary

TP work access does not displace professional qualification recognition, licensing, registration, or occupation-specific requirements. SKVC’s Ukraine-specific guidance says no formal recognition is required for an unregulated profession and the employer decides whether qualification evidence is needed; regulated professions require recognition by the competent authority. No current Ukraine-wide temporary professional licensing/recognition waiver was established here. Do not name one universal recognition office: the competent authority depends on the profession. [S13, S16]

## 2026 changes

- **TP work basis and start date:** current Art. 94 and Migration Department guidance support work from the TP application point for a person entitled to TP; a physical residence document need not first be issued. [S01–S03]
- **2026 ordinary foreign-employment changes:** amendments effective 2026-05-22 changed ordinary foreign-worker/employer rules, including employer liability and some pending-permit/continued-work scenarios. Those changes are not a replacement for Art. 94 and do not establish that every pending permit authorizes work. [S01, S08]
- **Employment Service exception after TP:** Migration Department’s guidance distinguishes the TP work right from an employment-based residence-permit application after TP and describes a labour-market decision exemption for specified first post-TP / same-employer cases. Verify conditions for the exact route and employer. [S04]
- **Minimum pay:** Resolution No. 700 set 2026 rates effective 2026-01-01. Resolution No. 509 already enacted the 2027 rates effective 2027-01-01; these are future amounts at the research date. [S09–S12]
- **Employer filings / Sodra:** Sodra’s current 2026 guidance requires the ordinary 1-SD, SAM, applicable contributions and 2-SD sequence. No separate Ukraine TP employer filing procedure or general exemption from these filings was found. [S03, S06–S07]
- **Labour Code changes:** no TP-specific change to the underlying work right was located in reviewed 2026 guidance. This review does not catalogue all general Labour Code amendments; apply the current Code to working conditions and payroll. [S05, S08–S09]
- **Regulated professions:** no temporary TP-wide recognition/licensing exemption was located in current SKVC guidance. [S13]

## Claims not established / unresolved issues

1. **Sodra foreign-worker notice scope by TP document/cohort.** Art. 62(8) specifies reporting foreign-worker information through Sodra to VDI and UŽT, while TP-specific official guidance emphasizes exemption from the Employment Service work-authorization document. The reviewed sources do not explicitly state whether every Art. 94 TP case has an exception from the Art. 62(8) information report or give the exact fields/timing for every category. Do not conflate that reporting with a work permit; employer should confirm the current Sodra/UŽT workflow for the actual worker.
2. **Individual activity before physical TP residence document/VMI acceptance.** Migration Department says employed work can begin after TP application and the statute includes self-employment, but VMI’s general third-country national process asks for a Lithuanian residence permit. The official public pages reviewed do not expressly reconcile these for an applicant still waiting for the TP document. Check with VMI/Migration Department before giving a specific registration checklist.
3. **Work after TP refusal, withdrawal, expiry or issuance of a different title.** The sources support rights within the TP entitlement/protection period and a separate narrow pending-TRP rule, but do not settle all overlapping-status and transition scenarios. Do not infer automatic continuation or conversion.
4. **Seasonal employment mechanics.** Seasonal work falls within employment in principle under the broad Art. 94 right, but this review did not examine seasonal-contract requirements, permitted seasons, worker-specific sector restrictions, or seasonal benefit/tax implications.
5. **Minimum-pay application details.** This review establishes statutory 2026 and enacted 2027 amounts and the unqualified-work limit on MMA; exact treatment of hours, supplements, collective terms and public-sector pay requires the current job and pay system. No universal net amount can be stated.
6. **Ukraine-specific subsidies or temporary regulated-profession measures.** No general automatic employer subsidy or temporary nationwide licensing/recognition waiver was established in reviewed current official sources.

## Drafting boundaries

### Safe to state

- A person entitled to temporary protection in Lithuania can work after applying for TP; current Migration Department guidance says a physical residence document need not first be issued.
- Law Art. 94 grants the covered person work and self-employment rights and expressly exempts the person from a work permit while the statutory TP conditions/period apply.
- A separate Employment Service document authorizing work is not required for the covered TP group described by UŽT.
- TP authorization is distinct from ordinary employment-based TRP/Blue Card rules and does not make every pending residence application a work authorization.
- For subordinate work, ordinary written-contract, pre-start Sodra registration, payroll/social-insurance, working-condition and safety duties remain.
- Sodra 1-SD is generally due at least one working day before work starts; SAM and applicable contributions recur monthly and 2-SD is filed at termination.
- Current 2026 minimum rates are €1,153 gross/month and €7.05/hour from 2026-01-01. The monthly minimum may only be paid for unqualified work.
- From 2027-01-01, enacted Resolution No. 509 sets €1,245 gross/month and €7.61/hour.
- Regulated professions retain professional recognition/licensing requirements; nonregulated qualifications are assessed by the employer.

### Require qualification

- Tie the start of work to applying for Lithuanian TP and being legally entitled to TP, not simply being Ukrainian or having arrived.
- Describe the post-TP employment-permit exemption only for the specified first application/same-employer route and conditions.
- Explain self-employment separately: TP work authorization does not complete VMI registration, tax/social-insurance steps or professional licensing; individual VMI document edge cases remain unresolved.
- Describe Sodra foreign-worker reporting as a distinct employer process from permission to work; have employer verify the current workflow for the exact TP category.
- Explain monthly minimum as the unqualified-work minimum for the relevant monthly working norm, not an all-jobs standard; use the hourly floor as applicable.
- State 2027 rates only as future amounts effective 2027-01-01.

### Do not assert

- That arrival, any receipt, an unrelated registration, or any pending residence application grants work permission.
- That a TP applicant must wait for a physical card before starting work under the official current guidance.
- That every Ukrainian citizen qualifies for TP or that the TP exemption waives professional rules and employer duties.
- That no Sodra / VDI / UŽT foreign-worker reporting applies to TP hires, or that the employer must make a separate direct Migration Department notification for every hire, without checking the current category-specific procedure.
- That all seasonal, self-employment, second-title or post-TP situations follow the same route.
- That €1,153 is net pay, applies to skilled work as a ceiling, or is payable irrespective of hours; do not state a fixed net minimum.
- That TP holders have a lower minimum wage or a guaranteed hiring subsidy.
- That TP access automatically recognizes qualifications or licenses a regulated profession.
