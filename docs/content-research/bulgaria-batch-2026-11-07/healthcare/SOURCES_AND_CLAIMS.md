# Bulgaria — Healthcare

**Research date / LastCheckedAt:** 2026-11-07
**Scope:** exactly `healthcare-access`, `healthcare-proof`, `healthcare-urgent`. Research only.

## Research verdict

Bulgarian temporary protection (TP) has two related but distinct effects: the Health Insurance Act lists TP holders as a compulsory health-insurance category and gives them access to medical care under the Health Insurance Act and Health Act on the same terms as Bulgarian citizens (subject to the stated EU-coordination exception); Decree No. 69 separately allocates who pays contributions and for how long. State-paid contributions are not the same as a promise that every adult's contributions are paid for the full TP period or that every service is free. Ordinary insurance entitlements, state-funded services for uninsured people, emergency care, and private/self-paid care must be kept distinct.

The reviewed official evidence supports safe, qualified drafting for all three aspects. It does not establish a universal provider document checklist, blanket free planned care, automatic full medicine or dental reimbursement, or that every TP card presently in circulation is valid without renewal/replacement.

## TP healthcare vs public insurance

1. **TP statutory category:** Health Insurance Act Art. 33(1)(4a) includes TP beneficiaries in compulsory insurance. Under Art. 34(3), TP holders have rights to medical care/services under that Act and the Health Act on Bulgarian-citizen terms, except assistance under EU social-security coordination (S01).
2. **Who pays contributions:** Decree No. 69 starts the contribution obligation/rights on the TP grant date. State-funded contributions run for the whole TP period for under-18s and women aged 63+ / men 65+, and 90 days for adults aged 18 to below those thresholds, unless another statutory basis applies; after that, ordinary contribution rules apply (S03).
3. **Active NHIF coverage and cost:** A legal category/TP entitlement does not make all adult contributions state-paid, guarantee an uninterrupted NRA status record, or remove ordinary package limits, provider rules and copayments. Verify current status with NRA and the provider. Where the ordinary insured-person route is unavailable, specified Health Act services and emergency care remain separate state-funded routes; other care may require another legal entitlement or self-payment.
4. **Private care:** Private or non-contracted care is not automatically reimbursed by NHIF. Confirm provider contract, service package, and price before non-emergency treatment.

## Insured vs uninsured boundaries

- **Ordinary insured route:** a person whose contributions/status support active rights can use the NHIF package through eligible contracted providers and the applicable GP/referral rules. Insured employment generally has employer and employee contribution shares handled through the employer; self-insured/self-employed people pay under their own statutory basis; some benefit/state-paid categories have distinct payers and eligibility (S01/S09).
- **Uninsured/no active rights:** “uninsured” does not mean no emergency aid. Health Act Art. 82 includes emergency care and other expressly listed state-funded categories. An uninsured woman has a defined preventive/obstetric provision under ministerial rules. These are bounded public services, not a full NHIF package (S02).
- **Status lookup:** NRA's official service reports the registry status and missing months. If TP evidence and the NRA record conflict, request correction/clarification from NRA and retain current TP proof; do not treat an online result as a replacement for the TP card or as a final decision on service scope (S05/S15).

## Medicines, dental, and provider boundaries

- **Medicines:** NHIF home-treatment reimbursement applies to listed medicines, indications and prescription procedures, and can be partial. A prescription alone does not establish that NHIF pays the full price. Emergency stabilization does not automatically cover all medicines prescribed after discharge (S10/S11).
- **Dental care:** the NHIF package covers defined activities for eligible insured persons at contracted dentists; service limits, copayments, materials and out-of-package work can produce charges. No universal TP-only full dental benefit was established (S10/S12).
- **GP/specialist/hospital:** GP selection and referral arrangements are documented for insured people with the relevant rights. No TP-specific universal provider acceptance rule or universal GP registration guarantee was found. Emergency care is independently available as a statutory category; planned specialist/hospital care follows ordinary eligibility and provider/payment rules (S07/S08/S10/S13).
- **Charges:** “state funded” and “same terms as Bulgarian citizens” do not mean zero copayment for each covered activity. NHIF sources describe user fees and partial payment; exact exemption and charge depend on patient and service (S10).

## Post-TP boundaries

TP-specific status-based rights and Decree No. 69 payment depend on TP being granted and in force. After it ends, there is no evidence of automatic continuation solely because the person previously held TP. Ordinary coverage may continue or begin under a separate qualifying employment, self-employment, lawful residence, unemployment-benefit, dependent, pension/age or other statutory category. If no such basis applies, applicable self-payment or state-funded uninsured-service rules govern. Confirm the cessation date, contribution payer, and NRA record with the relevant authority; processing and transition details for each individual are not established here.

## Critical questions — direct answers

1. **Does TP automatically confer Bulgarian public health-insurance membership?** **Yes, in the statutory compulsory-insurance category:** Health Insurance Act Art. 33 includes TP holders. But this does not mean the state pays every adult's contributions throughout TP or that uninterrupted active NHIF rights are always reflected in the record.
2. **Can an uninsured TP beneficiary receive healthcare, and on what basis?** **Yes, within applicable routes:** TP equal-treatment rights under Health Insurance Act Art. 34(3)/Health Act Art. 83(1a), and separately the Health Act's defined emergency and other state-funded services. This is not a promise of a full NHIF package.
3. **Who pays for that care?** Contributions are paid by the state only for Decree No. 69 age/time categories or another state-paid statutory basis; employers and employees contribute under employment rules, self-insured people pay their own contributions, and uninsured emergency/other specifically listed services are financed through the applicable state mechanism. Other care may be payable by the patient/private insurer. Exact payer for every post-emergency service was not established.
4. **Are there age/vulnerability differences?** **Yes for contribution payer:** state contributions for under-18s and women 63+ / men 65+ throughout TP; 90 days for the middle age band. Uninsured women also have specified preventive/obstetric services. No broad additional automatic full package for all vulnerable people was established.
5. **When does an employed TP holder become insured?** When a qualifying employment/insurance basis starts under the Act and employer reports/contributions apply; individual contract/activity exceptions matter. The precise effective date must be confirmed from the employment basis and NRA record. TP itself starts its own statutory basis from the date specified by Decree No. 69.
6. **Who pays contributions during employment?** The employer and employee shares are handled through the employer under the statutory payroll rules; the employee share is not a state-paid contribution. A qualifying self-employed person pays their own contributions.
7. **Can a TP beneficiary choose a GP?** **If they have the relevant active ordinary-insurance rights, generally yes** through NHIF's registration-form process and a contracted GP. A universal TP-only right or a guarantee that a specific GP must accept was not established.
8. **What document proves healthcare entitlement?** A current SAR TP registration card is official evidence of TP and includes ЛНЧ; it does not itself prove active NHIF contribution status. For ordinary insured care, use the NRA status record and requested GP/insurance documents. No universal provider checklist was established.
9. **Is there an official online insurance-status check?** **Yes.** NRA provides an official status service after identification; NHIF also lists status lookup among its e-services. The result reflects the authority's registry, not every legal entitlement.
10. **Does an uninsured person have emergency-care rights?** **Yes.** Health Act Art. 82(1)(1) provides emergency medical care outside compulsory insurance; the state organizes and funds the emergency-care system.
11. **Is emergency care entirely free, with no later limits/costs?** **Not established.** Emergency services are statutorily provided/funded, but this does not establish zero charges for every ambulance, follow-up, discharge medicine or later planned service.
12. **Are prescribed medicines automatically covered?** **No.** NHIF reimbursement is for eligible listed medicines/indications and may be partial; prescriptions outside that route can require patient payment.
13. **Is dental care automatically covered?** **No.** Only a defined NHIF dental package for eligible insured persons; limits and copayments apply, and uninsured TP-only full dental coverage was not established.
14. **What happens after TP ends?** TP-only entitlement/contribution basis ends with TP; ordinary insurance can continue under another qualifying legal basis. No automatic continuation from former TP alone was established.
15. **Is there a separate current Ukraine/TP healthcare programme on 2026-11-07?** **Not established.** No distinct current official programme/package beyond the statutory TP rules, Decree No. 69 and general Health Act/NHIF schemes was found.
