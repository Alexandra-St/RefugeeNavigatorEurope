# Bulgaria — Social Assistance and Family Benefits

**Research date / `LastCheckedAt`:** 2026-11-07
**Repository baseline:** `93a9462d72e1dfaba5d6f31a4e33df7eed19822c`
**Exact scope:** `social-subsistence`, `social-application`, `social-child-benefits`. Official-source research only; this is not an individual benefit decision.

## Research verdict

Bulgarian law and the Social Assistance Agency (ASP) confirm that a person with Bulgarian temporary protection (TP) can apply for general social assistance. TP status is an eligible status category, not an automatic award: income, household composition, property and other statutory conditions still apply. A general one-time assistance and a seasonal heating benefit are distinct from regular monthly assistance, housing-provider support, unemployment insurance and disability benefits.

Family cash benefits are not one single right. The Family Allowances for Children Act sets separate benefits, age bands, income and other conditions; it does not clearly list TP as a stand-alone foreigner category. Official administrative-court decisions have interpreted temporary-protection rights to include some family benefits, including school grants, but the official central ASP guidance does not resolve a universal entitlement for each benefit. Do not promise automatic child, parental or birth payments based on TP alone. Insurance-based maternity and childcare benefits are separate again and depend on social-insurance conditions.

All sources were checked on **2026-11-07**. Amounts below are the official euro values published for 2026; where no current amount was established, the document says so rather than converting an older BGN amount.

For general monthly assistance, ASP describes a household assessment that considers income, assets/property, family status, health, age, work and study. The monthly amount is calculated by reference to an individually differentiated income threshold, not as one common minimum-income payment. Employment income may change that calculation; some unemployed applicants also face prior employment-service registration, participation and public-work conditions. No general earnings disregard or rule that starting work automatically ends all assistance was established from the reviewed current primary material.

## Application and authority boundaries

- **General assistance:** submit the prescribed declaration to the DSP for the current address. ASP's TP information requires a valid Bulgarian TP/status document and current-address registration. If a person cannot objectively document an address, ASP says to contact the DSP where they are staying. The DSP investigates the circumstances and the director/authorized official decides.
- **Methods:** in person, licensed post, DSP official email or secure electronic filing/e-service are published channels. Identity/secure-service requirements differ by channel. Ask the competent DSP which route and attachments fit the claim.
- **Evidence categories, not universal checklist:** status/identity and address; applicant/household and dependent family; income and available assets/property; health, work/study, age and other means of support; and event-specific evidence for a one-off need. The DSP may seek information through the social inquiry. ASP forms enumerate data, but do not establish that every item/document applies to every claimant.
- **Timing:** ASP publishes 27 days for general social-assistance orders after the application and social inquiry/report. Family-allowance decisions are generally within 14 days, extended to 30 days in specified verification or in-kind cases. These are decision periods, not a universal first-payment date.
- **Change reporting:** for social-assistance recipients, written notice of statutory relevant changes is due within one month. Family-benefit recipients have separate statutory reporting duties (commonly 30 days for specified monthly benefits). Check the decision/form for which changes apply.
- **Separate applications:** general assistance, one-off and heating assistance have different legal bases/forms; family benefits have distinct declarations by benefit; disability support uses its own assessment/benefit process; insurance maternity and childcare compensation are filed through the insurer/employer and NSSI. One application does not cover all.

## Family-benefit boundaries

- **Ordinary monthly child allowance:** may cover a dependent child through secondary education, no later than age 20; income thresholds from **1 Aug 2026** are €415 per person for full payment and €415.01–€466 for 80% payment. 2026 published amount is €25.57 for one child, €56.25 for two, €84.37 for three and €89.48 for four, with the stated additional-child rule. School attendance and other conditions apply (S07–S08).
- **Pregnancy and child under one:** the 2026 pregnancy grant is €115.04 and the monthly under-one family benefit is €102.26. From 1 Aug 2026 the preceding-12-month household income threshold is €466. The under-one allowance is distinct from insurance compensation and generally addresses cases without the relevant NSSI maternity benefit (S07–S09).
- **Birth and other one-time family payments:** ASP lists birth grants of €191.74 (first), €460.17 (second), €230.09 (third), and €153.39 (fourth and later); twin, adoption, student-mother and other categories have separate rules. These are not monthly child allowance and should not be presented as TP-only (S08).
- **School assistance:** for 2026, the official listed amount is €153.39 for eligible pupils in grades 1–4 and grade 8, subject to the specific law, application and school-attendance conditions. Lower administrative courts have found TP beneficiaries entitled in certain disputes, but this is not a blanket promise for all current or future family-benefit categories (S08, S10).
- **Child disability/orphan benefits:** separate monthly categories have their own disability determination, age, placement and other criteria. For children with specified long-term disability, the ASP 2026 table lists €230.09, €291.44 or €603.33 by assessed disability tier and recipient/foster-care category; a separate monthly child-without-survivor-pension category is €76.70. Do not infer eligibility from a parent's or child's TP status (S07–S08).
- **Insurance-based maternity and parental/childcare compensation:** NSSI pregnancy/birth benefits are insurance-based, calculated at 90% of reference insured earnings subject to statutory rules, normally requiring active insurance for sickness/maternity and 12 months of qualifying insurance service. A separate benefit for childcare up to age two also requires insurance and approved leave; no current 2026 fixed amount was confirmed in the reviewed official budget material. The NSSI rules are separate from non-insurance ASP family allowances (S11–S12).
- **TP and foreigner eligibility:** the Family Allowances Act's general foreigner clause uses permanent-residence/treaty language and does not itself say “temporary protection.” Bulgarian administrative courts have applied the Temporary Protection Directive and domestic protection law to allow some family benefits, including school payments. Those decisions are important evidence of a possible claim route, but do not establish that TP alone automatically meets each scheme's additional residence, income, child, schooling, insured-status or document requirements. Resolve individual denial/coordination disputes through ASP and appropriate legal advice.
- **EU coordination:** if the family has employment, insurance, residence or benefit connections in more than one Member State, Regulations 883/2004 and 1231/2010 may determine which state pays and whether benefits are aggregated/supplemented. This depends on facts; a Bulgarian TP card alone does not establish the responsible state.

## Disability and humanitarian-support boundary

- TP status is included in general social-assistance access, but disability cash support requires the type/degree of permanent disability to be determined under Bulgarian law and satisfaction of scheme-specific conditions. ASP's current 2026 adult monthly financial-support amounts are €27.34 (50–70.99%), €58.59 (71–90%), €97.66 (over 90%), and €117.19 or €222.66 for specified over-90% cases with assessed need for assistance and qualifying pension categories. Amounts are tied to the 2026 poverty line and are not a TP allowance (S13–S14).
- Disability target aids, rent support for specific municipal tenants, personal assistance, social services and health-related support have separate assessments and evidence. No universal disability payment solely for TP, or one universal TP emergency disability grant, was established.
- General one-off assistance may be requested for an individual incident/vital need; disaster/crisis programmes may offer support for defined events. No TP-only cash humanitarian programme was found. Accommodation assistance or payment to a hosting provider is a different route and must not be described as cash social assistance to the displaced person.

## Evidence boundaries

- TP status clearly supports an application for general social assistance, but not a guaranteed award. Each monthly, one-off, housing-rent and heating route has separate conditions.
- No general 2026 monthly minimum-income cash payment of €117.19 or €390.63 is established; €117.19 is the differentiated-income calculation base and €390.63 is the poverty-line benchmark.
- The exact household definition, asset exclusions, countable-income treatment, public-work exceptions and recalculation for a particular job require applying current law to the actual family. No general earnings disregard or automatic job-start termination rule was established.
- The Family Allowances Act's wording and TP-specific court interpretations must be read together. The judgments found are individual administrative decisions, with some concerning school payments; they do not settle automatic access to every birth, pregnancy, child, disability-child or school benefit. ASP's published summary does not supply an exhaustive TP-specific ruling for each category.
- Social-insurance maternity/childcare entitlement depends on insured employment/service, contributions, leave and NSSI rules. TP status is not a substitute for insurance history. A current official fixed 2026 child-to-two amount was not established.
- EU coordination may alter the responsible state only if the case meets the regulations' personal, material and cross-border scope. Do not infer coordination from TP status or nationality alone.
- A 2026 one-off hardship grant, heating amount, child allowance amount or disaster payment is tied to its own effective dates and conditions; none is a permanent universal Ukraine cash programme.

## Critical questions — direct answers

1. **Can a TP beneficiary apply for general means-tested social assistance?** Yes. Bulgarian law and ASP include TP beneficiaries in the social-assistance category; the DSP still assesses all claim conditions.
2. **Does TP automatically create entitlement to payment?** No. TP establishes access to apply, not automatic award or amount.
3. **Are household income and property counted?** Yes. Household composition, income and property/assets are assessed; the particular exclusions/calculation depend on the scheme.
4. **Does starting work automatically end assistance?** No automatic termination rule was found. Earnings can change the means test and award; some applicants must satisfy employment-service and work-participation conditions. No general earnings disregard was established.
5. **Is there an official 2026 minimum-income/basic-assistance amount?** There is no one flat monthly payment. The official 2026 poverty line is €390.63 and calculation base for differentiated income is €117.19; the actual threshold/payment is individualized.
6. **Is there a housing component in general assistance?** A narrow municipal-housing rent payment exists for specified categories and conditions; there is no general private-rent supplement established.
7. **Is there one-off/emergency cash assistance?** Yes. A general incident-based one-off payment may be granted once yearly, up to €1,171.89 in 2026. It is assessed and not guaranteed; event-specific crisis grants have their own rules.
8. **Who decides general social-assistance claims?** ASP's local Social Assistance Directorate (DSP); the director/authorized officer issues the individual order after assessment.
9. **Which address determines where to apply?** Normally the current address. ASP guidance directs TP beneficiaries to DSP by current address; where objectively unable to document an address, contact DSP where staying.
10. **Which evidence categories are confirmed?** Status/identity and address; family/household composition; income and assets; health, work/study and other means of support; event-specific evidence. Exact documents vary by claim.
11. **Is there one universal checklist?** No. Forms and evidence differ by benefit and case.
12. **Is a dedicated TP/Ukraine cash programme active on 2026-11-07?** No dedicated direct cash programme was established in the reviewed current sources. TP holders can apply for ordinary social assistance; accommodation/provider support and event-specific grants are separate.
13. **Does TP alone give ordinary child/family allowance?** No automatic entitlement is established. The family-benefit law has separate foreigner/residence and benefit conditions; some lower administrative courts have upheld TP families' access in specific disputes, so the individual claim may require case-specific assessment.
14. **Does TP alone give parental allowance?** No. Non-insurance family benefits have their own eligibility rules; NSSI maternity/parental childcare compensation requires insured status and qualifying insurance history.
15. **Is there a separate childcare benefit route for TP families?** A child-under-one family allowance and NSSI child-care benefits exist as separate routes, but neither is an automatic TP-only payment. Income, age, insurance and benefit-coordination rules apply.
16. **Do any family benefits require employment/insurance?** NSSI maternity/birth and child-care compensation require qualifying social insurance (normally 12 months for maternity/birth). Some non-insurance family benefits instead require low income or no receipt of insurance maternity compensation.
17. **Which child ages matter?** Family child allowance may continue through secondary education to at most 20; under-one family care is to age one; NSSI childcare can run to age two; school support targets specified grades; disability-child benefits have separate age/education limits.
18. **Who administers family benefits?** ASP/DSP decides Family Allowances Act benefits. NSSI administers insurance-based maternity/child-care benefits, generally through information submitted by the employer/insurer.
19. **Can EU coordination change the result?** Yes, potentially in a qualifying cross-border case under Regulations 883/2004 and 1231/2010. Competent-state/payment rules depend on residence, work and insurance; no universal result follows from TP.
20. **Does TP alone qualify someone for ordinary disability cash support?** No. The Bulgarian disability assessment and applicable degree/age/other conditions are required.
21. **Is separate humanitarian/emergency disability cash assistance established?** No universal TP-specific disability grant was established. General incident-based assistance or separate disability/health support may be assessed; they are not automatic TP cash.
22. **Can one application cover all listed schemes?** No. General assistance, one-off/heating, family benefits, disability support and NSSI insurance benefits use separate legal routes and/or forms.

## Final topic verdict

**SUPPORTED WITH QUALIFICATION — READY WITH QUALIFICATION FOR CONTENT DRAFTING.** Primary Bulgarian sources establish application access to general social assistance for TP holders and publish 2026 values for several means-tested and family-benefit schemes. Preserve the difference between eligibility to apply and a benefit award; between ASP cash assistance, family benefits, NSSI insurance benefits, accommodation support and unemployment insurance; and between explicit law, case-specific court interpretation and an individual decision. Do not promise automatic TP cash, universal child benefit, disability payment, housing allowance, emergency grant, or one application covering every scheme.
