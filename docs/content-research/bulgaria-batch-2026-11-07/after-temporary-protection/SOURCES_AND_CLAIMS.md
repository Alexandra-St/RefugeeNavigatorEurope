# Bulgaria — Residence Options During or After Temporary Protection

**Research checked:** 2026-11-07
**Scope:** focused legal and procedural review for exactly `residence-routes`, `residence-conditions`, and `residence-filing`. This is not an article, legal opinion, or individual eligibility decision.

## Research verdict

**READY FOR CONTENT DRAFTING WITH QUALIFICATIONS.** Bulgarian law provides several ordinary, purpose-based residence routes, and one clear national route for an already lawfully resident person to apply in-country for a single residence-and-work permit. It does not create automatic conversion from temporary protection (TP). The single-permit statute accepts a visa D or an existing Bulgarian residence permit, but the reviewed primary text does not expressly say whether a TP registration card qualifies for that in-country alternative. By contrast, the Blue Card provision expressly excludes a person using temporary protection; Blue Card mobility and its 90-day pending-status rules concern holders of an EU Blue Card from another Member State, not TP holders. Standard residence grounds under Article 24 generally require a D visa, subject to specific statutory exceptions. TP holders retain their TP-based stay and employment rights while TP remains effective; filing another application does not itself extend TP, guarantee lawful stay after TP ends, or grant additional work permission. EU long-term residence excludes persons while they are beneficiaries of temporary protection. Whether prior TP months can later count under Bulgarian national long-term or permanent residence calculations is not expressly resolved for TP in the sources reviewed. A 2026 bill was recorded at first reading but was not an enacted TP transition route as of the last published official parliamentary action reviewed.

## Route boundaries

- **Employment:** holders can work under the TP right while TP remains effective. The ordinary single permit is a distinct route under Article 24i. Its in-country provision applies to a qualifying person with an existing Bulgarian residence permit, but the law text reviewed does not expressly confirm that a TP registration card fits that term. Ordinary employment after TP ends requires the relevant residence/work authorization unless another right applies.
- **EU Blue Card:** distinct high-skilled route under Article 33k and Labour Migration and Labour Mobility Act. It requires a high-skilled employment relationship, qualification and a salary tied to the statutory average-wage formula. Article 33k(30)(1) expressly excludes a person using TP from paragraph (1); do not describe Blue Card as a pre-end TP conversion. The special Blue Card mobility route is limited to specified holders of another Member State’s Blue Card.
- **Business/self-employment:** the general business-job-creation and freelance-permission rules are not waived by being a TP holder. A business purpose, a registered company, or TP employment rights alone does not establish a right to self-employment residence.
- **Study/research/special activity:** admission, activity, financial and supporting-document requirements are purpose-specific. Student mobility from another Member State is distinct from initial Bulgarian TP and does not create a universal domestic TP transition.
- **Family:** ordinary family reunification depends on the sponsor and statutory family category. TP family reunion rights arise under the TP framework and should not be conflated with a residence permit under the Foreigners Act.
- **Family of a TP beneficiary:** Asylum and Refugees Act Article 39a permits reunification with the spouse and unmarried minor children if they also wish to join; the AREF chair may authorize certain other close relatives who shared the household and were dependent in the country of origin, after an individual assessment. Reunified relatives receive TP rights. The Bulgarian Identity Documents Act lists a one-year residence-permit document for a family member of a TP beneficiary. This remains part of the TP family framework and does not create a general post-TP residence conversion.
- **Humanitarian/other national grounds:** narrow Article 25a merits grounds, Article 28a minor-specific rules and Article 27 extensions of existing permissions are not a general route for all TP holders. A request for international protection is a separate status procedure.
- **Special transition and automatic conversion:** no enacted Bulgaria-specific provision was located that converts TP automatically or creates a general post-TP transition permit. The 2026 bill record is legislative history only; the last official action located is first reading.

## Pending-status and work-right boundaries

### Direct answers to the eight critical questions

1. **Can a current TP holder file for a Bulgarian residence permit before TP ends?** **Only with qualification; no universal TP-specific filing right is established.** Article 24i permits in-country filing for qualifying holders of a Bulgarian residence permit, but the reviewed statute does not expressly establish that a TP registration card qualifies. The Blue Card route expressly excludes a TP beneficiary. Other Article 24 routes generally require a D visa unless a specific statutory exception applies.
2. **Is visa D required for every route?** **No.** Many Article 24 grounds require it, while the law lists exceptions and Article 24i / Article 33k allow filing on specified existing residence statuses. These are route-specific and do not create a universal TP-holder exemption.
3. **Does another residence permit automatically terminate TP when granted?** **Not established as an automatic consequence.** Article 82 lists TP expiry, EU Council termination and individual withdrawal; grant of an ordinary permit is not listed there. The law does not clearly address simultaneous use of every status/benefit combination, so do not promise that both remain concurrently usable.
4. **Does a pending residence application grant lawful stay?** **Not as a general bridge established by these sources.** An active TP status itself supports stay until it ends. No general rule was found that an ordinary pending application extends TP or preserves stay after TP termination.
5. **Does a pending application grant work permission?** **No general pending-application work right was found.** Work is supported by TP for its duration, or by a granted work/residence permit or another express statutory basis. Do not equate filing with work authorization.
6. **Does TP time count toward EU long-term residence or Bulgarian permanent residence?** **EU long-term:** it does not accrue while a person is under TP because TP beneficiaries are excluded from Directive 2003/109 scope. Whether earlier TP months can be credited after a later status change is **Not established** in the reviewed national text. **Bulgarian permanent residence:** TP counting is **Not established**; do not infer from the law’s failure to name TP in a list of exclusions.
7. **Is there a special post-TP transition route in Bulgarian law?** **No enacted TP-specific route was found.** Ordinary residence grounds remain available if their conditions are met; no automatic transition or general temporary bridge was established.
8. **Are there separate Blue Card in-country filing rules?** **Yes, but they do not provide a TP-holder exception.** Article 33k allows certain Blue Card applications at Migration units and includes specific inter-EU Blue Card mobility and pending-stay rules. Article 33k(30)(1) expressly excludes people using TP from the ordinary Blue Card paragraph.

### Separation of legal effects

- **Stay:** the TP status and its validity are the basis for the TP holder’s right to remain. A pending ordinary residence file is not itself an established extension of TP.
- **Work:** TP law gives a work right while TP remains effective. An ordinary pending residence application does not itself establish a new work right; a granted single permit/Blue Card has its own work authorization rules.
- **Border crossing:** no general entry/re-entry or travel guarantee follows from the filing of an ordinary residence application. Document validity and right to re-enter are separate questions.
- **TP end:** Asylum and Refugees Act Article 82 provides for termination on expiry of the relevant period, EU Council decision, or an individual withdrawal decision. It then applies the Foreigners Act unless the person has lodged an international-protection application. It does not name the grant of an ordinary residence permit as an automatic TP termination event.
- **Blue Card mobility:** the law’s 90-day interim residence document and early work permissions concern defined mobility from another Member State’s valid Blue Card. They are not a pending-TP bridge.

## Final topic verdict

**READY FOR CONTENT DRAFTING WITH QUALIFICATIONS**

Primary law supports a useful comparison of ordinary work, business/freelance, study, family, Blue Card, national permanent and EU long-term residence routes. The crucial boundaries are route-specific D-visa and filing conditions, the express Blue Card exclusion while someone uses TP, TP-based stay/work rights that last only with TP, and no general pending-application bridge. No automatic conversion or enacted post-TP transition route was identified. Leave the status of a TP registration card under the single-permit in-country filing clause, post-TP pending-stay/work effects, and retrospective national residence-time credit as not established, and refresh the law/bill record before giving live operational advice.
