# Bulgaria — Education

**Research date / LastCheckedAt:** 2026-11-07
**Repository baseline:** `8201fcc3292f6f50cc0b60de3168b2049407e83c`
**Scope:** Bulgaria's `education-school-enrolment`, `education-pupil-language` and `education-higher-admission` aspects. Official-source research only; no catalog or application-content changes.

## Research verdict

Official sources support all three topics with qualification. Children seeking or holding temporary protection can access Bulgarian public education, while compulsory attendance, local capacity, placement and individual support decisions follow separate rules. Bulgarian universities have a specific but limited state-university admission route for temporary-protection holders; recognition of a Ukrainian school qualification and admission are separate decisions. No generalized guarantee of a chosen school, a specific language-course schedule, university admission, credit transfer, or tuition waiver is supported.

The 2026/27 state-university intake allocation for temporary-protection holders is published by the Ministry of Education and Science (MES). Its existence does not guarantee a place to an individual applicant. All claims below were checked on 2026-11-07.

## Critical questions — direct answers

1. **Does a child with TP have the right to attend Bulgarian public school?** Yes. Official government/MES guidance recognizes access to state/municipal kindergartens and schools through the RDE process, subject to placement/capacity.
2. **Is Bulgarian schooling compulsory for children with TP?** The general compulsory-age rules apply; no universal TP exemption was found. Individual legally authorized alternative routes are separate.
3. **What age/class boundaries apply?** Compulsory preschool starts for children in the calendar year they turn four; school normally starts in the school year of the year they turn seven, with possible start at six when ready; compulsory school education continues to 16. Class assignment is not determined by age alone.
4. **Can a family choose any school?** A family may state a preference, but there is no guarantee of its chosen school or any school regardless of capacity.
5. **What if the preferred school has no places?** The responsible RDE/municipality uses the applicable referral/placement process and may direct the child to another institution. Exact administrative steps vary locally.
6. **Which enrollment documents are officially confirmed?** An application and information needed to establish the child, protection/status and circumstances, plus available educational records are relevant. A universal exhaustive checklist is not established; ask the responsible RDE for its current form and case-specific list.
7. **What if Ukrainian school records are missing/incomplete?** Contact/apply through the RDE and provide any available evidence. Official RDE guidance provides an assessment route for missing records; no automatic denial or grade guarantee follows.
8. **Who determines grade/class placement?** The competent school/RDE process does. Sofia guidance assigns different review roles by grade band and uses assessment/commission procedures; it is not automatic nationwide placement by age.
9. **Is Bulgarian-language support free?** Public-school additional Bulgarian support is provided within the public education/support framework; no separate tuition charge was identified. Access and arrangement depend on need/process and local delivery.
10. **Is a specific number of hours/course guaranteed?** No. Ordinance No. 6 gives stage-based maximum hours, not a guaranteed schedule or entitlement to a fixed course.
11. **Can Ukrainian online school replace compulsory Bulgarian attendance?** No automatic substitution was found. It should not be described as satisfying Bulgarian compulsory attendance absent an approved individual legal route.
12. **Can Bulgarian school and Ukrainian online study be combined?** Ukrainian online materials can be supplementary. Formal dual-enrollment status and recognition effects are not established nationally.
13. **Can a TP holder apply to a Bulgarian university?** Yes. A specific facilitated, limited route applies to state universities under Decree No. 264; ordinary institution-specific admissions remain relevant.
14. **Who recognizes a Ukrainian secondary diploma?** NACID handles foreign secondary qualification recognition when a formal certificate is required and provides Ukraine-specific guidance. Applicable treaty verification may alter the evidence route. The university, not NACID, decides admission.
15. **Who decides transfer of previous university credits?** The receiving accredited university/rector assesses prior study and credit transfer; it is not automatic.
16. **Is there a universal TP tuition exemption?** No. The state-university Decree No. 264 route charges the Bulgarian-citizen rate; it does not establish a universal zero-fee exemption. It does not cover all private universities.
17. **Do public/state and private university rules differ?** Yes. Decree No. 264 expressly covers state HEIs and includes the capped route. No matching nationwide private-university TP concession is established; private admission and fees are institution-specific.
18. **Are there official scholarships/support specifically relevant to TP/Ukrainian students as of 2026-11-07?** Yes, for students admitted under Decree No. 264, the decree provides scholarship and dormitory/canteen rights on the specified state-university basis; the 2026/27 allocation is published. This is limited by eligible route/places and does not establish a universal scholarship or an amount for every applicant.
19. **What happens without a full education-document set?** For school placement, RDE/school assessment routes can be used when records are absent. NACID may pause a formal recognition file and request missing items. Prior university credit assessment is receiving-university-specific. These procedures must not be conflated.
20. **Does every university programme require Bulgarian proficiency?** No universal language threshold was established. The Decree No. 264 state route has Bulgarian-language competitive exams (except specified foreign-philology exams) and first-year Bulgarian preparation; other language and programme requirements are institution-specific.

## Compulsory-school and placement boundaries

- **Access, obligation and place assignment are separate.** Official guidance confirms access for Ukrainian children with temporary protection. The general Preschool and School Education Act provides compulsory preschool from age four (according to the calendar-year/school-year rule), school entry normally in the school year beginning in the year the child turns seven (possible at six if ready), and compulsory schooling until 16. No general TP exemption was identified. This does not itself guarantee a chosen institution or answer the child's individual cohort/placement.
- **RDE is the appropriate starting point.** Bulgaria for Ukraine and MES direct families to the Regional Department of Education. The RDE considers age, previous education, location/residence and parent preference, and routes the applicant to an institution. Do not promise admission at a named school.
- **No universal document checklist.** Identity, protection status, residence details and available school evidence help the process. Local RDE forms and requirements may differ. Health/vaccination papers were not established as a universal school enrollment requirement by reviewed sources; verify any preschool or health-specific rule locally.
- **Records and class placement.** If Ukrainian school documents are missing, contact the RDE rather than assuming enrollment is impossible. Sofia RDE says grades II–XII without educational records use the Ordinance No. 3 assessment route; it assigns grade I–VI completed-document assessment to the school director and grades VII–XII to an RDE commission. This is an official local example, not a nationwide allocation rule. No automatic class/grade placement is established.
- **Capacity.** The Sofia RDE procedure describes escalation if a school lacks capacity and referral to another school if it cannot accommodate the child. National guidance supports RDE placement, but exact municipal/RDE steps vary. No family can be promised a preferred school or specific seat.
- **Unaccompanied minors.** The protection admission procedure recognizes a designated representative acting for an unaccompanied minor. The particular representative/child-protection arrangements should be confirmed with the responsible authority.
- **Fees and transport.** Public compulsory schooling is free under the Act. Targeted 2026/27 transport funding for some children from accommodation sites is documented; universal transport and school-meal entitlements are not.

## Bulgarian-language and Ukrainian-online-study boundaries

- **Right to support:** official rules provide additional Bulgarian-language education for pupils who need it, with Bulgarian-as-a-foreign-language provision. The responsible school/RDE determines and arranges the applicable support.
- **No guaranteed specific course:** Ordinance No. 6 sets maximums over up to 12 months: preprimary 60 pedagogical activities (up to 2 per week), primary 90 lesson hours (up to 3 per week), lower secondary 120 (up to 4 per week), and first upper-secondary stage 180 (up to 5 per week). These caps do not promise that every pupil receives the maximum or a separately staffed course. The additional program is need/request-based; no mandatory course solely by reason of TP status is established.
- **Other support:** psychologists/resource teachers or adaptation measures are needs-based. The evidence does not establish automatic assignment to every newly arrived pupil, a uniform duration, or a school-specific service guarantee. Any assessment/exam accommodation is individual and must follow applicable school rules.
- **Ukrainian online study:** official pages list Ukrainian online learning as a resource. They do not state it replaces Bulgarian compulsory attendance. Treat it as supplemental unless an individual statutory alternative/exemption has been approved by the competent authority. Simultaneous study may be possible as a practical supplement, but no universal dual-enrollment or recognition rule was established.

## Recognition and higher-education boundaries

- **TP holder admission:** Higher Education Act Art. 68(10) authorizes facilitated admission under a Council of Ministers act. Decree No. 264 creates this route for temporary-protection holders at Bulgarian state HEIs, with up to 400 state-subsidized places annually outside ordinary annual intake. The MES published the 2026/27 allocation on 2026-07-13 by field/program. Applicants must meet the actual year's eligibility, place and institution requirements.
- **Diploma recognition is not admission.** NACID's Ukraine guidance addresses recognition/evidence of Ukrainian secondary diplomas and a treaty-based route for specified qualifications. Depending on diploma type/year and purpose, a NACID certificate or verification may be needed. The university separately applies its admission criteria, entrance examinations, language and capacity requirements.
- **Missing school papers:** NACID recognition applications can be paused and missing items requested. This is distinct from school placement, where RDE/school assessment can proceed without complete prior records. Do not promise automatic diploma recognition, admission, or that all incomplete cases use the same proof route.
- **Prior university study:** the receiving accredited Bulgarian university/rector assesses completed study and potential credit transfer. NACID is not a universal credit-transfer decision-maker. Admission to a regulated degree programme and later professional licensing/recognition are separate procedures; this research does not establish professional recognition.
- **Language:** Decree No. 264 provides Bulgarian-language competitive exams for its state route, with the stated foreign-philology exception, and first-year Bulgarian language preparation. This does not establish a universal proficiency level for all institutions or courses. Check the target university/programme.

## Fees and scholarship boundaries

- **Schools:** public compulsory education is free under the Preschool and School Education Act. This does not imply free meals, transport or optional services in all circumstances. The 2026/27 Ministry of Finance notice documents targeted transport funding for children with TP from some accommodation sites; eligibility and local coverage are not a universal individual entitlement.
- **State higher education:** Decree No. 264's specific TP admission route requires fees at the rate applicable to Bulgarian citizens. This is fee parity, not an exemption from tuition, and not a universal rule for all TP students admitted through any route.
- **Private higher education:** the decree is expressly for state institutions. No universal private-university TP fee reduction/exemption was found; private university admissions and fees are institution-specific.
- **Scholarships and student support:** Decree No. 264 provides scholarship and state-subsidized dormitory/canteen rights for students admitted under its specified route and terms. The annual intake is limited and 2026/27 allocations are program-specific. No universal scholarship, guaranteed amount, or general Ukrainian/TP stipend was established.

## Evidence boundaries

- Exact local admission documents, available places, school assignment and any municipality-level process must be checked with the child's RDE; Sofia's workflow must not be generalized nationally.
- The child's precise compulsory cohort and any lawful individual exception require the relevant birth date, school-year timing and authority assessment.
- No national exhaustive school-enrollment document checklist, universal vaccination document rule, universal meal entitlement or universal transport entitlement was established.
- The additional-language regulations set maxima, not a guaranteed schedule. Actual assessment, course, intensity, staffing, psychological/resource support and accommodations depend on the pupil and institution.
- No official basis was found for ordinary Ukrainian online attendance automatically substituting Bulgarian compulsory schooling or for universal formal dual enrollment.
- The current 2026/27 Decree No. 264 place distribution is program-specific; eligibility, remaining places, exam dates and individual outcome require the target state university. This route does not cover private universities.
- NACID's route depends on Ukrainian diploma type, issue year, authentication/EDBO evidence and purpose. Recognition is distinct from admission. The receiving university decides prior higher-study credit transfer.
- Tuition parity and scholarship rights under Decree No. 264 apply to its specified state route; they do not establish universal fee exemption/support. No generalized language threshold or professional recognition result for regulated occupations is established.
