# Residence Options After Temporary Protection

> Research draft — unpublished; pending human editorial review. No content in this draft is approved or marked Verified.

**Summary:** Possible routes for training, study, recognition and qualified work have different requirements; none is an automatic conversion from §24.

## RULES

German law has separate residence titles for vocational training (§16a), university study (§16b), recognition measures (§16d) and qualified work (§§18a/18b AufenthG). Other routes include the EU Blue Card (§18g) for qualifying employment and the Opportunity Card (§20a) for job search or recognition measures, with limited work permission. Each route has its own qualifying purpose and evidence. For example, §16b requires admission to full-time study at a recognized institution; §16a requires a qualifying training place and may require Federal Employment Agency approval or proof of German skills. A skilled-worker route requires a recognized or comparable qualification and a qualified job offer; a regulated profession can also require a licence. General requirements such as identity, livelihood and the applicable visa rule may matter, with statutory exceptions.

## TASKS

Start with the concrete goal, not a form: obtain the training contract or university admission; or, for a profession, ask the recognition authority whether your qualification is equivalent and whether the job is regulated. Prepare your passport, current residence document, qualification certificates and translations if requested, admission or contract, proof of financing where applicable, and professional licence if needed. Under §16d, a recognition notice that specifies a compensatory measure may support a separate recognition-residence route; it is not a general work permit.

## PLACES

If you currently hold a German residence permit under §24, §39(1) AufenthV allows an application for another title in Germany while you hold a residence permit. This is a procedural route, not approval: the substantive requirements still apply. The competent Ausländerbehörde decides the application. The route is not universal: a Chancenkarte may be granted to someone already in Germany only if they hold a residence title under Division 3 or 4. Because §24 is in Division 5, a §24 holder should not assume §39(1) allows an in-country switch to a Chancenkarte; ask the Ausländerbehörde whether an application from abroad is required. For a qualifying person still in the UkraineAufenthÜV 90-day exemption, §3 of that ordinance and Germany4Ukraine also describe obtaining a necessary title in Germany. Do not treat those provisions as a universal exemption from every visa or eligibility rule.

## NEXT STEPS

Apply early enough to understand the effect on your current document. Under §81(4) AufenthG, a timely application for renewal or another title before the existing one expires generally continues that title while the authority decides; §81(3) has a different effect for some people lawfully present without a title. A late application does not automatically preserve the old permit. Read any Fiktionsbescheinigung and its work entry.

The EU has adopted a further extension of temporary protection from 5 March 2027 through 4 March 2028; the current period runs through 4 March 2027. Separately, current UkraineAufenthFGV §2 continues specified §24 permits valid on 1 February 2026 through 4 March 2027, subject to the regulation’s conditions. These dates concern different legal statuses; the EU protection period does not itself set the validity date of an individual German residence permit.

Ask the competent Ausländerbehörde to confirm the rule for your own document in writing before relying on a date. A §24 holder may be granted a permanent settlement permit under §26(4) if the requirements in §9(2) are met; this is not automatic. EU long-term residence is a separate route: §9a(3)(1) excludes a holder of a Division 5 permit such as §24 unless they have been recognised as a beneficiary of international protection. Sections 9a and 9b have route-specific residence-time rules, so do not assume every period under §24 is credited in the same way. Ask the Ausländerbehörde to assess your status history. Family and other humanitarian routes are outside this guide.
