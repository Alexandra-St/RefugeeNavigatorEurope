## Findings from primary sources

### Temporary protection and German residence permits

Council Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/1912, Article 1, extends EU temporary protection until **4 March 2028**. That EU period does not itself amend or determine the validity of an individual German residence permit.

As checked on 16 October, the current consolidated UkraineAufenthFGV §§2–3—last amended on 22 October 2025—say that specified §24 permits valid on 1 February 2026 continue through **4 March 2027** and that the regulation expires on that date. Germany4Ukraine’s registration page contains inconsistent 2028 statements: it describes the February 2026 cohort as continuing until March 2028 and separately announces a further automatic extension for Ukrainian permits still valid on 1 February 2027. The Germany4Ukraine labour page also says §24 permits are currently valid until March 2028. The consolidated ordinance does not yet reflect that later extension. No source was used to infer a personal title expiry or work right.

This is an unresolved inconsistency in official legal/administrative information, not a basis to guess. Articles 1, 2 and 4 now identify the current statutory text, attribute the portal statements, separate EU protection from national permit validity, and tell readers to obtain written confirmation from the competent Ausländerbehörde before relying on an individual expiry date. The Temporary Protection and Work Authorization matrices retain `CONFLICTED` evidence labels.

### Military-obligation conditions

The EU Decision conditions new grants of temporary protection on satisfying Ukrainian military obligations, with proof where applicable. The official corrigendum changes the exception to people who enjoyed protection in a **given Member State** by 4 August 2026 and continuously retain that status in that same state afterwards; Article 2 applies from 5 August 2026. Physical presence somewhere in the EU is not the text of this exception.

Germany4Ukraine’s notice dated 23 September says the condition does not apply to people “already living in the EU,” including if military duties arise later. This is broader than the corrected EU wording. The drafts attribute both statements and advise people with prior protection in a different Member State or a cross-state move to ask the competent authority how the rule applies. No universal document checklist or individual eligibility outcome is asserted.

UkraineAufenthÜV §2(3) is separate: for covered people entering Germany from 9 October 2026 who are subject to Ukrainian military duties, the 90-day exemption from the German residence-title requirement does not apply unless they show compliance. This is not the EU grant test and does not itself decide an application for temporary protection.

### Benefits-law reform and current schemes

On 16 October 2026, BMAS showed the Leistungsrechtsanpassungsgesetz as **“Im Prozess”** and final completion as **“Noch nicht veröffentlicht.”** Bundestag DIP listed **“Überwiesen.”** The published BT-Drs. 21/3539 remains a government bill; no enacted text or commencement date was identified in the official material checked. Therefore, the proposal does not replace current SGB II, SGB XII or AsylbLG rules. The four related topics—employment, healthcare, housing and social assistance—keep current law separate from conditional descriptions of the proposal. A pending bill alone does not block integration; recheck its status before release and update affected topics together if it is enacted.

### Education and childcare

The current SGB VIII §24(1) includes the specified parent circumstances for support before a child’s first birthday: development-related need, parent employment, active job search, education/vocational training, or receipt of SGB II benefits for integration into work. Section 24(4) sets the school-age entitlement and treats it as met during holidays when qualifying §11 youth-work activities are offered; Land law may permit a facility to close for up to four weeks per year during school holidays. The Ukrainian, English and Russian drafts now state both limits. Land-level school and childcare administration remains local.
