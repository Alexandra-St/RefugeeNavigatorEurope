# Applying for Temporary Protection and Checking Residence Dates

> Research draft — unpublished; pending human editorial review. No content in this draft is approved or marked Verified.

**Summary:** Separate the visa-waiver stay, an application under section 24 AufenthG and the residence document; check the military-obligation rules and conflicting extension dates.

## RULES

Section 24 of the Residence Act (AufenthG) is the German route for issuing a residence permit to a person granted EU temporary protection who declares willingness to be received in Germany. Eligibility depends on the category set by EU law; nationality or flight from Ukraine alone does not decide it.

A different rule, the Ukraine Residence Transition Ordinance (UkraineAufenthÜV), exempts specified people from holding a German residence title for 90 days from first entry, if they entered by 4 December 2027 and have not already received temporary protection in another EU Member State. It is temporary lawful stay without a title, not a §24 permit or a decision granting temporary protection. The exemption stops if a negative residence-title decision is issued.

From 9 October 2026, §2(3) also removes that exemption for people entering Germany who are subject to Ukrainian military duties unless they prove compliance. This is a German condition on the no-title entry/stay exemption. Separately, EU Decision 2026/1912 makes compliance with military obligations, with proof where applicable, a condition for new grants of temporary protection from 5 August 2026. The EU carve-out is limited to people who held protection in the same Member State by 4 August and continuously retain it there. Germany4Ukraine’s news notice describes people already living in the EU more broadly; the wording is not identical and needs legal clarification for moves between Member States.

## TASKS

At first contact with the local arrival or immigration process, keep your passport or other identity papers, evidence of nationality and of where you lived or held protection in Ukraine, and documents showing family links or protected status where relevant. Also keep your first German entry date and any earlier temporary-protection decision from another Member State. The authority may ask for other evidence; the legislation does not prescribe one universal checklist.

Follow the local instructions for initial registration, allocation, address registration and the residence-title application. They are separate steps even where a municipality combines appointments. For people covered by the 90-day exemption, Germany4Ukraine says an application under §24 or for another purpose can be made during that period; the ordinance permits the necessary title to be obtained in Germany. The time limit is tied to the applicable entry exemption, not a universal deadline for every applicant.

## PLACES

The competent Ausländerbehörde (immigration authority) decides the §24 application and issues the German residence permit. Keep the application receipt and every written decision. If the authority issues a Fiktionsbescheinigung (provisional certificate), read its legal basis and work entry; submitting an application does not give everyone identical rights. Under §81 AufenthG, the effect while a case is pending depends on whether the person was lawfully present and whether an existing title was renewed or changed before expiry.

## NEXT STEPS

The EU extended temporary protection until 4 March 2028. Separately, the current text of UkraineAufenthFGV §2 says specified §24 permits valid on 1 February 2026 continue through 4 March 2027; the Germany4Ukraine registration page repeats 2027, while its labour-law page says 2028. These are inconsistent official statements about German permit continuation. Do not infer the validity date of your document from the EU decision or from another person’s permit. This conflict remains a publication blocker and must be reconciled against the operative German rule before this guide is published.
