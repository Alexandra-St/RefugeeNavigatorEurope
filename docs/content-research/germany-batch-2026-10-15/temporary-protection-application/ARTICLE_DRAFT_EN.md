# Applying for Temporary Protection and Checking Residence Dates

> Research draft — unpublished; pending human editorial review. No content in this draft is approved or marked Verified.

**Summary:** Distinguish the temporary exemption from holding a German residence title from EU temporary protection and a §24 permit; check the conditions and date that apply to your own document.

## RULES

Section 24 of the Residence Act (AufenthG) is the German route for issuing a residence permit to a person granted EU temporary protection who declares willingness to be received in Germany. Eligibility depends on the category set by EU law; nationality or flight from Ukraine alone does not decide it.

Separately, the Ukraine Residence Transition Ordinance (UkraineAufenthÜV) temporarily exempts specified categories from the requirement to hold a German residence title for 90 days from first entry. Conditions include entry by 4 December 2027 and not already having received temporary protection in another EU Member State; additional category conditions apply to third-country nationals. This title exemption is not ordinary visa-free travel for a short visit, a §24 permit or a decision granting protection. It ends if the residence-title application is rejected.

From 9 October 2026, §2(3) also removes that exemption for people entering Germany who are subject to Ukrainian military duties unless they prove compliance. This is a separate German condition on the title exemption.

Separately, EU Decision 2026/1912 makes compliance with military obligations, with proof where applicable, a condition for new grants of temporary protection from 5 August 2026. The EU exception covers people who held protection in the same Member State by 4 August and continuously retain it there.

A Germany4Ukraine notice describes the exception more broadly, for people already living in the EU; that wording is not the same as the corrected Decision. If you previously held protection in another Member State or moved between states, ask the Ausländerbehörde how the rule applies before relying on the exception; physical presence somewhere in the EU is not the same as continuous protection in one state. The cited official guidance does not set out one universal evidence checklist.

## TASKS

At first contact with the local arrival or immigration process, keep your passport or other identity papers, evidence of nationality and of where you lived or held protection in Ukraine, and documents showing family links or protected status where relevant. Also keep your first German entry date and any earlier temporary-protection decision from another Member State. The authority may ask for other evidence; the legislation does not prescribe one universal checklist.

Follow the local instructions for initial registration, allocation, address registration and the residence-title application. They are separate steps even where a municipality combines appointments. For people covered by the 90-day exemption, Germany4Ukraine says an application under §24 or for another purpose can be made during that period; the ordinance permits the necessary title to be obtained in Germany. The time limit is tied to the applicable entry exemption, not a universal deadline for every applicant.

Germany4Ukraine’s Entry page says to check with the local immigration authority whether you need an appointment or can apply online. Its registration page links to “Apply for a residence permit now”; that online service identifies a Brandenburg state ministry as its provider. This does not establish that it serves every municipality. Use it only if the instructions from the authority responsible for you point to that route.

## PLACES

The competent Ausländerbehörde (immigration authority) decides the §24 application and issues the German residence permit. Keep the application receipt and every written decision. If the authority issues a Fiktionsbescheinigung (provisional certificate), read its legal basis and work entry; submitting an application does not give everyone identical rights. Under §81 AufenthG, the effect while a case is pending depends on whether the person was lawfully present and whether an existing title was renewed or changed before expiry.

## NEXT STEPS

The EU has extended temporary protection through 4 March 2028, but that does not by itself set the validity date of an individual German permit. The current text of UkraineAufenthFGV §2 continues specified §24 permits valid on 1 February 2026 through 4 March 2027, subject to the regulation’s conditions.

As of 16 October 2026, the open Germany4Ukraine registration page also stated 4 March 2027. The indexed text available for Germany4Ukraine’s Labour Law page stated 4 March 2028; that page could not be opened directly during this check. The official material therefore does not give one consistent answer for every document.

Do not infer your document’s validity or work permission from the EU extension date or a general portal notice. Ask the competent Ausländerbehörde to confirm in writing the rule for your document. The discrepancy remains unresolved.
