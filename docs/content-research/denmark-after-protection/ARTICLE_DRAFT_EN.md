# After a residence permit under Denmark’s Ukraine Special Act

> **EDITORIAL DRAFT — UNPUBLISHED — NOT VERIFIED FOR PUBLICATION**
> Sources rechecked 15 October 2026. This information is general and does not decide whether a person qualifies for a permit or what they should apply for.

## A temporary residence basis, with conflicting extension information

A permit under Denmark’s Special Act for people displaced from Ukraine is a temporary residence permit. A regulation in force from 1 October 2026, **BEK no. 828/2026**, says that a permit under the Act can be extended until **17 March 2028**, unless grounds for revocation under section 10 apply. The Danish Immigration Service is to assess extension cases on its own initiative; the regulation says the cases have suspensive effect while they are being processed. This wording does not establish the outcome or date in an individual case.

There is an unresolved conflict in the official information: the Immigration Service’s Ukraine information page and its 2025 extension notice still say **17 March 2027** and contain March 2026 processing dates. Those dates cannot be reconciled here. Check your own decision and current messages from the authorities, and ask the Danish Immigration Service to clarify your case before relying on an end date.

## Routes linked to work or education

The official **Labour Market Attachment Scheme** page says that a person with a valid residence and work permit under the Ukraine Special Act may apply before that permit expires. The scheme has separate requirements. The page describes at least two years of legal, continuous employment with the same employer, without state or municipal wage subsidies paid to the employer, with pay and employment conditions corresponding to Danish standards, and generally full-time work. Some industries may treat fewer than 37 hours as full time. Self-employment cannot be used as the basis for this scheme. Meeting the listed conditions does not guarantee that an application will be approved.

The official **Educational Attachment Scheme** page also describes an application route for a person whose Ukraine Special Act permit is still valid: apply before it expires and document enrolment when applying. The programme and institution must meet the scheme’s rules; the page describes full-time study as the general rule and requires a programme that may lead to a job function on a relevant **Positive List**. This is a specific scheme, not a general right to change residence basis by enrolling in any course. Approval is not guaranteed.

Other work or study permits have their own criteria. For example, the **Pay Limit Scheme** requires a qualifying job offer and other scheme conditions; an ordinary higher-education permit requires admission and full-time study, among other requirements. These general pages do not establish that a Ukraine Special Act permit holder automatically qualifies for another route or that filing an application preserves residence or work rights after the current permit ends. Ask the responsible authority, **SIRI** or the Danish Immigration Service, which rules apply to the specific route and case.

## Permanent residence and proposed legislation

The Immigration Service says that a permit under the Special Act is not itself a basis for permanent residence and that time under that Act is not included in the eight-year residence calculation described on its page. That page does not answer how a later permit on another basis would affect an individual calculation. That question needs a separate check.

As checked on 15 October 2026, **Bill L 43 (2026–27)** was listed by the Folketing as introduced, with a first reading scheduled for 13 November 2026. It was a proposal on the check date, not a rule in force. Its status must be checked again before publication.

## Where to check

Read the exact authority page for the route you are considering and check your decision letter and messages from the Danish authorities. For a case-specific answer, contact the authority responsible for that application—SIRI or the Danish Immigration Service—or seek advice from a qualified Danish immigration professional. This draft cannot tell you which application to submit or whether you may remain or work while an application is pending.
