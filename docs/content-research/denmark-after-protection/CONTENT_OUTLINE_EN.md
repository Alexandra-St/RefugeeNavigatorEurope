# After a Ukraine Special Act permit: Denmark

> **DRAFT — NOT VERIFIED FOR PUBLICATION**
> Unpublished research dated 15 October 2026. This is not legal advice and does not determine any person's residence eligibility.

## Possible structure for a future article

### What is currently established about permit validity

- A Danish regulation in force from 1 October 2026 provides that a permit under the Special Act may be extended until **17 March 2028**, unless grounds for revocation apply. The Immigration Service assesses extensions on its own initiative; this does not guarantee an extension in an individual case.
- The Immigration Service's English information page still says 17 March 2027. This conflicts with the later regulation. Check your own decision letter and current authority messages before relying on a date.

### Can someone apply on another residence basis?

- The sources do not establish that every person under the Special Act can switch to any other permit type.
- The official **Labour Market Attachment Scheme** page expressly includes people with a valid Special Act permit. An application must be submitted before that permit expires. Separate criteria apply, including at least two years of qualifying employment with the same employer and Danish-standard pay and terms. Self-employment cannot be the basis for this scheme.
- The **Educational Attachment Scheme** expressly covers some holders of a valid Special Act permit: they must apply before it expires and document enrolment. Further programme and institution conditions apply, including full-time study and a connection between the programme and a job function on a relevant Positive List.
- Ordinary work and study permits have their own requirements. Applying in Denmark generally requires lawful stay; check the separate criteria for the specific route.
- Family reunification depends on the relationship, the family member's status in Denmark, and other requirements. A Special Act permit alone does not show that those requirements are met.

### What these sources do not determine

- Whether residence and work rights continue while a particular application is pending after a Special Act permit ends.
- Whether filing a new application affects an existing permit or its conditions in a particular case.
- Whether a later permit changes the permanent-residence calculation in an individual's case. The Immigration Service only states that time under the Special Act is excluded from the 8-year calculation described on its page.

### Where to check an individual situation

Use the relevant **New to Denmark** route page and contact the Danish Immigration Service or SIRI, depending on the application. A qualified Danish immigration adviser may be needed for an individual decision. Do not present this draft as a recommendation about what a person should do.
