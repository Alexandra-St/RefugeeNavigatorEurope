# Denmark: residence after the Ukraine Special Act

> **UNPUBLISHED RESEARCH — NOT LEGAL ADVICE — NOT VERIFIED FOR PUBLICATION**
> Research checked: **15 October 2026**. This is a dated research snapshot, not a promise that rules will remain unchanged.

## Scope and method

This review addresses official Danish information about residence permits for people who currently hold a permit under the Special Act on temporary residence permits for persons displaced from Ukraine. It distinguishes rules written specifically for Special Act permit holders from ordinary permit schemes. Official pages and legal materials were opened and read; search results were used only to locate the official pages. Sources were checked on the date above. The Danish Immigration Service (Udlændingestyrelsen) and SIRI (the Danish Agency for International Recruitment and Integration) publish the relevant service guidance; Retsinformation is the official Danish legal-information system.

This review does not determine anyone's eligibility, assess a personal case, or replace legal advice. A permit, ability to remain while an application is pending, and permission to work depend on the specific route, facts, decision letters, and applicable rules.

## Main findings

1. **The governing extension regulation now sets 17 March 2028.** BEK no. 828 of 23 September 2026, effective 1 October 2026, says a permit under the Ukraine Special Act can be extended to 17 March 2028 unless there are grounds for revocation under section 10. The Danish Immigration Service decides extension cases on its own initiative; processing has suspensive effect. This is a legal instrument, not a guarantee that an individual permit will be extended.
2. **There is an official-page conflict.** The Immigration Service's English Ukraine information page and its October 2025 extension notice still say 17 March 2027 and contain processing dates in March 2026. These pages were still accessible on 15 October 2026. They are stale against BEK no. 828. Do not reproduce the old date as current guidance; seek human legal/editorial review and recheck the authority page before drafting or publication.
3. **Two explicit, conditional transition routes were identified.** The Labour Market Attachment Scheme expressly names people with a valid Ukraine Special Act permit, with an application required before that permit expires. Its general conditions include at least two years of qualifying employment with the same employer, legal and continuous employment, and Danish-standard salary and terms. The Educational Attachment Scheme expressly covers valid Special Act permit holders who submit before expiry and document current enrolment; further education and lawful-stay conditions apply. Neither route is automatic or available to everyone.
4. **Ordinary work, study, and family routes remain route-specific.** Official pages describe general schemes and in-country application rules, but those rules do not establish that every Special Act holder qualifies or that every application can be made from Denmark. For work and family applications, the applicant generally must be legally in Denmark to submit there; a specific scheme's requirements still apply. A study route requires admission/enrolment and other conditions. Family reunification depends on the applicant and sponsor's relationship and status.
5. **Special Act residence does not itself lead to permanent residence.** The Immigration Service says a permit under the Special Act is not a basis for permanent residence and that time under it cannot be included in the ordinary 8-year calculation described on that page. The source does not establish how a later change of residence basis affects an individual calculation; that requires a separate check.
6. **Current rules also have individual exceptions and active legislative changes.** From 3 September 2026, special rules apply to some Ukrainian men aged 23–59 and to some applicants under 23. Separately, bill L 43 (2026–27) was introduced on 8 October 2026 and, on the Folketing's status page checked 15 October, was listed as introduced with first reading scheduled for 13 November. It is a proposal, not an enacted rule on the check date.

## Research answers

### A–B. Current basis and validity

The Special Act is a temporary residence basis. The controlling current extension measure found in this review is BEK no. 828/2026: eligible permits can be extended to 17 March 2028, subject to possible revocation grounds under section 10. The Immigration Service is to assess cases on its own initiative. A person should check the individual decision/permit and official messages; the regulation does not mean every permit is unconditionally extended.

The Immigration Service's general Ukraine information page still states 17 March 2027. That conflicts with the later, effective regulation. This is an editorial hold point: the page should be rechecked, and the discrepancy should be resolved by a human reviewer before any public article states an individual deadline or processing steps.

### C–D. Changing to another residence basis

The strongest direct evidence is scheme-specific:

- **Labour Market Attachment Scheme:** the official page expressly includes a person who still has a valid permit under the Ukraine Special Act and says to apply before that permit expires. It also describes a seven-day window after a final decision revoking a permit or refusing extension. The route's conditions include two years of qualifying employment with the same employer and Danish-standard salary/terms; the application page must be read in full. Self-employment is not a qualifying basis for this scheme.
- **Educational Attachment Scheme:** a current Special Act permit holder may apply before the permit expires, but must document enrolment at application time. The page describes additional conditions for the educational institution and programme, including full-time study and a connection to a job function that can form the basis for a permit under a Positive List. For a permit already revoked/refused extension or an expired EU right, the page imposes lawful-residence and timing conditions; do not generalize those special rules beyond the page.
- **Ordinary work schemes:** for example, the Pay Limit Scheme requires an offer meeting the applicable scheme criteria. When applying in Denmark, the applicant must be there legally. The page does not make the route available merely because a person holds a Special Act permit.
- **Higher education:** an ordinary student permit requires admission to an eligible programme, full-time study, and other conditions. If applying in Denmark, legal presence is required. This general page does not itself confirm that every Special Act holder may switch to a student basis.
- **Family routes:** New to Denmark describes family-reunification and accompanying-family categories. In-country submission is normally possible for someone staying legally, but only if the specific relationship, sponsor-status, and other requirements are met. A Special Act permit is not evidence by itself that those requirements are met.

No evidence reviewed supports a general statement that any person under the Special Act can transition to any other permit. Do not say an application is a “conversion” or that a new permit is guaranteed.

### E–G. Applying in Denmark and consequences

For some routes, official guidance permits submission from Denmark if the person is legally present; work and family pages state this generally, and the study guidance also requires legal presence. The specific Special Act transition routes have their own deadlines and criteria. The reviewed sources do **not** establish that filing any alternative application automatically preserves the Special Act permit, residence rights, work rights, benefits, or ability to remain after expiry. They also do not establish that an application automatically cancels the existing permit. This point is **UNCLEAR** and must be referred to the competent authority or a qualified adviser before public guidance gives procedural advice.

### H. Permanent residence

The Immigration Service says a person cannot obtain permanent residence on the grounds of a Special Act permit, and that time under the Special Act cannot be included in the 8-year calculation described on its page. Do not imply that the period counts because the person was physically resident in Denmark. Whether later residence under a different basis changes an individual calculation is outside what this source establishes.

### I. Deadlines and restrictions

- For direct Special Act-specific transition routes found here, the permit expiry matters: the Labour Market Attachment and Educational Attachment pages require an application before expiry for current Special Act permit holders.
- For people whose Special Act permit has been revoked or extension refused, specific pages describe a seven-day window for certain routes. These are route-specific and may depend on a final decision and lawful stay; do not present them as a general grace period.
- Special Act extension can be denied if revocation grounds apply under section 10.
- The 2026 military-status amendment affects some age/application groups; current pages state that it applies to relevant applications submitted from 25 June 2026 and distinguishes earlier applications.
- The proposed L 43 is not current law on the research date. Its status and any later enactment must be checked again before publication.

## Content recommendation

The material is **sufficient to begin a carefully qualified editorial draft**, but **not sufficient for publication or verification**. Keep the draft out of production. The draft should lead with the 2028 regulation, clearly flag that an Immigration Service page still says 2027, describe only the two directly documented conditional transition routes, and direct readers to the exact authority pages. Before publication, a reviewer should resolve the official-page conflict and verify how an application affects the reader's current residence/work rights and pending period.

Proposed article shape:

1. What the current regulation says about permit extension (and note to check the individual decision).
2. What the official sources say about employment- and education-linked routes, with eligibility conditions and pre-expiry deadlines.
3. Family and other routes: only as links to the authority's individualized criteria, not as a promise of a transition.
4. What this page cannot determine: eligibility, right to stay/work while a new case is pending, and the effect on permanent-residence calculations after a change of basis.
5. Official contacts and source links; advise getting case-specific guidance from the responsible authority or qualified legal adviser.
