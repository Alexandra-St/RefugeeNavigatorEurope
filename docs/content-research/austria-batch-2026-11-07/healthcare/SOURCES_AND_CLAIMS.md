# Austria — Healthcare Access, Proof and Urgent Care

**Research checked:** 2026-11-07

**Scope:** exactly `healthcare-access`, `healthcare-proof`, and `healthcare-urgent`. Research only; individual insurance and treatment decisions belong to the relevant insurer and provider.

## Research verdict

**READY FOR CONTENT DRAFTING WITH QUALIFICATIONS.** Austria’s special compulsory health-insurance inclusion for displaced people from Ukraine ended on 2025-05-31; a six-week transitional entitlement applied to people previously covered. On 2026-11-07 TP and the displaced-person card alone do not establish active health insurance. Depending on the person, employment or self-employment, eligible family co-insurance, self-insurance, unemployment-benefit coverage or insurance within means-tested basic care may provide a basis. The e-card and insurance entitlement are distinct; an insured person may be treated before receiving the physical card through the prescribed substitute arrangements. Ordinary services, medicines and dental care are not universally free. Emergency first aid and public-hospital duties for cases that cannot be deferred must be distinguished from the later billing and coverage of continuing care. [S01–S07, S10–S15]

## Coverage and proof boundaries

ÖGK’s 2025 notice states that the Ukraine-specific compulsory health-insurance inclusion ended at the close of **2025-05-31**, including affected children, with a six-week statutory tolerance for people already covered (S01). BMI’s current page continues to describe the end of that special inclusion and identifies employment, self-insurance, family co-insurance and basic-care insurance as possible alternative bases (S02). No reviewed 2026 source reintroduced a general automatic TP-wide compulsory scheme. TP remains a residence status; insurance depends on an applicable current basis.

Insurable employment can create coverage from the start of employment; the employer normally registers the worker before work begins. Self-employed persons follow their own insurance rules. Receiving unemployment benefits can provide a separate insurance basis, subject to the applicable award (S01–S02, S06). After employment ends, continued entitlement and the next insurance basis depend on the individual’s circumstances; a universal duration is **Not established** here.

An insured person can generally receive an e-card after registration if an eligible photo is available or a statutory exception applies. If no photo is available, S04 explains photo registration, the 150-day period after first request, and an electronic substitute certificate. Before a card arrives or after loss, an insured person can use an Austrian social-insurance number and photo ID or the insurer’s substitute proof; the provider can check entitlement in the e-card system. Report loss to the insurer and arrange replacement (S04–S06). A negative electronic check should be clarified with the insurer; a physical card does not independently create entitlement. **No universal provider document checklist established.** Identity, status, insurance, prescriptions and referrals are different matters.

## Ordinary and urgent care boundaries

Insured persons generally access covered necessary care through contracted doctors and facilities, subject to the relevant insurer and service rules (S07). Specialists may require a referral or authorization depending on the service; this review establishes no universal referral rule. Wahlärzte generally bill the patient first and partial reimbursement may be possible; fully private providers can be self-paid (S09). Medicines may have a `Rezeptgebühr`, exemptions can apply, and not every product is automatically covered (S07, S10–S11). Dental treatment and prostheses have defined coverage and can involve patient costs or private charges (S07, S17). An e-card does not mean every service is free.

For immediate threats to life or serious harm, call **144** or **112**. **1450** provides health advice for non-emergency questions; **141** is an on-call doctor service (S14). S12 describes public-hospital admission for legally non-deferrable cases, including imminent childbirth, and S13 distinguishes emergency first aid from uninsured people’s general duty to meet health-service costs. Emergency access is not proof that all ambulance, hospital or subsequent treatment is free. For an uninsured person with TP, check eligibility for Grundversorgung or another insurance basis and available local support; no blanket planned-care or follow-up entitlement is inferred (S02, S12–S15).

## Critical questions — direct answers

1. **Every TP beneficiary automatically insured?** No: the special inclusion ended in 2025; coverage needs a current basis (S01–S02).
2. **2025/2026 change?** The special inclusion ended 2025-05-31 with six-week tolerance for previously covered people. Current BMI guidance lists other insurance bases; no renewed universal 2026 scheme was found (S01–S02).
3. **Ordinary routes?** Qualifying employment/self-employment, unemployment-benefit insurance, eligible family co-insurance, self-insurance or insurance through eligible basic care, according to circumstances (S01–S03).
4. **Employment?** Insurable employment can create coverage; not all marginal arrangements do (S02, S06).
5. **Family co-insurance?** Possible for qualifying family members, not automatic for everyone with TP (S02, S08).
6. **TP card proves active insurance?** No; it proves displaced status, while coverage must be checked (S01–S02, S05).
7. **e-card issued?** Normally after insurance registration when a photo or exception is available (S04, S06).
8. **Care before card arrives?** For an insured person, number plus photo ID or a substitute certificate can support the provider’s eligibility check (S04–S06).
9. **How check insurance?** Provider checks the e-card system using card or insurance number; the insurer can clarify discrepancies (S05–S06).
10. **Ordinary doctors/hospitals universally free?** No; coverage and patient costs depend on insurer, provider and service (S07, S09, S17).
11. **Medicines universally free?** No; charges, conditions and exemptions can apply (S10–S11).
12. **Dental universally covered?** No; covered services and costs depend on treatment and insurer (S07, S17).
13. **Uninsured TP holder?** Emergency first aid/non-deferrable hospital care and possibly eligible basic care or local assistance; universal routine coverage is not established (S02, S12–S15).
14. **Emergency without insurance/card?** Emergency first aid and legally non-deferrable public-hospital admission are distinct from insurance verification (S12–S14).
15. **Emergency necessarily free?** No universal cost-free episode established; emergency first aid and subsequent billing must be distinguished (S12–S13).
16. **Numbers?** 144 ambulance and 112 emergency; 1450 advice, 141 on-call doctor (S14).

## Drafting boundaries for Phase 22 content

- TP is not automatically active insurance under the current source set; the `Ausweis für Vertriebene` is not necessarily proof of current health-insurance entitlement.
- An e-card is a means of identifying/checking insurance, not a guarantee of active coverage or free services. Insured persons without the physical card may have substitute arrangements.
- Public/contracted, elective and private providers differ; medicines, dental treatment, hospital stays and other services may involve fees or cost-sharing.
- Emergency access is not equivalent to universally free subsequent care. Avoid implying that a missing card should delay an emergency call.
- Historic Ukraine-specific insurance and its 2025 tolerance period must not be presented as current automatic 2026 coverage.
- No general pre-established entitlement to all maternity, child, planned or follow-up care for uninsured TP holders was found. Verify the individual's basis and local support.
