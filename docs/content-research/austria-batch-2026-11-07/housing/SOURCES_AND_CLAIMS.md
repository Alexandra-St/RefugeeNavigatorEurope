# Austria — Housing

**Research date / LastCheckedAt:** 2026-11-07
**Scope:** exactly `housing-initial`, `housing-private-rental`, and `housing-financial-support`. Research only; no article, catalog, or application changes.

## Research verdict

**READY FOR CONTENT DRAFTING WITH QUALIFICATIONS.** Austrian temporary-protection holders may apply for provincial `Grundversorgung` where they are in need, but temporary-protection status alone does not make support automatic. The federal–provincial agreement establishes a common framework and cost ceilings; it expressly does not create an individual right. The Länder administer applications and accommodation, with operational routes varying by province and sometimes delegated to service providers. Organized accommodation depends on eligibility and available capacity. The current BBU information describes a small, limited-capacity 24/7 first-arrival centre in Linz and warns that placement may take days, with no support during the wait; this is not a national bed guarantee.

For privately housed eligible recipients, `Grundversorgung` can include rent and food support. The latest federal cost ceilings located are €165/month rent for a single person or €330/month per family (two or more persons, total), plus food ceilings of €260/month per adult and €145/month per minor. These are cost ceilings in the federal–provincial framework, not a guaranteed payment to each person; provincial implementation and the individual need decision matter. Organized lodging instead has a €25/person/day accommodation-and-food cost ceiling. Do not conflate provider/cost reimbursement with money paid to the recipient.

Ordinary private rental is a separate landlord–tenant contract. Temporary-protection status does not itself establish landlord acceptance or a special housing entitlement. A lease can generally be oral (S20), but fixed-term agreements in the full or partial MRG scope require a written term and must meet applicable duration rules (S13). The MRG applies fully, partly, or not at all depending on the property and tenancy. There is no universal statutory deposit cap; the generally cited six-gross-monthly-rent boundary is case-law guidance, not a statutory ceiling, and a higher deposit can be justified by exceptional security interest.

There is no single Austria-wide `Wohnbeihilfe`: the nine Länder have separate rules. No nationwide temporary-protection-specific deposit grant was established in the reviewed official sources. Municipal and subsidized housing eligibility is also local and commonly includes residence, status, and income criteria; TP alone does not automatically qualify someone.

## Compulsory support, placement and arrival boundaries

The Art. 15a agreement is a common federal–provincial framework. The 2022 amendment includes people with temporary protection under §62 AsylG within its potential coverage. However, Art. 1(5) says the agreement does **not** create an individual legal claim. Art. 2 defines need by whether a person/household can meet basic living needs from their own resources or support from others. Thus the defensible answer is: an eligible TP holder may apply for Grundversorgung, but TP status itself is not automatic acceptance or an unconditional promise of an accommodation unit.

For Ukraine-related applications, BBU says police recording is required before an application and the application is made in the federal state where the person is registered. Contact the appropriate Land Grundversorgungsstelle; channels may be a Land office, district authority, municipality, or a contracted service organization. BBU can provide orientation and initial-arrival information. The agreement assigns the Länder core operational responsibilities; federal functions include coordination and contingency capacity. Do not describe BBU as the sole nationwide placement authority.

The recognized models are organized lodging and individual/private accommodation. Organized lodging combines suitable accommodation and food; private Grundversorgung can include a rent component and food support. Neither means every TP holder gets a free bed or the same cash benefit. The framework’s mass-arrival provisions refer to accommodation in facilities subject to capacity. Current BBU information says organized placement can take several days and provides no support during the waiting period. Its current first-arrival page lists one small 24/7 centre in Linz with limited capacity. Verify local availability before arrival; this snapshot does not amount to a national first-arrival scheme or guaranteed overnight place.

Unaccompanied minors have a distinct enhanced-care framework, including needs-adjusted organized or supported placement and, when needed, social-pedagogical/psychological support. The framework also sets a special accommodation ceiling for care-dependent people. These provisions do not establish a universal priority schedule for all families, pregnant people, older people, or people with a disability/illness. Report specific needs to the competent Land and ask how they are considered.

No uniform nationwide rule for resident contributions, house rules, inter-province transfers, ending an individual placement, or appeal procedure was established from the reviewed public sources. The federal maximum cost amount is not evidence of a free bed or recipient cash entitlement.

## Private rental and residence registration boundaries

An ordinary lease follows Austrian tenancy law. The MRG can apply in full, in part, or not at all; relevant factors include the building/permit/funding and ownership/tenancy setup. Where the MRG does not control a specific issue, ABGB rules and the contract may be central; KSchG consumer rules may also matter depending on the landlord’s business status. Do not label a dwelling “fully regulated” based only on its being an apartment in Austria.

A lease may generally be oral, so “every lease must be written” is incorrect. The OGH confirms oral formation where the essential elements are agreed. A written contract is strongly advisable to establish the unit, parties, agreed rent and components, utilities/heating, deposit, duration, handover condition and termination terms. In MRG full/partial scope, a fixed-term residential lease must be written and meet current minimum-duration rules; the term can be affected by whether the landlord is an entrepreneur under the KSchG. No universal fixed-term statement should be applied to MRG-exempt leases without checking their governing rules.

A deposit is by agreement, not a universal statutory requirement or statutory capped amount. The Justice portal’s usual three-month practice and six-month general guideline should be labeled as such. A higher amount may be justified by a special security interest. If money is held by the landlord it must be invested in the described protected, interest-bearing form; if there are no justified claims after lease end, the tenant generally receives the deposit and interest back. Condition evidence at handover is useful; no exact deduction formula is supplied here.

After moving into a dwelling, file the ordinary residence registration within three days at the competent registration authority. For a paper Meldezettel the actual accommodation provider signs; this is not necessarily the property owner (e.g. a main tenant can be the provider to a flatmate). Online registration uses its prescribed electronic route. TP police/BFA registration is separate. BFA/BMI say keeping the residence registered/current is important so the displaced-person card can be delivered and BFA can reach the holder. The reviewed sources do not establish a universal second BFA filing after the Meldeamt update; Grundversorgung recipients should also notify their own Land/provider/BBU when their residence or circumstances change, as applicable.

## Financial support and regional boundaries

Three systems must not be mixed:

1. **Grundversorgung:** A means-tested, provincial-administered support framework; eligible private residents may get rent/food support, while organized lodging is supplied in kind. The Art. 9 figures are ceilings in a federal/provincial cost agreement, not an automatic award. The federal share of costs with the Länder is a government-to-government settlement and never a beneficiary entitlement.
2. **Wohnbeihilfe / Wohnunterstützung:** A Land-level housing benefit, not a nationwide TP programme. Each of the nine Länder sets its own rules. Local residence, status, income, household, rental-contract and dwelling criteria must be checked with the responsible Land; eligibility for TP holders across all provinces is **Not established**.
3. **Sozialhilfe / Mindestsicherung:** Separate provincial social assistance rules may include housing-related amounts or discretionary help, but temporary-protection beneficiaries should not be told they automatically qualify. The BBU and federal guidance direct them to Grundversorgung. Broader Socialhilfe interaction is outside this package’s scope.

No nationwide temporary-protection-specific deposit assistance or rent guarantee was established. Individual Länder, municipalities, charities or hardship programmes may provide local help, but the present research does not establish a national entitlement or inventory such programmes. Municipal, subsidized and cooperative housing are also separate allocation systems; residence duration, status, income and waiting-list rules vary. The federal overview lists Vienna’s two-year main-residence condition as an example and does not establish that TP alone meets Vienna or other municipalities’ status criteria.

## Critical questions — direct answers

1. **Can Austrian TP holders receive Grundversorgung accommodation support?** They may apply if in need and otherwise eligible; temporary-protection status alone does not guarantee admission or a place (S01–S05).
2. **Is it automatic?** No. Police recording and an application through the relevant provincial route are part of the current process (S05).
3. **Is financial need required?** Yes. The framework defines need by inability to meet basic needs sufficiently from own resources or support from others/institutions. No single national income cutoff was established (S01).
4. **Is organized accommodation guaranteed?** No. It depends on assessment and capacity; no individual legal claim or universal guaranteed bed was established (S01, S06).
5. **Who administers placement?** The relevant Land’s Grundversorgungs office is the principal administrative route; some provinces use district/city authorities or service providers. Federal/BBU coordination and referral functions do not mean BBU allocates every bed (S01, S04–S05, S19).
6. **Is there currently a nationwide first-arrival accommodation programme?** No nationwide guaranteed programme/place was established. BBU currently lists one small, limited-capacity 24/7 first-arrival centre in Linz; some provinces have other first-arrival options (S04, S06).
7. **Can TP holders rent privately?** No TP-specific prohibition was identified in the general tenancy sources, but landlord agreement and ordinary contract/income/document requirements still apply. No landlord acceptance is guaranteed (S11–S13, S20).
8. **Must every residential lease be written?** No. A lease may generally be oral; fixed terms in MRG full/partial scope require written agreement and comply with MRG duration rules (S11, S13, S20).
9. **Is there a universal statutory deposit cap?** No. Six gross monthly rents is case-law guidance, not a statutory cap; more may be justified by a special security interest (S15).
10. **How does Meldezettel registration work after moving?** Register with the competent Gemeinde/Magistrat/Meldebehörde within three days; on paper the actual accommodation provider signs. Digital registration follows its online process (S07–S09).
11. **Is there a nationwide rent subsidy for TP holders?** No separate universal rent subsidy outside the needs-based Grundversorgung was established. Wohnbeihilfe is provincial (S04, S16–S17).
12. **Does private Grundversorgung include housing support?** It may include a rent component and food support following need review and acceptance (S01, S03–S05).
13. **Are payment amounts national or provincial?** The Art. 9 framework has national cost ceilings, while Länder administer awards and can have different implementation/amounts. A ceiling is not a guaranteed beneficiary payment (S01, S03–S04).
14. **Is Wohnbeihilfe nationwide or provincial?** Provincial: the nine Länder have separate rules (S16–S17).
15. **Does every TP holder qualify for Wohnbeihilfe?** No such universal qualification was established. Status, residence, income, household, lease and dwelling requirements vary and need Land-specific checking (S16–S17).
16. **Is there nationwide deposit assistance?** Not established in reviewed official sources. Local/provincial discretionary options may exist; check locally (S15–S17).
17. **Does employment/income affect Grundversorgung?** Yes. Report income; it is considered in the needs assessment and can reduce or end support. Exact outcome is individual/provincial (S05).
18. **Does TP automatically give access to municipal/social housing?** No. Local eligibility and allocation rules apply, often including status, residence-duration and income criteria; TP alone was not established as automatic qualification (S18).

## Evidence boundaries

- The agreement’s national framework must not be mistaken for an individual enforceable guarantee: Art. 1(5) expressly says it creates no individual legal claim.
- Federal cost-sharing and provider reimbursement ceilings are not payments owed directly to a beneficiary.
- Current operational facts (Linz arrival centre, provincial intake, Vienna organized-bed intake) are live administrative information; recheck before advising travel or placement.
- The last checked BMI page said a rent-ceiling increase was under discussion, while the consolidated legal text retained the published figures above. No enacted replacement amount was located by the research date; do not imply that a prospective increase is already effective.
- The reviewed sources do not establish a single national income threshold, complete provider document checklist, general contribution rule, resident house-rule/termination process, or vulnerability-priority ranking.
- No full nine-province Wohnbeihilfe eligibility review or nationwide municipal/social-housing eligibility survey was undertaken. National drafting should state the regional boundary and direct a person to their Land/municipality.
- No nationwide TP-specific deposit grant, loan or guarantee was located; absence from reviewed federal sources is not proof that no local hardship help exists.
- Housing support and Grundversorgung-linked health insurance are distinct components. Eligibility for health insurance through Grundversorgung does not establish that accommodation is automatic or available (S01; see Austria healthcare research package).
- No historical 2022 emergency hotel/arrival scheme is represented as current. The current arrival finding is based on BBU’s live information checked on the research date.

## Drafting boundaries for Phase 22 content

- State that Grundversorgung is need-based and requires application/assessment; it is not an automatic TP entitlement.
- Do not promise an available organized bed, a particular province/city, free lodging, or support during a wait.
- Explain that provincial implementation and intake routes differ; direct people to the relevant Land office and recheck current contact/availability.
- Keep organized accommodation and private-rent support separate. Organized-provider cost ceilings are not cash paid to the beneficiary.
- Do not present a federal/Länder reimbursement maximum as a beneficiary’s individual award. The current published ceilings are qualified maxima, with provincial/individual assessment and possible divergence.
- Do not claim one Austria-wide `Wohnbeihilfe`, universal Sozialhilfe housing component, or nationwide TP rent subsidy.
- Do not say every TP holder qualifies for Wohnbeihilfe, municipal housing, cooperative housing, or a particular deposit-assistance scheme.
- Do not claim a universal statutory deposit cap. If mentioning six gross monthly rents, label it a general case-law guideline, not statute; exceptional higher deposits may be possible.
- Explain that private-rental rules depend on MRG/ABGB applicability and, where relevant, KSchG status. An oral lease may exist; fixed-term writing rules apply within their legal scope.
- Separate ordinary Meldezettel registration from TP police/BFA registration. For an address change, keep ZMR/Meldeamt current and notify the responsible Grundversorgung office/provider where relevant; do not invent a universal extra BFA notice.
- Mark the Linz first-arrival centre and any province-specific intake rule as time-sensitive operational information; no historical Ukraine scheme should be described as current without a fresh official check.
