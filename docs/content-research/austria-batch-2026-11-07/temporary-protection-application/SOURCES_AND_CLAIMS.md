## Research verdict

**READY FOR CONTENT DRAFTING WITH EXPLICIT BOUNDARIES.** Austrian law and current federal guidance establish the principal covered groups, the Austrian term for the right and its card, the police registration/BFA document process, the current national end date through 4 March 2028, and examples of evidence for the new military-obligation condition. Registration details are unusually specific, including treatment when documents are missing. Two important qualifications must remain explicit: the continuity exception to the military rule has conflicting Austrian official dates (30 July versus 4 August 2026), and the BFA’s published card-renewal information addresses already registered holders with a registered Austrian residence rather than every possible new-card scenario. The research supports bounded drafting, but not an individual military-obligation decision or a definitive account of the contradictory cutoff.

## Compulsory categories, exclusions, and terminology boundaries

- **EU core:** Ukrainian nationals resident in Ukraine before 2022-02-24 and displaced on/after that date by the invasion; stateless and third-country nationals with international or equivalent Ukrainian national protection granted before that date and displaced on/after it; and qualifying family members (S01 Art. 2(1)).
- **Austria’s adopted categories:** Austrian `Vertriebenen-Verordnung` §1 covers the Ukrainian-national group, the protected stateless/third-country group, and family as defined in §2 (S02). Austrian family scope includes spouses/registered partners, minor unmarried children, and other close relatives who lived with and were wholly/mainly dependent on the displaced person before displacement and were already in Ukraine as family before 2022-02-24.
- **Austria’s national Ukrainian-national routes:** §3 separately addresses defined Ukrainian citizens already lawfully in Austria on 2022-02-24 or holding an Austrian residence title then, where statutory later non-renewal/expiry and inability-to-return conditions are met (S02). These provisions do not establish a general extra cohort for all Ukrainian former residents.
- **Excluded/qualified cases:** Austrian law excludes Article 28 exclusion grounds and a person already holding temporary-protection residence authorization in another Member State (S02 §1(2)). BMI says a third-country national with only an ordinary Ukrainian residence permit, without Ukrainian asylum or comparable protection, is not in the rule (S04).
- **Terminology:** use `vorübergehendes Aufenthaltsrecht` / temporary right of residence and `Ausweis für Vertriebene` / displaced-person card. The right is not the same thing as the physical card; the card documents the right (S04, S07, S12).
- **Limited rights statement:** the federal portal says the card documents the temporary residence right and that holders have unrestricted Austrian labor-market access (S12). Avoid adding broader welfare, education or travel claims in this three-aspect package.

## Initial registration and document boundaries

The Austrian sources describe a statutory right for named categories and a police data-capture process for issuing the document; they do not describe an ordinary asylum application. Eligible people are directed to police recording locations. The BMI publishes location-specific appointment information; Vienna has an electronic appointment booking flow. That booking is not an online application, and no general proxy-registration route was established (S04, S06).

At registration, police record personal and document data, take fingerprints from people aged 14 and older, photograph each person, and collect the completed/signed data sheet. The official BFA form includes fields about the applicant’s identity and Ukrainian residence, family relationship, minors/guardians, travel route and entry, Austrian residence permissions, military obligations/evidence, criminal convictions, and current address (S04–S06). A form field does not by itself prove that every listed document must be supplied in every case.

BMI asks people to bring, **if available**, a passport, birth/marriage/other civil-status records, and other identity documents. It expressly says people who could not bring all documents should still register if they fall within the described group. BFA can contact the person for a follow-up conversation or missing information; in some cases further fingerprints or a passport photo may be needed (S04, S06). Therefore, do not publish a universal document checklist or tell a person without a passport to wait before registering.

BFA issues the `Ausweis für Vertriebene` after the police data are available and any further clarification is complete; BMI says it is mailed to a registered or specified delivery address and can take several weeks. Residence registration is operationally important for delivery. A fee, nationwide appointment rule, nationwide online application, proxy route, initial processing deadline, and complete procedure for unaccompanied minors are **not established** in the reviewed sources. The BFA form records whether a minor is accompanied and asks for guardian information; this alone does not establish a full child-specific process (S04–S06).

## EU and Austrian date boundaries

Keep three different dates separate:

1. **EU scheme:** Decision (EU) 2026/1912 extends the EU temporary-protection period to **2028-03-04**, with the extension under Article 1 applying from **2027-03-05** (S09).
2. **Austrian right:** the RIS §4 annotation and current federal guidance state that the applicable Austrian temporary residence right continues through **2028-03-04** (S02, S04, S07–S08). This endpoint is confirmed in Austrian official sources; the reviewed evidence does not identify a separate Austrian duration-extension act. BGBl. II No. 304/2026 is the military-condition amendment, not that duration instrument.
3. **Individual physical card:** BFA’s 2026-08-05 materials say the right continues automatically from 2027-03-04 to 2028-03-04; people already registered with Austrian residence registration are to receive a replacement card automatically, with an extended printed date. The underlying right remains after an old card’s printed 2027-03-04 expiry (S07–S08). Exact printed validity for every card issued to a newly registered person after the latest change, and the timing for all exceptional cases, are not established. Do not infer that a card is invalid merely because an old printed date has passed, or promise identical renewal mechanics to every person.

## Military-obligation rule and unresolved transition

At EU level, Article 2 of Decision (EU) 2026/1912 requires compliance with military obligations under Ukrainian law and proof **where applicable**; Article 2 applies from **2026-08-05** (S09–S10). The corrigendum says the rule does not apply to a person who had temporary protection in a given Member State before or on **2026-08-04** and continuously retains it in that same State afterward (S10).

Austria’s national amendment, BGBl. II No. 304/2026, was promulgated on **2026-10-15** and adds the military condition to the `Vertriebenen-Verordnung` (S02–S03). Austrian federal BMI guidance describes the continuity cutoff as **2026-08-04** and says the exception applies to continuous status in the same Member State (S04). However, the Austrian regulation’s §5(3) transition clause uses **2026-07-30**, and a BFA FAQ dated 2026-08-05 also repeats July 30 (S02, S06). The EU corrigendum was published on 2026-08-05. The reviewed sources do not explain the national date mismatch or the effect of the Austrian amendment’s 2026-10-15 effective date on the intervening period, despite a BMI press release describing the rule as applying to new arrivals from early August (S11).

Austria’s current BMI page and the BFA registration sheet establish that authorities ask about compliance/exemption and that the applicant may provide, as examples, evidence of legal exit from Ukraine (such as a passport stamp) or an official Ukrainian Ministry of Defence paper/electronic confirmation, including Reserv+ (S04–S05). The sources do not establish an exhaustive proof checklist, an age range, a complete Austrian screening protocol, which exact office decides disputed evidence, or an appeal/time-to-decision process. Do not characterize the rule as applying only to men or only to a stated age band; BMI says obligations may exist irrespective of age or sex, while the legal text remains qualified by “where applicable.”

## Drafting boundaries for Phase 22 content

### Safe to state

- The Austrian name for the measure is a temporary right of residence for displaced persons, documented by an `Ausweis für Vertriebene`.
- The EU core cohorts and Austria’s specific statutory family definition, with all relevant residence/date/displacement conditions.
- Austria’s separate, carefully bounded national routes for certain Ukrainian citizens already lawfully in Austria on 2022-02-24.
- First registration/data capture is through police recording locations; BFA issues the card; an asylum application is not required for this route.
- Fingerprints are taken at age 14 and above; each person is photographed; listed identity/civil-status papers should be brought if available; missing papers are not a reason to postpone registration according to BMI/BFA guidance.
- Current Austrian right and EU scheme end on 2028-03-04, while distinguishing the EU decision, Austrian implementation, and the physical card. The BFA’s automatic replacement-card statement can be limited to existing registered holders with active Austrian residence registration.
- The military rule is “where applicable”; official Austrian guidance lists examples of proof but not an exhaustive checklist.

### Require qualification

- Describe the Austrian family definition precisely; do not import the EU or another country’s definition where Austrian §2 differs.
- Call the online tool an appointment booking for Vienna, not online status application. Note that locations/appointments are operational details subject to refresh.
- State that missing documents may lead to BFA follow-up, additional data collection, fingerprints or a photograph; do not promise card issuance solely on an incomplete self-declaration.
- For military evidence, use “examples published by BMI” and state that the sources do not establish an exhaustive acceptable-proof list.
- Identify the unresolved cutoff conflict (30 July in the Austrian regulation/BFA FAQ versus 4 August in the EU corrigendum/current BMI page); do not tell a reader that a particular grandfathering date definitively applies in Austria.
- Identify that the Austrian amendment was promulgated on 15 October, while EU Article 2 applies from 5 August; do not make a legal conclusion for the period between those dates.
- A dated card and the legal right are distinct. BFA’s current replacement-card instructions cover a defined existing-holder group and do not establish all new-card situations.

### Do not assert

- Eligibility for every Ukrainian national regardless of pre-war residence, displacement, national exceptions, exclusions, or another Member State’s TP status.
- Austrian eligibility for every stateless/third-country national with ordinary residence in Ukraine, or for all permanent residents without protection status.
- That a person needs an asylum application, that an online/proxy application exists, or that every police station accepts registration without an appointment.
- A universal complete list of required documents, a fee amount or blanket fee waiver, instant card issuance, fixed processing period, or universal procedure for unaccompanied minors.
- A military-obligation age/sex rule, an exhaustive proof list, or a settled Austrian continuity exception date while the official materials conflict.
- That the EU scheme date, Austrian right, and individual card’s printed expiry are interchangeable.

## Final topic verdict

**READY FOR CONTENT DRAFTING WITH EXPLICIT BOUNDARIES**

Primary sources establish enough to draft the three aspects without misleading readers, provided that the military continuity cutoff and August-to-October implementation window are explicitly left unresolved, and card replacement claims are limited to the group described by BFA. Recheck the BMI/BFA operational pages and the military transition before using this as a live procedural checklist or making an individual eligibility determination.
