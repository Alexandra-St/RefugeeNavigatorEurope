# Austria — Finding a Job: Sources and Claims

**Research checked:** 2026-11-07

**Scope:** exactly `employment-vacancies`, `employment-institutions`, and `employment-assistance-conditions`. Research only; not an individual eligibility decision. Read with Austria Phase 22.3 employment-rights research.

Keep public vacancy browsing, AMS jobseeker registration, permission to work, programme participation and unemployment-insurance cash benefit distinct.

## Research verdict

**READY FOR CONTENT DRAFTING WITH QUALIFICATIONS.** AMS `alle jobs` and the public search in `eJob-Room` are available without registration; EURES offers public search and mobility information independently of Austrian AMS registration. An Austrian displaced-person cardholder can register with AMS for job-search support; AMS directs people to bring the card to the first visit. Registration does not itself confer a work right or a cash benefit. AMS services include vacancy proposals, counselling and, where assessed appropriate, training; places and funding are not guaranteed. ÖIF publishes Austria-wide German courses specifically for Ukrainian displaced persons, subject to consultation and availability. Unemployment benefit requires a separate AMS application and individual assessment, including qualifying insured employment and availability for work. Ukrainian insurance periods are not automatically credited on the evidence reviewed. [S01–S10, S12–S14]

## Registration, services and benefit boundaries

A displaced-person cardholder may register with AMS as `arbeitssuchend` (jobseeking). AMS’s Ukraine information requests the `Ausweis für Vertriebene` at the first visit and, if possible, the completed skills questionnaire; personal details, education, experience and competencies are discussed (S03–S05). It does not establish that police registration alone, an absent card, or any single universal document checklist suffices. Address/residence determines the regional office for unemployment registration (S10).

For a cash claim, AMS says an application can be made through **MeinAMS** or personally at the responsible office; applying also records the unemployment registration. A separate `Meldung zur Stellensuche ohne Antrag auf Geld vom AMS` exists (S07). AMS may require an in-person appointment; S10 describes a personal consultation for people never previously registered or returning after more than two years. Do not imply every first online submission has the same mandatory procedure.

`Arbeitssuchend` describes seeking work and need not imply the person has no job. `Arbeitslos` and entitlement to `Arbeitslosengeld` depend on the applicable legal conditions and AMS determination. Public `alle jobs` / eJob-Room browsing requires neither status. AMS can provide advice, matching, referrals and, where appropriate, training or qualifications support, with cooperation and appointment obligations varying by status and benefit (S01–S03, S07–S10).

AMS decides and pays `Arbeitslosengeld` after application and eligibility review (S07–S08). The general first-claim insurance condition is 52 weeks in the previous 24 months, with different conditions for repeat claims and certain first claims under age 25. Benefit claimants must also satisfy unemployment, availability, willingness and ability conditions; the usual 20-hour rule has a qualified 16-hour child-care exception (S08). These are not conditions for browsing vacancies. TP alone does not establish entitlement. Ukrainian insurance-period credit is **Not established in reviewed official sources**.

## Ukraine-specific support and recognition

ÖIF’s current Ukraine information describes **ÖIF German courses for displaced Ukrainians**, A1–C1, across Austria; its counselling asks for the displaced-person card and `Meldezettel`. It also offers online German learning and describes job fairs, mentoring, career and recognition-related support (S12–S13). Course places, childcare, schedule, programme suitability and funding must be checked with ÖIF; no individual place is guaranteed. AMS’s Ukraine information separately describes registration, vacancy proposals and help with German or qualifications where needed (S03). A current distinct nationwide AMS programme name with a verified eligibility and funding period was not established; this does not negate access to general AMS support.

The official recognition portal distinguishes **regulated** professional authorization from evaluation of foreign qualifications (S14). Recognition or a licence may be required for a regulated occupation. It is not a universal prerequisite for every job; an employer can assess qualifications for a non-regulated position. This is a high-level boundary, not a substitute for identifying the competent profession-specific authority.

## Critical questions — direct answers

1. **Browse AMS vacancies without registering as unemployed?** Yes: `alle jobs` and eJob-Room public search (S01–S02).
2. **Current AMS products?** `alle jobs`, its app, and eJob-Room; accounts unlock extra eJob-Room features (S01–S02, S05).
3. **EURES without Austrian AMS registration?** Public EURES services/search are available independently; non-EU national work rules still apply (S11).
4. **TP cardholder register with AMS?** Yes, as a jobseeker; the card is requested for the first visit (S03–S05).
5. **Registration equals benefit entitlement?** No; AMS separately assesses an application and insurance conditions (S07–S08).
6. **Services?** Counselling, vacancy proposals/matching and appropriate training/qualification support (S03, S09).
7. **Guaranteed programme or language place?** No; assess eligibility, capacity and funding (S03, S09, S12).
8. **Already-employed TP holder?** Public search is available; job-search-without-benefit registration exists. Individual counselling/programme entitlement depends on AMS assessment (S01–S02, S07, S10).
9. **Who decides/pays unemployment benefit?** AMS (S07–S08).
10. **Insurance condition?** Generally 52 insured weeks within 24 months for a first claim, with specified repeat/under-25 exceptions and other conditions (S08).
11. **TP alone qualifies for cash?** No; individual insurance and other conditions apply (S07–S08).
12. **Universal qualification recognition?** No (S14).
13. **Regulated professions differ?** Yes; formal recognition/authorization may be necessary (S14).
14. **Current Ukraine-specific nationwide support?** ÖIF publishes Austria-wide German-course provision for displaced Ukrainians, subject to availability and assessment; AMS also publishes Ukraine-specific registration/support guidance (S03, S12).
15. **AMS registration grants work authorization?** No; the cardholder’s work access is based on the applicable status and AuslBG exemption (S06, S16).

## Drafting boundaries for Phase 22 content

- Current vacancy products and listings can change; a listing is neither exhaustive coverage nor a hiring or employer-response guarantee.
- Vacancy browsing is not AMS registration; AMS registration is not immigration status or work authorization; work authorization is not unemployment status.
- AMS jobseeker services and unemployment cash benefit are separate; TP alone does not create cash entitlement.
- Training, language courses and programme access depend on eligibility, assessment, funding and places. Do not promise a course, subsidy or job.
- A jobseeker may be employed; do not call all jobseekers unemployed. A benefit claimant has distinct insured-work, work-availability and cooperation conditions.
- Recognition is occupation-specific: regulated professions may require authorization; do not impose formal recognition as a condition for every vacancy.
- Preserve the Phase 22.3 boundary: cardholding displaced persons need no ordinary AMS employment permit, and no general post-TP pending-work bridge was established. Disregard stale AMS references to an older permit requirement or 2027 end date for current status.
