# Austria — Social Assistance and Family Benefits

**Research date / `LastCheckedAt`:** 2026-11-07  
**Exact scope:** `social-subsistence`, `social-application`, `social-child-benefits`  
**Research basis:** official Austrian legislation and government/administrative guidance. This is not an individual benefit decision.

## Research verdict

**READY FOR CONTENT DRAFTING WITH QUALIFICATIONS.** Austria has a needs-based `Grundversorgung` system administered through the Länder, while ordinary `Sozialhilfe` is governed by different federal principles and provincial implementing laws. Temporary protection alone does not create automatic subsistence support. The federal Social Assistance Principles Act generally limits ordinary benefits to citizens, recognized refugees, and other permanently settled foreign nationals with five years’ lawful actual residence, subject to narrow mandatory EU/international-law exceptions assessed individually. Ukrainian displaced people should therefore be directed first to the applicable Land’s Grundversorgung office, not promised ordinary Sozialhilfe.

As of 2026-11-07, a significant recent rule applies to family benefits: from 2026-07-01 Ukrainian displaced persons may receive `Familienbeihilfe` if ordinary child/family conditions are met and they do not receive Grundversorgung. The severe-disability exception is limited to the statutory conditions. The earlier employment/AMS-registration condition applied only during 2025-11-01–2026-06-30 and no longer applies. `Kinderbetreuungsgeld` is a separate, application-based benefit: TP status is now an eligible residence category through the temporary-protection period, but the ordinary requirements still apply, including actual receipt of Familienbeihilfe and a qualifying shared household. It is not automatic, universal, or a generic cash allowance. Insurance-based `Wochengeld` and unemployment insurance remain separate.

## Social-assistance and Grundversorgung boundaries

Three routes must remain distinct:

1. **Grundversorgung:** a needs-based framework for eligible vulnerable people, including potentially displaced persons. The Art. 15a agreement allows suitable accommodation, food, medical insurance, advice and related components, including partial support where sufficient. The relevant Land assesses need and administers or delegates the case. It is not automatic from TP status (S02–S04).
2. **Ordinary Sozialhilfe / Mindestsicherung:** a separate Land-administered system under SH-GG. Its general national status rule is not satisfied merely by holding a temporary-protection card; recognized refugee status and some other qualifying categories are treated differently. The law preserves case-specific mandatory EU/international equality findings and leaves Länder implementation details. Do not promise ordinary Sozialhilfe or an ordinary rent component to a TP holder without current Land-specific legal assessment (S01).
3. **Other benefits:** insurance-based unemployment and maternity benefits, child/family benefits, disability/care benefits and local emergency schemes each have separate eligibility conditions. TP status may satisfy a residence/status element in certain family laws, but does not replace the remaining criteria (S05–S14).

The Art. 9 Grundversorgung figures current in the reviewed agreement are **cost maxima**: organized accommodation plus food €25/person/day; private accommodation rent €165/month for one person or €330/month total for families of two or more; private food €260/month per adult, €145/month per minor and €260/month per unaccompanied minor. The text makes these inclusive of taxes/charges. They are not an Austria-wide beneficiary entitlement or guaranteed payment, and the amounts paid in an individual Land/case cannot be inferred from them (S02). Federal/Land cost allocation and reimbursement are government-to-government arrangements and not cash owed directly to a recipient (S02).

For application, BBU’s current Ukraine guidance says to complete police registration, register the place of residence as required, and then apply to the Grundversorgung office in the relevant Bundesland if in need. The channel and supporting evidence vary; use current Land instructions. A recipient starting work or receiving income must tell the responsible Grundversorgung office/provider. Because the system is subsidiary, income is assessed and may reduce or end support. Exact disregard/deduction rules are not national and must not be generalized from Vienna’s 65/35 example (S03–S04).

## Family-benefit and insurance boundaries

### Familienbeihilfe and Kinderabsetzbetrag

BGBl I 84/2026 changed the rule. From 2026-07-01, displaced Ukrainians may claim Familienbeihilfe retroactively if they meet general family-benefit requirements and are not receiving Grundversorgung. The statute contains a significant-disability exception; BKA describes it for parents who care for a significantly disabled child in the household or predominantly maintain that child. Confirm unusual household or partial-benefit circumstances with Finanzamt Österreich rather than assuming the exception applies. Employment and AMS-registration ceased to be an additional TP requirement from 2026-07-01; that requirement applied only during 2025-11-01–2026-06-30. The current family-benefit TP rule is tied to the temporary-protection period, currently stated by BKA as through 2028-03-04 (S05–S06).

Familienbeihilfe is generally application-based. Ordinary conditions still apply, including the statutory child relationship/dependency and age/education conditions as relevant. TP status is not a blanket family allowance award. Finanzamt Österreich decides the claim. Kinderabsetzbetrag is paid with Familienbeihilfe without a separate application; the 2026 value is €70.90 per child/month (S05–S06, S09–S10).

### Kinderbetreuungsgeld

From 2026-07-01 TP status again appears as an eligible residence category in KBGG through the end of the temporary-protection entitlement. The general conditions remain: Familienbeihilfe must actually be received for the child; applicant and child must have the required Austrian center of life; a shared household generally requires at least 91 continuous days at one address and matching main-residence registrations; parent-child examinations and applicable income limits/options also matter. It requires an application and is not automatic. Because family allowance is ordinarily unavailable during GVS receipt (subject to the statutory significant-disability exception), the KBG prerequisite will normally also prevent an award while the family is receiving GVS; do not say this without checking any exception and all KBG conditions (S05–S08, S14).

### Wochengeld and unemployment insurance

`Wochengeld` is a maternity cash benefit through statutory health insurance. Official ÖGK guidance describes insured employees, some marginally employed persons with qualifying self-insurance, recipients of unemployment benefits and certain follow-on cases; amount depends on the applicable basis. TP alone does not create health-insurance or Wochengeld entitlement (S11; see the separate Austria Healthcare research package for current insurance routes).

`Arbeitslosengeld` is contributory unemployment insurance. In the usual first-claim case the AMS requires 52 insured weeks in the last 24 months; a first-time claimant under 25 generally needs 26 weeks in the previous 12 months. Further routes (including repeat claims) differ. TP may allow access to work, but it does not substitute for the contribution record or other AMS conditions (S12; see the separate Austria Employment Rights/Finding a Job research packages for labour-market access).

## Direct answers to critical questions

1. **Does TP alone automatically grant monthly subsistence?** No. No nationwide automatic TP subsistence payment was established. Grundversorgung requires application/need assessment; other benefits have separate rules (S01–S04).
2. **Can TP holders apply for Grundversorgung?** Yes, where they are in need and otherwise eligible; the Land decides the case (S02–S04).
3. **Is Grundversorgung automatic?** No. Police registration and an application/individual assessment are part of the current Ukraine route (S04).
4. **Does a TP holder automatically qualify for ordinary Sozialhilfe/Mindestsicherung?** No. SH-GG generally requires citizen/refugee or qualifying permanent settlement (five years’ lawful actual residence); TP alone is not that category. Individual binding EU/international exceptions and Land implementation require case-specific review (S01).
5. **What does “need” mean for Grundversorgung?** Broadly, inability to meet basic needs sufficiently from own means or other support; exact evidence and calculation are handled by the Land. No uniform national income threshold was established (S02–S04).
6. **Can employment/income affect Grundversorgung?** Yes. Report it. It is counted in the subsidiary needs assessment and may reduce or end support; exact deductions vary and Vienna’s displayed rule is not national (S04).
7. **Who receives a Grundversorgung application?** The Grundversorgung office of the relevant Bundesland, or its designated administrator/provider. BBU can direct applicants but is not the sole Land decision maker (S03–S04).
8. **Which documents are confirmed?** BBU describes police registration and available identity/status/civil-status evidence. A complete universal checklist is **Not established in reviewed official sources**; confirm the Land’s current requirements (S04).
9. **Is there an application deadline common to all Länder?** **Not established in reviewed official sources.** Apply promptly and ask the Land about its current process (S04).
10. **Is there a nationwide fixed Grundversorgung cash rate?** No individual rate was established. Art. 9 has system cost maxima, which are not personal cash entitlements; Länder decide individual support (S02–S04).
11. **What are the current official Art. 9 maxima?** Organized room/food €25/person/day; private rent €165/month single or €330/month family (2+ total); private food €260/adult/month, €145/minor/month, €260/unaccompanied minor/month. They are inclusive cost ceilings, not guaranteed recipient payments (S02).
12. **Does private Grundversorgung include housing/subsistence support?** It can include rent and food support after eligibility/need assessment. Do not guarantee maximum rates or cash to the beneficiary (S02–S04).
13. **Can a TP holder get a one-off national Ukraine emergency grant?** **Not established in reviewed official sources** as of the check date. Do not relabel family benefits or GVS ceilings as such (S01–S06).
14. **Can families with TP receive Familienbeihilfe in 2026?** From 2026-07-01, yes if general conditions are met and they do not receive Grundversorgung; a significant-disability statutory exception applies. TP alone is insufficient (S05–S06).
15. **Is employment or AMS registration still required for Ukrainian TP family allowance?** No, not from 2026-07-01. The additional condition covered 2025-11-01 through 2026-06-30 only (S05–S06).
16. **Who handles Familienbeihilfe?** Finanzamt Österreich; generally submit an application and provide evidence requested (S06, S09).
17. **Is Kinderabsetzbetrag a separate TP payment?** No. It is paid alongside Familienbeihilfe without a separate application; 2026 value €70.90 per child/month (S09–S10).
18. **Can a TP holder receive Kinderbetreuungsgeld?** TP is an eligible status from 2026-07-01 through the TP period, but actual Familienbeihilfe receipt and all ordinary KBG conditions are required; it is application-based (S07–S08).
19. **Is Wochengeld available based only on TP or pregnancy?** No such entitlement is established. It depends on an eligible insurance basis/category; check the insurer (S11).
20. **Is Arbeitslosengeld available based only on TP?** No. It requires availability and the applicable unemployment-insurance record, usually 52 insured weeks within 24 months for a first claim, or 26 weeks/12 months for a first claim under age 25 (S12).

## Evidence boundaries

- This is a national boundary review, not a full audit of nine Land Sozialhilfe enactments, local emergency funds, municipal aid or provincial family services. Confirm the person’s province before making a local benefit statement.
- Art. 9 Grundversorgung values are framework cost maxima including taxes/charges. No reviewed federal source proves a recipient’s individual amount, payment recipient or the exact Land’s current income disregard.
- No comprehensive nationwide Ukraine-specific one-off emergency payment was identified; this is a finding about reviewed current official federal material, not proof that no municipal or narrowly targeted support exists.
- The SH-GG status boundary is strong for general national drafting, but mandatory EU/international-law equal treatment is preserved in the statute and can require individual authority/court assessment. Do not state that every possible claim is categorically barred.
- The severe-disability family-benefit exception is statutory and fact-specific. Do not expand it to all disability or care cases; confirm with Finanzamt whether the child meets the defined status and whether household/maintenance requirements are met.
- The current BKA pages are administrative summaries and may lag a legal amendment. BGBl I 84/2026 and the current RIS consolidated statutes control.
- Historical 2022–2025 inclusion rules and the 2025–June 2026 employment/AMS condition must not be presented as current. From 2026-07-01, no employment/AMS prerequisite applies to displaced persons under the new family-benefit rule, but Grundversorgung receipt is a current exclusion subject to the stated exception.
- Kinderbetreuungsgeld’s TP status category does not waive its actual Familienbeihilfe, shared-household, application, parent-child-pass or model-specific income conditions.
- Wochengeld and unemployment benefits are insurance-based routes, not TP-only social assistance. Basic-care-linked health insurance is a separate eligibility component and does not automatically establish a cash maternity or unemployment benefit.

## Drafting boundaries for Phase 22 content

- State that TP does not itself create an automatic general subsistence payment.
- Describe Grundversorgung as needs-based, requiring application and individual Land assessment; direct users to the relevant Bundesland’s office.
- Do not present BBU as the sole decision maker for all Länder or use a Vienna rate/deduction rule Austria-wide.
- Clearly distinguish Grundversorgung, ordinary Sozialhilfe/Mindestsicherung, Familienbeihilfe, Kinderbetreuungsgeld, Kinderabsetzbetrag, Wochengeld and Arbeitslosengeld.
- Never present federal reimbursement/cost ceilings as cash owed to each beneficiary. Label the Art. 9 figures as cost maxima and identify their unit and lodging model.
- Do not promise an ordinary Sozialhilfe award to a TP holder; SH-GG’s general five-year/permanent-settlement boundary applies, subject to individual binding-law exceptions and provincial implementation.
- For Familienbeihilfe from 2026-07-01, state: general conditions plus no Grundversorgung; mention only the statutory significant-disability exception. Employment/AMS registration is no longer an additional requirement.
- Treat Kinderbetreuungsgeld as application-based; require actual Familienbeihilfe and the ordinary statutory conditions. Do not promise it to every TP family or conflate it with Kinderabsetzbetrag.
- Explain that Wochengeld and Arbeitslosengeld depend on the applicable insurance basis and contributions; TP alone is insufficient.
- State that income/employment must be reported to the Grundversorgung administrator and may reduce/end support. Do not invent a national disregard, cutoff or deadline.
- Do not claim a nationwide Ukraine-specific emergency one-off cash payment; local and targeted programmes require current local research.
- Mark the 2026 rules as date-sensitive and refresh the official BKA/RIS pages before any later release.
