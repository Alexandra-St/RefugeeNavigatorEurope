## Research verdict

**NOT READY — MATERIAL EVIDENCE GAP**

Ireland has a specific, open in-country transition scheme (TPTS), and official sources establish several ordinary permission routes, their broad conditions, and selected change-of-stamp procedures. The public sources reviewed do not establish whether a Temporary Protection beneficiary may file most ordinary applications from inside Ireland, what happens to Temporary Protection while those ordinary applications are pending, or whether filing provides bridging permission/work rights. Even the TPTS public notice sends applicants to a portal for detailed policy; the publicly accessible notice does not state a closing date, fee, full document list, pending-case protections, or all renewal conditions. Those are material to an article advising a current TP holder how to remain lawfully in Ireland.

The prior Phase 25.1 research is carried forward as context only: EU TP continues to 4 March 2028, but Irish validity/renewal of existing TP documentation after 4 March 2027 remains unresolved. That unresolved document issue is not re-researched here; it makes any generic “apply before TP expires” timetable unsafe without individualized current permission advice.

## Required special findings

### TPTS

- **Open?** Yes. ISD says applications opened 30 September 2026 and provides an online portal [IE-RES-S03].
- **Who qualifies?** At the published level: TP beneficiary with at least one year residence in Ireland before 30 September 2026; six months employment/self-employment immediately before opening; average gross income of €29,432 (annualised if a full year is unavailable); continued employment at application and decision; independent accommodation at application [IE-RES-S04]–[IE-RES-S06]. It is not a route for every TP holder. Full policy criteria/exclusions and documentary proof are not established in the public pages reviewed.
- **What does it grant/how long?** Stamp 4 permission for up to two years, renewable in further two-year periods [IE-RES-S04], [IE-RES-S05]. Renewal criteria beyond the public summary are not established.
- **What happens to TP?** TPTS is an alternative permission, not a TP renewal. Government says TPTS Stamp 4 and TP cannot be held at the same time [IE-RES-S04], [IE-RES-S05]. Exact switch/cancellation timing, pending-applicant protection, refusal effect, and return to TP are not established.
- **In-country?** Yes, the current TP beneficiary applies from Ireland via the official online portal [IE-RES-S03]–[IE-RES-S05].
- **Deadline, fee, documents, asylum application exclusion?** No public authoritative closing date, fee, full checklist, or explicit TPTS rule for a pending international-protection application was established. The portal notice directs applicants to detailed policy in the portal; do not substitute third-party summaries.
- **Reckonable time?** TPTS time counts toward naturalisation, TP time does not [IE-RES-S05]. Whether TPTS counts toward the separate employment-permit-based Long Term Residency scheme is **NOT ESTABLISHED** [IE-RES-S17].

### Employment transition

Employment-permit holders and immigration permission holders are distinct. A TP beneficiary has a statutory work entitlement while TP permission is valid [IE-RES-S01], [IE-RES-S02]; this does not turn TP into an Employment Permit or prove that an employment-permit-based residence route is met. DETE says in-country employment permit applicants must hold one of Stamp 1, 1A, 2, 2A, or 3 [IE-RES-S08], and the ordinary ISD change page does not list TP as a source category [IE-RES-S07]. Therefore, **whether TP → employment-permit-backed Stamp 1 can be filed in-country is NOT ESTABLISHED**. If a person independently obtains an employment permit, a separate residence permission/registration is still required. Current broad thresholds and permit-to-Stamp-4 timing are in the route matrix [IE-RES-S09], [IE-RES-S10], [IE-RES-S18].

### Study transition

Study is a separate permission, normally subject to the approved programme/course and student conditions. Current ISD change routes include some Stamp-to-Stamp applications, but do not specify TP-to-Stamp-2. It is **NOT ESTABLISHED** whether a TP holder may switch in-country or must apply from outside. Do not claim that enrollment alone changes status or that TP holders categorically must depart [IE-RES-S07], [IE-RES-S11], [IE-RES-S12].

### Family transition

The June 2026 Non-EEA Family Reunification Policy excludes TP sponsors under its §60 wording; the 2026 Act contains the new §237 framework, and the policy does not reconcile that reference. The exact policy effect for §237 beneficiaries is **NOT ESTABLISHED**. TP family reunification under the Directive is separate. A TP holder with an Irish/EEA/UK/non-EEA family relationship may have a route under that relationship's own rules, but the current TP-to-family-permission in-country filing path is **NOT ESTABLISHED**. TPTS may include the family unit already resident in Ireland under TP; that does not create sponsorship rights for family abroad [IE-RES-S02], [IE-RES-S04], [IE-RES-S13], [IE-RES-S14].

### Long-term residence / Stamp 4 / citizenship

- **Long Term Residency:** ISD describes a 60-month employment-permit-based scheme requiring eligible Stamp 1/4 history and current employment. It is not a generic five-year lawful-residence award. TP time is not identified as qualifying; TPTS time is not expressly addressed. Both are **NOT ESTABLISHED** for this scheme [IE-RES-S17].
- **Employment-based Stamp 4 upgrade:** 21 months under specified CSEP/hosting agreement routes or 57 months under GEP/ICT, with registration on the corresponding Stamp 1/1H basis. TP time does not itself satisfy the listed qualifying period [IE-RES-S18].
- **Naturalisation:** the Minister states TP time is non-reckonable and TPTS time counts toward naturalisation. This is citizenship reckonability, not Long Term Residency eligibility [IE-RES-S05]. Standard qualifying stamp categories are separately described by ISD [IE-RES-S11], [IE-RES-S19].

### International protection

ISD permits a TP holder to seek international protection, but says both cannot be availed of at once; §237 restricts a TP grant while an international-protection application is under consideration. This remains a separate protection procedure, not an ordinary residence transition. The reviewed public sources do not establish the precise sequence for withdrawing TP, commencing an asylum claim, or restoring TP after withdrawal/refusal [IE-RES-S01], [IE-RES-S02].

## Cross-route conflicts and limits

1. **Generic change rules versus TP-specific eligibility.** ISD says valid permission holders may sometimes change status and submitting a change application does not extend permission [IE-RES-S07]. Its examples/tables list specific stamps and do not identify Temporary Protection. DETE permits in-country Employment Permit applications only for specified stamp categories [IE-RES-S08]. There is no direct conflict to resolve; the gap is TP-specific eligibility and filing authority. Do not infer an in-country right from generic valid residence.
2. **Work right versus permit route.** TP grants a direct right to work while valid [IE-RES-S02], while DETE says employment permits and immigration permissions are distinct and an employment permit is not residence permission [IE-RES-S08], [IE-RES-S20]. Thus a job or TP work entitlement cannot be described as automatic conversion to ordinary Stamp 1.
3. **Family policy, new statute, and TP family-unity rules.** The June 2026 ordinary non-EEA family policy excludes TP beneficiaries under §60 wording [IE-RES-S13]; the 2026 Act provides a §237 route and transitional references [IE-RES-S02]. The public policy text does not reconcile those references, so the result for §237 beneficiaries is unresolved. TP family reunification under the Directive is separate. TPTS includes the family unit resident in Ireland under TP [IE-RES-S04]. Do not combine these into a general family-sponsorship right.
4. **TPTS details are portal-led.** The public launch notice confirms open date and online filing but routes applicants to portal-only detailed guidance [IE-RES-S03]. Conditions can be summarized from the Minister's October answers [IE-RES-S05], [IE-RES-S06], but public sources reviewed do not settle deadline, fee, full documents, pending asylum cases, renewal details, or protection while pending.
5. **Permission/stamp/IRP distinctions.** A Stamp identifies permission conditions; an IRP records/proves registered permission; neither a job offer nor an Employment Permit is by itself residence permission [IE-RES-S07], [IE-RES-S08], [IE-RES-S11], [IE-RES-S20].
6. **EU TP end versus individual Irish permission expiry.** Phase 25.1 found EU TP through 4 March 2028 but Irish document instructions still referring to 4 March 2027. That remains unresolved in the carried-forward research; no route-specific application should be timed against an assumed Irish document extension [IE-RES-S01] plus Phase 25.1 source findings.

## Readiness per aspect

- **`residence-routes`: Ready with qualification.** TPTS, employment permits/Stamp 4, study, family routes, STEP, international protection, and employment-based Long Term Residency exist as distinct pathways; IIP is closed. Eligibility from TP is affirmatively established only for TPTS. For most ordinary routes, TP-holder eligibility/in-country access remains unresolved.
- **`residence-conditions`: Ready with qualification.** TPTS's published core thresholds and route-level employment, study, family, STEP and long-term residence conditions are documented with official sources. Full TPTS policy/checklist and some route-specific eligibility details are not publicly established.
- **`residence-filing`: Not ready.** TPTS can be filed in-country. Generic ISD change rules and DETE in-country permit rules are documented, but neither answers the major question whether a TP holder may file the ordinary employment, study, or family route from inside Ireland. Pending stay/work rights and bridge arrangements are also not established. Do not draft a practical route-selection article until this gap is resolved.
