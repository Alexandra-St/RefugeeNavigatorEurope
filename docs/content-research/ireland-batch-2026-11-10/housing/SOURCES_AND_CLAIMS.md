# Ireland — Housing: initial accommodation, private rental and financial support

Research checked: 2026-11-10
Baseline: `b2cbf38f1a4e4230fbaffbe864fdd0d72f978d3c`
Scope: `housing-initial`, `housing-private-rental`, `housing-financial-support`.

## Research verdict

Current official guidance describes up to 30 days in a designated centre for new arrivals seeking accommodation, alongside a separate planned withdrawal of commercial accommodation and a needs-based continued-accommodation route. The current public UCTAT page still describes the application process as forthcoming; its opening and detailed procedure cannot be verified from the reviewed sources. The Department of Housing explicitly describes people on Temporary Protection as excluded from social housing under existing policy, which also matters for HAP. Rent Supplement has a separate flexible TP route. RTB's 2026 national rent-control rules require accommodation type, tenancy date and exceptions to be retained. The older 90-day new-arrival model is superseded.

## Required distinctions

- TP permission is not an automatic indefinite State-accommodation guarantee or a private tenancy agreement; scheme eligibility and available placement matter.
- Initial 30-day accommodation differs from the continued-accommodation process; commercial State accommodation differs from Rapid Build and from private tenancies.
- ARP is a contribution to qualifying hosts, not rent paid to the TP beneficiary. Private tenancy does not itself establish HAP eligibility.
- HAP and Rent Supplement are distinct schemes and social-housing approval does not mean immediate allocation.
- RTB registration is a tenancy obligation, separate from immigration registration; PPSN is an identifier, not a housing entitlement.
- EU TP duration does not extend a particular housing programme or individual placement automatically.
- Apply the appropriate pre- or post-1 March 2026 tenancy rules, accommodation type and exceptions; national scheme rules do not replace local-authority decisions and limits.

## Unresolved / conflicting points

- The continued-accommodation page dated May 2026 says the application process would open in June and details would follow. No later official procedure, opening confirmation, deadline or full assessment criteria were found in this research. The current individual intake path and re-entry treatment outside the stated pre-March 2024 commercial cohort likewise remain unclear.
- ARP pages differ on the proposed effective period of the reduction and retain older €600 wording. The May announcement says October, while the FAQ says September with payment in October. Actual implementation and amount need direct confirmation; avoid publishing a precise current figure.
- DHLGH's June 2026 announcement describes TP exclusion as existing policy and the new habitual-residence criteria as a proposed bill. The bill's enactment/commencement and any later changes were not established here; do not present a proposal as enacted law.
- The reviewed RTB guidance does not resolve a universal immigration-document condition for tenancy validity or every landlord evidence request. Accommodation-specific contribution rules and local emergency homelessness access were not established comprehensively.
