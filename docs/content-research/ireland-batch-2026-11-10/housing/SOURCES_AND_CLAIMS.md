# Ireland — Housing: initial accommodation, private rental and financial support

Research checked: 2026-11-10
Baseline: `9f3e43174e818bc2cd598ddc1faba6b97ab2e9fe`
Scope: `housing-initial`, `housing-private-rental`, `housing-financial-support`.

## Research verdict

Current official guidance describes up to 30 days in a designated centre for new arrivals seeking accommodation, alongside a separate planned withdrawal of commercial accommodation and a needs-based continued-accommodation route. A 23 September 2026 ministerial answer confirms that a self-declaration intake for continued accommodation did open and close for the specific residents affected by Citywest's closure; it does not establish a general open UCTAT intake or full national procedure for other cohorts. The initial request channel and several continued-accommodation mechanics remain unverified. The Department of Housing explicitly describes people on Temporary Protection as excluded from social housing under existing policy, which also matters for HAP. Rent Supplement has a separate flexible TP route. RTB's 2026 national rent-control rules require accommodation type, tenancy date and exceptions to be retained. The older 90-day new-arrival model is superseded.

## Required distinctions

- TP permission is not an automatic indefinite State-accommodation guarantee or a private tenancy agreement; scheme eligibility and available placement matter.
- Initial 30-day accommodation differs from continued accommodation. The confirmed 30 June–17 July 2026 self-declaration intake was for residents affected by Citywest's closure; its dates/outcome do not establish a current general UCTAT route or apply automatically to other commercial residents, independent residents or Rapid Build residents.
- UCTAT's published accommodation email is a query contact, not proof of a formal initial application channel. Commercial State accommodation differs from Rapid Build and from private tenancies.
- Refusal of a suitable offer under the Refusals Policy differs from voluntary relinquishment for travel. The policy says no further offers under its scope after refusal; a resident who relinquishes for travel may apply for a new offer on return if capacity permits.
- ARP is a contribution to qualifying hosts, not rent paid to the TP beneficiary. Private tenancy does not itself establish HAP eligibility.
- HAP and Rent Supplement are distinct schemes and social-housing approval does not mean immediate allocation.
- RTB registration is a tenancy obligation, separate from immigration registration; PPSN is an identifier, not a housing entitlement.
- EU TP duration does not extend a particular housing programme or individual placement automatically.
- Apply the appropriate pre- or post-1 March 2026 tenancy rules, accommodation type and exceptions; national scheme rules do not replace local-authority decisions and limits.

## Unresolved / conflicting points

- The continued-accommodation page dated 26 May 2026 still says an application process will open in June and details will follow. A later ministerial answer confirms that a self-declaration intake did open 30 June and close 17 July 2026 for residents affected by the Citywest closure; approvals and refusal appeals were handled, but the answer does not publish a general/open national procedure or exhaustive assessment criteria. Treat the Citywest process as cohort-specific, not as evidence that applications remain open for other residents.
- The exact current initial-arrival request channel, checklist, appointment/arrival handoff and capacity-failure process remain unclear. The UCTAT accommodation email is a public query contact, not a published application route. No general first-time request route for TP holders already living independently was found.
- Re-entry depends on the nature of the previous exit. The current absence protocol permits an existing accommodation resident who relinquishes the place for travel to apply again on return if space is available; refusal of a suitable offer and unapproved absence have different consequences under the Refusals Policy. No universal permanent ban or universal right to re-entry is established.
- ARP pages differ on the proposed effective period of the reduction and retain older €600 wording. The May announcement says October, while the FAQ says September with payment in October. Actual implementation and amount need direct confirmation; avoid publishing a precise current figure.
- DHLGH's June 2026 announcement describes TP exclusion as existing policy and the new habitual-residence criteria as a proposed bill. The bill's enactment/commencement and any later changes were not established here; do not present a proposal as enacted law.
- The reviewed RTB guidance does not resolve a universal immigration-document condition for tenancy validity or every landlord evidence request. Accommodation-specific contribution rules and local emergency homelessness access were not established comprehensively.
