# Ireland — Employment Rights During Temporary Protection

Baseline: `5067d6d8c934a7c06dbb7f6fd3a313eeb0d666c7`

## Research verdict

**READY FOR CONTENT DRAFTING WITH QUALIFICATIONS**

Current Irish statute and official guidance establish the core work right for a person who has been granted valid Temporary Protection (TP), the general employment standards, and the hourly minimum wage effective on the research date. Carefully distinguish the legal permission from proof documents and from the ordinary employment-permit system. Employer-checking and record-retention rules specific to TP are not fully detailed in public guidance; describe only the established general duty and the official renewal evidence route. The 2027 adult minimum wage has been officially announced but no enacted 2027 order was found as of the research date.

## Claims matrix

### `work-permission`

| Material claim | Assessment | Evidence, safe wording and prohibited inference |
|---|---|---|
| A person with a valid Irish TP permission may take employed work. | Supported | International Protection Act 2026 §237(11)(a) grants the valid permission holder the right to seek and enter employment like an Irish citizen (S01). The official TP page also says eligible TP beneficiaries may seek employment (S05). Say “a person granted valid Irish Temporary Protection permission”; do not generalize to all Ukrainian nationals or everyone displaced from Ukraine. |
| TP authorizes full-time or part-time work and change of employer, without the job-specific restrictions of an Employment Permit. | Supported with qualification | §237(11)(a) gives the broad work right without hours/employer limits in the TP provision, and ISD says any employment may be sought/entered (S01, S05). Ordinary labour laws and any applicable profession-specific rules still apply. Do not transfer GEP/CSEP employer-change restrictions to a valid TP holder. |
| TP permits self-employment/business activity. | Supported | §237(11)(a) expressly includes engaging in any business, trade or profession; ISD Ukraine FAQ confirms employment or self-employment (S01, S05). Ordinary tax/business registration and regulated-profession requirements still apply (S25–S27). |
| A separate General or Critical Skills Employment Permit is required for a valid TP permission holder to exercise the TP work right. | Official guidance says no | The TP statute itself gives the work right (S01); the Employment Permits Act contains an exception for permission-based work without a permit (S03). DETE's older public list uses broad permission-based categories but does not specifically label TP (S04); avoid implying a separate permit is needed. |
| TP's Employment Permit exemption is the same thing as an employment permit or residence permission. | Official guidance says no | Employment Permits Act §8(1)(f) and TP permission rights interact, but remain distinct instruments (S01, S03, S04). TP is the residence permission that carries the work right; GEP/CSEP are separate instruments for qualifying permit employment (S29–S30). |
| General Employment Permit, Critical Skills Employment Permit and ordinary Stamp 1 are interchangeable with TP. | Official guidance says no | GEP/CSEP are job/employer/occupation-specific permits; DETE describes each as distinct from residence permission (S29–S30). Ordinary Stamp 1 is a residence permission category whose work conditions depend on the permit or explicit no-permit basis (S08). Permit holders changing employer must follow the applicable permit process; GEP/CSEP require reissue before starting the new job (S31). TP's valid permission is a separate statutory basis (S01). |
| All Ukrainians may work in Ireland without an Employment Permit. | Official guidance says no | The work right here attaches to a valid Irish TP permission (S01, S05), or another independently qualifying permission such as Stamp 4 (S08), not nationality alone. |
| A successful TPTS holder has the ordinary Stamp 4 right to work without an Employment Permit and may establish/operate a business, subject to the actual permission conditions. | Supported with qualification | TPTS is described as Stamp 4 (S09–S10); ISD says Stamp 4 permits employment without a separate permit and business operation (S08). TPTS-specific terms should be checked in the holder's permission/portal guidance; this is a distinct route and does not expand TP rights. |
| The TP work right begins merely on arrival or while an initial TP request is pending. | Official guidance says no | Statutory entitlement under §237(11) belongs to a holder of a valid TP permission (S01). DETE states a person can work once they receive the letter confirming TP grant (S06); ISD's FAQ treats the grant letter as proof (S05). No official basis was found for work before grant or merely on arrival. |
| The TP work right begins only after receiving PPSN or a physical IRP card. | Official guidance says no | The legal basis is a valid TP permission (S01/S02); the grant letter is official proof (S05/S06). PPSN supports public-service/tax administration (S14–S15), and IRP is registration evidence rather than the source of §237 work rights. Do not treat an expired card as automatically invalidating an otherwise extended permission; document validity and current extension rules must be checked separately. Phase 25.1's unresolved Irish-document position after 4 March 2027 remains outside this phase; do not infer Irish document validity through 2028 from the EU date alone. |
| A TP holder whose renewal is pending may continue using the ordinary renewal evidence route. | Supported with qualification | ISD specifically says an OTPR receipt email proves permission while the renewal application awaits decision, and a grant email can prove registration before the new card/certificate arrives (S07). This applies to the specified renewal situation, not a pending initial application, and assumes the underlying permission carried work rights. |
| TP work authorization automatically licenses a person to practise a regulated profession. | Official guidance says no | TP establishes immigration/work access; professional registration or qualification recognition can still be mandatory in regulated roles (S25–S26). NARIC comparison is not a professional licence. Not every job requires formal recognition. |
| Ordinary Stamp 1, Stamp 4 and TP are interchangeable. | Official guidance says no | Stamp 1 commonly depends on an employment permit or a specific Justice no-permit letter, with its conditions (S08); Stamp 4 has broader work/business conditions (S08); TP has its own statutory permission and right (S01–S02). |

| Material claim | Assessment | Evidence, safe wording and prohibited inference |

| On 2026-11-10, the adult National Minimum Wage is €14.15 per hour gross, effective 1 January 2026. | Supported | Current 2025 Order legally sets €14.15 hourly from 1 January 2026 (S23); WRC confirms age rates and hourly gross-reckonable-pay test (S22). It is not a weekly/monthly amount and not net take-home pay. |
| The 2026 rate is the same for all ages and statuses. | Official guidance says no | Under 18: €9.91/hour (70%); age 18: €11.32 (80%); age 19: €12.74 (90%); age 20+: €14.15 (100%) (S22–S24). Minimum wage applies to most employees regardless of full/part-time/temporary/seasonal status. |

| National Minimum Wage is always the final lowest rate for every sector. | Official guidance says no | WRC confirms legally binding sectoral minimum rates may be set by Employment Regulation Orders and Sectoral Employment Orders (S22). Check the applicable sector/order; do not catalogue or apply a sector rate without matching the job. |
| Board or lodging can never count in the minimum wage calculation. | Official guidance says no | From 1 January 2026, limited statutory amounts can count: board/meals €1.27 per hour worked; accommodation only €33.42 per week or €4.77 per day (S22–S23). Such credits are capped by statute and do not convert the hourly gross wage into a net rate. |
| Every sum shown on a payslip counts as minimum-wage pay. | Official guidance says no | WRC defines reckonable and excluded components. Expenses, overtime/public-holiday premiums, some tips/service charges and benefits in kind generally cannot be used as reckonable minimum-wage pay; test average gross reckonable pay against hours in a pay reference period no longer than a month (S22). |

State it only as an announced future rate, intended for 2027; use €14.15 for the research date. Do not present the future amount or future youth rates as already effective law. |

## Required distinctions

- Temporary Protection work right ≠ Employment Permit.
- Employment Permit ≠ immigration residence permission.
- PPSN ≠ work authorization.
- IRP card ≠ necessarily the legal source of the work right; a grant/permission and its valid evidence matter.
- Minimum wage ≠ net pay.
- National Minimum Wage ≠ every sector's final applicable rate.
- Work authorization ≠ regulated-profession licence.
- TP work right ≠ exemption from tax, PRSI or ordinary employment law.

## Unresolved / conflicting points

- DETE's public employment-permit webpage is older and does not name Temporary Protection explicitly in its list, while the current TP statute directly grants the work/business right and the 2024 Employment Permits Act includes a permission-based exception. State the combined statutory position with a precise TP scope; do not paraphrase the old DETE list as excluding TP.
- No universal TP-specific employer document-copy retention or separate notification procedure was established. ISD's 2025 employer notice confirms acceptable evidence during a TP renewal-pending period only.
- Phase 25.1 identified unresolved Irish TP document validity after 4 March 2027. This phase does not revisit or resolve that date issue; it states work rights only for a valid permission.
- No general legal requirement that contract terms be translated was found. Health-and-safety information/instruction/training have a separate understandable-language obligation.
- Budget 2027 announced €14.94 for adults aged 20+; no 2027 order was located in force at the research cutoff. Recheck the Irish Statute Book before publishing post-2026 rate claims.
