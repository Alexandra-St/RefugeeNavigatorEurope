**Research date:** 15 November 2026
**Scope:** final evidence check for four unresolved questions only: a new §237 application from inside Ireland; Irish permission/document validity after 4 March 2027; ordinary residence filing and pending rights by route; initial State accommodation and the no-place boundary.

This pass used the Ireland Core/Compare Gap Assessment 01, Targeted Core and Compare Research 01, and the three scoped Ireland research packages for Temporary Protection, Residence After Protection, and Housing. ## A. §237 first application from inside Ireland

### Official evidence

- The **International Protection Act 2026, §237**, makes the Minister the authority who gives the temporary-protection residence permission and keeps the beneficiary register. The Act commenced on **12 June 2026**. The commencement date is confirmed by S.I. No. 244/2026.
- Current ISD Ukraine guidance includes an eligibility update effective **5 August 2026**. It describes an arrival/border interaction with an immigration officer, available identity documents, and the additional departure-evidence rule for affected new applicants. It does not set out a complete new-§237 filing workflow for someone already inside Ireland.
- ISD's current **Required Documents** page says the ordinary first-registration checklist is not applicable to beneficiaries already holding a current/extended yellow Temporary Protection Certificate because the Ukraine Response Team manages that registration upon arrival. Its TP document list is for renewal of existing certificate holders; it is not a new-§237 application checklist.
- A gov.ie **Temporary Protection** page, last updated **12 April 2025**, says an applicant may apply by attending Citywest Convention Centre, Cork City PSC, or Limerick Intreo Centre, and says no appointment is needed. This is an official, historically published in-country route, but it predates §237 and has no post-12 June 2026 confirmation. Its current operational status cannot be established from the page.
- For a specific Ukrainian TP query, an official 2026 IPO help page directs the person to **UkraineSupportMailbox@justice.ie**. This confirms a contact route for instructions; it does not say that an email itself files or grants a §237 application.

### Established rule

The statutory decision/permission authority is the Minister, with ISD administering the immigration guidance. A border/on-arrival route is confirmed in current ISD guidance. The older named-office route was officially published, but its operation for a new §237 applicant after the June 2026 law change is not confirmed.

### What is not published

Current official guidance reviewed does not confirm whether a person already in Ireland should use one of the offices listed on the April 2025 page, whether a new appointment or portal submission is required, whether the border route is mandatory, what complete documents are required, or whether the process differs for replacement/reissue versus a first §237 grant. The law establishes the permission and authority but does not provide those operational details.

### Safe drafting boundary

Say that the current published route is border/on-arrival contact with an immigration officer. For a person already in Ireland, say to obtain current individual instructions from ISD before attending; the official Ukraine-specific contact listed by government is UkraineSupportMailbox@justice.ie. If mentioning the Citywest/Cork/Limerick offices, label that information as coming from a page last updated 12 April 2025 and say its post-§237 status is unconfirmed. Do not present it as a current guaranteed route.

### Status

**OFFICIAL PROCEDURE NOT PUBLISHED — SAFE BOUNDARY ESTABLISHED** for a new §237 application by a person already in Ireland. A 2025 in-person route exists in government guidance, but its current validity after the statutory change is not confirmed.

## B. Post-4-March-2027 Irish permission/document validity

### Official evidence

- **EU scheme:** the corrected consolidated Council Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/1912 extends the EU Temporary Protection regime for the covered groups until **4 March 2028**; the extension applies from **5 March 2027**.
- **Irish §237 permission:** §237(9), effective from **12 June 2026**, says a permission is valid for at least one year and may be extended in accordance with Article 4 of the Directive. It does not give each holder an individual expiry date of 4 March 2028.
- **Irish legacy-document instruction:** ISD's current “Renewal to 2027” page describes a specified group whose yellow certificate was valid until 4 March 2026. ISD says their permission/certificate proof may be used through **4 March 2027**, no new yellow certificate is needed for that period, and their IRP card must be renewed. This page does not state a post-4 March-2027 rule.
- **Current ISD maintenance instruction:** the current Ukraine page says existing beneficiaries were not affected by the 5 August eligibility change, should keep applicable registration requirements current, and will be advised if further registration or document action is required. It does not announce that further action is unnecessary through 2028.
- The Minister stated in the Dáil on **30 September 2026** that TP remains in place through March 2028 for people who do not take the TPTS alternative. That statement concerns the protection scheme/rights; it does not set individual certificate, permission-letter, IRP, or card validity.
- A separate gov.ie Ukraine overview page updated **12 June 2026** still says “currently to March 2027.” That duration line was superseded by the later EU decision and is not used as a current end date.

### Established rule

The EU regime continues until **4 March 2028**. Irish §237 permissions have a statutory minimum duration of one year and may be extended. ISD has a specific current instruction through **4 March 2027** for the legacy certificate group described on its renewal page, including IRP renewal.

### What is not published

As of 15 November 2026, reviewed official Irish material does not establish a universal post-4-March-2027 validity date for an individual Irish permission, letter, certificate, IRP, or other physical proof; it does not announce automatic renewal to 2028, a replacement-document timetable, or a common holder action after that date. Section 289 of the 2026 Act is not relied upon for TP-document transition: its title concerns continued application/effect of the 2015 Act, and the reviewed material does not make it a TP validity rule.

### Safe drafting boundary

State that EU Temporary Protection runs through 4 March 2028, but this does not itself change an individual's Irish document expiry. For the specified legacy yellow-certificate group, quote ISD's 4 March 2027 and IRP-renewal instructions with the cohort limitation. For any later date or document, direct the holder to current ISD updates and their own IRP details. Do not promise an automatic extension or no-action period to 2028.

### Status

**OFFICIAL PROCEDURE NOT PUBLISHED — SAFE BOUNDARY ESTABLISHED** for individual Irish permission/document validity after 4 March 2027.

## D. Initial State accommodation procedure

### Current rule

DJHAM's current policy says people newly registering for TP from **10 November 2025** who need State accommodation are offered a maximum of **30 days** in a Designated Accommodation Centre, with food and services. The Designated Accommodation Centres page was last updated **4 June 2026** and names Punchestown and Wallslough for the 30-day model. These are published centres, not a guarantee of a place, a chosen location, or a specific bed on arrival.

The current accommodation refusals policy, last updated **22 January 2026**, provides for one suitable short-term offer for a BOTP requiring accommodation. Type, location and duration depend on availability; needs such as disability or medical requirements are considered where possible. Refusing a suitable offer can mean leaving temporary/emergency accommodation immediately and no further offer under that policy. That rule concerns refusal of an offer, not a situation where no placement is available.

### Request route

- For a new arrival, the published route is to follow current border/arrival instructions and tell the authorities that State accommodation is required. The reviewed current pages do not publish a separate online form, appointment, walk-in desk, or detailed referral sequence for requesting the initial TP placement.
- The government public-services contact page, last updated **2 March 2026**, lists the **Ukraine Crisis Temporary Accommodation Team (UCTAT)** and the email **ukrainetempaccom@equality.gov.ie** for accommodation questions. Treat this as a contact/query channel, not a published application form or promise of placement.
- Government homelessness guidance says people experiencing homelessness or at risk should contact the Dublin Region Homeless Executive in Dublin or the local authority outside Dublin for information about services. This is a separate homelessness-service channel; the source does not promise a bed or establish that every BOTP meets local service criteria.

### Availability

Official State policy makes the short-term offer subject to what accommodation is available. Capacity, a particular centre, an alternative placement, and a minimum length of stay are not guaranteed. The maximum 30-day period is not an entitlement to 30 days in a chosen location or a guarantee of long-term accommodation.

### If no place is available

No current TP-specific official procedure reviewed explains the interim placement, waiting list, transport, emergency alternative, or escalation route when no State bed is available at the time of request. The general homelessness contact route is a separate service to ask about options; it must not be described as automatic fallback housing for TP holders.

### What is not published

The reviewed official sources do not provide a complete current first-arrival intake form/appointment/referral sequence, a capacity-failure procedure, a universal local-authority role in TP placement, or a guarantee of a family/vulnerability exception. The closed Citywest or continued-accommodation processes for existing residents do not establish the current general initial route. No universal accommodation contribution rule is asserted in this report.

### Safe drafting boundary

State the published maximum-30-day model, the one-suitable-offer policy, and the availability limitation. Direct a newly arrived person seeking a place to follow border instructions and use UCTAT's published contact for accommodation questions. For someone already without shelter, give the separate Dublin Region Homeless Executive/local-authority contact as an information route only, with individual eligibility and availability left to the responsible service. Do not promise accommodation or a universal fallback.

### Status

**CONFIRMED WITH QUALIFICATION** for the initial 30-day model, one-offer policy, current UCTAT query contact and separate homelessness contact. **OFFICIAL PROCEDURE NOT PUBLISHED — SAFE BOUNDARY ESTABLISHED** for a no-place interim/fallback procedure.

**Do not claim:** that every TP holder can file ordinary routes in-country, must leave for all routes, automatically converts status, retains TP/work rights because an ordinary/TPTS application is pending, or can hold TP and a new registered permission concurrently after grant.
