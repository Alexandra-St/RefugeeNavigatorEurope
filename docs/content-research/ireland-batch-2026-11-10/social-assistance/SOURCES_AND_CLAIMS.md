# Ireland — Income support, applications and child benefits

Research checked: 2026-11-10

Baseline: `737ba88dcfccf96e569c14605458854a06c5cde8`

Scope: exactly `social-subsistence`, `social-application`, `social-child-benefits`.

## Research verdict

A distinct income-tested Beneficiary of Temporary Protection (BOTP) Weekly Payment is described for residents of designated accommodation centres; the latest reviewed 2026 government page repeats the €38.80 adult / €29.80 child net weekly figures. Designation and the actual scheme matter: not every State-supported accommodation type is shown to be a designated centre. Those leaving a centre can apply for ordinary payments subject to conditions; a change of address does not automatically award a new rate. Child Benefit and Working Family Payment remain possible in a designated centre, subject to their own requirements.

Habitual Residence Condition (HRC) is scheme-specific. DSP's January 2026 operational guidance says holders of the legacy §60 TP permission have the right-to-reside component and describes a separate factual assessment; it does not establish a blanket current exemption for every TP holder and scheme. A March 2022 ministerial statement described a special deeming approach for people fleeing Ukraine, but its present scope after later changes and application to new §237 permissions are not established by the current general guidance. Preserve this distinction rather than silently resolving it.

## Required distinctions

- TP provides a status and conditional access, not automatic entitlement to every welfare payment. PPSN identifies the claimant; application is not an award.
- HRC is scheme-specific: right to reside and factual habitual residence are different components; an exemption for one payment is not an exemption for all.
- Designated State accommodation differs from independent living for specified welfare rules. The physical label “State accommodation” does not alone prove designation.
- Basic SWA is separate from Jobseeker's Allowance; ANP addresses assessed additional needs and is not a universal weekly payment.
- Child Benefit, the BOTP child component and WFP are separate payments with separate conditions. A child or TP status alone does not automatically award them.
- Work permission differs from welfare eligibility; starting work can reduce or change some payments and need not terminate all support. Housing assistance is distinct from ordinary cash social welfare.

## Unresolved / conflicting points

- A 2022 ministerial statement said Ukraine TP beneficiaries would be deemed to satisfy HRC; DSP's January 2026 general operational guidance names the legacy §60 permission as establishing the right-to-reside limb and explains a separate factual assessment. Neither source reviewed clearly reconciles the present reach of that special practice across schemes or new §237 permission holders. Do not describe a universal waiver or automatic failure.
- DSP's 2025 TP page retains a 90-day and Citywest arrival process; the June 2026 designated-centre guidance describes the later 30-day cohort. Exact current BOTP Weekly Payment intake location and exhaustive documents are not established. Scheme-specific CWS/Intreo contact remains supported.
- DSP's WFP service page allows an application with a basic MyWelfare account, while the February 2026 operational guidance says verified MyGovID. Both provide a paper route; check the live service and current account requirements.
- Reviewed sources do not establish whether every Rapid Build or commercial site is designated, a universal welfare rate for each category, or automatic reassessment timing when a resident moves. Confirm the specific premises and claim with DSP.
