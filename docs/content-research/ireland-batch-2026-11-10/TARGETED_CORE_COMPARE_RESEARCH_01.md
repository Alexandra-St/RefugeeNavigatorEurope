### Established facts

- Under §237, the Minister is the statutory authority who gives the temporary-protection residence permission and maintains the beneficiary register. ISD publishes the current immigration guidance.
- Official Irish public guidance supports presenting at the border to an immigration officer for an initial protection request. The current ISD eligibility page also requires new applicants in the relevant cohort to provide acceptable evidence concerning authorization to leave Ukraine under Ukrainian law; it does not publish an exhaustive list of acceptable documents.
- The generic immigration-registration appointment portal and the yellow-certificate renewal checklist concern registration/renewal processes. They do not establish the initiation or decision procedure for a new §237 grant.
- A permission, PPSN application, IRP/card, and accommodation request are separate administrative steps. No reviewed current source says that a PPSN or accommodation request itself grants Temporary Protection.

### Safe Core assertion

People seeking Irish Temporary Protection should follow the current ISD instructions and, on arrival, present to an immigration officer as directed by Irish arrival guidance. For new applicants covered by the 5 August 2026 rule, ISD says acceptable evidence of authorization to leave Ukraine may be required. ISD’s public pages do not provide a complete current application checklist or explain every step after border contact.

For someone already in Ireland who has not yet been granted protection, the reviewed official sources do not clearly identify a current application channel, appointment requirement, or post-border handoff. Direct them to obtain current instructions from ISD; do not send them to the generic IRP registration portal or promise that a border interaction alone completes the process.

### Safe Compare assertion

Ireland’s published initial route is border-level contact with an immigration officer; current official material does not publish a complete new-applicant checklist or a confirmed post-border filing process for someone already in Ireland.

### Qualifications

- **Confirmed:** Ministerial statutory role under §237; border-level initial contact; new-grant departure-evidence requirement as phrased by ISD; ordinary IRP registration is distinct.
- **Conditional:** acceptable evidence is required for the affected post-5 August cohort; ISD does not enumerate all acceptable evidence.
- **Not established:** whether a separate appointment, online form, biometrics, fee, new-§237 proof card/letter, or additional in-person visit is required; exact grant timing; full process for a person already in Ireland.
- **Do not claim:** an exhaustive document list, that a PPSN or accommodation registration is the protection application, that a generic registration appointment is required, or that the old Citywest process is still the current general workflow.

### Established facts

- **EU scheme date:** Council Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/1912 extends the covered temporary protection until **4 March 2028**. The extension under Article 1 applies from **5 March 2027**. Article 2 has a separate condition effective **5 August 2026** and, as corrected, excepts people who enjoyed and continuously retain protection in the same Member State across the stated date.
- **Irish new-permission duration:** §237(9), in force from **12 June 2026**, says an individual permission is valid for at least one year and may be extended in line with Article 4. The statute does not set every person’s expiry to 4 March 2028.
- **Legacy Irish documents:** the ISD renewal page currently says the described group’s permission/certificate proof extends only to **4 March 2027**, with IRP renewal required; the page says no new yellow certificate is needed for the stated period. Its operational wording predates/resists the later EU scheme extension and is not a post-March-2027 instruction.
- **Transition-source correction:** the previous gap assessment described §289 as preserving the prior-law framework for existing TP permissions. The official Act contents identify §289 as “Continued application and effect of Act of 2015,” but the reviewed material does not substantiate using it to determine TP permission/document validity. This report excludes that inference.
- **Holder action:** ISD’s current general TP page says beneficiaries should keep registration requirements current and that the Department will advise them if further action or renewal is required. It does not say that no action is required through 2028, nor give a universal replacement/renewal date for certificates, §237 letters, or IRP cards after 4 March 2027.
- **Separate documents:** an individual IRP card has its own printed expiry/renewal details. The EU scheme date, Irish permission validity, and physical document expiry must not be equated.

### Safe Core assertion

The EU Temporary Protection scheme for the covered groups is extended through 4 March 2028. This does not by itself establish the expiry date printed on an individual Irish permission or document: §237 permissions are valid for at least one year and may be extended, while current ISD guidance for a specified legacy certificate group describes validity only through 4 March 2027 and says holders will be advised about any further document action.

### Safe Compare assertion

EU temporary protection runs through 4 March 2028, but Irish individual permission/document validity after 4 March 2027 is not yet set out in the reviewed public guidance; follow current ISD updates and the expiry printed on the person’s IRP.

### Qualifications

- **Confirmed:** EU scheme end date; Irish §237 minimum duration; current ISD’s 4 March 2027 instruction for its specified legacy group; IRP renewal requirement for that group; ISD’s promise to advise if further action is needed.
- **Conditional:** the 2027 certificate instruction is limited to the group described by ISD (yellow certificate valid until 4 March 2026); it must not be generalized to new §237 grants or every legacy document.
- **Not established:** individual Irish permission/certificate/letter validity through 4 March 2028; IRP dates after 4 March 2027; a universal automatic extension, renewal application, replacement document, or no-action rule after that date.
- **Do not claim:** that EU extension automatically changes every Irish document’s expiry, or that the Irish permission scheme itself ends on 4 March 2027.

### Established facts

- DJHAM publishes the State’s Ukraine accommodation policy. Its current published model says a new TP registrant who needs State accommodation is offered a maximum of **30 days** in a Designated Accommodation Centre, with food/services and integration support. This is a short initial placement, not a promise of long-term housing.
- The current refusals policy says an applicant requiring accommodation receives **one suitable offer**. Type, location, and duration depend on what is available; individual medical/disability needs are considered where possible, and the policy explicitly notes shortages. Refusing a suitable offer means no further offer under that policy and may require leaving temporary/emergency accommodation immediately.
- UCTAT is listed as a public contact for accommodation queries. The reviewed pages do not publish a complete current nationwide application form, appointment route, or capacity-failure/alternative-placement procedure.
- The Citywest continued-accommodation self-declaration intake opened 30 June and closed 17 July 2026 for the affected Citywest residents. Approved applications received offers; refusal appeals were cohort-specific. That process is closed and does not establish current intake for independent residents, Rapid Build residents, or all commercial-accommodation residents.
- Private rental, social housing, and homelessness services are separate from this short initial TP placement. The sources reviewed here do not establish that Temporary Protection itself creates a private tenancy, social-housing approval, or a universal homelessness placement.

### Safe Core assertion

If a newly arriving Temporary Protection beneficiary needs State accommodation, Ireland’s published policy provides for one suitable short-term offer, currently described as up to 30 days in a Designated Accommodation Centre. The type, location, and duration depend on availability; the published policy notes shortages, and refusal may end further State offers. UCTAT is a public accommodation-query contact, but official pages reviewed do not set out a complete current general intake or fallback procedure for someone already living independently.

### Safe Compare assertion

Ireland’s published initial offer is short-term (up to 30 days) and availability-dependent; it is not guaranteed long-term or chosen housing. A complete current general intake/fallback route for other cohorts is not published in the sources reviewed.

### Qualifications

- **Confirmed:** 30-day maximum for new TP arrivals seeking State accommodation from 10 November 2025; one suitable offer under the refusals policy; location/type/duration depend on availability; UCTAT contact; closed Citywest-specific continued-accommodation procedure.
- **Conditional:** accommodation is for new arrivals who seek State accommodation and need assistance; current capacity and suitable placement affect the actual offer; earlier cohorts have different published terms.
- **Not established:** universal entitlement or guaranteed bed, current application channel for a person already in Ireland/living independently, waiting period or capacity-failure procedure, comprehensive eligibility test, universal contribution rules, or a general current continued-accommodation intake.
- **Do not claim:** every TP holder receives housing; 30 days is an assured minimum or long-term stay; the Citywest form is still open or applies nationally; private rental, social housing, or homelessness service access is automatic because of TP.
