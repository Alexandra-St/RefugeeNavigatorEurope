# Belgium — Education under Temporary Protection: Sources and Claims

**Research checked:** 11 November 2026
**Repository baseline:** `96c0624514f84e659680e095db0558ee54fbd407`
**Aspects:** `education-school-enrolment`, `education-pupil-language`, `education-higher-admission`
**Overall verdict:** **READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS**

This is an evidence ledger, not article copy. School and higher-education rules are primarily Community matters. In Brussels, families choose between the Dutch-language Flemish system and the French-language Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles (FWB) system; these have different rules and entry routes. The German-speaking Community (DG) has its own schools and a small higher-education sector. Temporary protection (TP), an A card, school admission, higher-education diploma equivalence, tuition, and student aid are distinct questions.

## Findings at a glance

- Every child has access to school regardless of nationality or residence situation. A TP child does not need an A card as a condition for the basic right to enrol. Flemish government guidance specifically says children without official residence documents may enrol and must not be refused on that ground. Ask a school directly; municipal residence registration is not a universal school-admission prerequisite. Administrative identity data can still be requested for records, and rules for enrolment into a particular year/track remain. (S01–S05)
- Compulsory education in Belgium is generally from the school year beginning in the calendar year a child turns five until age 18, with the precise end date and exceptions governed federally. It is **compulsory education, not necessarily school attendance**: approved home education or other legally recognized routes may satisfy it. Foreign minors’ compulsory-duty commencement is tied in the general statutory rule to the 60th day after register entry; that timing does not extinguish their earlier right to attend school. (S01–S04, S06)
- Contact a nearby school or the appropriate Community information point, explain the child’s age, previous schooling and languages, and ask which class or newcomer route has capacity. Do not wait for an A card or Ukrainian transcripts before making an enquiry. A preferred school is not guaranteed if full; ask it for the refusal/waiting-list procedure and another placement lead. (S02–S05, S07–S09)
- Flanders changed primary newcomer provision from 1 September 2026: **taalheldklassen** replace newcomer reception from the second primary year; secondary OKAN remains an intake year for eligible recently arrived 12–17-year-olds with insufficient Dutch. French Community secondary newcomer support is **DASPA**, generally 12 months with possible extensions to 18 or 24 months in specified cases; the official FWB flyer gives a certificate-of-admissibility route for DASPA pupils lacking school records after at least six months. German-speaking schools use **Sprachlernklassen/Sprachlernkurse** for first-arrival pupils, with language and school placement decisions through the school and its support council. Availability is local. (S10–S13)
- There is no verified nationwide entitlement to Ukrainian-language instruction, Ukrainian-speaking mediators, or continued Ukrainian online school as a substitute for Belgian compulsory education. Belgium’s home-education and recognized-foreign-school rules apply; no general recognition of the Ukrainian remote-school route was found. (S06, S14–S16)
- Higher education is applied to directly through an institution in Flanders and generally through the chosen institution in FWB, with Community-specific diploma rules. A foreign diploma is not automatically recognized; prior credits are assessed by the receiving institution. Programme language standards and admission tests remain applicable. (S17–S27)
- **TP fees are Community-specific.** In Flanders, TP beneficiaries fall within a statutory protected category for purposes of the institution’s power to set freely increased tuition; exact programme/contract fees still need checking. In FWB, the 2026–27 ARES fee tool treats people able to benefit from TP as assimilated to EU-category students for the additional foreign-student contribution; the standard 2026–27 minerval is €1,194 before any approved income-based reduction or grant. This is not a waiver of the base minerval. No comparable DG-wide TP fee waiver was established. (S28–S33)
- A Flemish study grant is a separate, means-tested application; Ukraine TP is expressly named among qualifying nationality/status cases if the person holds a valid TP residence permit on 31 December of the academic year. FWB’s published 2026–27 grant-nationality route names recognized refugees/stateless people/subsidiary protection for one year, not TP as such; other routes may apply. DG has a means-tested 2026–27 study grant, but a TP-specific nationality rule was not found. (S34–S38)

| System | Practical route | First contact | Community boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flemish Community | Dutch-language school in Flanders; Dutch-language schools also operate in Brussels. | French Community / FWB | French-language school in Wallonia or Brussels. Contact the selected school; for a newcomer who needs DASPA, use the official list of DASPA schools and contact the school directly. | School of choice; FWB enrolment assistance service if the family cannot find a place. | Brussels families may choose Dutch- or French-language education; once chosen, the respective Community’s rules apply. (S03, S08–S09) |
| German-speaking Community | German-language school in the DG; enquire directly with the school director. School board/administration procedures apply for first-arrival pupils. | Local primary/secondary school; DG Ministry/education administration if a school needs newcomer assessment or equivalence. | The DG’s first-arrival and German-language support is separate from OKAN and DASPA. (S11–S13) |

**Age and obligation:** Belgium’s general rule is education from age five through 18. Full-time obligation generally lasts to age 15 if the first two years of full-time secondary education have been completed, otherwise to 16; from 15/16 a young person may satisfy the obligation through an approved part-time education/work route. A qualifying secondary diploma may end the obligation earlier. The exact end date in the year of turning 18 depends on the statutory timing; do not simplify it to “until the 18th birthday” for every child. Foreign-national compulsory-duty timing is subject to the statutory register-entry rule, while school access exists earlier. (S01, S04, S06)

**TP and registration cases:**

- **TP granted and A card issued:** approach the local school under ordinary Community admission rules. The A card is useful evidence of identity/status but is not the source of the child’s right to education. (S02–S05)
- **TP granted but A card pending / Annex 15 or federal attestation:** request school enrolment now. No Community-wide rule requiring the A card as a precondition was found; Flanders expressly says residence papers are not required for the right to enrol. Whether a particular administrative form accepts a specific interim document should be settled with the school, not inferred from healthcare rules. (S02, S04–S05)
- **TP application pending:** the child’s basic school-access right is based on being a child in Belgium, not on a completed TP decision. A pending immigration application does not guarantee a seat at a preferred school or a particular grade. (S02–S05)
- **Privately hosted / temporary accommodation / no permanent domicile:** contact a school serving the actual area of stay. The reviewed sources do not make permanent housing or completed domiciliation a universal admission condition. The local school registration system may still ask for an address/contact details. (S02–S05, S07–S09)
- **No identity or prior-school papers:** still contact the school and explain what is missing. Do not assume missing Ukrainian reports block initial attendance. Identity information may be needed to open/complete the pupil record; ask the school how to record the child while documents are being recovered. For later secondary placement/certification, Community-specific evidence or assessment can matter. (S02, S04–S05, S09, S12)

**Placement and capacity:** No Belgian-wide Ukrainian-to-Belgian grade-conversion chart was found. The school/education administration assesses age, prior schooling, language and the pupil’s present level; placement may be in a newcomer pathway or a mainstream class with support. In FWB, the school of choice must accept eligible DASPA enrolment while it has a place and must give a request/registration certificate if it is full. In Flanders, eligible newcomers have a right to register at a chosen school subject to admission and capacity, with a special temporary admission power when newcomer provision is full. A preferred school is not guaranteed. (S08–S10)

**Brussels:** parents can choose a Dutch-language school (Flemish Community) or French-language school (FWB); the chosen school system’s admission and newcomer rules then apply. Do not treat Brussels as a fourth education Community or assume a single regional school-allocation office. (S02–S03)

**Costs in compulsory/basic education:** public and publicly funded compulsory schooling is not charged as tuition in the same way as higher education, but “free school” does not mean every family expense is zero. Meals, transport, materials outside free-supply rules, trips and optional activities can create costs. FWB prohibits an enrolment fee and regulates allowable school charges; Flanders also has Community rules on school costs. Do not promise that meals, trips or every material are free. (S02, S39–S41)

## 2. Newcomer language support and Ukrainian-language boundary

|---|---|---|---|
| Flemish Community | Primary: **taalheldklas** (from 1 Sep 2026 replaces newcomer reception from second year of primary); secondary/full-time and part-time: **OKAN / onthaalonderwijs voor anderstalige nieuwkomers**. (S10) | Primary newcomer criteria include age seven by 1 January, non-Dutch home language, insufficient Dutch and limited recent Flemish schooling; residence status/duration does not matter. (S08–S09) | DASPA is for eligible recently arrived/non-French-speaking pupils, normally 12 months, extendable to 18 months and up to 24 months for specified learners. The course includes intensive French and other adapted content. (S08–S09) | Parents contact a listed DASPA school. Schools accept eligible pupils while places remain; if full, the school gives a request certificate and family must seek another place. It is not a guaranteed Ukrainian-language class. (S08–S09) |
| German-speaking Community | **Sprachlernklasse / Sprachlernkurs für erstankommende Schüler** (German-language class/course for first-arrival pupils). (S11–S13) | Current DG decree criteria include age 2.5–18, language below A2 and residence/usual stay in one of the nine DG municipalities; school/administration evaluates and records the pupil. (S12) | Separate schools/classes are organized according to need; local provision has changed as numbers changed. As of Oct 2026 official DG records still show school support councils and secondary language-class structures. No guaranteed class at every school or Ukrainian-speaking teacher is promised. (S11–S13) |

**Ukrainian language, interpreting and online school:** no current national entitlement to a Ukrainian-speaking teacher, interpreter, separate Ukrainian class, or mother-tongue course was located. Schools may arrange local support, and schools/authorities may use interpreting services, but availability is not guaranteed. No current Belgian Community-wide programme was found that makes continuing Ukrainian online school a substitute for Belgian compulsory education. FWB expressly distinguishes distance teaching from home education; foreign schooling only satisfies the obligation through the applicable recognition/declaration route. In Flanders and DG, parents likewise must follow the Community’s approved home-education/foreign-school process. Seek written confirmation from the competent Community before relying on Ukrainian remote schooling as the sole route. (S06, S14–S16)

**Older learners 16–18:** compulsory education continues, but from 15/16 the pupil may follow an approved part-time learning/work route if statutory conditions are met; full-time secondary remains available. Flemish OKAN eligibility has upper age limits and a CDO route for eligible young people; FWB DASPA is for eligible pupils, with placement/referral dependent on age and prior schooling. A 17-year-old with a Ukrainian upper-secondary diploma should ask the school/education authority about equivalence and higher-education entry rather than assume the same class placement as a pupil with unfinished secondary school. (S01, S04, S10, S17, S22)

## 3. Higher-education admission and recognition

| Community | Application route | Ukrainian/foreign secondary qualification | Language / credits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flemish Community | Apply directly to the chosen university or university of applied sciences/arts; there is no single national admission application for ordinary programmes. Institutions set programme requirements. (S17–S19) | Most foreign secondary diplomas are not automatically equivalent. A bachelor applicant generally needs a diploma that gives access to higher education in the country of award and an access declaration; the Flemish institution assesses the application. NARIC-Vlaanderen is a recognition authority, but formal NARIC equivalence is not automatically required for every admission. (S17–S20) | Programme language (Dutch/English etc.), accepted proof, admission tests and additional documents vary. Recognition of prior credits/exemptions is an institution decision based on submitted records; no automatic Ukrainian ECTS transfer was established. (S17–S19, S21) |
| French Community / FWB | Apply to the university, haute école or arts institution under that institution’s admission process. For bachelor entry, foreign secondary-diploma equivalence by FWB’s secondary equivalence service is generally required unless the diploma/category is exempt; academic-year filing windows apply. (S22–S24) | The FWB service lists a temporary-recognition decree for specified Ukrainian complete-secondary certificates. A TP holder can submit the TP attestation to be exempt from the administrative fee for that equivalence application. This is not automatic admission and is not the same as tuition exemption. (S23–S24) | French or programme-specific language proof and any entrance test are institution/programme rules. Prior-study credit exemptions are decided by the receiving institution/jury from official academic evidence. (S21–S22, S25) |
| German-speaking Community | Apply directly to the relevant higher-education institution; the principal local provider is the Autonomous Hochschule Ostbelgien (AHS). Some applicants study in Flemish/French systems under those systems’ routes. (S26–S27) | The DG Ministry handles equivalence of foreign secondary qualifications for DG purposes; AHS admissions specify when an equivalent certificate is required. Ukrainian recognition is not an automatic nationwide Belgian decision. (S26–S27) | AHS programme language and entry requirements vary; some programmes require German B2 or French B1, and teacher-training programmes have an admission process. Prior studies/credits must be assessed by the receiving institution. (S26–S27) |

**Missing or lost academic documents:**

- For compulsory-school enrolment, missing Ukrainian report cards should not be presented as a general bar to attendance. Ask the receiving school to assess the pupil and explain how it will record the year/level while papers are unavailable. (S02, S04–S05, S08–S12)
- In FWB, the DASPA certificate-of-admissibility pathway is specific to eligible pupils enrolled in DASPA for at least six months and permits access to most secondary years despite missing documents. It does not erase every final-year/diploma requirement. (S08–S09)
- For higher education, FWB has a special Ukrainian secondary-equivalence fee exemption for TP holders, but no general waiver of diploma/evidence requirements was established. Flemish institutions may assess foreign qualifications individually; in all three systems, ask the admission office what alternative evidence they will consider. Do not promise provisional admission, a document waiver, or a particular credential without written institutional confirmation. (S17–S24, S26–S27)

**Admission equivalence is not professional recognition:** a secondary-school equivalence may determine access to a study level; NARIC/Community diploma recognition may establish academic level; professional recognition for regulated work is a separate route. Do not tell a student that university admission alone recognizes a Ukrainian professional qualification. (S20, S23, S26)

**Application fees and extra programme charges:** these may be separate from tuition/minerval and scholarship. A tuition rule does not automatically waive application, test, materials, housing, insurance or programme-specific costs. No universal TP waiver of application fees was identified. (S17–S19, S28–S33)

### Scholarships / student financial aid

- **Flanders:** the study grant is means-tested and subject to study-progress and other conditions. The official nationality rules expressly include a person from Ukraine who holds a valid temporary-protection residence permit on 31 December of the relevant academic year; grant receipt can qualify the student for the grant tuition tariff. Application is required. (S34–S35)
- **FWB:** the 2026–27 grant requires a qualifying programme, income/resource conditions, a household composition in Belgium and a qualifying nationality/status. The listed protection route is recognized refugee, stateless or subsidiary-protection status held for at least one year by 31 October 2026; TP is not named in that category. Other nationality/family/work routes could apply. Do not claim a TP holder is automatically ineligible or eligible without checking the student’s facts. (S31, S36–S37)
- **German-speaking Community:** a means-tested 2026–27 study grant is available for eligible secondary and higher-education students, with updated household-income thresholds and a 30 November 2026 application deadline. The current source does not identify a TP-specific status rule; ask the DG Ministry about an individual TP case. (S38)

**Keep separate:** admission to a programme ≠ base tuition amount ≠ exemption from a foreign-student surcharge ≠ a means-tested scholarship/study grant. TP does not waive programme language, entrance test, diploma/equivalence or credit-assessment conditions unless a specific rule says so. (S17–S38)

## 8. Readiness and drafting boundary

### `education-school-enrolment` — READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS

There is a safe route: contact a local school without waiting for an A card, explain age/language/prior schooling and ask about capacity and placement. Official evidence supports access regardless of residence paperwork. Community-level admission and placement procedures still differ; preferred school and final grade placement are not guaranteed.

### `education-pupil-language` — READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS

Current Community programmes and eligibility are documented, including the Flemish primary change effective September 2026. Local capacity varies, and Ukrainian-language provision is not a nationwide entitlement.

### `education-higher-admission` — READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS

Application, diploma and 2026–27 fee boundaries can be stated by Community. TP fee protection is verified for Flemish increased tuition and FWB additional foreign-student contribution, but neither equals a universal tuition waiver. German-speaking Community’s TP-specific fee treatment and lost-document higher-admission pathway remain unestablished.

**Blocking gaps:** None. Non-blocking material uncertainties to preserve: exact school seat and local mid-year process; particular school’s administrative acceptance/document identifier; alternative evidence for missing higher-education documents; exact current 2026–27 AHS fee and TP handling; FWB/Flemish programme-specific language and credit decisions. These do not prevent a constrained practical article but must not be guessed.

**Article boundary:** **Draftable with constrained scope.**

### Safe to state

- Contact a local school promptly; the child’s basic right to attend does not depend on TP completion or A-card issuance.
- Explain age-five-to-18 compulsory education carefully, with full-time/part-time transition and home-education distinction.
- Explain school admission, language support and class placement separately for Flemish, French and German-speaking systems.
- Explain that missing school documents should be disclosed but are not a general reason to postpone initial school contact; FWB DASPA has a defined later certification route.
- Apply directly to higher-education institutions and check the correct Community’s qualification, language, fee and credit rules.
- TP has specific but limited higher-ed fee treatment in Flanders and FWB; distinguish surcharge from base tuition and student grant.

### Must qualify

- A card pending, TP application pending, no local domicile, or private hosting: access right is established, but local registration/data handling and school availability remain practical issues.
- Foreign secondary diploma equivalence, specific year/track, missing evidence, language standards, entrance tests and prior-credit exemptions.
- OKAN, DASPA, taalheldklas and DG language class eligibility, duration, exceptions and local capacity.
- 2026–27 fee amounts, current valid TP proof, application deadlines and separate grant eligibility.
- Online Ukrainian school can count only if the competent Community’s recognized home-education/foreign-school requirements are met.

### Do not assert

- A child must wait for the A card or municipality registration before asking for school enrolment.
- Missing Ukrainian school documents automatically block initial enrolment, or a Ukrainian report automatically maps to a Belgian grade.
- Every newcomer receives a place at a preferred school, OKAN/DASPA/local language class, Ukrainian-language teaching or interpreter.
- Ukrainian online school automatically replaces Belgian compulsory education.
- TP automatically admits an applicant to university, recognizes a diploma/credits, waives language requirements, or makes all tuition free.
- A Flemish, FWB or DG fee/grant rule applies identically in another Community.

## 9. Cross-topic consistency

- Topic 1’s distinction is preserved: the federal TP attestation and municipal A card are different documents/stages. Education access must not be inferred from healthcare’s use of TP evidence before A card. (S02–S05)
- Topic 6’s housing finding is preserved: actual residence and a lease are separate from municipal registration. For school, the right of access is broader and does not depend on a completed housing/domicile process. (S02–S05)
- The compulsory-duty rule’s register-entry timing is not misrepresented as the start of the child’s education right; register timing and access are separate. (S01–S06)
