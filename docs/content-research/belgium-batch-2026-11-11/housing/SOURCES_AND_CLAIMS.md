# Belgium — Housing under Temporary Protection: Sources and Claims

**Research checked:** 11 November 2026
**Repository baseline:** `5efbf4babf8930fe28a5ca87914980cca97c26cf`
**Aspects:** `housing-initial`, `housing-private-rental`, `housing-financial-support`
**Overall verdict:** **READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS**

This is a research record, not public article copy or legal advice. It concerns housing for people covered by Belgium’s temporary-protection (TP) arrangements, principally people displaced from Ukraine. Belgian housing law and support are regional and local. TP, municipal residence registration, a private lease, and entitlement to an individual CPAS/OCMW benefit are separate matters.

## Findings at a glance

- A person who has no place to stay should tell Fedasil at the Immigration Office TP registration service point. Fedasil can screen the accommodation need and route a person to available emergency or municipal/temporary accommodation; placement depends on the situation and available places. The person is not placed automatically into the ordinary asylum reception network. Neither a guaranteed bed nor guaranteed long-term housing is established. (S01–S04)
- The practical route is: attend the current TP registration point; state plainly that there is no accommodation and disclose urgent vulnerability or a same-day risk; ask Fedasil at the service point for emergency routing; after registration, contact the municipality’s Ukraine/local housing coordinator and the CPAS/OCMW for a local assessment. If there is no bed tonight, use the region’s emergency shelter referral service. None of these steps guarantees a place. (S01–S06, S13–S17)
- The current schemes are narrow and time-limited. Flanders’ general municipal reception-place funding ended on 1 April 2026; its page says Ghent’s nooddorp remains the active route for new displaced people with an accommodation need coming from the federal Ariane facility. Wallonia says four collective centres can accommodate displaced Ukrainians from early April 2026 through March 2027, with requests routed by local coordinators/communes to the provincial governor via TempHoWal. Brussels and German-speaking Community sources describe local or temporary routes, but do not establish general 2026 intake or live capacity. (S07–S12)
- Ordinary private renting is possible under regional tenancy law, but official sources reviewed do not establish that every landlord must accept a TP attestation in place of an A card, that a lease can always be signed before municipal registration, or a universal maximum for advance rent. Do not confuse first rent/contractual payments with the security deposit. (S19–S31, S36–S38)
- Deposit caps differ: Flanders generally allows up to three months for a principal-residence lease; Brussels caps leases concluded/renewed from 1 November 2024 at two months; Wallonia and the German-speaking Community follow the Walloon regime, with two months for an individual blocked account and up to three months for a bank guarantee. (S20–S23, S26–S28, S31)
- Registering a principal residence is based on actual residence and a municipal inquiry; a lease and the population-register entry are related but distinct. A reference address is an administrative mail/contact mechanism, not housing. (S32–S34)
- CPAS/OCMW can assess individual deposit, rent, installation, emergency-housing or other social support under applicable rules; no automatic payment of rent to a landlord or TP-specific general rent allowance is established. Social housing is generally an application and waiting-list route, subject to regional eligibility and allocation rules. (S35, S39–S52)

### If a newly arrived person has nowhere to stay

1. Attend the current Immigration Office TP registration service point and explicitly state that there is no place to stay. The TP application/attestation is a separate process from housing. (S01–S03)
2. Ask Fedasil staff at that service point for an emergency accommodation assessment/referral. Wallonia’s current Ukraine guidance describes possible Fedasil emergency help where a person has no family/friends accommodation and, because of vulnerability or lack of lodging by the end of the day, needs emergency accommodation. This regional guidance does not prove that every case qualifies or that a bed is free. (S02–S04)
3. Ask for the commune/local Ukraine coordinator route for onward temporary housing. In Wallonia, the local coordinator/commune requests a place through the provincial governor/TempHoWal process; a TP beneficiary cannot self-assign to a centre. In Flanders, the current central route is limited to the remaining Ghent nooddorp for people with an accommodation need arriving via Ariane. (S07–S09)
4. Contact the municipality and its CPAS/OCMW for local emergency housing and individual social assessment. If no bed is available tonight, contact the regional emergency shelter service (in Brussels, Bruss’Help/Samusocial) and explain any children, disability, health risk or immediate danger. A bed remains capacity-dependent. (S13–S17, S35)

**Eligibility/priority limits:** The sources do not establish a single Belgium-wide TP housing entitlement, automatic Fedasil placement, or a national fixed priority list for families/children. Wallonia’s current emergency text refers to vulnerability and no lodging by end of day; local schemes can have their own availability and prioritisation. Do not promise that private hosting either disqualifies someone or guarantees later public placement. Whether a person may refuse one placement and reapply later is not established by current official guidance reviewed. The Ariane Centre is described as short-term/emergency coordination, not permanent accommodation. Historical 2024 counts and 2022 reception arrangements below are not evidence of present capacity. (S02–S04, S07–S12, S18)

### Emergency homelessness after arrival or loss of accommodation

- **Brussels:** Bruss’Help orients people without housing; Samusocial provides emergency accommodation; the municipality/CPAS can assess local emergency or transit housing. These are referrals/services, not a guaranteed bed. Seasonal cold-weather plans and past Ukraine-specific shelter places must not be represented as current. (S13–S17)
- **Flanders:** Contact the local OCMW for individualized prevention/emergency social aid. An OCMW can intervene in an eviction-prevention context, but the official page does not promise replacement accommodation. (S35, S39)
- **Wallonia:** Contact the commune/local Ukraine coordinator and CPAS; for the Ukraine collective-centre route, only the local coordinator/commune may submit the request, and the governor considers availability and priority. (S04, S09, S39)
- **German-speaking Community:** Contact the local ÖSHZ; official Community guidance describes emergency flats for people homeless due to financial, social or psychological circumstances, following ÖSHZ confirmation/assessment. (S53)
- **Children/vulnerability:** State the immediate risk clearly to Fedasil, the commune, CPAS/OCMW and shelter service. Official sources reviewed do not establish a uniform national family-priority queue or guaranteed child/family bed. In immediate danger, contact the local emergency service. (S02–S04, S13–S17)

The security deposit is not the same as the first month’s rent. The reviewed official sources do **not** establish a Belgium-wide or region-specific general cap on rent paid in advance, nor whether demanding several months upfront is lawful in every contract configuration. Do not invent a “one month maximum” or tell a reader that multiple months are always lawful. Check the signed lease and obtain qualified local advice if a landlord demands unusual advance payment. The reviewed sources likewise do not establish a uniform TP-specific checklist of passport, A card, income evidence, or bank account that every landlord must accept. (S19–S31)

## 4. Address registration, no fixed address and CPAS competence

- **Actual main residence:** Belgium’s federal guidance says main residence is determined factually; the municipality may check it, commonly through a local police inquiry. A landlord or lease cannot turn an address into someone’s main residence if the person does not actually live there, and the lease is not itself the municipal registration decision. Brussels invalidates a no-domiciliation clause for leases under its 2024 regime. Flanders permits a principal-residence exclusion only under statutory conditions; do not generalize that regional rule to Brussels/Wallonia. (S19, S24, S32–S33)
- **TP registration and A card:** Federal TP attestation and municipal residence registration/A card are separate stages. Topic 1’s sequence remains: obtain the TP decision/attestation, then approach the commune of actual residence for local registration. A housing contract alone does not establish eligibility for TP or guarantee A-card issuance. (S02–S03, S32–S33)
- **No fixed address:** A reference address can provide administrative correspondence/registration in specified circumstances, including through a CPAS for an eligible homeless person. It is not a dwelling, shelter booking, lease, or proof that the person has accommodation. Eligibility depends on individual statutory conditions; TP status does not make a reference address automatic. (S34)
- **Which CPAS/OCMW:** For TP beneficiaries entered in the population/foreigners register, federal social-integration guidance generally assigns competence to the CPAS of the municipality where registered. Before registration, or where the person is homeless/no fixed residence, the territorial rules require a fact-specific assessment (including where the person actually usually stays); do not send everyone to one assumed municipality. Hosted residence can affect both the facts and the competent office. (S35–S36)
- **Pending A card / lease before registration:** No current official source reviewed establishes a universal right to rent before receiving the A card, that an Annex 15 or TP attestation must be accepted by every private landlord, or that refusal solely because the A card is pending is lawful/unlawful in every case. Mark this as **not established** and avoid giving categorical advice. Discrimination rules still apply to protected characteristics and individual facts. (S19, S23–S31, S41–S42)

## 5. CPAS/OCMW, social housing and rent support

### Individual assistance

CPAS/OCMW may assess emergency accommodation referrals, deposit guarantees, rent arrears, installation assistance or other social aid under the person’s circumstances, local competence, income/resources and applicable programme. This is individual assessment, not a TP housing allowance. A grant/loan or landlord payment is not automatic, and aid should not be represented as guaranteed rent paid directly to a landlord. An eligible homeless person may qualify for a one-time installation allowance when taking a principal residence; it is not a deposit or a rent cheque. (S35–S40, S53)

### Utilities and charges

The lease should distinguish rent and charges according to the applicable regional rules; do not assume utilities are included. No general TP-specific energy/utility payment that automatically follows from being hosted or renting was established in this housing research. An OCMW/CPAS may assess individual energy-related social aid, but detailed tariff and energy support are outside scope. (S19, S24–S25, S35, S53)

## 6. Discrimination and eviction boundary

- **Discrimination:** Nationality/origin, protected ethnic characteristics, social-assistance status and source of wealth/income are relevant protected grounds in housing rules. A landlord may assess ability to pay on an individualized, lawful basis; not every refusal is discrimination, and an individual refusal requires evidence/context. Unia is the contact for Brussels/Wallonia; the Flemish Human Rights Institute (VMRI) handles Flanders. A TP holder should document the advert, messages, requested documents and stated reason for refusal and seek advice from the applicable body. (S41–S42)
- **Eviction:** A private landlord cannot lawfully carry out a physical eviction by self-help; court authority and enforcement through an authorized bailiff are required in the regional procedures reviewed. In Flanders the OCMW can offer eviction-prevention support; in Brussels regional rules also provide a court/bailiff process. This section is only a safety boundary, not advice about deadlines, defenses, or litigation. If a lockout is threatened, contact the local police/CPAS/OCMW and qualified housing help. (S39, S57–S58)

## 10. Readiness and drafting boundary

### `housing-initial` — READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS

Safe route is established: report no accommodation at the Immigration Office TP registration service point, ask Fedasil for assessment/referral, then contact the local commune/CPAS/OCMW and emergency shelter service. Regional temporary schemes are documented, but capacity/eligibility and some current intake are uncertain. Never promise a bed or imply permanent housing.

### `housing-private-rental` — READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS

Regional lease, deposit and registration rules are sufficiently documented for a practical overview. Must qualify the lease-registration distinction, current regional date rules, advance rent, TP-document acceptance before A card and case-specific insurance/charges.

### `housing-financial-support` — READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS

Regional social housing and rent/deposit programmes can be summarized with their application conditions and ordinary status. CPAS/OCMW assessment is discretionary/individual; TP alone does not establish an award. German-speaking Community new rent-subsidy applications are closed under the former scheme.

**Blocking evidence gaps:** None. Material limitations are non-blocking qualifications: live bed/capacity at a particular centre; individual eligibility and current Brussels referral intake; signing/landlord documentation before A card; any advance-rent ceiling; and current application availability for certain niche Walloon/DG supports. These unknowns must remain explicit, not be filled with assumptions.

**Article boundary:** **Draftable with constrained scope.**

### Safe to state

- Tell Fedasil at TP registration if there is no accommodation; ask for assessment and referral, then contact the municipality/local coordinator and CPAS/OCMW.
- There is no verified automatic or guaranteed initial/permanent housing placement for every TP beneficiary.
- Explain active schemes with their exact region, referral route, time limit and capacity qualification.
- Explain private leases and deposit caps by region; distinguish deposit from rent in advance.
- Register the actual main residence with the municipality; a reference address is not a dwelling.
- Apply separately for social housing and rent/deposit assistance; normal eligibility, waitlists and individual assessment apply.

### Must qualify

- Any Brussels, Wallonia or Flanders Ukraine-specific temporary housing: scheme availability, intake, duration and capacity may change; live webpage is not proof of a bed.
- CPAS/OCMW aid, homelessness priority, host arrangements, rent subsidies, deposit loans and proof-of-status requirements.
- Address registration and A-card timing; the municipality checks actual residence.
- The Brussels two-month deposit cap applies to leases concluded/renewed from 1 November 2024; older lease regime differs.
- No advance-rent maximum or universal A-card-pending rental acceptance was established.

### Do not assert

- Every TP applicant has a right to Fedasil accommodation, receives a guaranteed first-night bed, or qualifies for the regular asylum reception network.
- Emergency or collective accommodation is permanent, universally free, automatically available, or directly self-bookable.
- TP status automatically grants social housing, rent subsidy, deposit payment, host compensation, utility aid or CPAS payment to a landlord.
- One nationwide rental deposit maximum, one regional process for all of Belgium, a universal advance-rent cap, or a mandatory document checklist that every landlord must accept.
- A lease itself establishes TP, A-card eligibility or municipal residence registration.

## 11. Cross-topic consistency

- Topic 1’s current sequence is preserved: Immigration Office decides TP and issues the federal attestation; municipality registers actual residence and issues the A card. The housing route does not replace either process. (S01–S03)
- Topic 1’s Fedasil finding is refined only with current regional evidence: report need at the TP service point; emergency accommodation/referral is need- and capacity-dependent, not permanent or guaranteed. (S02–S04)
- Topic 5’s CPAS competence and reference-address cautions are preserved: actual habitual residence/register status matter; a reference address is administrative, not housing. (S34–S36)
