# Belgium — Healthcare under Temporary Protection: Sources and Claims

**Research checked:** 11 November 2026
**Repository baseline:** `25c0ad6a0f18f520bde82700b46cb067969f899d`
**Aspects:** `healthcare-access`, `healthcare-proof`, `healthcare-urgent`
**Overall verdict:** **READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS**

Research ledger for a practical article on healthcare in Belgium under temporary protection (TP). This is not medical advice or public article copy. It separates TP from health-insurance affiliation and reimbursement, and separates emergency medical response from the Belgian legal/social-aid mechanism called *urgent medical aid* (AMU/DMH). The TP-specific rules principally concern displaced people from Ukraine covered by Belgium’s temporary-protection scheme. Do not generalize them to every immigration status or every person who has merely applied for TP.

## Findings at a glance

- TP is not itself a health-insurance card and does not mean a person is automatically affiliated. A TP beneficiary can affiliate as a resident under the INAMI rule, and must complete affiliation with a mutuality or CAAMI/HZIV to obtain ordinary reimbursement. Employment is not required for this TP resident route. (S01–S04)
- The current INAMI circular, applicable from 20 August 2024, says a TP beneficiary may prove resident status for affiliation with the TP attestation or the official registration proof; status may also be verified electronically through the Crossroads Bank for Social Security. The TP-specific Social Integration Service FAQ additionally identifies Annex 15 as acceptable proof of resident status. A-card issuance is therefore not a universal precondition under the INAMI rule. (S01, S05)
- Once enrolled, coverage takes effect retroactively from the first day of the calendar quarter in which the TP attestation or registration proof was issued. This is not automatic membership: register with an insurer, submit the documents it requests and give it bank/payment details. The special rule is not a general entitlement for a TP application still pending. (S01–S05)
- Before federal TP grant, the 4 November 2024 federal FAQ says people displaced from Ukraine who declare to the CPAS that they seek TP may request CPAS-funded medical aid while waiting, including people who have not yet filed, have Annex 3, have not received their TP certificate, or have the certificate but not yet gone to the commune. This is a special Ukraine-related bridge; it is not ordinary insured coverage and must not be confused with the general statutory AMU definition. (S05–S07)
- CAAMI/HZIV is the public auxiliary health-insurance fund. It administers the same compulsory insurance benefits as a mutuality and charges no supplementary contribution; it does not offer supplementary insurance. The optional supplementary services/contributions of private mutualities are distinct from compulsory insurance. (S02–S04)
- Ordinary care is not universally free. Depending on the service, provider, insurance status and third-party-payment arrangement, a patient may pay the whole fee and later be reimbursed or pay a personal share (ticket modérateur/remgeld), plus any lawful extra or non-reimbursed cost. (S16–S17)
- A life-threatening emergency: call 112. For urgent but non-life-threatening problems outside normal GP hours, 1733 routes to local on-call GP services in covered areas; availability is still being rolled out, so check the local service. These calls do not make care free. (S13–S14, S16)

## 2. `healthcare-access` — route by situation

### A. TP application pending

Ordinary TP-resident insurance affiliation is not established before the person has TP proof/status. For people displaced from Ukraine who declare to the CPAS that they seek TP, the federal SPP Intégration sociale FAQ (version 4 November 2024) states that medical aid may be requested while awaiting status. It explicitly includes: people who have not yet taken steps but intend to apply; people holding Annex 3 and waiting for registration; people who attended the registration centre but received no certificate; and people who have a TP certificate but have not yet approached the commune. The CPAS assesses the request and the applicable medical aid/payment conditions. (S05–S07)

The FAQ calls this aid *aide médicale urgente* (AMU). That label is not interchangeable with emergency department care. The current Primabook general definition says statutory AMU is social aid principally for indigent people without a valid Belgian residence right, and its *MediPrima* process is distinct from medical urgency. The TP-specific Ukraine FAQ is a special route and is the controlling practical reference for those pending stages; do not generalize it to a pending applicant under an unrelated immigration route. (S05–S07)

No current national source reviewed establishes that a TP-pending person is automatically insured, can claim mutuality reimbursement, or can rely on a pending application alone for ordinary insured care. For a non-urgent need before TP proof, contact the CPAS that can assess the person’s situation; for a life-threatening emergency call 112. (S05–S07, S13)

### B. TP granted; federal attestation received; municipal registration/A card pending

The attestation establishes TP, not issuance of the A card. Under the INAMI circular effective 20 August 2024, a TP beneficiary may be registered as a resident with an insurer on the basis of TP proof; the circular expressly accepts the TP attestation and permits electronic status verification. The MI-IS TP FAQ says Annex 15 may also prove resident status for affiliation. Therefore, the stronger current evidence supports starting the insurance-enrolment process before the A card, after TP has been granted. (S01, S05)

The affiliation effective date is retroactive to the first day of the quarter in which the TP certificate/accepted registration proof was issued. It is not retroactive to arrival or to a pending application. Ask the chosen insurer to process the affiliation and confirm the effective date; providers may need the insurer’s confirmation before final billing. The exact handling of a claim incurred while the application is being processed is not guaranteed for every provider, although the entitlement’s effective date is retroactive. (S01, S04)

**Official-source conflict:** CAAMI’s public Ukraine registration page still lists the A card among the documents to send. The later INAMI circular says the TP attestation can establish resident status for affiliation and the MI-IS TP FAQ recognizes Annex 15. For legal entitlement, prioritize the INAMI circular; for CAAMI’s current submission workflow before an A card, verify directly with CAAMI rather than promise acceptance or delay affiliation until the A card without asking. (S01, S04–S05)

### C. Annex 15 / municipality pending A card

Annex 15 is an interim stay document, not the A card. For TP health-insurance registration, MI-IS’ Ukraine-specific FAQ explicitly accepts it as evidence of resident status; INAMI’s circular provides the legal resident-registration framework and accepts TP proof. The effective coverage date remains tied to the quarter when the TP certificate/accepted proof was issued, not necessarily the date Annex 15 was printed. Annex 15 alone does not mean the insurer has completed affiliation. (S01, S05)

### D. A card issued; not yet affiliated

The A card is proof of limited stay, not proof of insurance membership. Choose a mutuality or CAAMI/HZIV and complete affiliation. A TP beneficiary’s insurance start remains the retroactive quarter date under the TP rule once the status evidence and registration are processed. An A card is commonly requested on CAAMI’s current Ukraine page, but the later INAMI rule accepts the TP attestation as resident proof. (S01–S04)

### E. A card plus mutuality/CAAMI affiliation

Use ordinary compulsory-insurance routes: GP, specialist, hospital, pharmacy and other benefits covered by the official reimbursement rules, subject to service-specific reimbursement, patient share and authorization conditions. Providers can electronically consult current assurability through the authorized social-security network; patients may need to present an electronic residence/identity document, ISI+ or other accepted insurer proof. (S10–S12, S16–S22)

## 3. Health-insurance affiliation, CAAMI and coverage timing

### TP-specific eligibility and start date

The INAMI directive applicable from 20 August 2024 states that TP beneficiaries can be enrolled as resident beneficiaries even if they may have healthcare rights in Ukraine. The TP attestation or accepted registration proof establishes resident status for this purpose; enrollment takes effect on the first day of the quarter in which that document was issued. This is a retroactive effective date, not automatic enrollment and not a general waiting-period rule for every migrant. The TP FAQ identifies Annex 15 as another way to prove resident status. (S01, S05)

No employment is required for the TP-resident category. Employment is a separate insurance basis and an employer’s ONSS declarations do not, by themselves, affiliate the worker to a chosen mutuality. The person should enroll with an insurer and confirm their recorded rights. (S01–S03, S24)

Children who meet the regulatory dependant conditions can be registered as dependants of a TP beneficiary; a child’s dependant registration takes effect on the same date as the resident’s registration. Other family members have separate dependant criteria, including same-principal-residence conditions for spouses/cohabitants/ascendants. The TP special financial rules can change when a household includes a non-TP dependant; do not assume the same BIM outcome for a mixed household. (S01)

### Mutuality versus CAAMI/HZIV

A mutuality administers the compulsory national health-insurance benefits and may bundle supplementary services. INAMI says members generally pay contributions to the private mutuality; when those contributions are in order, the fund also provides supplementary insurance/services. CAAMI/HZIV is the public auxiliary institution with the same compulsory-insurance function and benefits; it charges no supplementary contribution and offers no supplementary insurance. It is an official route, so a user should not be told they must buy private insurance to access compulsory cover. (S02–S04)

The individual must still affiliate. INAMI’s general rule says affiliation is necessary for reimbursement and the mutuality verifies insurability. CAAMI is a choice, not an automatic default for every unregistered resident; the CPAS may assist or arrange affiliation in cases where it provides aid. (S02–S03, S06)

### Increased reimbursement (BIM / intervention majorée)

Do not state that every TP beneficiary automatically has BIM. The 2024 INAMI TP circular says TP can be a low-income indicator, but for people whose TP attestation was issued from 1 September 2023 onward an income inquiry is required; a signed application/declaration and household-income test apply. The Ukraine-specific CAAMI page still says TP beneficiaries receive increased reimbursement, and older INAMI/CAAMI pages describe automatic BIM. For a newly granted 2026 TP holder, the later 2024 rule and current INAMI BIM process support “may qualify, subject to the applicable income/household process,” not “automatically qualifies because of TP.” Ukraine-origin income is disregarded under the TP-specific circular’s income inquiry. Household composition and later reviews matter. (S01, S04, S23)

BIM lowers the patient share for covered care. It does not make all services free. Current INAMI information says GPs must apply third-party payment for BIM patients in defined situations and the rules have widened; other providers may also apply it. (S16, S23)

## 5. Ordinary care: GP, specialists, hospitals, pharmacy and mental health

- **GP / primary care:** A patient can choose a GP; the Global Medical File (DMG) is opened at the patient’s request and can improve the reimbursement for some GP consultations. This is useful but not a condition for obtaining care. A GP is a practical first contact; no general rule requiring a referral for every specialist consultation was established. (S18–S19)
- **Specialist:** A GP referral is beneficial for an enhanced reimbursement in certain specialist consultations; INAMI’s guidance describes this as a financial advantage, not a universal access requirement. Hospital outpatient services and specialist pathways otherwise depend on the service/provider. (S19)
- **Hospital:** Attend the appropriate hospital/emergency service according to the need. Bring an electronic identity/residence document and insurer information if available; CAAMI says alternatives can be used if the patient lacks an ID at admission. Hospital invoices can include reimbursed charges, co-payments, supplements and non-medical items; no blanket free-hospital-care claim is supported. (S12, S16)
- **Pharmacy / prescriptions:** A pharmacy dispenses medicines that may be available without prescription or require a prescription. Reimbursement is medicine- and eligibility-specific; it depends on the compulsory-insurance rules and patient category. This research does not give a medicine list, guarantee reimbursement, or state that TP proof alone is enough for subsidized dispensing. (S21–S22)
- **Pregnancy and maternity:** No separate TP-only prenatal pathway was established. Once insured, apply the ordinary covered-care route; INAMI’s compulsory insurance includes maternity-related benefits and services subject to the usual eligibility rules. (S02, S24)
- **Children:** Children meeting the dependant conditions can be enrolled under a TP resident; the dependant start date follows the resident’s registration date. This establishes insurance, not a complete regional preventive/vaccination/school-health guide. (S01)
- **Mental health:** The INAMI convention makes specified first-line psychological support directly accessible without prescription through participating psychologists/clinical orthopedagogues in the national mental-health networks. It is not a promise of free or unlimited therapy for every provider or need; the target group, available convention hours, type of intervention and patient contribution depend on the program. No continuing Ukraine-only psychological scheme is relied on. (S20–S21)

## 6. Costs, reimbursement, and employment

Belgian compulsory insurance reimburses covered services wholly or partly at regulated rates. In ordinary fee-for-service care, the patient may pay the full amount and submit an attestation/eAttest for reimbursement; if third-party payment is used, the insurer pays its part directly and the patient usually pays the personal share and any applicable supplement/non-reimbursed charge. Third-party payment is not universal at every provider/service. (S16–S17)

There can be a co-payment (ticket modérateur/remgeld), supplements, non-reimbursed care, medicines, hospital charges or other patient costs. BIM can reduce the personal share for covered care, and the maximum-bill mechanism may limit certain costs if eligibility conditions are met. TP alone does not make care universally free. (S16, S23)

A worker’s employer files social-security employment declarations; the employee still needs health-insurance affiliation through a mutuality/CAAMI or another qualifying insurance basis. Employer registration and mutuality registration are different administrative acts. The INAMI TP resident route does not require employment. (S01–S03, S24)

## 7. `healthcare-urgent` — emergency response versus AMU

### Emergency medical response

| Situation | Action | Cost / coverage boundary | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Life-threatening emergency | Call **112**; free, 24/7 medical emergency line. The call operator dispatches/coordinates the appropriate response. | Calling is free; it does not itself establish health-insurance cover or eliminate hospital/ambulance charges. | S13, S16 |

### Belgian *aide médicale urgente* (AMU / dringende medische hulp)

AMU is a public-assistance/payment mechanism administered through the CPAS, not a synonym for a hospital emergency department. In the general legal description, it is for indigent foreign nationals without a valid right of residence; it can cover medically certified preventive or curative services, including outpatient, pharmaceutical and hospital care, and is not limited to an immediate life-threatening event. The CPAS investigates need and insurance/other coverage; the medical character/necessity must be attested by a doctor or dentist under the applicable procedure. (S06–S07, S15)

The 4 November 2024 federal Ukraine FAQ specifically states that displaced people declaring to a CPAS that they seek TP may request AMU while waiting through several pre-card stages, including before filing, with Annex 3, awaiting the federal certificate, or after certificate but before commune registration. This is a special Ukraine-related administrative bridge and official FAQ wording. The general *MediPrima* page describes statutory AMU as exclusively for irregular stay, creating a difference in terminology/scope. Preserve both: do not tell a TP applicant that AMU is simply “emergency treatment,” and do not generalize the FAQ’s special Ukraine case to all legally resident uninsured people. (S05–S07)

For legally resident people with TP who are uninsured or cannot meet remaining medical costs, CPAS medical assistance may be considered under the ordinary social-assistance/MediPrima rules after assessment; the intervention is subsidiary to available insurance/guarantees and depends on need. Contact the CPAS rather than assume AMU applies. (S06–S07, S15)

## 8. No fixed address or delayed municipal registration

- **TP granted, certificate available:** INAMI’s resident-insurance framework accepts TP proof and does not make an A card the only legal evidence; MI-IS’ TP FAQ also lists Annex 15. Start insurer affiliation and ask the fund to resolve its current document/identity workflow. CAAMI’s web Ukraine page still requests an A card, so acceptance before that card must be confirmed with the selected fund. (S01, S04–S05)
- **No fixed address:** The Social Integration Service says CPAS jurisdiction for ordinary social assistance is usually based on habitual residence; for a homeless person not in an institution, the CPAS where they are staying at the time of application must examine the request. A reference address through a CPAS may be available if the eligibility conditions are met and can help with register-dependent social entitlements. The TP-specific exception places some CPAS cases with the municipality of population/foreigners-register entry, so a person not yet registered should ask the CPAS to determine competence rather than assume which commune is responsible. (S07–S09)
- **Pending status and incomplete registration:** the Ukraine-specific federal FAQ provides a CPAS medical-aid route while TP is pending and before the commune step. Do not promise a universal CAAMI workaround for no address, a universal BIS issuance timetable, or that a CPAS will pay without social/medical assessment. (S05–S09)

## 14. Article drafting boundary

**Decision: Draftable with constrained scope.**

### Safe to state

- Temporary protection and health-insurance membership are separate; a person must enroll with a mutuality or CAAMI/HZIV for ordinary reimbursement.
- INAMI’s 2024 TP rule allows resident affiliation using TP status proof before the A card; effective date is retroactive to the first day of the quarter in which TP proof was issued.
- Annex 15 can serve as TP-related resident proof under the federal Social Integration Service FAQ; it is not a health-insurance card.
- CAAMI/HZIV administers compulsory insurance without supplementary contribution/services; private supplementary contributions are separate.
- For a TP-pending person displaced from Ukraine, the federal FAQ describes a CPAS medical-aid bridge at specified pre-registration stages.
- 112 is for life-threatening emergency; 1733 is an urgent non-life-threatening out-of-hours GP route where active.
- Ordinary care can involve copayments, supplements and non-reimbursed charges; third-party payment may reduce what is paid upfront.
- Children can be enrolled as dependants if conditions are met; current BIM qualification is not automatic for a newly arrived TP household and requires income/household assessment.

### Must qualify

- CAAMI’s current public Ukrainian-refugee page requests an A card despite the later INAMI affiliation rule; tell the reader to verify submission mechanics with the chosen insurer.
- CPAS assistance depends on an assessment and differs from insurance reimbursement; special pending-TP FAQ is Ukraine-specific.
- No fixed address and no municipal registration require case-specific CPAS and insurer guidance; do not promise a universal workaround.
- Care costs and reimbursement vary by treatment, medicine, provider status, insurance registration and BIM eligibility.
- The 1733 number is not active in every region; confirm local coverage.
- Mental-health, maternity and children’s care follow applicable ordinary covered-service/dependant rules; do not promise specific free or regional services without separate research.

### Do not assert

- “A card = health insurance,” “TP automatically enrolls you,” or “the National Register/BIS number alone provides cover.”
- “No care is available until the A card,” because current INAMI guidance permits earlier TP-based affiliation; or “every fund will process every pre-card claim identically.”
- “All care is free,” “every TP beneficiary automatically qualifies for BIM,” or “all medicines/psychological therapy are reimbursed.”
- “AMU means emergency department,” “all legally resident uninsured people qualify for AMU,” or “112 care is free regardless of insurance.”
- “Every TP-pending person can receive the same CPAS aid” without limiting the statement to the documented Ukraine-related rule and CPAS assessment.
