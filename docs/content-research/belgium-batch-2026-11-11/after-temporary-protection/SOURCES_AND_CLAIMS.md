# Belgium — Ordinary residence routes during/after temporary protection

**Research checked:** 11 November 2026  
**Baseline:** `6664e02d447fa4d51d2d7e53321c03fc71faec47`  
**Canonical aspects:** `residence-routes`, `residence-conditions`, `residence-filing`  
**Overall verdict:** **PARTIALLY READY — BLOCKING EVIDENCE GAPS**

This is an official-source research record, not public article copy. It concerns ordinary Belgian residence routes and transition effects only. It does not modify the Topic 1 temporary-protection research record. “Not established” means the official sources reviewed do not support a safe affirmative statement; it does not prove a route is impossible.

## Findings at a glance

- A person holding Belgian temporary protection (TP) may seek another Belgian residence basis if they independently meet that route's requirements. A Belgian EMN national report says that approval changes the residence status, and that TP can be reactivated if the other status ends before TP ends, provided the Immigration Office has not revoked TP and the person has not renounced it. This is strong official research evidence, but it is a national report, not an operative individual decision or detailed application instruction. (S01)
- The general Immigration Office rule for work, study and residence applications says a foreign national who already legally resides in Belgium may lodge the residence request at their municipality; otherwise the normal channel is the competent Belgian consular post abroad. Route-specific laws and procedures still control. The sources do not expressly say in every route that a TP A card satisfies the lawful-residence prerequisite. (S02–S04)
- For a >90-day single permit, the employer submits the joint work/residence application through the regional one-stop shop. Flanders' current official procedure accepts an application where the employee is in Belgium, but asks for a valid Belgian residence permit showing legal residence. The region decides work authorization; the federal Immigration Office decides residence; the municipality issues the electronic combined permit. (S05–S07)
- The ordinary single-permit EU directive expressly excludes people authorized to reside under temporary protection from that directive's ordinary scope. The Council's 2025 nonbinding transition recommendation says Member States should allow TP holders to apply under the Single Permit, Blue Card and Students/Researchers directives provided they would not concurrently hold TP with the resulting EU-law authorization. Belgian implementation / specific Blue Card eligibility while TP is active is not made explicit in the reviewed Belgian route pages. Do not recommend an EU Blue Card as an available TP-holder route without confirmation or cessation/transition of TP. (S08–S10)
- Student residence has a clear in-country filing provision: a person already admitted or authorized to stay in Belgium in another capacity may apply at the municipality before that permission expires if already enrolled full-time in a higher-education institution. Main statutory requirements include qualifying admission/enrolment, sufficient means and comprehensive health insurance. (S03, S11)
- Family reunification is generally filed abroad, with enumerated in-country exceptions under Article 12bis. The usual family route depends on the sponsor's specific status, relationship and statutory conditions. A TP holder is not thereby a qualifying ordinary sponsor; a separate Belgian law route for family of TP beneficiaries must not be confused with ordinary family reunification. (S03, S12–S13)
- During TP, recognized beneficiaries are exempt from the professional card for self-employment; this exemption is tied to the TP category. The Brussels regional authority says first applications are made at a Belgian enterprise counter if the person has the specified valid Belgian residence document, or at the diplomatic/consular post if abroad. The regional page does not explicitly confirm that a TP A card alone meets the document requirement for an ordinary professional-card application. (S14–S16)
- There is no general rule in the reviewed material that a pending ordinary application itself preserves lawful stay or work rights after TP ends. A specific Article 12bis in-country family-reunification procedure has its own municipal-registration and decision-time rules; that route-specific rule must not be generalized to other applications or assumed to cover every TP holder. The EMN report describes rights under the granted status, not a general interim bridge. (S01–S04, S11–S13)
- EU temporary protection is currently extended through **4 March 2028**. The Council's 2025 transition recommendation is policy guidance, not Belgian legislation. No current Belgian national transition scheme, automatic conversion, grace period, general pending-case protection, or post-4 March 2028 return arrangement was found. (S09, S17–S18)
- Belgium distinguishes national settlement/unlimited residence (Law articles 14–15; five years' regular uninterrupted Belgian residence for establishment subject to statutory conditions) from EU long-term resident status (Article 15bis). TP beneficiaries and pending TP applicants are expressly excluded from EU long-term resident status; TP residence time therefore does not count toward its five-year calculation. Qualifying ordinary residence after transition may count, subject to exclusions and conditions. The separate counting rule for national establishment during/after TP is not expressly resolved by the reviewed official text. (S03, S19–S20)

## 2. Core distinction and transition rule

Temporary protection is a specific residence authorization under Articles 57/29–57/36 of the Belgian Immigration Act. It is not itself an ordinary single permit, EU Blue Card, student authorization, ordinary family-reunification permit, professional card, national settlement authorization, or EU long-term resident status. Eligibility and filing must be tested under the separate statute for each route. (S03, S08–S10, S17, S20)

The Belgian EMN national report records that TP beneficiaries residing in Belgium may apply for other residence permits/visas on other migration grounds. If granted, the residence status changes and a different permit is issued. If the new authorization ends before TP itself ends, TP can be reactivated if Immigration Office has not revoked it and the person has not renounced TP. Treat this as the Belgian-reported transition practice, not as a guarantee for a particular case; obtain current written confirmation before advising on a return to TP. The EU recommendation separately says EU-law permits should not be concurrently held with TP. (S01, S09)

The TP statute itself says the minister/delegate may end a TP stay authorization when the temporary-protection regime ends; it does not provide an ordinary-application pending extension after the EU endpoint. At present that endpoint is 4 March 2028. (S03, S17)

### Lawful stay and work while another application is pending

- **While TP remains valid:** TP residence and its rights continue according to the Belgian-reported status substitution practice, but when another residence status is granted, status/rights change; the source does not establish concurrent entitlement to all benefits. (S01, S07, S09)
- **After TP expires or the EU regime ends:** no general Belgian statutory continuation, grace period, or interim document has been located for an ordinary application that is still pending. Do not equate a receipt, employer application, municipal appointment, or pending visa with lawful residence. (S03–S04, S11–S13, S17)
- **Specific Article 12bis family-filing exception:** for the enumerated in-country cases, including a qualifying applicant already admitted/authorized for more than three months in another capacity who files before that permission ends with the required documents, the municipality issues an acknowledgment. If the Immigration Office has not notified the municipality of its decision within five months, the applicant is entered in the foreigners register and receives proof of registration. If no decision is taken within nine months from the acknowledgment (subject to formally authorized extensions under the statute), admission to stay is recognized. This is a narrow family-reunification statutory process; do not treat it as a general pending-application rule or assume that any person holding TP qualifies for the underlying in-country category. (S03, S13)
- **Work:** the TP exemption permits employment while the beneficiary is authorized as TP. Under ordinary single permit, work authorization arises only after the competent region's authorization and the federal residence decision/combined permit; no interim work right from filing alone was found. Work rights under a granted student or family status are indicated by the residence document and have route-specific restrictions. (S05–S08, S11–S13)
- **Limited family-route work authorization:** FPS Employment lists people invoking a right to stay based on Immigration Law Article 10/10bis while that family-reunification application is being examined among special residence situations with work authorization, with a stated exception for family members of students. This applies only to that statutory family category and required status/document; it is not a general work right while any residence application is pending. (S07)
- **Narrow student exception:** if the Immigration Office intends to terminate/refuse renewal for certain institution-compliance reasons, Belgian law permits a 30-day move to another equivalent institution and permits stay until a final decision on that transfer request. This is not a general student-permit or TP-expiry bridge. (S03)

## 5. Effect on temporary protection when another permit is granted

Belgian EMN's national report states that approval of an ordinary residence authorization changes the residence status and replaces TP status for as long as the ordinary status applies. If the new authorization ends before TP ends, the report says TP can be reactivated where the Immigration Office has not revoked TP and the person has not renounced TP. The report does not specify a form, deadline, municipal card replacement/surrender process, or the current post-2028 position. (S01)

For EU-law authorizations, including Blue Card, single permit and student/researcher routes, the Council Recommendation states beneficiaries should not concurrently hold TP and an authorization under those directives. It is a nonbinding EU recommendation, not a Belgian route-specific withdrawal instruction. No Belgian official material reviewed states whether grant automatically terminates TP, whether a formal Immigration Office withdrawal is issued, whether the A card must be surrendered, or how to reactivate TP after a later permit ends. Confirm these practical steps with the Immigration Office before switching. (S03, S09)

## 7. TP end and post-2028 transition

- Current EU law extends TP to **4 March 2028**. This endpoint is not itself a Belgian ordinary residence permit, A-card validity date, grace period, or automatic conversion rule. (S17)
- The Council adopted a 2025 Recommendation calling for Member States to facilitate applications for national residence statuses where applicants meet national conditions, and, for qualifying individuals, applications under EU Blue Card, single permit and student/researcher directives without concurrent TP. This is policy coordination, not self-executing Belgian legislation. (S09)
- Commission/Council material calls on states to step up transition preparation, but no current Belgium-specific national scheme, automatic conversion, dedicated permit, blanket transition grace period, pending-case extension, or post-2028 return procedure was found in the official Belgian sources reviewed. (S09, S17–S18)
- An ordinary application should be filed early enough to satisfy the route's own rules while the applicant still has the required permission. That practical advice does not itself guarantee lawful stay/work after TP ends. TP A-card renewal mechanics from Topic 1 remain separate and unresolved; this package does not alter that finding. (S01–S04, S17)

## 11. Readiness verdicts and blockers

### `residence-routes` — READY WITH QUALIFICATION

The main ordinary routes and the principle that TP holders may seek a different status are established. Do not promise automatic conversion, simultaneous status, or a universal route. The TP-replacement/reactivation statement comes from official national research rather than route-specific current instructions, and Blue Card eligibility while TP remains active is unresolved.

### `residence-conditions` — READY WITH QUALIFICATION

Material conditions are established at a route-comparison level for employment/single permit, Flanders Blue Card, student, family, and professional card. The exact thresholds are regional/date sensitive. National establishment residence-time counting during TP is unresolved; EU-LTR excludes TP time.

### `residence-filing` — NOT READY

- **Known:** General lawful-resident in-country path; single-permit employer filing via region with a federal residence decision; student statute expressly permits municipal in-country filing before current permission expires; family law has defined in-country exceptions; professional-card filing routes depend on region and residence document. (S02–S06, S11–S16)
- **Missing:** Federal confirmation that Belgian TP A-card is accepted as “lawful residence” for the in-country procedure under each ordinary route; a nationally consistent in-country vs abroad decision tree for TP beneficiaries; Blue Card eligibility and mandatory sequence; route-specific permit/TP cessation and card process; lawful stay and work rights for an application pending on 4 March 2028; interim document and municipality treatment.
- **Why material:** Filing location, lawful presence during processing, and whether work may continue determine whether a person can safely pursue the route without losing residence or work rights.
- **Official evidence needed:** Current Immigration Office circular/FAQ or written case-specific guidance on TP-to-ordinary route transition, plus regional current guidance that explicitly accepts TP residence documents; statutory amendment or federal instruction covering pending files at TP termination.
- **What can still be safely drafted:** Explain each route's ordinary filing rule and explicitly label conditional routes. State that a pending application is not proof of continued residence/work after TP expiry and direct users to obtain written Immigration Office/municipal confirmation before the TP end date.

### Overall topic verdict — PARTIALLY READY — BLOCKING EVIDENCE GAPS

An article can be written only with a constrained scope: a route map with authoritative ordinary-route conditions, no guarantee that every TP holder can file in-country, no claim that an application pending on 4 March 2028 preserves stay/work, and clear warning that Blue Card transition and TP/card mechanics require confirmation.

## 12. Article drafting boundary

**Decision: Draftable with constrained scope.** This research itself does not create an article.

### Safe to state

- Belgium has several ordinary residence routes; TP is not itself one of those permits.
- Applicants must meet the independent conditions of each route.
- In-country filing exists under specified conditions for single permit, student and family routes; the standard route is not uniformly “always in Belgium” or “always abroad.”
- A TP holder's work authorization and professional-card exemption are based on the active TP status.
- EU-LTR is distinct from national settlement; TP time does not count toward EU-LTR qualifying residence.
- EU TP is extended to 4 March 2028; the Council transition recommendation is guidance and Belgium-specific post-2028 rules have not been identified.

### Must qualify

- “TP holder can switch” — Belgium's EMN national report supports applying and status replacement; route-specific eligibility, filing evidence and formal steps remain.
- “Apply inside Belgium” — only if the exact route's statutory/document conditions are met and current TP residence is accepted for that procedure.
- “Return to TP if another permit ends” — only if TP has not ended, was not revoked, and was not renounced; exact application procedure should be confirmed.
- Blue Card threshold and single-permit requirements — region- and date-specific.
- Family reunification — distinguish sponsor status and ordinary family routes from TP-family reunification.
- Professional-card exemption — only while the relevant TP authorization applies, unless another exemption independently applies.

### Do not assert

- Automatic conversion to an ordinary permit.
- Every TP A-card holder can apply in Belgium under every route.
- A pending ordinary application keeps the person lawfully resident or entitled to work after TP ends.
- Active TP can be held concurrently with Blue Card/single-permit/student authorization under EU directives.
- TP time counts toward EU long-term resident status, or a blanket five years of TP produces Belgian permanent residence.
- Belgian TP cards are automatically valid through 2028, or Belgium has already adopted a post-2028 conversion/grace-period policy.
