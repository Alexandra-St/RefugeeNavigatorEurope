## 4. Temporary Protection consistency audit

Topic 1 establishes the Belgian eligibility cohorts, federal decision-maker and high-level sequence. Topics 4–8 document downstream systems with their own triggers and evidence. These different rules do not contradict one another and must not overwrite each other:

- **Work:** the supported practical proof is an A card or a qualifying Annex 15 issued after TP grant while the A card is pending. The federal TP attestation alone is not established as sufficient employee work proof.
- **Healthcare:** INAMI permits TP-attestation-based resident affiliation before A-card issuance; the TP-related Annex 15 may also support proof under the identified route. Affiliation with a mutuality/CAAMI must still be completed.
- **Education:** school access does not generally wait for an A card; Community enrollment and placement procedures still apply.
- **Social assistance:** TP attestation plus proof of municipal presentation may open the CPAS application route before an A card; an award is not automatic.
- **Housing:** the federal TP attestation is not a housing placement or A card. Fedasil initial referral is capacity-dependent, and private rental/municipal residence steps remain separate.

Therefore the shared status chain must preserve: TP grant/attestation → municipality and local residence process → A card, while each service's own accepted evidence and decision remains distinct. The federal attestation is not itself an A card, work proof, completed insurance membership, school admission or family-benefit decision.

## 5. Residence consistency audit

The focused Residence Options package supports route maps for employment/single permit, study, family reunification and self-employment/professional-card; it also describes other specialist routes only within its evidence. Preserve these boundaries:

- A route's existence and substantive conditions are not a safe universal instruction to file from inside Belgium. In-country filing depends on the specific route, status and current evidence requirements.
- Route conditions do not establish lawful-stay or work protection while an ordinary application is pending, especially if TP ends.
- There is no automatic conversion from TP to an ordinary permit. Any TP replacement/reactivation statement is conditional on the package's specific limits and does not create a general pending bridge.
- No automatic Belgian post-2028 transition scheme is established.
- **Blue Card:** do not say that an active TP holder can apply for or hold a Blue Card concurrently. The EU transition recommendation is nonbinding; Belgian implementation and sequence are not established in the focused package. Keep the Blue Card route at the exact constrained level the package supports.

## 6. Employment and job-search consistency

The two packages are consistent when they distinguish permission to work from employment-service registration and benefits:

- The four regional employment services are VDAB, Actiris, Le Forem and Arbeitsamt/ADG.
- Searching vacancies or creating a portal account is not formal jobseeker registration. Formal registration is not work permission and does not itself establish unemployment-benefit entitlement.
- Regional document and registration conditions differ. Forem lists an A card and only specified Annex 15 situations; Actiris uses its general valid-stay/work-authorization criteria without a current TP-specific document list; Flanders directs TP beneficiaries to VDAB; ADG describes a voluntary Ukraine route.
- TP employee work begins only within the granted-TP plus supported residence-document boundary. Keep the A card distinct from a qualifying TP Annex 15; pending TP and federal attestation alone are not established work proof.
- The RMMMG is **€2,189.81 gross/month from 1 April 2026** under the covered CCT 43 framework for workers aged 18 and over. It is an average monthly guarantee, not a universal hourly minimum or necessarily the simple base wage each month. Applicable sector CCT minima may be higher.

## 7. Healthcare consistency

The focused package supports three separate tracks: ordinary reimbursed care after mutuality/CAAMI affiliation; status-based TP resident eligibility; and urgent/CPAS medical routes. TP status is not automatic insurance membership. INAMI's current TP evidence allows affiliation using the federal attestation before an A card, and the identified TP-related Annex 15 can support resident proof; the A card alone does not prove membership or active reimbursement. The CPAS bridge for a pending Ukraine TP case is not ordinary insurance.

Keep **112** (emergency response) and **1733** (out-of-hours GP service) distinct. Belgian AMU/DMH is a social-medical aid concept with its own eligibility/CPAS procedure, not a synonym for “emergency department.” The healthcare package records a discrepancy between the CAAMI public checklist and later INAMI/MI-IS evidence: present it as an operational documentation qualification and direct readers to confirm current CAAMI submission requirements. The package rated all three aspects Ready with qualifications, so this discrepancy is not an aggregate blocker.

## 8. Housing consistency

The focused package rates initial accommodation Ready with qualification. Fedasil assessment/referral and emergency routes are capacity-dependent; no bed, placement duration or permanent housing is guaranteed. Regional Ukraine measures are narrow and time-limited. Unknown live capacity is not a reason to downgrade the explicit package rating.

Private rental follows regional law. Preserve the package's deposit distinctions: Flanders up to three months; Brussels two months under the applicable post-1 November 2024 regime; Wallonia/German-speaking Community two-month blocked account or bank guarantee up to three months under the applicable framework. Do not turn deposit rules into an advance-rent cap: no universal advance-rent maximum was established. CPAS housing assistance, social housing and regional rent support are separate, individual applications.

## 9. Education consistency

Preserve the Community split: Flemish Community, French Community/FWB, and German-speaking Community. School access generally does not wait for an A card, but placement or a preferred seat is not guaranteed. The language routes are not interchangeable: OKAN/taalheldklas, DASPA and DG language classes have separate systems and availability. Ukrainian online school does not automatically replace Belgian compulsory education.

Higher-education admission, recognition, fees and support depend on the Community and institution. TP treatment is not a countrywide tuition waiver: in Flanders it affects the increased-fee category but does not universally waive tuition; in FWB it is treated as EU-assimilated for the additional foreign-student contribution while the ordinary base minerval remains; in DG no general TP fee exemption was established. Do not generalize any one Community's rule across Belgium.

## 10. Social-assistance consistency

After TP grant and the municipal-presentation stage, the normal CPAS route is **equivalent financial social assistance**, not automatic statutory RI/leefloon. The current 1 September 2026 RI reference rates are €911.56, €1,367.34 and €1,847.89; they are not guaranteed TP payments. The federal 70%-to-100% integration proposal is not enacted current law in the focused evidence.

CPAS competence depends on foreigners-register, actual habitual-residence, homelessness and special-setting rules. An A card is not required before every application; a pending TP request is not a general cash-assistance route. Unemployment benefits are separate. Flanders, Brussels, Wallonia and DG family benefits must remain separate. Do not resolve or generalize the focused package's open question about family-benefit treatment in the equivalent-assistance means test.

## 13. Blocking gaps

Only aspects explicitly rated `Not ready` in their focused packages are blocked. The qualifications and open questions attached to the other 21 aspects remain qualifications.

### `protection-application` — Blocked — material evidence gap

**Known:** the federal Immigration Office screens and decides applications at Rue Belliard 68, Brussels, on working days at 08:30; each applicant is expected in person. Applicants should bring available identity and eligibility evidence. If granted, the federal TP attestation precedes the municipal residence-card process. Fedasil can be approached at the service point for accommodation needs, subject to need and capacity.

**Missing:** current appointment/walk-in/pre-registration mechanics; complete accepted identity and eligibility checklist; acceptance of originals, copies, photos or Diia for general identity/category proof; the route when passport or category evidence is lost; current child/guardian and translation/interpreter instructions; processing-time and fee rules; national process without a registrable address; Belgium-specific acceptance process for post-2026 military evidence; and required proof/process where another Member State previously granted TP.

**Why material:** these facts determine how applicants can actually lodge and complete the procedure, especially people without documents and families. EU-level evidence guidance does not establish Belgium's operational intake or acceptance rules.

**Official evidence needed:** a current Immigration Office instruction/checklist/circular or written federal confirmation for appointment access, evidence and no-document handling, children, processing and fees, municipal-address limitations, military evidence and prior protection in another Member State; current local municipal guidance only for explicitly local card steps.

**Scope blocked:** a complete, reliable practical filing guide, including assurance about appointments, document substitutes or a no-document route.

**What can still be safely drafted:** identify the current federal centre and weekday 08:30 appearance; tell applicants to bring every available identity/category document; explain the federal attestation then municipality/A-card sequence; say to ask Fedasil about accommodation if needed. Do not promise acceptance or outcome where evidence is missing.

### `protection-dates` — Blocked — material evidence gap

**Known:** the EU temporary-protection scheme endpoint is **4 March 2028**. The focused package says Belgian law provides six-month renewal of the stay authorization while protection continues and municipal residence-title renewal on the holder's request before expiry. A current Belgian regional page still shows **4 March 2027** for A cards.

**Missing:** an authoritative Belgian instruction reconciling the EU endpoint with individual A cards; 2027–2028 extension/replacement mechanics; whether the holder must act and by when; whether an existing physical card must be replaced; individual document validity and travel/service effects; and interim proof during replacement.

**Why material:** **EU scheme endpoint ≠ Belgian physical A-card expiry.** Treating them as identical could cause a holder to rely on an expired document, miss a renewal step or misunderstand travel and proof of residence.

**Official evidence needed:** a dated current Immigration Office instruction to beneficiaries/municipalities stating individual card validity, renewal or replacement, holder action and timing, and what document proves status while a new card is pending.

**Scope blocked:** any unqualified claim about a Belgian individual's A-card validity or renewal/no-action requirements through 2027–2028.

**What can still be safely drafted:** state the EU endpoint as 4 March 2028 and explicitly distinguish it from the individual's Belgian card validity. Refer holders to the Immigration Office and their municipality for current document instructions; do not infer that all cards are automatically valid through 2028.

### `residence-filing` — Blocked — material evidence gap

**Known:** ordinary residence routes and route-specific conditions exist. The focused package identifies a general lawful-residence in-country route; an employer-led single-permit process; a student in-country statutory route under specified admission/enrolment and timing conditions; defined family-reunification in-country exceptions; and region-specific professional-card procedures. The official Belgian EMN report describes status replacement and possible TP reactivation within the limits stated in that package.

**Missing:** route-by-route federal confirmation that a TP residence document permits filing from inside Belgium; exact treatment of an active TP holder applying for an ordinary route, including Blue Card sequence; lawful stay and work rights if TP ends while a normal application is pending; any pending status document; and the effect of an ordinary permit grant on TP where not established by route-specific evidence.

**Why material:** filing location, lawful stay while pending, work permission and whether TP ceases or can reactivate determine whether a person can safely pursue each route. A route's existence or substantive eligibility is not enough to instruct a TP holder to file in-country.

**Official evidence needed:** current federal Immigration Office circular/FAQ or case-specific written guidance for each route that explicitly covers TP status and filing location, transitions and pending documents/rights; route-specific regional instructions where the region decides work or professional-card matters; authoritative rules for pending applications at TP end and the effect of an ordinary grant.

**Scope blocked:** a universal or route-wide instruction that TP holders can file ordinary residence applications inside Belgium, and assurances of lawful stay/work after TP ends while a case is pending.

**What can still be safely drafted:** describe supported ordinary route types and their independent substantive conditions; present filing rules only as conditional route-specific rules; direct TP holders to obtain written federal/municipal confirmation before relying on in-country filing or pending rights. Do not promote a known route into a safe universal filing instruction.
