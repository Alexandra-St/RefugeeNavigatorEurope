# Belgium — Employment Rights under Temporary Protection: Sources and Claims

**Research checked:** 11 November 2026
**Repository baseline:** `e6b991e8037a727770df2ade7bd99bc85ceec37f`
**Aspects:** `work-permission`, `work-employer-duties`, `work-minimum-pay`
**Overall verdict:** **READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS**

This is a research ledger for “Working in Belgium under temporary protection,” not public article copy or legal advice. It distinguishes work as an employee from self-employment, and the federal TP decision from the residence document issued through the municipality. Ordinary residence-transition rules, dismissal disputes, jobseeker registration and individual benefit entitlement are outside scope. Only official Belgian and EU sources are inventoried; a dated official source is identified as legacy when a current source or change has superseded it.

## Findings at a glance

- A person with granted Belgian temporary protection has unlimited access to salaried work **once they hold the residence document confirming the status**. Current federal employment rules exempt authorised TP residents from a regional employee work permit; the official Belgian TP work guide and regional guidance identify the A card or qualifying Annex 15 while the A card is being issued as work-evidence documents. The federal TP attestation is the decision/certificate used to approach the commune, not itself the A card or a generally established work document. (S01–S07)
- A person whose TP application is still pending has no established permission to work. The government’s TP guidance says the pre-registration Annex 3 does not authorize work; no source reviewed gives a work right solely from a pending request or an interim registration receipt. (S05–S07)
- An Annex 15 is not generically work-authorising for every immigration basis. The exemption applies when the person otherwise meets an enumerated category and temporarily holds Annex 15 while awaiting the corresponding residence document. For TP, the official 2022 work guide specifically describes Annex 15 pending an A card. (S01–S03, S05)
- A TP beneficiary working as an employee does not need a single permit or labour-market test. Self-employment is a separate route: TP beneficiaries are exempt from the professional-card requirement in the current Flemish and Brussels guidance and the current Walloon list, but ordinary business registration and self-employed social-insurance obligations remain. (S01–S04, S08–S11)
- Employers must check the third-country national’s valid residence/work-authorising document before work starts, keep a copy or the data available to inspection at least for the employment period, and declare employment start/end. DIMONA is an employer declaration: IN before work starts, OUT no later than the next working day after employment ends. Social-security identity is NISS, either National Register number or valid BIS number; the administration’s current instructions say an employer may request a NISS for a worker who does not know theirs. (S12–S16)
- The generally applicable federal RMMMG/GGMMI is **€2,189.81 gross per month from 1 April 2026** for workers aged 18+ covered by CCT 43. It is an average monthly minimum income, not a universal hourly rate or necessarily an amount of base salary payable in each month; qualifying annual payments can count. Applicable sectoral CCT scales may be higher and bind the employment relationship. (S37–S40)
- General probation clauses remain abolished for ordinary contracts made from 1 January 2014; student and temporary/interim contracts are exceptions with specific rules. General working-time baseline is normally 8 hours/day and 40/week, with 38 hours/week effective or averaged on the applicable reference basis; exceptions and compensatory-rest systems matter. From 1 June 2026 night work is no longer generally prohibited, but working-time, rest and introduction procedures remain. (S25–S30)

## 2. `work-permission` — TP, documents and regional authorization

### Status and start of work

| Position/status | Employee work right established? | Evidence / boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Granted Belgian TP and holds valid A card | Yes. Unlimited access to employee labour market; no regional employee work permit/single permit is needed while the TP residence basis remains valid. The residence card’s labour-market field evidences the status/market access. | FPS lists people authorised to stay as TP beneficiaries under Article 57/29 among work-authorization exemptions; Flemish rules state the TP temporary-residence category can take any job with any employer. (S01–S03, S05–S07) |
| TP granted; federal Attestation of Temporary Protection issued; municipal residence document not yet issued | **Not established as sufficient for starting employment.** The attestation establishes the TP decision and is used to approach the municipality; the official work guide names the residence title (A card) or temporary residence document Annex 15 pending A card as proof. | Keep federal attestation distinct from residence document. Current official process pages identify the stages, while the dedicated work guide is dated 2022 but remains aligned with the live federal exemption and regional document rules. (S01–S07) |
| TP granted; municipal Annex 15 while A card is being issued | Yes, where Annex 15 is the valid temporary residence document for the TP residence status pending the A card. The federal guide expressly says A card **or Annex 15 pending A card** enables work; federal exemption rule also covers qualifying people temporarily holding Annex 15 while waiting for a residence document. | Annex 15 is not a universal work permit for any immigration case. It must correspond to a status otherwise included in the exemption and remain valid. (S01–S02, S05–S06) |
| Pending application before TP decision / Annex 3 declaration of arrival | No work right established. Official TP guidance says Annex 3 before registration/decision does not allow work. No current official source reviewed allows work based solely on an appointment, TP application receipt or pending federal decision. | Do not begin work until the status/document authorizes it. There is no evidenced exception or regional permit route for a pending TP request in the sources reviewed. (S01, S05–S07) |
| Employment vs self-employment | Different permissions: TP residence category is work-authorized for salaried employment; self-employment has a professional-card exemption for TP residents but still requires ordinary business and social-insurance formalities. | Do not confuse an employee work permit exemption with all business rules or regulated-profession permissions. (S02–S04, S08–S11) |

**Exact practical trigger supported by official evidence:** after the federal TP grant, the person must obtain the municipal residence document that evidences the right to reside—normally A card or a qualifying Annex 15 while the A card is pending—before beginning employee work. The TP attestation alone and a pre-decision Annex 3 are not established as work-authorizing. The official sources reviewed do not provide a separate “effective-at-the-minute” rule beyond the document/status condition. (S01–S07)

### Permit exemption and regional difference

The employment authorization exemption is based on the TP residence category and the federal work-authorization framework, while regions administer ordinary economic-migration permits. For employed TP beneficiaries with authorized residence, the current rules exempt from a regional work permit/single permit and no labour-market test is identified. Flanders expressly says the status permits work with any employer; Brussels explicitly states Ukrainian TP residents are exempt from employee work authorization; the federal source lists Article 57/29 TP beneficiaries. The Walloon source’s live TP-specific page confirms sequence and residence proof but does not provide a similarly clear employee exemption statement, so the broad nationwide employee exemption rests on federal law, not on inference from that page. (S01–S03, S05–S07)

### Pending TP applicant

- **Can search/apply for jobs?** This topic does not revisit job search. The distinction here is that a public vacancy search does not authorize employment.
- **Can start work?** Not on a pending application or Annex 3, based on official TP guidance. No permitted start date before the status/residence evidence was found. (S05–S07)
- **Can employer rely on pending application or appointment?** Not established; the employer must verify a valid residence permit/other authorization before employment and maintain the document data for inspection. (S12–S13)
- **Does the person need a regional permit while TP is pending?** No TP-specific interim regional authorization route was found. Do not assume that a pending TP applicant can receive an ordinary permit independently of residence eligibility; that question belongs to economic migration/residence research outside this topic.

## 3. Self-employment boundary

TP beneficiaries authorised to reside under that status are exempt from the professional-card requirement in the current Flemish, Brussels and Walloon sources. This is a professional-card exemption, not a business-formation exemption: a self-employed person must complete applicable enterprise registration, join a social insurance fund before starting, and comply with applicable tax/VAT, sector and qualification rules. The federal portal states the basic CBE/social-insurance steps. (S02–S04, S08–S11)

One old Walloon Ukraine help page (S53) still says Ukrainians need a professional card; this conflicts with the live Walloon exemption list S04 and the 2022 decree implementation page S10. Prefer the current exemption list and flag the old page as inconsistent. Brussels and Flanders also publish their exemptions on live regional work/business pages. The exemption applies to beneficiaries authorised to reside under TP, not simply anyone who has filed a TP application. (S04, S08–S10, S53)

## 5. Contract, probation, hours, rest and leave

### Contract

- An ordinary indefinite full-time contract is not generally required to be written for validity; the employment relationship may be established by agreement/conduct. The fixed-term or clearly defined-work contract must be in writing individually by the start of work; without the required writing, it is treated as indefinite. Part-time work has a written-form requirement for its schedule/terms. Student employment requires its own written fixed-term contract signed no later than commencement. (S19–S22)
- Employers must provide specified information about the employment relationship in writing or electronically by the first workday, even where the underlying ordinary indefinite contract itself need not be written. Electronic signatures/contracts are possible subject to legal requirements; electronic records have archiving conditions and some require mutual agreement. (S18, S21)
- This is a practical boundary summary, not a full contract checklist. The contract type, part-time schedule, student/interim status and applicable sector CCT affect terms. (S19–S24)

### Probation

General probation clauses have been abolished for employment contracts concluded from 1 January 2014. Current exceptions are student contracts and temporary/interim work contracts: normally the first three actual working days constitute the trial period, with special restrictions against successive trials. Do not use pre-2014 advice describing general employee probation or old 7–14-day student trial rules. (S22–S24)

### Working time, overtime, rest and night work

| Matter | General official baseline | Qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Daily/weekly time | Normal framework generally 8 hours/day and 40 hours/week, with 38 hours/week effective or averaged over the applicable reference period. | Sectoral CCT/company schedules may set shorter hours and formal averaging or compensatory rest. Not every worker works 38 hours on every calendar week. (S25–S26) |
| Overtime | Overtime is exceptional and must fit an authorized statutory/sectoral case. Ordinary general overtime premium: 50% for weekday (including Saturday) overtime; 100% Sunday/public-holiday overtime. | Specific sectors/regimes have exceptions and compensation rules; do not convert this into a universal blanket overtime right or maximum. (S26) |
| Daily rest / weekly rest | Normally at least 11 consecutive hours between work periods; joined with weekly Sunday/rest period to provide 35 consecutive hours. | Specific statutory derogations exist, including some force-majeure/shift circumstances. (S27) |
| Break | If a work period exceeds six hours, a break is required; absent CCT or royal order, default is at least 15 minutes no later than when work reaches six hours. | A sector/company CCT may set duration/method. (S27) |
| Sunday | General Sunday-rest principle remains; authorized sector/activity exceptions exist and compensatory rest applies under relevant rules. | Sunday work is not universally allowed or prohibited; verify the applicable regime. (S28, S30) |
| Night work | Night work means generally 20:00–06:00. From 1 June 2026 it is no longer generally prohibited in covered sectors. | Employers must still follow work-time/rest rules and procedures. Certain distribution-sector premium rules changed for new workers from the same date. (S29) |

### Annual leave and public holidays

- Ordinary annual-leave entitlement is based on work in the preceding calendar/vacation year and depends on schedule/status; the ordinary legal maximum is generally four weeks in the worker’s current work regime. A newcomer who starts a first Belgian job in the current year should not assume a full ordinary entitlement in that same year. (S31–S32)
- Additional/European leave can allow a qualifying new starter who worked at least three months/90 days in the calendar year to reach up to four weeks during that year; it is a separate, request-based scheme with distinct pay/offset mechanics, not extra paid leave without conditions. Youth/senior leave has its own prerequisites. (S32–S34)
- Belgium has ten statutory public holidays for covered private-sector employment. Work on an authorized public holiday is paired with compensatory rest; a statutory premium can apply if the hours are overtime, while sector CCTs may add benefits. (S30)
- Holiday pay differs between employee and worker categories; this ledger does not calculate holiday pay or decide a person’s full entitlement. (S31, S34)

## 6. Minimum pay, sector scales, payroll records

### Current national interprofessional floor

The CNT/NAR reports **€2,189.81 gross RMMMG per month from 1 April 2026** under CCT 43 for workers aged 18 and over in employment contracts. It is a guaranteed **average monthly minimum income**, not a statutory universal hourly figure and not always the exact base wage payable as €2,189.81 in each month: some eligible annual payments (for example, an end-of-year premium/13th month) count toward whether the average guarantee is met. The CCT 43 general guarantee does not apply to 18–20-year-olds employed under student contracts, young people in alternating training, or those aged 16–17; those groups have separate or age-specific rules. (S35–S37)

### Sector scales

Many sectors have binding minimum gross pay scales set by their Joint Committee CCT, often according to job classification, qualifications, experience/tenure and indexation. The applicable committee depends on the employer’s principal activity, not simply a worker’s job title. The official FPS database is the current search tool; the committee number appears on the payslip. A sector minimum above the interprofessional floor governs; the individual contract cannot set less. (S35, S38–S40)

Do not state “Belgian minimum wage is €X/hour.” No single universal statutory hourly number is established by these sources. Do not confuse the RMMMG with separate salary thresholds used to obtain a regional single permit for other immigration routes. (S35–S40, S54)

### Payslip and records

The employer must provide a pay statement/payslip at each final pay settlement, showing how pay and deductions were calculated; sector CCTs may specify the fields. The individual account is a separate social document, though payslip copies may substitute for some individual-account entries when permitted. Employers generally retain listed social documents for five years under document-specific start rules. Gross pay is the contractual/statutory wage before deductions; this ledger does not calculate net salary or personal tax. (S41–S43)

## 7. Equal treatment, inspection and undeclared-work boundary

Workers are entitled to the applicable pay and working conditions for comparable lawful employment; TP status does not remove ordinary labour-law rights. Discrimination protections and competent complaint bodies depend on ground: Unia handles many protected grounds including racial/ethnic-origin discrimination in its remit; the Institute for the Equality of Women and Men handles sex/gender-related grounds. Both describe confidential and free reporting/help routes. (S12, S48–S50)

For unpaid wages, minimum scales, working time, rest, public holidays, contracts or social documents, the federal FPS Labour Inspectorate’s regional Control of Social Laws office is the practical authority. It says complaints are handled confidentially unless the complainant waives anonymity; the source does not promise every complaint can remain anonymous at every investigative step. Police deal with criminal matters and are distinct from labour inspection; this ledger does not detail litigation. (S45–S47)

An employer’s failure to declare employment is a social-law violation. An undeclared arrangement does not become lawful because the worker has a TP card, and cash payment does not replace DIMONA or other formalities. Conversely, official labour-inspection guidance recognises that wages and employment-condition protections can still apply to workers in an irregular situation; do not advise someone to abandon an earned-pay claim based only on an employer’s breach. (S12–S13, S44–S47)

## 9. Practical worker checklist — research sequence

1. Confirm the TP decision and the currently valid document authorizing stay/work: A card or qualifying Annex 15 pending the A card. Do not treat a TP application, federal certificate alone, or pre-decision Annex 3 as sufficient proof. (S01–S07)
2. Employer checks the valid document before employment and keeps a copy or its data available to inspectors at least for the employment period. (S12–S13)
3. Get the employment terms in writing where required (fixed-term, defined-work, part-time and student situations) and receive essential employment information by the first workday. (S18–S24)
4. Employer files DIMONA IN before work starts and subsequent DmfA; employee supplies/obtains NISS or BIS where applicable. (S14–S17)
5. Identify the applicable Joint Committee/job classification and compare gross base pay against the current sector minimum; use RMMMG only as the interprofessional fallback/guarantee. (S35–S40)
6. Receive and keep the pay statement; compare paid amounts/deductions with agreed hours and pay. (S41–S43)
7. Keep personal records of hours, breaks, leave and pay. General statutory limits are modified by legal/sector/company regimes, so check the schedule and applicable CCT. (S21, S25–S34)
8. For violations, contact the competent regional Control of Social Laws inspection office; for discrimination, use Unia or the Institute according to the ground. (S45–S50)

## 11. Readiness

### `work-permission` — READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS

The core start-work and document distinction is supported: A card or qualifying Annex 15 after TP grant; pending request/Annex 3 alone is not enough. Federal employee exemption and regional self-employment professional-card exemptions are supported. Keep the federal TP attestation distinct from residence/work-document proof and distinguish employee from self-employed activity. The dedicated TP work guide is dated 2022, but its core document rule is reinforced by current federal law and regional rules; its historical dates/process details are not relied upon.

### `work-employer-duties` — READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS

Employer checking/retention, DIMONA/DmfA, NISS/BIS, contracts, probation, hours, rest, holidays, records and inspection routes are established from current official sources. Detailed expiry-event notifications and unusual schedule/contract exceptions remain outside a concise practical article; qualify by employer/sector/status.

### `work-minimum-pay` — READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS

Current 2026 interprofessional amount and date are established, and the official sector database gives current minima by joint committee. Do not turn RMMMG into an hourly national rate; worker category, sector CCT, work schedule and average-income components matter.

**Overall:** **READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS**. Evidence is sufficient for a practical, region-aware article about when TP employee work can start and core labour/pay protections. A narrow reservation is that the federal TP brochure is dated while the clearest live operational document wording remains regional; no current official source reviewed explicitly states an exact hour/date trigger for an individual Annex 15 issuance/expiry event beyond “hold the valid residence document.”

## 12. Blocking gaps

No aspect is rated Not ready; no blocking gap prevents drafting. Retain these material qualifications:

- The sources do not establish that the federal TP attestation, by itself and before a municipal residence document, authorizes work. The supported practical route is to wait for A card or qualifying Annex 15.
- The online federal rules do not list the appearance of every TP A card’s labour-market annotation in the same place; verify the individual valid card/document and its status with the issuing authority if unclear.
- No bespoke rule was located for an employer when a TP residence document expires, is renewed late, or is being challenged; ordinary work requires a currently valid authorization.
- Monthly/sector salary calculations, student/under-18 rates, public-sector conditions and sector-specific hours/rest/leave cannot be collapsed into one figure.
- Wallonia has a live Ukraine municipal-help page that contradicts the current professional-card exemption page; the current exemption list is stronger evidence, but public guidance should not reproduce the old statement.

## 13. Article drafting boundary

**Decision: Draftable with constrained scope.**

### Safe to state

- TP employee access begins after TP is granted and the person holds the residence document proving authorized stay: A card, or qualifying Annex 15 pending the card.
- Federal TP attestation, A card, Annex 15 and Annex 3 are distinct; pre-decision Annex 3 does not authorize work according to the official guide.
- TP employee work is exempt from regional work authorization/single permit while the qualifying residence status is active; TP self-employment has a separate professional-card exemption, while ordinary registration/social-insurance obligations remain.
- Employer verifies valid proof, files DIMONA and maintains employment records; employer must provide pay statements and follow labour rules.
- Current interprofessional RMMMG is €2,189.81 gross monthly from 1 April 2026 for covered 18+ employees, as an average-income guarantee. A higher sectoral minimum may apply.
- Ordinary trial period is abolished, with student and temporary/interim exceptions; working-time, rest, night-work and leave rules are detailed and contain exceptions.
- Labour inspection can receive complaints on pay/hours/rest/leave and handles them confidentially; discrimination complaints go to the body competent for the ground.

### Must qualify

- The exact valid document, any printed work-market annotation, and whether Annex 15 is the TP-linked temporary document rather than a different Annex 15 form.
- Employment permit exemption applies to authorised TP residence, not a pending application; do not infer work from a scheduled appointment or federal certificate alone.
- Work as employee vs independent business, regulated occupation, business registration and social-security duties.
- Sectoral CCT, category, part-time/student arrangements, reference periods, exceptions, overtime and leave eligibility.
- Minimum-pay figure is dated to 1 April 2026 and should be rechecked when the article is published; it is not a flat hourly or net amount.
- Old Walloon page conflicts on professional card; do not repeat its legacy instruction without checking the current authority page.

### Do not assert

- TP application/pending status or federal attestation alone gives an unconditional right to start work.
- Any Annex 15, regardless of purpose/validity, proves work authorization; or TP and A card are the same document.
- Employer need not verify because the worker has TP, or employee personally files DIMONA.
- One universal Belgian hourly minimum wage, a guaranteed full year/four weeks of ordinary leave for every new arrival, or a universal 38-hour actual schedule.
- Probation is generally allowed for ordinary contracts; the pre-2026 blanket night-work prohibition still applies; or professional-card exemption removes all self-employment formalities.

## 14. Scope limits

This topic does not determine jobseeker-registration eligibility, unemployment benefits, ordinary residence transitions, individual work-permit categories other than TP, salary net calculations/tax, detailed dismissal or severance, occupational injury/health rules, platform-work classification, or profession-specific licensing. Those issues require separate research.
