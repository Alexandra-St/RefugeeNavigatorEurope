# Belgium — Temporary Protection Application: Sources and Claims

**Research checked:** 11 November 2026  
**Repository baseline:** `6789a22aa12fd9987ab4c91e81af199a1146bf0e`  
**Aspects:** `protection-scheme`, `protection-application`, `protection-dates`  
**Overall verdict:** **PARTIALLY READY — BLOCKING EVIDENCE GAPS**

This is a research record, not public article copy or legal advice. It uses official Belgian and EU sources only. Belgium's federal Immigration Office is the decisive authority for registration and the temporary-protection decision; municipalities issue and renew the Belgian residence document. Regional and municipal pages supply local procedures only. No catalog, comparison, runtime, content draft, localization, test or deployment changes were made.

## Findings at a glance

- Belgium currently requires an applicant to appear in person at the centralized Immigration Office Registration Centre, **Rue Belliard 68, 1000 Brussels**, on a working day at **08:30**. The Immigration Office currently publishes this address; Wallonia says applicants should arrive before 08:30. Each applicant is expected to appear. (S01–S03)
- No current official Belgian source reviewed states whether an appointment is required, whether walk-ins are accepted, or whether online pre-registration is available. The online appointment/QR system found in the official 2022 history is historical and must not be reused as current instructions. (S13)
- Bring identity documents and evidence for the claimed eligibility category, including relevant evidence of residence in Ukraine before 24 February 2022. From 5 August 2026, EU rules also require evidence of compliance with military obligations where applicable. The European Commission's 18 August 2026 FAQ describes exit evidence and a fallback document route, but Belgium has not published a current country-specific acceptance list in the official sources reviewed. (S01–S03, S09–S10)
- If eligible, the Immigration Office issues an **Attestation of Temporary Protection** (`attestation de protection temporaire`; `attest tijdelijke bescherming`). This federal decision/certificate is the step used to approach the municipality. It is distinct from the municipal **A card** (`carte A / A-kaart`, “A. Limited stay”), which is the residence permit/card issued after local registration. (S02–S05)
- A person without a Belgian housing solution should tell Fedasil at the registration service point; emergency accommodation is described as subject to need and capacity. The reviewed material does not establish a national alternative municipality/card process for a person without a registrable address. (S02)
- EU temporary protection is extended to **4 March 2028**. The current Belgian regional guidance reviewed still gives an A-card date of **4 March 2027** and contains no 2028 renewal instructions. Belgian law provides that the temporary-stay authorization renews in six-month periods while protection continues, while a residence title is extended or renewed by the municipality **on the holder's request before expiry**. The practical 2027–2028 card, travel-document, and holder-action instructions remain unresolved. (S03, S05, S09, S11)
- Belgium's law allows the Immigration Office to refuse a temporary-protection stay authorization where the person is authorized to stay in another EU Member State. EU law says rights under temporary protection are enjoyed in one state at a time and the 2026 Council Decision says a state is not obliged to issue a permit to someone who already received one elsewhere. The Belgian evidence reviewed does not specify a national transfer/cancellation checklist or what proof Belgium requires to show the earlier status ended. (S05, S09)

The EUAA Belgium profile attributes the following implementation categories to Immigration Office guidance. The post-5 August 2026 military-condition layer comes from current EU law and Commission guidance; no separate Belgian checklist was found. (S02, S05–S10)

## 3. Current application and registration route — `protection-application`

### Current route

1. **Confirm the cohort.** The decision is made by the Belgian Immigration Office; do not treat a Ukrainian residence permit, pre-war presence in Belgium, or temporary residence in Ukraine as automatically sufficient. For a post-5 August 2026 request, separately consider the military-obligation condition and evidence requirements. (S02, S05–S10)
2. **Prepare identity and eligibility evidence.** Official Belgian guidance says to bring identity documents and documents proving the qualifying category, including Ukraine residence before 24 February 2022 where relevant. The European Commission's current EU-level military guidance describes a Ukrainian border exit record/stamp and, where lawful exit cannot be confirmed, official, easily verifiable exemption/compliance documentation such as a paper/electronic Reserv+ document. This is not a Belgian exhaustive list or a guarantee that another document will be accepted by Belgium. (S01–S03, S10)
3. **Appear in person at the federal centre.** Current address: **Rue Belliard 68, 1000 Brussels**. The federal Immigration Office page lists working days at **08:30**; Wallonia says applicants should be there before 08:30. Current federal material does not state full opening hours. (S01, S03)
4. **Registration, identity checks and decision.** The Immigration Office registers/assesses and grants temporary protection if eligible; an ineligible application receives a refusal decision. Fingerprints may be taken where needed to establish identity under Belgian law. The EUAA profile says the Attestation of Temporary Protection is issued upon registration if conditions are met, but no current official Belgian source guarantees same-day issuance or a fixed processing time. (S02, S05)
5. **Tell Fedasil at the registration service point if there is no accommodation.** EUAA describes Fedasil receiving requests from people without housing and coordinating with regions/municipalities; emergency shelter is conditional on need and availability. This is an accommodation step, not a second temporary-protection decision. (S02)
6. **Go to the municipality where actually residing/hosted.** Present the federal TP attestation and apply for the Belgian residence title. Belgian law places registration in the Foreigners Register and issue of the residence title with the municipality; Flanders describes a residence check followed by Foreigners Register entry and an electronic A card. Procedures and appointment channels are municipality-specific. (S03–S05, S12–S13)
7. **Complete the local card process.** Where residence/address verification is positive, the municipality issues or arranges the A card. The reviewed national source does not establish a universal turnaround time, fixed national fee, or universal appointment system. (S03–S05, S12)

### Appointment, online pre-registration and walk-in

**Not established for November 2026.** The current federal Registration Centre page gives a weekday 08:30 appearance time but does not say whether an appointment is required, whether walk-ins are accepted, or whether online pre-registration is available. Wallonia's instruction to arrive before 08:30 does not itself settle appointment status. Do not reuse the old QR/Belgian-IP system. (S01, S03, S13)

### Personal appearance, family and minors

EUAA states that registration is centralized and each person must appear in person. Unaccompanied Ukrainian minors report to the same Immigration Office registration centre; the Immigration Office notifies the Guardianship Service, which appoints a legal guardian under the general rules. The sources reviewed do not establish a separate remote/parent-only registration route for accompanied children, whether every accompanying parent must attend, or the current document set for parental authority. Card biometrics are stated locally for Brussels; Belgian law allows the Immigration Office to take fingerprints where necessary for identity. (S02, S04–S05)

### Current authority and location

The authority that screens, registers and grants/refuses the status is the **Belgian Immigration Office** (`Office des étrangers`; `Dienst Vreemdelingenzaken`) of the FPS Home Affairs. Registration is centralized at **Rue Belliard 68, 1000 Brussels**, working days at 08:30 according to the live federal page. The location replaced older emergency locations, including Eurostation / Place Victor Horta in 2022. (S01–S03, S13)

## 4. Documents and no-document route

### What to bring / what is established

- Identity documents: the City of Brussels instructs applicants to bring identity documents; EUAA says required documents must prove membership of an eligible category. (S02, S04)
- Evidence of category and Ukraine residence before 24 February 2022 where applicable. The source set does not provide a current national exhaustive inventory or decide whether original, copy, photo, or digital evidence is accepted generally. (S02)
- For a post-5 August 2026 application potentially subject to the military rule: an official border-exit record/stamp is one form of evidence; if lawful departure cannot be confirmed, the Commission FAQ describes an official, easily verifiable paper/electronic document establishing exemption/compliance, such as Reserv+. It says alternative supporting documents may be considered by national authorities, which retain decision power. The FAQ encourages a sworn translation where possible; it does not create a Belgian translation requirement. (S09–S10)
- Belgian law says temporary-protection beneficiaries may be fingerprinted at the minister/delegate's initiative insofar as needed to establish identity; it does not make registration biometrics mandatory in every case. (S05)

### No-document route — unresolved

- **Can an applicant apply without a passport?** The Belgian pages reviewed request identity documents, but do not state that a passport is an absolute prerequisite or provide an alternative identity procedure for temporary-protection registration.
- **Can a person without original documents use copies/photos/Diia?** No current Belgian official temporary-protection source reviewed confirms acceptance of copies, photos, or Diia documents as substitutes. The Commission military-evidence FAQ accepts electronic evidence for that specific military/exit-verification purpose, but that does not establish a general identity or cohort-proof rule.
- **Lost/destroyed documents or identity verification by interview/embassy?** No current official temporary-protection procedure was found for this situation. The 2022 historical process described further investigation and referral for documents, but that cannot be treated as a current guarantee. (S13)
- **Does missing evidence delay rather than bar a case?** Not established. The Immigration Office can assess/reject a case; public current sources do not explain when it issues a registration certificate pending investigation, what must be produced later, or how absence of documents affects a decision.
- **Children without identity/family documents?** EUAA confirms unaccompanied minors report and the Guardianship Service is notified, but no current national no-document exception, child-specific proof substitute, or separate family-unit certificate/card process was found. (S02)

This is a material application blocker: safe public guidance can say to attend with every available identity and eligibility document, but cannot promise eligibility, acceptance of substitutes, or an outcome when those documents are missing.

### Municipality after federal registration

The applicant goes to the municipality of the place where they are staying to apply for the residence title. The A card is a municipal residence permit document, not the EU temporary-protection end date. Local address/residence verification is described by Flanders and the City of Brussels. Belgian law states the title's registration/issue and renewal are handled through the commune of residence. No uniform TP-specific first-registration deadline or a nationwide no-fixed-address substitute was found. A federal certificate may therefore precede the card, while a local address check/card delay does not erase the distinction between the federal eligibility decision and the later physical card process. (S03–S05, S12)

**Brussels City example only:** City of Brussels currently instructs applicants residing in that municipality to email the TP certificate, passport copy and address, then attend after contact. It lists Annex 15 (€10; 45 days), A card (€25; biometric), and approximately 10 days to collect the A card after application. These are not national fees or turnaround guarantees. The same page still says the A card expires 4 March 2023 despite its 2026 modification date, so the fee/time figures need confirmation with the municipality before being presented as current user-facing amounts. (S04)

## 7. Prior protection in another EU Member State

- **Already holds another Member State's TP residence permit:** Belgian Immigration Act Article 57/30(2) allows the Immigration Office to refuse the Belgian authorization if the person is authorized to stay in another Member State. The 2026 Council Decision says a Member State should reject a residence-permit request where it is apparent the person already obtained a TP permit in another Member State, and that the second state is not obliged to issue a permit. (S05, S09)
- **Applied elsewhere but has not received the other state's permit:** The Commission's general EU page says a second Member State cannot reject solely because the person applied elsewhere without yet receiving the TP permit. That page predates the 2026 Decision and is general EU guidance; no current Belgian-specific operational procedure was found. (S14; read with S09)
- **Previously held TP elsewhere, left, or asks to transfer to Belgium:** No official Belgian guidance in the reviewed material establishes a transfer application, required proof of cancellation/deactivation, surrender of the former card, authority-to-authority contact procedure, or treatment of a closed/inactive status. The EU Directive sets out a consent-based transfer framework and says the departure-state permit expires on a completed transfer; it does not prove how Belgium administers a fresh application in this case. (S07, S09)
- **New Belgian application and military rules:** A new application is not automatically exempt from the 2026 military-obligation rule merely because the person earlier had status in a different Member State; the exception is for continuous status in the same Member State. The Commission also says a different Member State from the initial EU-entry state may require proof of lawful Ukrainian departure/compliance. (S09–S10)

## 8. Travel and work-right interactions (limited)

- EU law provides movement to other Member States within the applicable short-stay framework after a residence permit is issued; Belgian law also contains a readmission provision for a TP resident found in/attempting to enter another participating Member State irregularly. These provisions do not establish that a TP certificate alone is a travel document or settle travel with an A card whose printed expiry is 4 March 2027. (S05, S07, S09)
- EU Directive Article 12 requires authorization for employed/self-employed work during temporary protection. Brussels City says its Annex 15 carries labour-market access and A card is issued after the local process; EUAA states the A card gives access without restrictions and notes this on the card. A safe statement can say the municipal pending document/A card is the documented Belgian proof in these sources. Whether the federal TP certificate alone is sufficient proof to an employer before the municipality issues Annex 15/A card is **not established** by current Belgian guidance reviewed; defer exact timing to the employment-rights research. (S04, S07, S02)

4. **Immigration Office checks and decides.** If eligibility is established, it issues the TP attestation; if not, it issues a refusal. No fixed processing duration is established. (S02)

7. **Obtain the A residence card** from the municipality. Keep the attestation, interim municipal document, register status and physical A card distinct. The 2027–2028 card-extension steps require confirmation from current Belgian authority. (S03–S05, S09)

## 13. Readiness and blocking gaps

### `protection-scheme` — READY WITH QUALIFICATION

Belgium-specific categories can be described from official Belgian/EUAA material read with the EU implementing decisions. Qualify the new military condition, the permanent-residence TCN return test, the family cutoff, and the absence of a published Belgian expansion for additional cohorts.

### `protection-application` — NOT READY

- **Known:** Federal Immigration Office; current centralized location and weekday 08:30 appearance time; personal attendance; federal TP attestation followed by municipality/A-card; applicant should bring identity and cohort evidence; fingerprinting may be used; Fedasil is the point for an accommodation request. (S01–S05)
- **Missing:** Current appointment/walk-in/pre-registration rule; an exhaustive current identity/cohort evidence list; whether original, copy, image or Diia documents are accepted; the route if passport or eligibility evidence is lost/unavailable; current child/guardian document procedure; interpreter/translation rule at registration; fixed processing-time/fee rule; national procedure without a registrable address; Belgian-specific acceptance process for post-2026 military evidence; and the required proof/process if TP was issued by another Member State.
- **Why material:** These determine whether and how a person can actually submit and complete the application. The research brief specifically requires reliable instructions for no-document applicants, appointment access and families. General EU rules do not fill a Belgian operational gap.
- **Official evidence needed:** Current Immigration Office temporary-protection instructions, checklist/circular or written federal confirmation covering each missing procedure; current municipal guidance where a local municipality-specific step is stated.
- **What can still be safely drafted:** Centralized in-person registration at Rue Belliard 68 at 08:30 on working days; bring all available identity/category evidence; eligible applicant receives the federal TP attestation and must then contact the municipality of actual residence for an A-card; ask Fedasil about accommodation at registration if needed. Do not promise acceptance or an outcome without documents.

### `protection-dates` — NOT READY

- **Known:** EU scheme endpoint 4 March 2028; Belgian statute provides automatic six-month renewal of the stay authorization while protection continues, and municipality-issued residence title renewal on holder request before its expiry; regional Belgian page still gives 4 March 2027 for A cards. (S03, S05, S09, S11)
- **Missing:** Current Belgian Immigration Office instruction reconciling EU 2028 extension with A cards showing 4 March 2027; whether/when a holder must attend the commune, whether the plastic/electronic card is automatically extended or replaced, the new card's validity date, effect on travel and services, and transition rules for people who were deregistered or have an expired card.
- **Why material:** An EU protection endpoint is not an individual card-expiry date. A reader could wrongly travel, fail to renew, or assume the physical card proves stay through 2028.
- **Official evidence needed:** Dated 2026/2027 federal Immigration Office circular/FAQ to municipalities and beneficiaries; current national instructions specifying card validity, municipal action, timing, and proof while a replacement is pending.
- **What can still be safely drafted:** State the EU scheme endpoint as 4 March 2028 and explicitly say it does not itself prove an individual's Belgian A-card validity. Explain that Belgian law makes the municipality responsible for card/title renewal on request before expiry, but direct each holder to the Belgian Immigration Office/commune for the 2027–2028 procedure. Do not say all cards are automatically valid to 2028 or that no action is needed.

### Overall topic verdict — PARTIALLY READY — BLOCKING EVIDENCE GAPS

`protection-scheme` is draftable with qualifications. The current registration location and high-level federal-to-municipal flow are known, but material Belgian application details (especially no-doc procedure) remain unresolved. The exact 2027–2028 Belgian A-card mechanics are also a blocking `protection-dates` gap. Do not draft the missing workflow as a generic EU procedure, and do not convert 4 March 2028 into a Belgian card-validity claim.
