# Belgium — Social Assistance and Family Benefits under Temporary Protection

**Research checked:** 11 November 2026
**Repository baseline:** 8d7acad7ee54fc938c226ca66498084ab9a58326
**Aspects:** social-subsistence; social-application; social-child-benefits
**Overall verdict:** READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS

This is an evidence package, not article copy. Social assistance is administered by the federal CPAS/OCMW/ÖSHZ under federal law; family benefits are regionally administered. The federal protection attestation, Annex 15, A card, the child’s family-benefit record and a social-assistance decision are separate things.

## Findings at a glance

- A person who has been granted temporary protection (TP) and has received the federal TP attestation can request financial social assistance from a CPAS after reporting to the municipality of residence. If eligibility and need are established, the relevant route is financial social assistance equivalent to the integration-income reference (aide sociale financière équivalente au revenu d’intégration / equivalent leefloon), not an automatic award of the statutory integration income (revenu d’intégration / leefloon). The application date is the normal start date of an award. (S01–S03)
- The A card is not a prerequisite to file or to begin the equivalent-assistance route. Federal guidance says the TP attestation plus proof that the person reported to the municipality is sufficient to demonstrate the required stages; Annex 15 is evidence, but another proof of the municipal visit is accepted if Annex 15 has not yet been issued. A positive residence check and A card follow later. (S01)
- Someone newly arrived from Ukraine who has not yet been granted TP does not qualify for the regular TP financial-assistance route on the basis of a pending claim alone. The federal CPAS FAQ describes urgent medical aid for this interim stage, and tells the person to proceed to the TP registration centre. Other legal-residence situations must be assessed on their own facts. Do not promise general cash or housing aid while TP is pending. (S01)
- For TP beneficiaries, territorial competence normally follows the municipality where the person is registered in the foreigners register. Before such registration, the current federal FAQ applies the ordinary rule: the CPAS for the place of actual habitual residence on the date of application. For a homeless person, a special actual-residence rule applies. A wrong first CPAS must transfer the request; it is not safe to direct the person to any CPAS without checking competence. (S01, S04–S06)
- CPAS aid is individualized and means-tested. The federal FAQ permits a lighter social inquiry for the first three months; it says to consider resources the TP beneficiary actually has, not the notional cadastral value of Ukrainian property, while actual foreign income/rent received is relevant. The situation is reassessed after three months. (S01, S09)
- Federal integration-income reference rates in force from 1 September 2026 are €911.56/month for category 1 (cohabitant), €1,367.34 for category 2 (single person), and €1,847.89 for category 3 (person with a dependent family). These are not guaranteed TP awards: CPAS decides whether aid is due, its form, household category and amount. Federal ministers announced a proposed 70%-to-100% integration-effort mechanism in March 2026, but the responsible minister told Parliament on a later review that government discussions continued after Council of State and CPAS federation opinions. It was not an enacted rule on the checked date. (S08, S11–S12)
- Filing may be oral, written or electronic. Ask for a dated receipt. A decision is generally due within 30 days; that is a decision deadline, not a promise of payment on a particular day. Lack of eID or a Belgian bank account is not an absolute filing/payment bar: a postal assignment payable at home, basic bank service, social-assistance account/easy card in relevant cases, or a reasoned direct payment may be used. (S01, S07, S36)
- Family benefits are four distinct systems. Granted TP may open entitlement, subject to each system’s residence, registration and application rules; a pending TP application is not a general substitute for residence authorization. School enrolment does not establish child-benefit eligibility. (S17–S29)

## 2. Social subsistence: which legal route applies?

### TP granted

The current federal Ukraine FAQ says a person who has received the TP attestation and presented to the municipality of residence may claim equivalent-to-integration-income social assistance if all opening conditions are met. This means a CPAS must assess the application; it does not mean every applicant receives the maximum rate. Financial aid may be supplemented or replaced by other individually suitable assistance. The current authority is the CPAS/OCMW/ÖSHZ, not Fedasil or the family-benefit payer. (S01, S13)

The statutory integration-income Act lists its nationality/status categories, including Belgian nationals, qualifying EU citizens and family members, foreigners registered in the population register, recognized refugees, stateless persons and beneficiaries of subsidiary protection. A TP beneficiary’s entry in the foreigners register is not, by itself, the “population register” category in that Act. This is why the federal guidance names a separate equivalent social-assistance route. A later, independent legal status may change the analysis. (S02–S03)

### TP not yet granted

The current federal CPAS Ukraine FAQ distinguishes someone who has just arrived because of the war and has not yet completed TP registration: the stated CPAS route at this stage is urgent medical aid, and the CPAS directs the person to the TP registration centre. If the person already lived in Belgium before the war, the CPAS must examine their existing residence status and the rights attached to it. This is not a general permission to receive equivalent cash assistance before TP grant. (S01)

## 3. CPAS competence and address

For a TP beneficiary covered by article 2(5) of the 2 April 1965 territorial-competence law, the specific rule normally points to the CPAS of the municipality where the person is registered in the population or foreigners register. For current Ukrainian TP cases, the federal Primabook explains that this is generally the municipality of foreigners-register registration. If the person has moved, the CPAS tied to the register remains competent until the new municipality registration takes effect; then competence transfers under the specific rule. (S01, S04)

Before population/foreigners-register entry, the federal FAQ says article 2(5) is not yet applicable and the ordinary rule is used: the CPAS for the place of actual habitual residence at the date the request is made. This is a fact-based residence test, not simply the lease address, planned address, or address of a host family. For a homeless applicant, article 2(7) instead points to the CPAS for their factual residence, subject to the special rule for certain residential institutions. (S01, S05–S06)

The first CPAS approached must register the application. If it considers itself territorially incompetent, the general procedure requires written transfer within five calendar days and written notice to the applicant. The original receipt date remains material. When CPAS centres dispute competence, the federal conflict-resolution route determines which centre must intervene; applicants should ask for a receipt and transfer details rather than starting again without a record. (S04, S07, S33)

No fixed address is not the same as no habitual/factual residence and does not itself establish entitlement. Homeless people may request an address of reference where legal requirements are met. The reference-address application has its own homeless-person eligibility and filing requirements; do not treat it as an automatic consequence of a social-assistance request. (S06, S13)

## 4. Means test, household and amount

### Needs and resources

The CPAS investigates actual need and the applicant’s personal and household situation. For TP financial social assistance, federal guidance describes a simplified inquiry for the first three months from the application date. It says there is no need in that period to value Ukrainian cadastral property or verify Ukrainian health insurance; only means the TP beneficiary actually has should be counted. A property that produces rent the person actually receives is relevant, and actual foreign benefits or other foreign income are relevant. The CPAS should reassess after the first three months. (S01)

The social inquiry is not an automatic exclusion based on having a job, savings or another adult in the home. It establishes what resources are available, what the household actually shares, and whether the person meets the need conditions. Income from work may reduce or remove the assessed supplement; it does not alone bar filing. Resources and household composition can change the category and amount. (S01, S08–S10)

### Household category

The federal Ukraine FAQ applies the integration-income family categories by social inquiry. Cohabitation turns on living under one roof and mainly managing household matters together, not just sharing an address. Where separate Ukrainian families are temporarily hosted by another household, the FAQ says each family can be assessed as a family with dependent family if conditions are met; hosting alone does not automatically merge them. Where hosted by a relative or close person, the CPAS examines the facts and the statutory resource rules. Collective accommodation has no standalone TP category; the CPAS decides based on whether residents actually form a shared household. Reassessment after the first three months is required. (S01, S08)

### Rate references as of 11 November 2026

The federal Primabook table lists these statutory RI monthly rates effective 1 September 2026:

| Reference category | Monthly RI rate | What it means for a TP case |
|---|---:|---|
| Category 1 — cohabitant | €911.56 | Reference only; category based on actual household facts |
| Category 2 — single person | €1,367.34 | Reference only; not automatically awarded to anyone hosted |
| Category 3 — person with dependent family | €1,847.89 | Reference only; not a per-child rate; depends on conditions and household situation |

For TP beneficiaries these amounts are reference rates, not a blanket entitlement or a guaranteed equivalent-aid award. The 2025 federal FAQ explains that State reimbursement of financial social assistance for a non-Belgian person is capped at the relevant article 14 RI amount; that reimbursement ceiling must not be mistaken for a promise that every TP recipient is paid that exact amount. (S01, S08)

### Family benefits in the CPAS means test

Do not use one national statement that child benefit is always ignored or always counted for TP equivalent aid. The federal FAQ says only resources actually available to a TP beneficiary are considered and actual benefits received abroad count; it does not fully resolve every regional child-benefit interaction under the later 2026 resource-rule amendments. For statutory RI from 1 March 2026, Primabook describes a specific case where family benefits paid to a cohabitant for the applicant can be included in that cohabitant-resource calculation, with a €240 monthly proxy if the actual amount attributable to the applicant is not established (subject to a disability-related exception). That RI provision must not be generalized to equivalent social assistance without confirmation from the CPAS. (S01, S09)

## 5. Work, integration, unemployment and other aid

- The federal FAQ says readiness to work is examined for TP equivalent financial aid as in other equivalent-assistance cases; health or equity reasons can justify an exception. Registration with the relevant regional employment service is an initial way to show availability. A PIIS may be used for financial social assistance, and the CPAS may tie aid to agreed steps under the applicable law. (S01)
- In Flanders, an official government page says temporarily displaced Ukrainians must register with VDAB under the regional decree in force since 16 May 2023. That jobseeker-registration rule is separate from whether ONEM unemployment benefits are due. This specific research does not establish identical regional compulsory-registration wording for Brussels, Wallonia and DG. (S16)
- Federal government announced in March 2026 a draft law to start certain protection-subsidiary and TP beneficiaries at 70% of the relevant RI rate, with possible increases tied to integration effort. The parliamentary record later states the bill remained under government discussion after Council of State and CPAS federation opinions. On the research date, do not publish 70% as current law or assume a future integration bonus is available. Check enacted law before publication. (S11–S12)
- TP holders may qualify for unemployment benefits only if they meet the ordinary unemployment insurance conditions; the federal Ukraine FAQ says no special Ukrainian category was created. Registering with VDAB/Actiris/Forem/ADG is not itself an unemployment-benefit award. (S01, S14–S15)
- Housing, deposit, food, energy and material aid are for individualized CPAS assessment and may be provided in different forms. Rent or deposit is not paid automatically by TP status. A qualified homeless person who finds a principal residence may be able to request an installation grant under the conditions described in federal CPAS guidance. (S01, S13)
- Urgent medical aid for people still at the pre-TP stage is a healthcare-specific bridge. It is not financial subsistence, ordinary health-insurance membership or family benefit. Topic 5’s healthcare findings should not be merged into this package. (S01)

### Pending TP, child abroad and residence

For all four systems, distinguish the federal TP grant from a pending request. The reviewed official regional evidence does not establish that a pending TP application alone opens child-benefit entitlement. Flanders’ current instruction explicitly holds a presumed-TP case without National Register entry pending rather than rejecting it; it tells the family to register with the municipality. Brussels requires residence authorization, a National Register number and official Brussels domicile. Wallonia’s TP-specific route is documented against the A card/data flow. DG requires authorized stay over three months and the child’s qualifying residence. (S17–S28)

Do not assume family benefit is payable for a child who remains in Ukraine or lives in another EU country. The child’s actual residence, the parent’s work/residence, applicable EU coordination or international agreements, and the specific regional law can change which country/payer is competent. Flanders and DG have residence-based domestic rules; Brussels and Wallonia also have regional residence requirements. This research does not establish an automatic TP-specific export route for a child in Ukraine. Refer cross-border cases to the regional payer. (S17–S29)

### Application evidence boundary

There is no single Belgian checklist. For an initial enquiry, have the child’s identity and birth information, any TP/residence document, evidence of where the child actually lives, the parent/beneficiary details, household data, and payment details available if possible. These are practical records, not a claim that every item is a statutory precondition in every region. Brussels expressly requires a valid stay and official domicile/Belgian National Register number for the TP route; Wallonia’s current Ukraine guidance identifies A-card data; Flanders uses register data but holds an unregistered suspected-TP case rather than rejecting it; DG law focuses on the child’s residence and authorized stay. Ask the responsible payer what is missing in the individual file. (S17–S29)

## 10. Readiness and drafting boundary

### social-subsistence — READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS

The basic legal scheme, start-stage evidence, territorial competence, means test and current reference amounts are established. Preserve that TP recipients normally seek equivalent social assistance, not statutory RI, and that rate/award is individualized. The 70%-to-100% integration proposal is not current law on the research date. Confirm its legislative status again immediately before publication.

### social-application — READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS

There is a clear federal route: TP attestation, municipal presentation, competent CPAS, dated application, social inquiry and reasoned decision. A pending case is narrower and should be described as the urgent-medical-aid/registration stage, not as a confirmed subsistence benefit. No eID or bank-account barrier is established. Address-of-reference decisions have separate homeless-person requirements.

### social-child-benefits — READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS

Regional TP access can be described separately. Flanders, Brussels and Wallonia have explicit TP material; DG uses its own legal-residence and child-residence rules, without a TP-specific DG instruction found. Keep amounts, start date, registration documents, supplements and cross-border situations conditional on the responsible regional payer.

**Overall:** READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS
**Blocking gaps:** None for a constrained article. Non-blocking issues to verify case by case: enacted status of the proposed federal 70% integration measure; CPAS assessment of family benefits within equivalent-assistance means; documentation while a Walloon A card is delayed; DG-specific TP case handling; children living outside Belgium and EU coordination. These gaps do not prevent a practical article that avoids promises about an individual award or cross-border entitlement.

### Safe to state

- TP grantees who have attended the municipality may apply for equivalent financial social assistance through the competent CPAS; the A card need not already be issued.
- A CPAS must assess the application and actual resources; aid is not automatic.
- RI reference rates are not automatic TP award amounts.
- The four regional child-benefit systems have different residence, status, filing and supplement conditions.
- Jobseeker registration, unemployment insurance, school access, medical support, housing support and family benefits are separate decisions.

### Must qualify

- A TP-pending applicant’s interim route, including the federal FAQ’s urgent-medical-aid wording.
- The CPAS responsible before and after foreigners-register entry, when homeless, or after a move.
- Household category, partner resources, actual foreign income and family-benefit treatment.
- RI category rates, the unpassed 70% proposal, and the three-month initial inquiry period.
- Start date, A-card evidence, registration, supplements, children abroad and retroactivity in each family-benefit region.

### Do not assert

- Every TP holder receives a full RI/leefloon amount, a fixed monthly payment, rent or a deposit.
- A pending TP request, federal attestation alone, school enrolment or a host-family address opens every social or child benefit.
- A card A is required before any CPAS application, or that applicants should apply to any CPAS.
- Child benefit is always excluded from or included in every form of equivalent-aid means test.
- A nationwide child-benefit rule, automatic payment for a child abroad, a universal supplement, or a national 70% TP rate.

## 11. Cross-topic consistency

- Topic 1: federal TP attestation and A card are different documents. Here, the attestation plus municipal-presentation proof supports the equivalent-assistance route; the card is not the same as TP grant.
- Topic 3: regional jobseeker registration and federal unemployment-benefit eligibility are distinct.
- Topic 5: healthcare coverage/urgent medical aid is not CPAS subsistence or family benefit.
- Topic 6: actual residence, registration, hosted accommodation and rent/deposit support are distinct; CPAS assistance does not guarantee rent.
- Topic 7: school enrolment does not establish child-benefit eligibility.
