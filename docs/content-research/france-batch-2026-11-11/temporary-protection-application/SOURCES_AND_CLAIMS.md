# France — Temporary Protection Application

## Scope

Research checked: 2026-11-11

Scope: initial temporary-protection application in France, eligibility and evidence, local intake arrangements, APS issuance and renewal, minors/family, prior protection in another EU Member State, dates, fees, and comparison readiness for `protection-scheme`, `protection-application`, and `protection-dates`.

This is an evidence record, not applicant-facing article text. “National” below means a rule in French law or current central-government guidance. A prefecture instruction is labelled by département and is not generalized nationally. Ordinary residence permits, asylum applications, ADA renewal, and post-temporary-protection routes are treated as separate processes.

## Current official framework

France applies temporary protection through the CESEDA and the EU Temporary Protection framework. Central French guidance describes the Ukraine-related eligible groups and directs applicants to the prefecture for their place of residence or accommodation. The CESEDA supplies the national adult application location, required categories of information and evidence, the six-month APS, and renewal rule. Each prefecture controls its local appointment/intake channel.

Council Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/1912 currently extends the EU protection through **4 March 2028**. From 5 August 2026 its Article 2 military-obligations condition applies to grants, subject to an exception for people who held and continuously retained temporary protection in the same Member State before or on 4 August 2026. This is an EU protection-duration rule; it is not the validity period printed on a particular French APS.

The legal/application baseline is clear enough to describe with qualifications. Two boundaries must remain explicit: new applicants should verify the applicable appointment and evidence checklist with their own prefecture; and the current EU end date should not be converted into a claim about the expiry date printed on an individual APS. The CESEDA’s general three-year wording and the subsequent EU extension are both recorded below rather than silently treated as equivalent dates.

## Procedure

The confirmed national sequence is limited to what the current code and central guidance state:

1. An adult applicant presents to the prefecture for the département of residence; in Paris, to the Police Prefecture. The central Ministry page also describes the place of accommodation as the point of contact. The applicant should first check that prefecture’s current Ukraine/temporary-protection instructions because appointment access varies by département. [FR-TP-C06, FR-TP-C09]
2. The applicant supplies the statutory civil-status and entry information, evidence/information establishing a covered group, four specified photographs, and proof of address. The current EU military-obligations rule and its exception must also be considered for grants from 5 August 2026. [FR-TP-C03, FR-TP-C07]
3. Central guidance says that when the file is complete and admissible, the person is protected and receives an APS; the person is then referred to OFII. The national rule also requires an explanatory TP document in an understood language at the admission request. [FR-TP-C08, FR-TP-C11, FR-TP-C12]

There is no single evidenced nationwide sequence for booking, digital pre-filing, appointment, delivery/collection, or the OFII handoff details. Those practical steps must remain local. The official evidence does not support a universal online pre-registration or a universal walk-in rule. No national processing-time promise was identified. [FR-TP-C09, FR-TP-C13]

## Documents

### Explicitly required nationally

For an adult’s initial application, CESEDA R. 581-1 lists:

- applicant’s civil-status particulars, and where relevant those of spouse and dependent children;
- information about the conditions of entry into France;
- any document or information establishing membership in a covered group;
- four recent, closely resembling, front-facing, bareheaded photographs, 3.5 × 4.5 cm;
- proof of address.

From 5 August 2026, proof of compliance with Ukrainian military obligations must be presented where applicable under the EU Decision. The EU Decision does not specify the accepted proof format. [FR-TP-S03, FR-TP-S11]

### Requested or specified by particular prefectures

- **Ille-et-Vilaine:** appointment; originals and copies; valid passport pages/identity and nationality evidence; proof related to military obligations (including both men and women in that local checklist); recent proof of address; one identity photograph; local APS request form. Local renewal appointment and filing window also published. [FR-TP-S14]
- **Seine-Saint-Denis:** online appointment form for first issue from 7 September 2026. This describes that department’s booking method. [FR-TP-S15]
- **Alpes-Maritimes:** a scheduled weekend monthly reception without appointment, with the page requiring a complete dossier; its dates and reception arrangement are locally announced. [FR-TP-S16]

These local lists should not be combined into a France-wide checklist. In particular, proof of a valid passport in one department is not evidence that the national law removes R. 581-1’s broader “document or information” wording.

There is a local/national checklist discrepancy to preserve: CESEDA R. 581-1 lists four photographs, while the Ille-et-Vilaine page lists one. The statutory national list is recorded above; applicants should verify the current local checklist before attending rather than treating the local page as an amendment to the code. [FR-TP-S03, FR-TP-S14]

### No passport / missing documents

Interior FAQ lists a passport or national identity card valid or expired less than two years, or a Ukrainian consular certificate, as possible nationality proof. It lists passport, identity card, or other civil-status document such as a family record book for civil-status evidence. The code also allows “any document or information” that evidences eligibility. The reviewed official evidence does **not** establish a complete no-document route, guarantee an oral-only process, or say what happens when none of these forms of evidence can be obtained. [FR-TP-S02, FR-TP-S03]

## Minors / family members

French central guidance lists a spouse, unmarried minor children, and dependent parents as family members, and says the applicant should attend with family members (spouse and children). CESEDA R. 581-2 defines a child in the relevant procedures by legally established filiation or adoption; a foreign adoption is subject to a regularity check. [FR-TP-S01, FR-TP-S04]

The evidence reviewed does not define a complete separate form, guardian/consent, or attendance procedure for accompanied minors, nor a current procedure for unaccompanied minors. Do not claim that every child must independently submit the adult document set. Dependent-parent eligibility is stated in the central Ministry guidance; no detailed proof standard for dependency was found. [FR-TP-S01, FR-TP-S03]

## Previous temporary protection in another EU country

CESEDA says a French provisional document may be refused where the applicant is already authorized to reside under a TP document in another Member State, unless the person qualifies under the family provision. The code provides a formal inter-state transfer pathway; the transfer request is referred by the competent authorities of the Member State where the person resides to the French immigration minister. It is not evidence of a right to submit a second independent national application while holding another State’s TP document. [FR-TP-S05, FR-TP-S09]

The law reviewed does not clearly settle a person’s case where the other-State TP has expired or ended and no current document is held. Nor does it specify what evidence each prefecture uses to establish a past grant. Those cases remain unresolved; do not infer a blanket bar or automatic eligibility from “previously had TP”.

## Dates and renewal

### EU protection duration

Council Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/1912 sets the current EU extension through **4 March 2028**. Its Article 2 military condition applies from **5 August 2026**; the exception is for people protected in the same Member State by 4 August 2026 and continuously retaining that status there. [FR-TP-S11]

### French APS validity

The French provisional stay document is an APS marked “bénéficiaire de la protection temporaire”, valid for six months. It may include provisional work authorization and permits work. CESEDA says the APS renews automatically for the period that TP remains in force, although the APS itself can be limited to the remaining protection period. [FR-TP-S01, FR-TP-S07]

### Renewal process and fee

National code supplies the automatic-renewal rule. In practice, prefectures publish different local appointment and document-return steps; follow the local prefecture page rather than assuming one national renewal portal or appointment window. For the APS tax, the first issue and first renewal are exempt; the €100 tax applies to subsequent issue/renewal under the 2026 Finance Law. [FR-TP-S07, FR-TP-S12, FR-TP-S13, FR-TP-S14, FR-TP-S16]

The general CESEDA text says one-year protection periods renewable within a maximum of three years, while the later EU decision extends the active Ukraine scheme to 4 March 2028. Current departmental government guidance also reflects the 2028 date. The materials reviewed do not explain this interaction at the level of a specific person’s existing APS or prove that an already printed document needs no further action. Keep the EU framework date and individual APS validity separate; verify the person’s current APS and prefecture instructions. [FR-TP-S05, FR-TP-S07, FR-TP-S11, FR-TP-S14]

Evidence: EU temporary protection through 4 March 2028 and six-month French APS validity/automatic renewal during the protection period are explicit in the current EU decision and CESEDA. [FR-TP-S07, FR-TP-S11]

Qualification: the CESEDA’s general three-year wording and the later EU extension are not reconciled in the reviewed materials at individual-document level. Do not say that each existing APS is itself valid until 4 March 2028 or that no holder action is needed. Use the EU framework date, the APS’s printed validity, and current prefecture instructions as distinct facts.

## Open gaps

- Nationally accepted evidence format for the military-obligations condition; current local instructions identify Reserv+ evidence, but that is not a published nationwide document standard.
- Full intake/post-border workflow after submission, including how an applicant receives or collects the APS, beyond the central “complete and admissible” statement and local examples.
- No official processing-time standard identified.
- Complete procedure where the applicant has no passport and none of the listed alternative documents or evidence.
- Detailed procedure for accompanied and unaccompanied minors, guardianship/consent and whether a child must attend.
- Treatment of someone whose prior TP in another EU State ended and who no longer holds a current permit; required proof of prior status.
- A national renewal appointment/checklist, as prefecture practice differs.
- How a prefecture reconciles the four photographs listed in CESEDA R. 581-1 with local checklists that state one photograph.
- How the CESEDA three-year wording, EU extension to 2028, and an individual APS’s printed expiry/action requirements interact in each case.
- Whether any French-wide post-TP transition process will exist; no future scheme is inferred from the EU transition recommendation or ordinary permit options.

## Editorial exclusions

Without additional official evidence, a future article must not claim:

- that all prefectures accept walk-ins, require an online appointment, or use one national portal;
- a guaranteed number of days for review or APS issuance;
- that one prefecture’s requested documents are required nationwide;
- that a person with no identity/civil-status or eligibility evidence is guaranteed an alternative procedure;
- a fixed guardian/attendance/form procedure for every minor, or an unsupported unaccompanied-minor process;
- that everyone who ever held temporary protection in another EU country is automatically barred, or automatically eligible after moving to France;
- that a six-month APS and the EU scheme end date are the same, or that an already-issued APS automatically proves permission through 4 March 2028 without checking its validity and local instructions;
- an unqualified resolution of the CESEDA three-year wording against the later EU extension;
- that APS is free on every renewal: the first issuance and first renewal are exempt, later renewals are subject to the €100 tax;
- that asylum, an ordinary residence permit, renewal of ADA, or a future post-TP status is part of initial TP application.
