## Research verdict

**READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS.** National rules and current official procedures support practical guidance on public-school access and enrolment, newcomer assessment and language support, and higher-education admission/qualification recognition. The article must keep local assignment distinct from the right to education, and must explain that higher-education entry, credit placement, fees and aid are separate decisions. In particular, the 2026/27 fee framework does not expressly list temporary protection among automatic domestic-fee classifications; individual fee treatment must be confirmed with the institution. Current Ukrainian TP-specific higher-education information exists for 2026, but no current Ukraine-specific school enrolment procedure or online-school equivalence was established.

## Required distinctions

- The right to education is not a place in a preferred school.
- School enrolment is not itself a decision about class/year placement.
- A residence permit is not the legal condition for a foreign child’s school access; ordinary identity, address, guardian and health-admission documents are separate issues.
- Missing Ukrainian school records do not erase the published newcomer assessment process; however, the exact local process and any provisional admission must be confirmed with the responsible school/authority.
- UPE2A/CASNAV framework support is not a guaranteed fixed number of individual hours.
- French school attendance and Ukrainian online schooling are not shown to be interchangeable.
- Higher-education application is not admission.
- ENIC-NARIC comparability is not a binding admission or credit-transfer decision.
- Prior university study recognition/credit transfer is institution-specific.
- Admission is separate from tuition classification, CVEC, scholarship and housing.
- Temporary protection is not shown to confer automatic university admission, domestic fees or a scholarship.
- National rules do not make academy, commune or university implementation identical.

## School enrolment

### Compulsory-age boundary

Children are subject to compulsory instruction from 3 through 16; instruction can be in school or, with authorization, in the family. At 16–18 there is a separate obligation to be in a recognized formation/training, work, apprenticeship, service or support situation; this is not simply compulsory school attendance to 18 (S01).

### Primary / école maternelle and élémentaire

For first installation in France, the usual published route is mairie registration, then admission at the school named on the mairie certificate (S02). The mairie’s primary checklist includes a document establishing the child’s and responsible adult’s identity and recent address (Service-Public says an attestation sur l’honneur may be used for address in the stated primary process); final admission requires the mairie certificate and age-required vaccination evidence or recognized contraindication (S02, S10). Ancillary services such as canteen may request additional local material (S02, S11).

For primary pupils aged six and over arriving from abroad, school skills are assessed to guide class placement. The assessment considers French and other language skills, literacy, and prior curricular learning such as mathematics. The mairie issues the registration certificate with the school once assessment results are known; contact the DSDEN if registration is difficult (S02). The school selected by the commune/sector is not necessarily the family’s preferred school (S12).

### Collège / lycée

For a first installation, contact the CASNAV serving the family’s residence. The pupil’s academic history is analysed and an educational assessment organized; results go to academy services, which assign a school based on skills and nearby places. Contact the assigned collège/lycée for the local document checklist; Service-Public explicitly says documents vary by rectorat (S03, S08–S09). A school outside sector requires local DSDEN contact. Age, previous schooling and current skills guide class placement; 16+ youth may still be received, although the compulsory-school attendance duty has ended (S03).

### Missing documents and mid-year arrival

A residence permit cannot be a condition for school registration of a foreign pupil (S04). For a child of compulsory instruction age, the official primary-school model rules provide provisional admission when one or more of the specified admission documents are missing; school staff do not verify immigration status (S05). Primary and secondary newcomer guidance also has an assessment process that can examine prior skills rather than requiring a French grade equivalence certificate in advance (S02–S03, S06). The reviewed sources do not set one exhaustive national alternative-document package for every missing passport, civil-status certificate, vaccination record or Ukrainian transcript. Contact the mairie/school or local CASNAV/DSDEN and provide available evidence.

The right and obligation of instruction do not end because the family arrives after the start of a school year; use the local route promptly. However, no national source promises a same-day place or a chosen school. Assignment depends on the responsible local authorities and, for secondary schooling, nearby available places (S01–S03).

### Ukrainian online schooling

No current French official source reviewed says that Ukrainian online education by itself substitutes for French compulsory instruction or establishes a TP-specific dual-enrolment scheme. Treat this as **not established**; do not cite 2022 emergency communications as current law. If a family seeks home instruction in France, the ordinary authorization framework applies (S01, S26).

### Costs boundary

Public education access does not mean every associated cost is waived. Primary school meal tariffs are municipal and may be income-adjusted; local assistance may exist. School materials, activities and transport also involve separate local/service arrangements (S11–S12). No exhaustive costs or local subsidies are assessed here.

## Language support

The national EANA framework centres on inclusion in an ordinary class, with French taught as a language of schooling (FLS). UPE2A can provide additional intensive or adapted support where available. Newcomers receive a needs-based language and learning assessment; UPE2A time is variable/revisable and balanced with ordinary-class participation (S02–S08).

CASNAV is an academic expertise/resource service supporting rectorat/DSDEN, schools and newcomer assessment; the particular contact channel and evaluation steps are academy-specific (S03, S08–S09). The national sources do not promise an individual teacher, fixed hours, a universal duration, a current Ukraine-only programme, or a professional interpreter for every family. Current school information should be requested from the local school/academy; the official newcomer framework says information should be clear and, where possible, translated (S06–S07).

## Higher education

### Admission

For 2026, the official Ukraine student page says Ukrainian TP applicants should contact their chosen higher-education institution directly and explain their situation; the institution can then direct them to CROUS for relevant support (S17). This is a Ukraine-specific current guidance route, not a guaranteed place or universal exemption from programme admission criteria.

Ordinarily, many first-year public higher-education courses use Parcoursup, while certain international first-year university applicants are subject to DAP and some programmes have direct institution admission (S13–S18). Master, transfer and other non-Parcoursup routes depend on the level/programme and institution. Confirm the application channel with the chosen institution; do not treat Parcoursup as the authority that makes the final admission decision.

### Ukrainian qualification recognition and prior study

ENIC-NARIC France offers TP beneficiaries priority and free handling of comparability requests upon proof of status, and flexibility for incomplete dossiers. It may refer applicants who lack academic documents to EQPR (S19). A comparability attestation is an expert aid to understand the level of a foreign qualification, not a binding admission decision; the receiving institution decides admission (S19–S21). Recognition of a period of study may document years validated, but no automatic transfer of credits or advanced-year entry was found. Ask each university to assess transcripts, syllabi and other evidence.

### Language

Some programmes or DAP routes may require French-language evidence or a test, subject to the route’s own exceptions (S16, S18). TP does not appear in the reviewed national exemption list. Confirm requirements and accepted proof with the specific institution/programme; do not promise a blanket exemption.

### Fees, CVEC and support for 2026/27

The Ministry’s current 2026/27 differentiated-fee FAQ, published 2 September 2026 after Decree 2026-385, expressly places refugees and beneficiaries of subsidiary protection (and their children) in a domestic-fee category; the listed category does not name TP. It also provides for institution-level individual exemptions based on personal circumstances under current caps (S22). Thus TP alone is not verified as an automatic domestic-fee classification or a universal tuition waiver. At the same time, do not tell every TP student they necessarily owe the differentiated rate: the institution must classify the person’s full residence/status, course and exemption circumstances. University tuition and CVEC are distinct charges (S22–S23).

For Ukrainian TP students, current Etudiant.gouv guidance says a scholarship notification confers CVEC exemption, describes the special CVEC treatment for TP students in FLE courses, and directs students to DSE/CROUS. The 2026/27 means-tested scholarship rules include temporary protection as an eligible status category subject to programme, financial and other conditions (S17, S24–S25). Emergency support can be requested through CROUS social services; an emergency grant or housing is individually assessed/subject to capacity, not guaranteed (S17, S22).

## Editorial exclusions

Future content must not claim without additional evidence that:

- TP guarantees a place at a chosen school or university;
- a child needs a physical French residence card before school registration;
- missing Ukrainian civil or school documents always block enrolment;
- Ukrainian online schooling automatically replaces the French instruction obligation;
- every child receives a dedicated UPE2A teacher or a fixed number of weekly hours;
- every family receives an interpreter;
- a local CASNAV/commune procedure applies nationwide;
- Parcoursup is used by every applicant or itself guarantees admission;
- a Ukrainian diploma is automatically equivalent to a French diploma;
- previous credits automatically transfer or guarantee advanced-year entry;
- TP removes French-language admission requirements;
- all Ukrainian TP students automatically pay domestic/EU fees or receive a full tuition waiver;
- CVEC exemption automatically means tuition exemption;
- TP automatically awards a CROUS scholarship, emergency payment or room;
- public school is free in every related service and cost.

## Scope limitations

This research does not cover detailed childcare, every school benefit, full school transport tariffs, social benefits, regulated-profession recognition, housing, residence transitions, or student visa rules unrelated to TP. Local school procedures and individual university decisions require current confirmation in the relevant commune, academy or institution.
