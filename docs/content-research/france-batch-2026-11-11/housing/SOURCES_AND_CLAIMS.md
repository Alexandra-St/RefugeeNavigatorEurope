# France — Housing: Initial Accommodation, Private Rental and Financial Support

Research checked: 2026-11-11

Scope: exactly `housing-initial`, `housing-private-rental`, `housing-financial-support`.

Baseline: `67be1d2351241dfebf637c919cbf83c5c74e6bf0`.

This is a source-and-claims research record, not article copy. “Temporary protection” (TP) here refers to the French temporary residence authorisation bearing the mention “bénéficiaire de la protection temporaire” (APS-BPT), principally documented in the reviewed France-specific sources for people displaced from Ukraine. Findings about APS-BPT should not be generalized to other residence statuses or to a different future TP cohort without checking the applicable rules.

## Research verdict

**NOT READY — MATERIAL EVIDENCE GAP.** Official evidence supports safe guidance on ordinary private rental and on several housing-support routes, with qualification. However, current national guidance does not establish a practically usable, nationally consistent initial State-accommodation request channel for a newly arrived TP beneficiary who has no housing. A current Haute-Garonne page describes a local orientation offer, but that is departmental implementation and does not establish a nationwide intake, placement guarantee, allocation criteria, duration, or re-entry route. General emergency homelessness services give an immediate fallback contact, but calling 115 is not a guaranteed placement and does not resolve the TP-specific State-accommodation gap.

## Required distinctions

- TP residence permission does not itself establish an automatic State-accommodation entitlement.
- State-supported emergency or temporary accommodation is not an ordinary private tenancy.
- The 115 emergency homelessness route is not a guaranteed bed, a long-term housing route, or the TP-specific placement system.
- Private rental is separate from social housing and from temporary State accommodation.
- A social-housing application (including one documented with APS-BPT) is not an allocation or a guarantee of priority.
- CAF housing aid may reduce eligible costs; it is not a rent guarantee or necessarily full rent payment.
- APL/ALF/ALS are distinct eligibility routes, not interchangeable labels.
- Visale secures qualifying rent obligations for the landlord; it is not a housing benefit paid to the beneficiary.
- FSL is assessed locally under departmental criteria; it is not an automatic national entitlement.
- A private-host arrangement is not automatically a tenancy, and no current national Ukraine hosting/payment scheme was confirmed here.
- A prefecture’s local process, including Haute-Garonne’s, is not a nationwide rule.
- General asylum accommodation pathways are not evidence of TP accommodation access and have not been used to fill the gap.

## Initial accommodation

### Current model and who handles it

The strongest current TP-specific operational evidence located is the Haute-Garonne Prefecture page (S02, updated 2026-09-09). It describes a local welcome process that can orient people who need it toward emergency accommodation and then temporary housing. Listed forms include collective places managed by associations funded by the State or local authorities, citizen-solidarity accommodation accompanied by an association, and social support toward/in housing. The prefecture gathers individual and collective housing offers through local authorities, especially Toulouse Métropole.

That page does not state how a newly arrived person makes a first housing request, where to report, which agency formally assesses need, how offers are prioritized, or whether a bed is available. It does not set placement duration, contributions, location choice, continuation, exit, or re-entry rules. No unified national TP accommodation intake was verified. Do not state that prefecture, DDETS, OFII, or a single operator currently controls every department on the basis of the evidence collected here.

### Practical route supported by evidence

1. **If there is no safe place to sleep now:** call **115** (Samu social), free and available 24/7, for emergency assessment/orientation (S01). This is the general homelessness route. It may lead to emergency accommodation if a suitable place is available; the call does not guarantee placement.
2. **For a TP-specific offer:** check the official prefecture/departmental Ukraine information for the department where the person is located and ask for the current local accommodation referral channel. Haute-Garonne currently describes a local orientation model (S02), but that exact implementation must not be copied for another department.
3. **If the person has no stable address:** ask the local CCAS/CIAS or prefecture-approved organisation about domiciliation (S22). This provides an address for correspondence and access to rights, not accommodation.

### Availability, location, duration, and continuation

Current nationwide rules for availability, selection, household/vulnerability prioritization, choice of city, initial stay length, contribution, transition after initial placement, or re-request after independent living were **not established**. Obtain those details from the local authority/operator before relying on any placement. Do not carry forward the Ministry’s 2022 emergency “sas” (one or two nights), several-month transition accommodation, or three-month housing stages as 2026 rules (S03 is legacy).

### Current Ukraine-specific hosting policy

No current national official source confirming an open Ukraine-specific hosting scheme, current host payment, beneficiary payment, or standard tenancy status was located. The 2022 Ministry FAQ’s citizen-offer matching and three-stage model are historical. A local authority may have a current programme; it must be identified and dated locally before describing it.

## Private rental

### Contract and move-in

For a principal residence, use a written lease that includes the required information; the statutory lease models were updated for leases signed from 1 October 2026 (S12). At handover, make and keep the written entry condition report attached to the lease (S13). At a high level, unfurnished leases generally have a three-year term and furnished leases a one-year term (nine months for a student), with separate exceptions and mobility-lease rules (S14).

### Deposit and rent payment

For a qualifying principal-home lease, the deposit is generally capped at one month’s rent excluding charges for unfurnished housing and two months for furnished housing; mobility leases prohibit a deposit. Payment frequency can affect whether a deposit may be demanded (S14–S15). A deposit is separate from rent, cannot be treated as the last month’s payment, and is normally returned within one month if the exit condition report matches or up to two months if it differs, subject to justified deductions (S15–S16).

Rent payment timing is set by the lease. If another periodicity is agreed, a tenant may request monthly payment. Do not describe advance rent as a way to evade statutory deposit caps. No money may be demanded merely to reserve a home before signing under the official tenant-file guidance (S11); the review does not try to enumerate every exceptional lease arrangement.

### Rental application file and TP documents

The national list limits what a landlord can request for identity, address, work status, income, and guarantor information (S11). A valid foreign passport is explicitly accepted as identity evidence; the page also lists certain residence cards but does not expressly list APS-BPT. Use only the documents actually applicable to the applicant; do not claim a French social-security number or private bank statement is required. The review does not establish that a landlord may insist on a permanent residence card, nor that APS-BPT alone is expressly listed in this closed list.

Origin and nationality are prohibited grounds for selecting tenants; objective financial criteria are allowed (S17). This does not guarantee acceptance or make every rejection unlawful.

### Guarantor and Visale

A landlord may assess a guarantor using the permitted guarantor-document list (S11). Visale is Action Logement’s free guarantee, not a grant to the tenant. In 2026 the scheme has categories involving age (including qualifying young people), employment and mobility, lease type, rent ceilings and pre-lease certification (S20–S21). The reviewed official sources do not directly establish that APS-BPT alone qualifies. Check eligibility and secure the visa before signing; no automatic eligibility should be promised.

### Rent controls

France does not have one identical rent-level cap covering all homes. Rent-change controls apply in designated tense areas; stricter reference-rent ceilings apply only in listed cities and territories. Check the property’s commune using the official checker and the current local rules (S18, S24). An annual IRL revision is subject to the lease and statutory conditions (S19); avoid quoting stale index figures.

## Financial support

### CAF housing aid: APL / ALF / ALS

Potential access is supported, but not automatic. National APL guidance requires, among other things, a valid stay document for a foreign applicant, eligible principal-residence housing and resource conditions; the APL itself applies to conventioned housing. Caf Yvelines specifically says a Ukrainian APS-BPT holder may receive personal housing aid (AL/APL) subject to criteria (S05–S06). APL, ALF and ALS have different bases (S08). Application is through Caf or MSA depending on the person’s social-insurance scheme (S09). The 2026 national Caf professional guide is relevant evidence that current TP guidance exists, but the linked file could not be read in this check (S07); detailed rules should be confirmed directly with Caf/MSA. No amount or automatic payment is established.

### Visale

Visale may help an eligible tenant provide a landlord guarantee, but is not a tenant payment. Eligibility is assessed under current age, employment/mobility, lease and rent-ceiling rules, and the required visa must be obtained in time (S20–S21). APS-BPT-specific eligibility remains unconfirmed in the reviewed evidence.

### FSL

Contact a social worker, Caf/MSA if already served there, or local mairie/CCAS or departmental service to check the relevant FSL and request process (S10). Depending on the département and individual assessment, a loan or grant may support a deposit, first rent, moving or agency costs, insurance, essential equipment, rent arrears, or utilities. Criteria, amounts and decision-making vary by département; no TP-specific automatic eligibility is established.

### Social housing

The national HLM application page expressly lists APS-BPT among accepted residence documents and allows online or paper application (S04). Applicants must document resources and meet applicable conditions; the waiting period depends in part on location. Homelessness and other circumstances may be relevant to priority assessment, but TP alone is not shown as automatic priority. Apply through the official national housing-demand service linked from S04; do not promise an allocation.

### Ukraine-specific support

No current national Ukraine-specific host or housing payment was confirmed. The old Ministry FAQ and Caf crisis page (S03, S23) are legacy evidence only. Potential ordinary Caf support for a Ukrainian APS-BPT holder is addressed above and remains subject to assessment; it is not a special guaranteed housing payment.

### Address / domiciliation

A person without a stable address may request domiciliation from a CCAS/CIAS or prefecture-approved organisation, or the mairie where applicable, subject to a link with the commune (S22). It provides a stable correspondence address and may facilitate access to benefits; it does not provide a dwelling or prove housing-aid eligibility by itself.

## Editorial exclusions

Future content must not state without additional evidence that:

- France automatically houses every TP beneficiary or guarantees an immediate bed;
- a new arrival can choose the city/region or receive a known standard stay;
- calling 115 guarantees accommodation;
- the 2022 Ukraine “sas”, three-stage placement, or host-matching policy is still current;
- every prefecture follows the Haute-Garonne process;
- TP automatically gives social housing or priority;
- a social-housing application guarantees allocation;
- APL/ALF/ALS starts automatically or pays all rent;
- every TP holder qualifies for Visale;
- FSL is identical across départements or automatically payable;
- a landlord can demand any document, a social-security number, or a permanent residence card;
- a bank statement or French bank account is a universal legal rental requirement;
- a deposit can be any amount or replaces final rent;
- rent-level caps are identical throughout France;
- a local prefecture rule applies nationwide;
- a private-host arrangement is automatically a tenancy or receives current national compensation.
