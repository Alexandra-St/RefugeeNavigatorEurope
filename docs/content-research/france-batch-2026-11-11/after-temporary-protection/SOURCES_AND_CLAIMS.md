# France — Residence Options During and After Temporary Protection

Research checked: 2026-11-11
Baseline: `ffec8b92f55739c7afd085ba5deb6588a14bb238`
Scope: exactly `residence-routes`, `residence-conditions`, `residence-filing`.

This is an evidence record, not applicant-facing article text. “TP holder” below means a person currently holding a French APS marked as a beneficiary of temporary protection (APS-BPT / APS « protection temporaire »). National rules and local prefecture procedures are distinguished. The local change-of-status instructions cited here establish what a named prefecture accepts; they do not establish one uniform process for every département.

## Research verdict

**NOT READY — MATERIAL EVIDENCE GAP** for practical, France-wide transition advice to a current TP holder.

Ordinary residence routes exist and their principal eligibility rules are documented. More importantly, several prefectures publish specific procedures for TP holders to seek ordinary residence permits in France. Seine-Maritime, for example, identifies routes including salarié, travailleur temporaire, étudiant, entrepreneur/profession libérale and family grounds; it accepts a dossier by local post, a department-specific online form, or at an APS renewal appointment, says ANEF cannot be used for this APS procedure, and instructs applicants to keep renewing the APS while the ordinary-title application is examined [FR-RES-S21–S23]. Essonne also publishes a TP-specific change-of-status guide [FR-RES-S24]. This confirms that in-country transition is a real administrative practice in at least some departments. It does not establish a nationally uniform entitlement, channel, list of routes, or treatment of every case.

The critical national gap is the interaction between the general long-stay-visa rule, temporary-protection APS, and different prefecture procedures. CESEDA L. 412-1 states the general first-issue visa rule; L. 412-2 lists specific exceptions and L. 412-3 permits discretionary exceptions for certain categories. Some prefecture Ukraine pages say the APS counts as a visa exemption for their local process, while national sources do not spell out one APS-specific change-of-status procedure [FR-RES-S01–S03, FR-RES-S21]. No official national source reviewed states a blanket right for every TP holder to convert every ordinary route from within France.

For pending cases, a key local protection is documented: in Seine-Maritime, applicants must continue to renew their APS under the existing procedure while the ordinary application is examined. The change-of-status application itself is not described as extending the APS. National ANEF guidance also distinguishes a non-status-confirming deposit receipt from a first-application extension certificate, and says that a first-application extension certificate does not authorize work unless work authorization has been obtained. It does not establish that the APS-specific local process automatically produces that certificate [FR-RES-S21–S22, FR-RES-S19].

## Required special findings

### Employment transition

The APS-BPT authorizes work for its validity period [FR-RES-S08–S09, S26]. It is not an ordinary salarié or travailleur temporaire card. Local TP-specific change procedures identify those routes and require the applicant to satisfy ordinary conditions; Seine-Maritime asks for applicable work-authorization/contract documents and directs the applicant to the department-specific channel [FR-RES-S21–S22]. National Service-Public employee guidance explains ordinary work authorization and status-change rules but does not identify a single APS-BPT nationwide application path [FR-RES-S07–S08]. Thus: **TP → salarié/travailleur temporaire in-country is confirmed as an available local administrative procedure in named departments, but not established as a uniform national entitlement or channel.** An approval produces the selected ordinary residence card; before approval, retain/renew APS where the local procedure requires it.

Talent is a distinct route, not a synonym for salarié. Eligibility depends on a specified talent category; some qualified/talent categories do not require employer work authorization, but generic filing guidance for in-France applicants is framed around visa or residence-title holders. An APS-specific nationwide transition is **Not established** [FR-RES-S11, S24].

### Study transition

Student status requires more than admission: the official initial route normally uses a student VLS-TS and requires enrolment plus resources; L. 412-3 allows the administration to waive the visa requirement for an L. 422-1 student card, but does not create an automatic waiver [FR-RES-S03, S10]. Some prefectures expressly include TP holders in a local in-country change procedure [FR-RES-S21, S24]. **TP → student is therefore locally documented, while nationwide APS-specific eligibility and a uniform channel are Not established.** Do not equate enrolment with a status change.

### Family transition

The parent of a minor French child who lives in France may receive a one-year VPF card when the parent effectively contributes to maintenance and education since birth or for at least two years; that statutory route expressly removes the long-visa requirement [FR-RES-S05]. The personal/family-ties route under L. 423-23 also expressly removes that requirement but depends on a proportionality assessment [FR-RES-S06]. A French spouse must meet marriage/community and registration conditions; the ordinary statutory spouse route is not among the enumerated L. 412-2 visa exceptions [FR-RES-S02, S04]. Seine-Maritime’s local TP change route includes French-spouse and French-child grounds [FR-RES-S21]. **These are fact-specific routes; none creates a general family-based TP transition right.**

Ordinary family reunification should not be conflated with joining family already in France or with TP family unity. A sponsor’s required status and residence period are specified; the “family already in France” procedure allows some APS periods into the residence-duration calculation but requires a qualifying one-year-or-longer sponsor card or a specified renewal receipt at filing [FR-RES-S17–S18]. APS alone does not satisfy the stated sponsor-card requirement.

### Entrepreneur transition

The entrepreneur/profession libérale card requires a principal and registered activity that is economically viable and yields sufficient means, with supporting documents depending on the activity [FR-RES-S12]. The national page asks for a long-stay visa or valid residence document and directs the applicant to the prefecture. Seine-Maritime names this route in its APS-holder change process [FR-RES-S21]. **Local in-country filing is confirmed there; nationwide APS-as-qualifying-document treatment is Not established.**

### Long-term residence

The 10-year resident card may be available through separate family, service-to-France or international-protection grounds [FR-RES-S16]. The long-term EU card generally requires five uninterrupted years under a temporary/pluriannual residence card or resident card, plus stable sufficient resources and health insurance [FR-RES-S14]. Service-Public lists qualifying card categories and does not list APS-BPT [FR-RES-S15]. **Whether time solely under temporary protection APS counts toward the five-year period is Not established; do not count it based on lawful stay alone.** The national framework reviewed does not establish a direct five-year TP-to-permanent route.

### International protection

Asylum is a separate protection procedure. A TP beneficiary may request asylum. During examination they remain under the TP regime, but TP and asylum-seeker status cannot be held simultaneously. If asylum is refused, TP continues as long as it remains in force [FR-RES-S20]. This is not an ordinary immigration change of status.

### Special post-TP transition scheme

No automatic conversion, dedicated national French post-TP permit, or nationally announced future transition scheme was located in the official national sources reviewed by 2026-11-11. This is a bounded research finding, not proof that no unreviewed departmental instruction exists. The documented prefecture procedures are ordinary, eligibility-based change-of-status processes while TP remains active; they are not a post-TP amnesty or automatic scheme [FR-RES-S01–S03, S20–S26]. EU-level recommendations or the EU temporary-protection end date do not themselves create an individual French residence permit.

### Pending application / bridging rights

**Local confirmed rule:** Seine-Maritime instructs a TP holder to continue renewing the APS under the current procedure while an ordinary-title application is examined [FR-RES-S21]. In that department, if the ordinary-title conditions are not met, its page says the person continues to benefit from TP while that protection remains in force. This supports continuity through renewal of the existing TP document, not an extension generated by filing the ordinary application.

**National document rules:** ANEF's deposit confirmation is not proof of regular stay. A first-application extension certificate, when issued as described by DGEF, can prove regular stay alongside the expired document, but it does not itself authorize work absent work authorization. For a renewal, an extension certificate maintains rights associated with the previous title, including work rights where applicable. The DGEF page does not establish that a TP holder using an ordinary local change route will receive a first-application extension certificate or that it replaces renewal of the APS [FR-RES-S19].

**Verdict:** no general nationwide bridge from ordinary filing has been established. For named local TP procedures, keep the APS valid and renew it as directed. The effect of a pending ordinary application after APS expiry, and any right to work under a separate pending-case certificate, is **Not established nationally**. Do not rely on a generic ANEF receipt.

## Cross-route conflicts and limits

- **Visa vs in-country filing:** CESEDA L. 412-1 is the general first-issue rule; L. 412-2 contains specific exceptions and L. 412-3 a discretionary student/trainee exception. Neither names all APS-BPT applicants as a general visa-exempt class [FR-RES-S01–S03]. Seine-Maritime's locally published Ukraine procedure says APS counts as a visa exemption for the local transition process [FR-RES-S21]. Preserve both facts; do not universalize that departmental interpretation.
- **APS vs ordinary title:** the government describes the Ukraine APS as a six-month renewable provisional document with specific rights. Its work permission does not make it an ordinary residence card [FR-RES-S08–S09, S26].
- **First application vs renewal:** the national ANEF guidance gives different effects to first-application extension certificates, renewal extension certificates and mere deposit confirmations [FR-RES-S19]. Do not apply renewal protections automatically to a first ordinary application from TP.
- **Work authorization vs residence permission:** work eligibility under TP, a separate employer work authorization, and a salarié/travailleur temporaire residence title are distinct questions [FR-RES-S07–S09, S21].
- **Generic lawful stay vs express TP eligibility:** several national generic service pages describe an in-France process for people holding a visa or titre de séjour but do not say whether APS-BPT qualifies. Use a TP-specific prefecture page for that conclusion [FR-RES-S10–S12, S21–S24].
- **ANEF submission vs right to change status:** ANEF provides many application services, but channel availability is not substantive eligibility or a bridge. A local TP procedure may use post, a dedicated form, or a prefecture appointment and may explicitly exclude ANEF [FR-RES-S19, S21–S24].
- **Refusal and continued TP:** the Seine-Maritime instruction says to renew APS during review and that a refused ordinary-title application leaves TP in place while protection is in force. This is local guidance; the document's printed validity/renewal still matters. Do not promise this nationwide without checking the responsible prefecture [FR-RES-S21].
- **Long-term time:** L. 426-17 describes qualifying years under residence cards; the reviewed national sources do not explicitly resolve APS-BPT counting. Mark it **Not established** [FR-RES-S14–S15].

## Readiness per aspect

### `residence-routes`

**Status: Ready with qualification**

**Evidence:** Ordinary routes are identified, and named prefectures provide specific in-country change-of-status procedures for TP holders, including employment, study, entrepreneur and family routes [FR-RES-S04–S13, S21–S24]. Official DGEF data also show former TP beneficiaries moving into ordinary titles in practice [FR-RES-S25].

**Qualification:** This is not a unified national transition scheme. Actual route and application path depend on the person’s facts and prefecture.

### `residence-conditions`

**Status: Ready with qualification**

**Evidence:** Official statutes and Service-Public pages establish the main route-specific conditions and distinguish ordinary permits from TP APS [FR-RES-S01–S20, S26].

**Qualification:** Each route needs case-specific assessment; Talent subclasses, family facts, work authorization and possible agreements tied to nationality can change the result. TP time toward long-term residence remains unresolved.

### `residence-filing`

**Status: Not ready**

**Evidence:** In-country APS-holder filing is directly confirmed for named departments, and the Seine-Maritime guide describes channels and requires APS renewal during review [FR-RES-S21–S24]. National guidance distinguishes document effects but does not establish a nationwide TP-specific change procedure or bridge after APS expiry [FR-RES-S19].

**Qualification:** Before giving practical filing advice, the responsible prefecture’s current instructions must be checked for the selected permit, accepted document, filing channel, deadline, and whether APS renewal is required. Nationally, the effect of pending filing on lawful stay/work rights after APS expiry is not established.

## Open gaps

- National rule and uniform procedure for APS-BPT → salarié/travailleur temporaire remain **Not established**; local procedures confirm availability only in named departments.
- APS-BPT → student is locally confirmed in some instructions; nationwide filing eligibility and ANEF compatibility remain **Not established**.
- APS-BPT → family/private-life routes depend on specific legal grounds. The parent-French-child and L. 423-23 visa exceptions are clear; uniform APS filing mechanics, especially for spouse of French national, remain **Not established**.
- APS-BPT → entrepreneur/profession libérale is locally confirmed; nationwide APS acceptance under the national document requirement remains **Not established**.
- Need to leave France: not a nationwide conclusion. Some routes ordinarily use a consular VLS, but local TP change procedures are documented and may waive/dispense with that condition. Check route and department.
- Pending ordinary application’s effect after APS expiry: **Not established nationally**; Seine-Maritime instructs continued APS renewal.
- Whether any first-application ANEF extension certificate is issued for an APS-originating ordinary change: **Not established**.
- Temporary document/récépissé work rights: national rules distinguish certificates and prior titles; case-specific document issuance/authorization for TP transitions is **Not established**.
- TP APS time toward the long-term EU five-year count: **Not established**; do not count it without explicit authority.
- Automatic/national post-TP scheme: none identified in national official sources reviewed as of 2026-11-11; future announced pathway remains unestablished.

## Editorial exclusions

Future article must not claim:

- “If you find a job, you can simply switch from TP to salarié.”
- “Enrollment automatically lets you change from TP to student from anywhere in France.”
- “Any TP holder can file every ordinary permit through ANEF.”
- “Any ANEF filing keeps you lawfully in France or lets you work.”
- “A TP holder must leave France for every ordinary permit.”
- “TP years count toward the five-year long-term resident card.”
- “The APS is equivalent to an ordinary residence card for every permit application.”
- “France automatically gives TP beneficiaries an ordinary residence permit after temporary protection ends.”

Use department-specific guidance and individual route conditions. State clearly when a filing path is locally confirmed and when the national rule, pending rights, or APS-specific document treatment remains unresolved.
