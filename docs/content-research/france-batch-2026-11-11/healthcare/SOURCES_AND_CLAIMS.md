# France — Healthcare Access During Temporary Protection

Research checked: 2026-11-11

Scope: exactly `healthcare-access`, `healthcare-proof`, `healthcare-urgent`.

## Research verdict

**READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS** — it is possible to write a safe practical article about the healthcare route, proof of rights, and urgent care, provided it distinguishes the explicit Assurance Maladie guidance for people displaced from Ukraine from the general rules for all temporary-protection (TP) beneficiaries. The national guidance says qualifying Ukraine arrivals with TP receive PUMa and Complémentaire santé solidaire (CSS) from arrival, usually through prefecture–CPAM information sharing, for 12 months including family members. The sources reviewed do not establish the same immediate, no-advance-of-cost arrangement for every TP beneficiary of every nationality, nor do they identify a healthcare-specific statutory TP exemption from the ordinary waiting-period rules. Do not turn the Ukraine operational policy into a universal TP rule.

Emergency contacts and a national rule requiring hospitals to ensure urgent care and admit patients even without civil-status documents or information about reimbursement are established. That rule is not a promise that care, later hospitalisation, or all associated charges are free.

## Required distinctions

- **TP permission ≠ Assurance Maladie registration.** APS evidences TP residence; a CPAM rights record/attestation is a separate health-insurance matter. Ukraine-prefecture/CPAM data sharing often opens the special cohort's rights without an individual form, but the source says “most of the time.”
- **Assurance Maladie/PUMa ≠ CSS.** PUMa is the basic public health-insurance entitlement; CSS is complementary cover subject to its own conditions. The Ukraine special page describes both for its defined group and 12-month period.
- **Social-security number ≠ healthcare entitlement itself.** NIA/NIR identify the person administratively; an NIA may support temporary reimbursements. A number alone does not prove current open rights.
- **Carte Vitale ≠ healthcare entitlement.** It is an administrative reimbursement tool issued after affiliation/opened rights; Ukraine-specific ameli guidance says it is not systematically issued.
- **Attestation de droits ≠ immigration status.** It documents the insurance rights shown on it, not TP permission.
- **Emergency access ≠ all care free.** Urgent admission may not be refused for absent papers/reimbursement information; later bills and ordinary patient shares remain distinct questions.
- **AME ≠ normal route for a valid TP beneficiary.** AME targets people in irregular status meeting conditions; valid TP APS ordinarily points to lawful-residence Assurance Maladie pathways.
- **Immigration proof ≠ universal healthcare proof.** APS is relevant to lawful stay; a rights attestation or CPAM decision evidences health coverage. The national sources do not establish that APS alone is accepted as proof of every health scheme or registration step.

## CPAM / Assurance Maladie registration

- **Eligibility.** General PUMa covers people who work or live in France stably and regularly. In the ordinary rule described by Service Public, non-working residents generally wait three months; some named groups meet the residence condition without delay. The ordinary source does not expressly list TP as a general exception. For people arriving from Ukraine after 2022-01-24 under TP, Ameli separately states PUMa and CSS start on arrival for 12 months, including family, with no advance of care costs.
- **Route.** Ukraine group: usually prefecture–CPAM transmission, with no individual application in most cases. If rights are not visible or confirmation is needed, contact the CPAM for the place of residence/accommodation. Standard foreign-born application: complete Cerfa S1106 and send it to the health-insurance body for the place of residence. The reviewed national materials do not confirm an online or universal appointment route for this application.
- **Documents.** The ordinary S1106 asks for current identity or stay evidence and other evidence appropriate to work/residence circumstances. Evidence for NIR certification includes a complete, legible, valid civil-status record (e.g., birth record with parentage), potentially with translation/legalisation formalities. RIB may be useful for reimbursement payment, but it is not evidence of immigration permission or entitlement. The national sources do not supply one exact file checklist for every TP holder. In the Ukraine-specific route, ameli says most arrivals need no application and provides distinct child proof rules; do not mix that rule with a universal checklist.
- **Children.** Ordinary guidance says minors remain dependants; they may be attached to parent(s) using S3705. For Ukraine, accompanied minors have the parent's rights with evidence naming the child, or S3705 where that evidence cannot be produced; unaccompanied minors are created as insured persons and the Ukraine page describes alternatives for missing official proof. Exact rules for other TP groups were not established here.
- **Number assignment.** Opening rights comes first; foreign-born applicants may receive a temporary NIA, which can support temporary identification and reimbursement. NIR certification follows civil-status verification. Standard guidance gives a nine-month period for NIA use and NIR instruction, but a particular person's actual timeline depends on complete acceptable documents.
- **Proof and card.** Ask for/download the attestation de droits for current coverage proof. Once rights are opened and a social-security number is available, the Vitale card may be requested; it is not systematically issued in the Ukraine special case. Use the attestation while waiting. Do not delay urgent care for a card/number.
- **Unresolved.** There is no nationally published complete checklist for every TP population, no general explicit TP waiting-period exemption established, and no universal ordinary-route missing-document process for adults. The Ukraine special guidance has a narrower cohort and was last updated 2025-03-10. CPAM should confirm individual rights and any items still required.

## Costs / reimbursement

- Under ordinary PUMa, Assurance Maladie reimburses eligible care under the general tariff and care-pathway rules; this is not blanket 100% coverage. A coordinated physician consultation is generally reimbursed at 70% of the conventional tariff.
- A €2 participation can apply to adult physician consultations/acts, radiology and laboratory tests; a €1 medical deductible may apply to medicines. Rates, ceilings, exemptions and other residual cost-sharing vary by care and personal situation. CSS can reduce eligible residual costs, subject to its terms.
- Medicines have different reimbursement rates; a deductible can remain even where a medicine is reimbursable. Do not state all prescriptions are free.
- From 2026-03-01, Service Public lists a €23/day hospital forfait (€17/day in psychiatric hospital units) and a €23 FPU after an emergency-department visit not followed by qualifying hospitalisation. CSS and specific situations may exempt or cover certain amounts; TP alone is not a general exemption listed in the cited sources.
- Ameli says the specified Ukraine TP cohort and family have PUMa plus CSS and no advance of care costs for 12 months. The page is cohort- and period-specific; it does not warrant a universal promise that every possible charge is erased or that all TP groups get the arrangement.
- Service Public's PUMa page notes a 2026 financing-law contribution for certain non-working residents was awaiting a decree as of its 2026-05-13 verification and says its existing information remains valid pending that decree. Recheck before future publication; do not speculate about an amount or application.

## Urgent care

- **Grave or life-threatening situation:** call **15 (SAMU)** or **112**; **18** reaches fire/rescue. **114** is the accessible emergency route listed for deaf, deafblind, hard-of-hearing and aphasic users (app, internet or SMS). These calls are free; the charge for treatment is a separate question.
- **Rapid medical advice/orientation:** call **15** for the Service d'accès aux soins (SAS) where a clinician is needed quickly and the situation is not clearly a life-threatening emergency.
- **Hospital urgences:** go to the nearest emergency department if the situation is grave and it is safe to travel. Under CSP R1112-13, a hospital must ensure urgent care and admit even without civil-status papers or information about reimbursement. Staff may gather identity/insurance information later; the law does not cancel later billing.
- **Non-urgent out-of-hours need:** Service Public points to on-call doctors and pharmacies; check the local mairie directory. Local availability and contact procedure vary.
- **Charge boundary:** an ED visit not followed by qualifying hospitalisation may incur the €23 FPU; an inpatient stay may incur the daily hospital forfait and other patient shares. Exemptions are specific. Emergency call or access without documents does not mean universally free treatment.
