# Hungary: applying for temporary protection

**Research checked:** 12 November 2026
**Canonical comparison aspects:** `protection-scheme`, `protection-application`, `protection-dates`
**Scope:** Official-source research for a future practical article. This is not an article and does not integrate content into the catalog.

## Executive finding

**Overall: PARTIALLY READY — BLOCKING EVIDENCE GAPS.** Hungary's ordinary in-country filing route, personal appearance, identity capture, interim evidence, grant document, and current 4 March 2028 document validity are substantially documented by current OIF pages and Hungarian legislation. Two material matters prevent safe end-to-end applicant guidance: (1) a direct conflict between the binding EU rule for new grants to persons subject to Ukrainian military obligations and OIF's current statement that the older rules remain unchanged for conscription-liable Ukrainians; and (2) official sources do not provide a complete route for a person with no identity documents at all. Appointment and border/remote filing edge mechanics also remain unestablished.

| Aspect | Readiness | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| `protection-scheme` | **Not ready** | EU law effective for new grants from 5 August 2026 requires evidence of compliance with Ukrainian military obligations where applicable; current OIF guidance dated 3 November says conscription-liable Ukrainian citizens can still qualify under the old rules. |
| `protection-application` | **Not ready** | Core in-country route is clear, but no complete official fallback is established for applicants with no identity documents; appointment and border/remote filing details are not fully established. |
| `protection-dates` | **Ready with qualification** | EU and Hungarian law, plus current OIF guidance, support 4 March 2028 for the relevant layers and no holder action. A live OIF validity page has an internally inconsistent stale 2027 statement. |

### Family-member rule

Hungary's Act refers to Article 2(4) of Council Decision 2022/382. That EU provision covers spouse; stable unmarried partner where the Member State's law or practice treats it comparably; unmarried minor children; and other close relatives who lived together as part of the family unit and were wholly or mainly dependent. OIF's practical summary describes spouse, a verified partner, under-18 child, and dependent relative (examples include an elderly parent or ill sibling). The family unit must relate to the relevant displacement facts; do not imply that any later-created relationship qualifies. OIF advises family members to attend/apply together, but each person needs recognition. Newborns in Hungary do not automatically acquire the parent's status; a separate application is required and the birth certificate is indispensable per OIF.

### Appointment, walk-in, and online

The OIF TP-specific procedure instructs applicants to attend during office hours and does not state that a prior appointment is mandatory. It also does not promise walk-in access or guaranteed same-day queue admission. A general OIF notice dated 27 October 2026 says large offices may serve non-booked visitors after appointment holders, may have longer waits, and may stop issuing queue numbers before closing. That notice is not a TP appointment rule. Treat appointment mechanics as **not established for this procedure** and verify with OIF before travel.

Enter Hungary is currently restored after an outage ending 27 October 2026. Its general availability does not turn optional TP data capture into online filing. OIF says the data upload is optional and the personal appearance remains required. The OIF page also retains an old embedded data-retention date of 31 December 2023 despite its March 2026 update; treat that text as stale and do not infer that the current portal is inactive.

### Personal appearance and biometrics

The application must be made in person. Each applicant's identity data is checked/recorded; OIF captures a facial image and fingerprints for applicants aged 14 or older. The form is signed before the authority at filing. The OIF instructions say the filing is considered submitted after personal identity-data checking/recording and biometric capture. A family member cannot simply be assumed to be covered by another member's appearance. Specific general exceptions for adult representation or a parent's absence are not established here. OIF's unaccompanied-minor guide says a child aged 14+ may apply independently; a child under 14 who wishes to apply but has no guardian may signal intent and the authority arranges a guardian.

### No-document route — material unresolved gap

OIF names alternatives to a passport, so an applicant with no passport but another accepted identity document has a documented path to present that document. However, neither the current OIF procedure nor the legislation reviewed explains a complete safe route where the applicant has **no identity documents at all**: no assured identity interview, alternate verification, consular process, or precise way to begin and continue filing is stated. The statute says the authority may hear an asylum applicant personally when needed to clarify facts, but that is not a guaranteed no-document procedure. Digital-only identity, expired passport, and missing originals for family/Ukraine-residence facts are likewise not resolved in enough detail for instructions. This materially affects a displaced population and blocks fully actionable guidance.

## 6. Children and families

- Each family member needs their own TP recognition. OIF recommends family members attend/apply at the same time.
- A child aged 14 or older may apply independently under OIF's unaccompanied-minor guidance. A child under 14 who wishes to apply but has no guardian may signal intent; the authority arranges a guardian. This is a high-level summary, not a full guardianship procedure.
- For a newborn, OIF says a separate application is required; parent(s) should personally appear shortly after birth and the birth certificate is indispensable even if other identity documents are pending.
- OIF requires identity/biometric capture at the application stage, with fingerprints at age 14+. Specific exceptions for a child's physical attendance are not established in the reviewed material.

## 7. Fees and processing

OIF's official authority-procedure disclosure lists the temporary-protection recognition application as having **no fee**. The sources reviewed do not separately establish a current fee for replacement/issuance of a card, so do not extend the no-application-fee finding to every later document service. Hungarian Asylum Act §77 sets a **45-day, non-extendable decision deadline**; OIF's procedure disclosure also states 45 days. No reliable official same-day decision, registration, or plastic-card production estimate was found.

## 9. Previous EU temporary protection and existing Hungarian status

OIF's 3 November 2026 notice says that if a person currently has temporary protection in another EU Member State, the Hungarian asylum authority checks and rejects an application in Hungary. Hungarian Asylum Act §25 also requires revocation of Hungarian beneficiary status if the person, with consent, is placed under temporary protection in another EU Member State. The reviewed sources do not explain a transfer process, evidence checklist, or how a previous status is terminated. Therefore: do not advise that an applicant can hold parallel protection, and do not invent a transfer route. Confirm the applicant's current status with OIF.

For another valid Hungarian residence status, no general coexistence/election rule or effect-of-application instruction was established. OIF does specifically exclude Ukrainian citizens who had arrived before 24 February 2022 and were already living, working, or studying in Hungary from the TP route; do not generalize that boundary to every current permit holder.

## 12. Blocking gaps

### `protection-scheme` — NOT READY

- **Known:** The core cohorts and the Hungarian exclusion of the Article 2(2) permanent-residence group are stated in current national law. EU Decision 2026/1912 sets a military-obligation condition for new grants from 5 August 2026, with an exception for continuously protected existing beneficiaries.
- **Missing:** An authoritative current Hungarian implementation clarifying whether/how the EU rule applies to newly recognized conscription-liable Ukrainian applicants.
- **Why material:** It changes eligibility for a broad, foreseeable applicant group; a practical article cannot safely promise either access or exclusion.
- **Official evidence needed:** Updated OIF instruction consistent with the EU decision or an authoritative Hungarian legal measure/interpretation resolving the conflict.
- **What can still be safely drafted:** Core eligibility categories and documented exclusions, explicitly excluding any definitive statement about military-liable new applicants.

### `protection-application` — NOT READY

- **Known:** Ordinary in-country filing channel, authority, personal attendance, biometrics, alternative-to-passport examples, interim proof, and granted document are documented.
- **Missing:** A complete no-identity-document route, including how identity/eligibility can be verified and whether an application can be initiated while evidence is obtained. TP-specific appointment/walk-in certainty and final border/abroad filing are also not established.
- **Why material:** The missing-document case materially affects displaced applicants and a false promise or denial could send a reader into an unusable process. Appointment uncertainty can cause a wasted trip but does not negate the documented ordinary route.
- **Official evidence needed:** Current OIF procedure for applicants with no identity evidence, plus explicit TP appointment/access and border/consular filing instructions if those routes are to be discussed.
- **What can still be safely drafted:** Constrained instructions for applicants who have at least one identity document, directing them to verify appointment/access with OIF and explaining that online data capture is not filing.

## 13. Article drafting boundary

**Decision: Do not draft yet** as a full practical article. A narrowly scoped draft could be prepared only after explicitly excluding the unresolved military-liability and no-document cases, but those are central enough that this research package does not authorize a general applicant-facing guide.

### Safe to state

- The official Hungarian terms and distinction between applicant, beneficiary, interim document, beneficiary card, and address document.
- The documented core eligibility cohorts and Hungary's stated exclusion of the specified permanent-residence/no-protection and pre-24-Feb-arrival categories.
- OIF/NDGAP's personal in-country filing route, optional Enter Hungary data capture, biometric capture (fingerprints age 14+), and distinction between application and online preparation.
- Alternative identity documents listed by OIF, with a clear caveat that this is not a complete no-document fallback.
- Pending humanitarian document names and the 45-day statutory decision deadline.
- Current 4 March 2028 EU and Hungarian document date, with no action for current holders solely because of the extension.
- No TP application fee per OIF's procedure disclosure.

### Must qualify

- Military-liable new applicants, family proof, exact documentary sufficiency, appointment/access, children’s attendance exceptions, border/foreign filing, address without fixed accommodation, prior EU protection, and other Hungarian residence permits.
- OIF's dedicated validity webpage has stale contradictory text, while current law and newer OIF notices support 2028.

### Do not assert

- That conscription-liable new applicants definitely qualify or definitely fail.
- That a person with no documents has a guaranteed interview, consular verification, or filing fallback.
- That Enter Hungary itself submits the TP application, an appointment is always required, walk-in is guaranteed, or police/border contact completes the application.
- That all residence cards or later card services are fee-free; that all Hungarian residence permits coexist or cancel; or that an EU-state transfer follows a defined process.
- That temporary protection automatically gives an address card or that earlier emergency reception centres remain current.

## 14. Scope limits

No research here on employment, job search, healthcare, housing eligibility, education, social benefits, ordinary post-TP residence routes, citizenship, or detailed travel rights. Border, other-member-state protection, address, and document references are limited to what is needed to explain application and status boundaries.
