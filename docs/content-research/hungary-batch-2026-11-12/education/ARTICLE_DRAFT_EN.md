# Education in Hungary under temporary protection

**Research draft — checked 16 November 2026.**

## School and kindergarten access

A foreign minor whose temporary-protection application has been formally filed may use public education from the filing date; they do not have to wait for final recognition. Recognized beneficiaries also have access. Public kindergarten/school instruction is free under the applicable rules, and OIF directs families to the institution serving their Hungarian accommodation. Contact that school; if you do not know the catchment school, ask the competent school-district centre (`tankerületi központ`). A catchment primary school must admit a compulsory-age child actually living in its area. This does not guarantee a place at a different preferred school. [National Public Education Act §92](https://njt.jog.gov.hu/jogszabaly/2011-190-00-00); [OIF education guidance](https://oif.gov.hu/tajekoztatok/menedekeseknek-nyujtott-ellatasok-es-tamogatasok).

Compulsory schooling normally begins in the school year in which the child turns six by 31 August and ends at the end of the school year in which they turn 16. Readiness, early-start, deferral and special-needs exceptions apply. Kindergarten attendance is generally compulsory from age three until compulsory school begins, subject to exemptions. Parents/guardians are responsible for enrolment and attendance. A child’s Hungarian address card (`lakcímkártya`) is not established as a universal prerequisite. Bring the TP/applicant document and official accommodation-address evidence; ask the receiving school which additional records it needs. [National Public Education Act](https://njt.jog.gov.hu/jogszabaly/2011-190-00-00).

Missing Ukrainian report cards or certificates are not established as an automatic bar to starting public school. The receiving school director decides admission and recognition of incomplete studies using records and assessment; if unable to decide, the law provides for seeking the education minister’s opinion. There is no automatic grade-for-grade conversion. Do not delay contacting the school while collecting records.

## Language support, meals and other school costs

Schools may organize catch-up, individual or Hungarian-language support, but availability is school-specific. Current official rules do not guarantee a separate Ukrainian class or a fixed language course for every child. A current funding route does exist for additional individual preparation: Government Decree 229/2012 §37/T and the parallel vocational-school provision allow eligible school maintainers to receive support when they provide a TP-eligible pupil **five additional individual preparation hours per week**. Government Decision 1083/2026 provides **HUF 130,000 per pupil per month** for eligible maintainers when those hours are delivered. This is provider funding conditional on delivery, not cash to the family or a promise that every school offers the hours. Ask the school what is currently available. [Decree 229/2012 §37/T](https://njt.jog.gov.hu/jogszabaly/2012-229-20-22.60); [Government Decision 1083/2026](https://njt.jog.gov.hu/jogszabaly/2026-1083-30-22).

Eligible TP households caring for a child may apply for free institutional and school-holiday meals for an initial six months without the ordinary income test; after that, income or ordinary eligibility rules apply. Free public instruction does not mean meals indefinitely, transport, supplies, trips or extracurricular activities are all free. This meal provision is separate from family cash benefits. [Decree 106/2022 §3/A](https://njt.jog.gov.hu/jogszabaly/2022-106-20-22).

Ukrainian online schooling is not established as a substitute for Hungarian compulsory attendance for a child living in Hungary. Secondary and vocational admissions depend on the school and programme; bring available prior records and ask how placement will be assessed.

## University and vocational study

Higher-education admission, recognition of prior qualifications and tuition funding are separate. For many programmes, apply through the central electronic Felvi (`E-felvételi`) system; some international, institutional and doctoral routes differ. Requirements, language tests and selection are programme-specific. A receiving university may assess a foreign certificate for further study; formal recognition by the Education Authority (OH/MEIK) is a separate procedure and is not always required before applying. Some official recognition proceedings allow an exception when war-related loss of documents is established, but this does not force a university to admit without required evidence.

A recognized `menedékes` may study in state-funded, partially state-funded or fee-paying programmes under the Higher Education Act, subject to admission and programme rules. TP does not guarantee admission, a funded place or free tuition. The Act does not clearly extend the same tuition category to pending applicants. Ukraine-related scholarships such as Students at Risk are competitive programme awards, not automatic TP benefits; the 2026/27 application deadline had passed by the research check. Verify future calls and deadlines directly. [Higher Education Act §39](https://njt.jog.gov.hu/jogszabaly/2011-204-00-00.105); [Felvi foreign qualifications](https://www.felvi.hu/felveteli/jelentkezes/felveteli_tajekoztato/FFT_2026A/kulfoldi_vegzettsegek); [OIF Ukrainian qualification guidance](https://oif.gov.hu/tajekoztatok/tajekoztato-az-ukrajnaban-megszerzett-kepesitesek-elismerteteserol).

**Evidence limits:** no school placement route for children without a known accommodation address, exhaustive nationwide enrolment checklist, universal language-support schedule, or guaranteed place at a preferred school was established. University admission, qualification review and scholarship awards remain institution- and programme-specific.
