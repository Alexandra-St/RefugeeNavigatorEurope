# Social assistance in Denmark: benefits and how to apply

**Status: Draft for human review. Not published.**

**Summary:** An overview of municipal cash assistance, family benefits and help with certain necessary expenses. Eligibility depends on residence status, income, assets and an individual assessment.

## Who can ask for assistance

Waiting for a decision under Denmark’s Ukraine Special Act is not the same as holding a residence permit and does not by itself establish entitlement to cash assistance. SIRI says that a person in a pending procedure cannot receive support under the general Active Social Policy rules while an immigration authority has a duty to maintain that person. If no such duty applies, the other legal conditions must still be met. While an application is pending, the Danish Immigration Service says an applicant may stay in an asylum centre, but this is not required; private accommodation, for example with family or friends, is also possible. Ask the Danish Immigration Service or the authority responsible for your accommodation which body handles your support.

A person holding a valid Special Act residence permit may apply to the municipality for cash assistance if they cannot support themselves and meet the other conditions. If the immigration authorities still cover maintenance because the person is accommodated in a reception centre, the route may differ until responsibility is transferred to the municipality. An application does not guarantee payment: the municipality assesses each case individually.

Other people living in Denmark are assessed under the general social-assistance rules in light of their residence situation and any other benefits or maintenance available to them. If you are unsure which authority is responsible, ask your municipality and the authority handling your residence case.

## Cash assistance for necessary living costs

The main cash-assistance benefit is called kontanthjælp. Since the reform that took effect on 1 July 2025, this is the name used for assistance in the cash-benefit system. It may be considered when a change in circumstances, such as illness, unemployment or the end of a relationship, leaves a person unable to meet necessary needs for themselves or their family and no other benefit covers that need.

The type and rate depend on factors including age, residence and work history, income and assets. General residence and previous-work requirements apply to certain rates. A spouse’s income and assets may also be considered. Separate provisions can apply to people under the Ukraine Special Act, so the municipality must determine which rules and rate apply to an individual case.

## Benefits for families with children

Child and youth benefit (børne- og ungeydelse) and child allowance in specific circumstances (børnetilskud) are different benefits. The general rules for child and youth benefit take account of the child’s age, the family’s residence in Denmark, the custodian’s tax status and qualifying periods of residence or work. Ukrainian citizenship or a Special Act permit alone does not confirm eligibility. Ask Udbetaling Danmark which conditions and application route apply to your family.

Certain child allowances may be available to people holding a Special Act permit if the other conditions are met. For some allowances, a qualifying period of residence or work in Denmark still matters. The rules depend on the type of allowance and family situation, so ask Udbetaling Danmark to assess your case.

The municipality may also consider an income-based subsidy towards childcare costs. This is a separate application and is not an automatic addition to kontanthjælp. See the Denmark × Education article for more on childcare and subsidies.

## Help with certain necessary expenses

A municipality may individually consider help with certain reasonable and necessary expenses, for example an unforeseen expense after a change in circumstances, some health or dental expenses, or moving costs. Separate provisions under integration legislation may apply to people covered by the Ukraine Special Act. The applicable route depends on the person’s status and circumstances.

Help is not automatic. The municipality considers whether the expense is necessary, the person’s income and savings, family situation, and whether another rule covers the cost. You should normally contact the municipality before incurring the expense. Detailed housing-benefit and deposit-loan guidance is covered separately in the Denmark × Housing article.

## Work, activities and reporting changes

If you receive kontanthjælp, the municipality may assess whether you need to be available for work, search for jobs or take part in activities. Requirements depend on an individual assessment and may take health or other circumstances into account. Failing to meet a requirement without a reasonable reason can affect payments.

As of 15 October 2026, people holding a Ukraine Special Act permit are outside the separate arbejdspligt regime. This does not mean there are no other appointments, integration-programme activities or benefit conditions. Bill L 43 proposes a change, but it had not been enacted when checked. This information must be rechecked before publication.

If you receive assistance, tell the municipality about changes that may affect eligibility or the amount, such as changes in income or household circumstances. Follow the requirements in your own decision and plan.

## How to apply

To ask about kontanthjælp, start with the municipality where you live or the municipality responsible for your case. The general application is made through the municipality or its digital service. The municipality will tell you what information and documents it needs. MitID, CPR and alternative application arrangements depend on the specific service and your circumstances; do not assume one requirement applies to everyone.

For child and youth benefit or a specific child allowance, contact Udbetaling Danmark. People who are not EU/EEA citizens can ask that agency directly about the application route. For an income-based childcare subsidy, contact the municipality.

If your application is refused, read the decision and its complaint instructions. A complaint about a municipal kontanthjælp decision is submitted to the municipality within four weeks of receiving the decision. If the municipality maintains its decision, it forwards the complaint to Ankestyrelsen. A Udbetaling Danmark family-benefit decision has a separate process; generally, the agency must receive a complaint within four weeks. Check the deadline and submission route in your own decision promptly.

## Where to get help

- Contact your municipality about kontanthjælp and help with necessary expenses.
- Contact Udbetaling Danmark about child and youth benefit and child allowances.
- If you are waiting for a Special Act decision or do not know who is responsible for your maintenance, contact the Danish Immigration Service and the authority handling your case.
- For job-search information and public employment services, see the Denmark × Employment article.
