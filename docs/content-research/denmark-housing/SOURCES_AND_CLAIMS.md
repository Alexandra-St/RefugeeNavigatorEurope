## Statutory scope and source discrepancy

The official consolidated Integration Act LBK no. 453/2026 was opened and checked directly on 15 October 2026. Section 12(1) states the municipal temporary-accommodation duty for a refugee assigned under §10 and permits housing as an alternative. Section 57(5) expressly says that a person granted a permit under §2(1) of the Ukraine Special Act is not to be assigned accommodation under §12, unless the Danish Immigration Service decides otherwise in very special personal circumstances. The rule is specific to §2(1); it is not a general exemption for every Ukraine Special Act permit holder and does not, by itself, establish that all municipality-assignment or integration responsibilities end.

SIRI’s FAQ “Boligplacering” (checked 15 October 2026) says broadly that people granted permits under the Ukraine Special Act are covered by §12 and may be assigned temporary accommodation or housing. It does not mention §57(5). This is an official-guidance conflict in scope. The article attributes SIRI’s statement, states the statutory exception separately, and directs readers to the Danish Immigration Service and responsible municipality for an individual determination. The FAQ is not claimed to have been updated or corrected.

The 15-weekday transfer of integration responsibility under Ukraine Special Act §30(2) is separate from the Integration Act §12 housing-placement rules. It should not be described as a right to accommodation. Earlier transfer by agreement is stated in the SIRI FAQ.

Amendment check through 15 October 2026: the LBK 453 preamble lists the consolidations included. Later-effective §18 provisions of Act no. 1750/2025 concern other Integration Act sections and do not amend §§12 or 57(5); their July 2026 commencement therefore does not change this housing clause. The 2026 Act no. 766/2026 concerns other amendments to the Ukraine Special Act and related acts; no amendment to Integration Act §57(5) was identified. L 43 is a pending proposal at this review date, not enacted law; its effects must be checked again if it is adopted. This review establishes the current text of §§12(1) and 57(5), not an individual eligibility outcome.

## Important limitations and unresolved questions

- S02, the Immigration Service page for people granted permits, was last updated on 18 August 2022. The 2026 municipal quota regulation supports continued allocation but does not refresh every accommodation and move detail. Recheck these passages with current Immigration Service guidance before publication.
- S04 is a useful official FAQ and directly addresses Ukrainians, but no single update date is shown. It directly describes temporary housing placement for Special Act permit holders, the possible alternative of permanent housing, and the 15-working-day responsibility-transfer period. The same page contains historical amounts; none are carried into the drafts. Recheck current lodging conditions, housing benefit treatment and the terminology for applicants before publication.
- A housing-benefit application and a deposit loan are separate routes. The drafts do not promise that a person will qualify for either. Udbetaling Danmark decides housing benefit; the municipality processes relevant deposit-loan applications.
- The sources do not establish a universal type, standard, cost or duration of municipal accommodation. Local service availability and individual arrangements must be confirmed with the assigned/current municipality.
- The address-change deadline is supported, but the reviewed sources do not comprehensively explain how changing municipality affects every Special Act integration arrangement. Contact the current municipality before moving if it assigned your residence or manages an integration programme.
- The national complaints-board guidance is in Danish. The Copenhagen English page is city-specific and should not be generalized nationally. Local municipality pages should be checked for current forms, fees, language support and which board handles a particular tenancy.
- We did not establish that any specific landlord practice is unlawful in a reader’s case. The drafts point to dispute bodies rather than make case-specific legal assessments.
