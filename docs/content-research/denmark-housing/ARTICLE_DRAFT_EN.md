# How to find housing in Denmark

**Draft status: Needs human content review — not published.**

## Where to start

Your options depend partly on whether you are waiting for a decision on a residence permit under Denmark’s Ukraine Special Act or already hold a permit. The Danish Immigration Service and your municipality handle different parts of the process. Ask the authority responsible for your current situation what accommodation and support are available to you.

## Accommodation through municipalities

While your Special Act application is being processed, the Danish Immigration Service says you may stay in an asylum centre, but this is not required; private accommodation with family or friends is also possible. This guidance is mainly for applicants in Denmark. Some municipalities may offer lodging and meals to people who have applied for a permit under the Special Act (or apply within five working days) if they are not obviously outside the Special Act’s target group and are not receiving other public support for accommodation and maintenance. A municipality may also offer this support to someone who has received a permit until responsibility passes to the assigned municipality. These offers are not automatic: each municipality decides whether and how to provide them. Ask the municipality what currently applies.

Municipal assignment and accommodation are separate questions. If the Danish Immigration Service assigns a municipality after a permit under the Ukraine Special Act, the applicable housing-placement rules depend on the legal route. SIRI’s general FAQ says that Special Act permit holders are covered by section 12 of the Integration Act: a municipality must designate temporary accommodation or may instead designate housing. However, section 57(5) of the Integration Act specifically says that a person granted a permit under section 2(1) of the Ukraine Special Act is not to be assigned temporary accommodation or housing under section 12. The Danish Immigration Service may decide otherwise where very special personal circumstances exist. SIRI’s general FAQ does not mention this exception, so its broader wording does not explain how section 57(5) applies. Do not assume that every permit holder is entitled to municipal accommodation or that this exception automatically removes other municipality-assignment or integration responsibilities. Where the ordinary section 12 rule applies, the municipality must designate temporary accommodation, but it may instead designate housing; a particular apartment, permanent home, or municipality of your choice is not guaranteed. Separately, where section 30(2) of the Ukraine Special Act applies, integration responsibility generally transfers to the municipality no later than 15 working days after the assignment decision; it may transfer earlier by agreement. This timing rule does not itself create an entitlement to accommodation. Ask the Danish Immigration Service and the responsible municipality how the rules apply to your case. Private renting is a separate option.

If you have been assigned a municipality and are considering a move, contact it before making plans. The rules for moving to another municipality can depend on your circumstances and the receiving municipality’s agreement.

## Renting housing independently

You can look for a private rental or a home through a housing association. Vacancies may be advertised online or found through people you know. DanmarkBolig.dk lists social housing; waiting lists and availability vary, and there is no single register of all private vacancies.

Before agreeing to rent:

- Read the lease carefully and make sure the rent, utilities and other terms are clear.
- Check how any deposit and prepaid rent are described; they serve different purposes.
- Do not pay a deposit before you have a lease.
- Inspect the home before signing, and follow the official guidance and lease for reporting defects after moving in.

A standard private rental contract is available in Danish and English. Keep a copy of the signed agreement and records of payments and messages with the landlord.

## Housing-related financial support

Housing benefit (boligstøtte) is separate from municipal accommodation. It is administered by Udbetaling Danmark. In general, the home must have its own kitchen and be your permanent residence. The amount depends on factors such as rent, home size, household members, income and assets. You must apply; payment is not automatic.

Official guidance says that a person with a residence permit under the Ukraine Special Act may apply if they have fixed residence in Denmark and meet the other conditions. This does not guarantee an award. The guidance reviewed does not treat an application alone as fixed residence for housing-benefit purposes. Check your situation and application with Udbetaling Danmark.

A deposit loan is a separate municipal scheme for certain homes and circumstances. The rules include people with permits under sections 1 and 2 of the Ukraine Special Act who apply within the first three years after receiving the permit, subject to conditions. Ask your municipality whether the scheme applies to the home you are considering and what documents are needed.

Municipalities also assess some requests for help with specific expenses, such as certain moving costs or short-term rent support in an eviction-risk situation. These are not the same as housing benefit or accommodation under the Special Act. Eligibility is assessed individually; contact your municipality before incurring an expense.

## Moving and address registration

When you move to another address in Denmark, report the change to the Civil Registration System (CPR). You can normally report it from four weeks before moving and must do so no later than five days after the move. The online service requires MitID; your local Citizen Service office (Borgerservice) can explain how to get help.

If your municipality was assigned under the Special Act or manages your integration programme, contact it before moving to another municipality. Also check whether you need to update a housing-benefit application with Udbetaling Danmark.

## Where to get help

- For accommodation while an application is pending or questions about municipal assignment, contact the Danish Immigration Service or the municipality handling your case.
- For housing benefit, contact Udbetaling Danmark. For a deposit loan or individually assessed help with expenses, contact the municipality.
- For a tenancy problem, first contact your landlord and keep written records. Depending on the type of home and dispute, the municipality’s Rent Control Board (Huslejenævn) or Residents’ Complaints Board (Beboerklagenævn) may be able to decide the case. These boards do not handle every dispute or provide general legal advice; check the local rules and the appropriate route.
