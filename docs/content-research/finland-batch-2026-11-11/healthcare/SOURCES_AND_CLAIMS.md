# Finland — Healthcare During Temporary Protection

Research checked: 2026-11-11

Scope: exactly `healthcare-access`, `healthcare-proof`, `healthcare-urgent`.

## Research verdict

**READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS.** A safe article can explain the healthcare route while reception services are active, the broader healthcare entitlement attached to a granted TP permit, the transition to services organised by the wellbeing services county after kotikunta, Kela's separate insurance/reimbursement role, and urgent-care contacts. It must not collapse these into one “TP healthcare” status.

For an adult who has applied for TP but is still waiting for a decision, Migri's current Ukraine FAQ states urgent and essential healthcare; reception-centre healthcare personnel assess and arrange the route. A person under 18 is entitled on the same basis as a Finnish child with kotikunta. After a TP residence permit is granted, Reception Act §26 and Migri say the person has the same healthcare entitlement as a resident, while the person remains a reception-system client the reception centre arranges care. Living in private accommodation does not end that client relationship.

A material 2026 transition needs a qualification: from 1 September, reception services for TP clients end three months after the person becomes eligible to apply for kotikunta, even if they do not actually obtain one; unaccompanied minors are excepted. Migri says reception-centre healthcare ends with reception services. The Reception Act §26 separately states that a TP beneficiary without kotikunta receives healthcare on the same basis as a person with kotikunta, and the Health Care Act requires arrangements with Migri for services under §26. National public instructions do not give a clear, uniform walk-in/referral/proof route for this post-reception, no-kotikunta transition. Do not say the statutory entitlement disappears; direct the person to confirm the current provider and proof route with Migri/reception centre and the local healthcare service.

Kotikunta is not Kela health insurance. Public healthcare entitlement after kotikunta and reception-law entitlement are separate from Kela insurance and the Kela card. A Kela card proves eligibility for Kela reimbursements; it is not required for public healthcare and does not prove entitlement to it. Care and prescription medicine costs are not universally free. Urgent care must be provided regardless of residence, but an invoice may follow.

## Proof and cost boundaries

### Before municipality of residence

- Reception client: contact the centre where registered, including when staying in private accommodation; the centre assesses, refers, or arranges the provider [FI-HEALTH-S04–S05, S19]. Show the reception client card to identify yourself at the centre [FI-HEALTH-S27]. Whether a particular county/private provider also needs a permit copy, referral, payment commitment or another certificate is locally determined; no nationwide proof list was found.
- Pending adult: state that the TP decision is pending and request an assessment of urgent/essential need. A pending certificate or TP document should not be described as a universal healthcare card [FI-HEALTH-S01–S03].
- Granted TP permit, still a reception client: contact the reception centre; the permit card documents immigration status but does not itself establish Kela benefits [FI-HEALTH-S02, S04, S15].
- Kela-insurance basis without kotikunta: ask Kela to assess and issue its Certificate of Entitlement to Medical Care if eligible. Kela says a Finnish personal identity code is not required to apply [FI-HEALTH-S11]. This is a separate route and decision.
- If reception services have ended but no kotikunta exists: ask the former reception centre/Migri for a transition contact and ask local healthcare services how they recognise the §26 entitlement. Do not assume Kela card is required or that the former centre continues to book care [FI-HEALTH-S02–S03, S08–S11].

### After municipality of residence

Kotikunta/DVV registration is the ordinary basis for public healthcare at resident client fees. Use the local health station/health centre and its local access instructions. A Kela card is not required to use public healthcare; a personal identity code is not a substitute for the municipality record [FI-HEALTH-S04, S11–S12, S15–S16].

### Kela card and medicines

The Kela card proves Kela reimbursement eligibility. It can support direct reimbursement for eligible prescription medicines and private care; reimbursement depends on Kela coverage and the medicine/service rules. Public healthcare is not reimbursed by Kela. The reception centre may help in hardship, for example with a prescription-medicine voucher; its allowance's small healthcare component does not make every medicine free [FI-HEALTH-S11–S14, S21].

### Costs

- **Reception system:** healthcare is arranged through reception services, but “included” does not mean a universal zero-cost promise. Reception Act §32 permits healthcare client fees and reasonable reception-service charges; fees depend on the statutory/individual rules. From 2026-10-16 the general reception-service fee ceiling is €15/day, with up to €3 per meal where meals are provided. This is not a per-treatment tariff, and a centre gives the applicable decision [FI-HEALTH-S02, S16, S18–S20].
- **After kotikunta:** public healthcare may charge resident client fees under national limits and local service schedules. Means-based fees must be reduced/waived when collection threatens the person's/family's livelihood or statutory maintenance duty; ask the organiser about reduction [FI-HEALTH-S18].
- **Kela reimbursements:** separate from public-care access. They can cover some eligible private-care, prescription medicine and travel costs for an insured person; Kela does not reimburse public healthcare fees [FI-HEALTH-S11–S14].
- **Emergency:** urgent care must be provided, but a person without kotikunta or an accepted cost-responsibility document may be charged later. Urgency is not a fee exemption [FI-HEALTH-S03, S11, S18].

## Language, identity, and adjacent care

- Reception centres provide interpretation/translation services as reception services; Reception Act §27 describes interpretation for relevant reception matters and, where possible, other matters affecting client rights/duties [FI-HEALTH-S02, S05].
- Healthcare staff must explain information so the patient sufficiently understands it; interpretation should be arranged where possible if language is a barrier. This is not a guarantee of a Ukrainian/Russian interpreter at every appointment [FI-HEALTH-S17].
- Employment-based occupational healthcare is a separate channel for an employed reception client; it does not replace the person's other public/reception healthcare entitlements. Its scope depends on the employer's arrangement [FI-HEALTH-S05].
- Finland's EHIC is for eligible cross-border necessary care during a temporary stay; it is not a Kela card, Finnish TP document or domestic public-care entitlement document [FI-HEALTH-S26].
- A personal identity code may be issued with the TP permit, but it does not create kotikunta or Kela cover [FI-HEALTH-S15–S16].

## Required distinctions

- TP application/status ≠ reception-services-client relationship ≠ kotikunta [FI-HEALTH-S01–S09].
- TP status ≠ Kela health-insurance coverage [FI-HEALTH-S11, S13–S15].
- Kotikunta ≠ Kela card or Kela coverage [FI-HEALTH-S11–S12].
- Kela card ≠ residence permit; Kela card ≠ proof of public-healthcare entitlement [FI-HEALTH-S12, S15].
- Personal identity code ≠ healthcare entitlement or insurance [FI-HEALTH-S11, S15–S16].
- Residence permit card ≠ proof of every healthcare right [FI-HEALTH-S02, S11–S15].
- Reception healthcare ≠ all public healthcare for a pending adult [FI-HEALTH-S01–S03].
- Urgent treatment ≠ automatically free treatment [FI-HEALTH-S11, S18].
- Child and adult reception-healthcare scope are not identical before TP grant [FI-HEALTH-S01–S03].
- Kela reimbursement ≠ public healthcare entitlement [FI-HEALTH-S11–S12].
- Private provider used by reception system ≠ private care chosen/paid for by the client [FI-HEALTH-S05, S19].
- Reception-centre client ≠ necessarily living in reception-centre accommodation [FI-HEALTH-S05].
- Public healthcare ≠ occupational healthcare [FI-HEALTH-S05].
- 112 ≠ non-emergency medical advice line; 116117 ≠ ambulance/emergency number [FI-HEALTH-S22–S23].
- EHIC ≠ Kela card [FI-HEALTH-S12, S26].

## Readiness per aspect

### `healthcare-access`

**Status: Ready with qualification**

**Evidence:** Reception Act §26 and current Migri guidance distinguish pending-adult, child and granted-TP scopes; the centre-led pathway and county transfer after kotikunta are established [FI-HEALTH-S01–S05, S10].

**Qualification:** The 2026 three-month reception-service end rule can precede actual kotikunta. Statutory §26 healthcare and the practical referral/billing route after reception exit without kotikunta need individual confirmation. Do not promise full routine care to a pending adult.

### `healthcare-proof`

**Status: Ready with qualification**

**Evidence:** Kela identifies the specific document it issues for some no-kotikunta entitlements, defines the Kela card narrowly, and distinguishes DVV kotikunta registration. Migri confirms reception-centre identification and its own referral route [FI-HEALTH-S04–S05, S11–S16, S27].

**Qualification:** No nationwide standard reception-client healthcare proof or post-exit/no-kotikunta proof/referral list was found. County/provider requirements vary; reception centre supplies local instructions.

### `healthcare-urgent`

**Status: Ready**

**Evidence:** Health Care Act §50, Kela and official emergency services establish urgent-care entitlement, costs, 112, and 116117's geographic exception [FI-HEALTH-S03, S11, S18, S22–S23].

**Qualification:** 116117 is not available in Åland; urgent care may be billed. Fees and facility routes vary by region.

## Open gaps

- **Post-reception/no-kotikunta handoff:** The Reception Act §26 TP-beneficiary entitlement and Health Care Act §72a arrangements coexist with Migri's statement that reception healthcare ends when reception services end. No national operational instructions identify the provider, self-referral path, proof document, payment commitment or invoice route for every person in this transition [FI-HEALTH-S02–S03, S08–S11].
- **Reception proof:** Reception client card identifies a client at the centre, but universal acceptance at every outside provider is not established [FI-HEALTH-S05, S19, S27].
- **Pending adult scope:** “Urgent and essential” is a clinical threshold; exact application to an individual's chronic, mental-health, dental, pregnancy or medicine need is assessed case by case [FI-HEALTH-S01–S03].
- **Fees:** No national bill calculator or complete schedule for centre-arranged care was identified; reception-service fee, healthcare client fee and prescription costs are distinct [FI-HEALTH-S02, S18–S21].
- **Kela/worker without kotikunta:** individual work-based coverage and certificate outcome depend on facts; the Ukraine FAQ still includes an explicitly 2023 earnings figure and should not be used for a 2026 threshold [FI-HEALTH-S11, S13–S14].
- **Local booking and referral:** reception centres and counties purchase/provide services through local arrangements; no single national booking channel for routine care exists [FI-HEALTH-S05, S19, S24].
- **Interpretation:** law requires understandable information and interpretation where possible, but no nationwide guarantee of a specific language at every appointment [FI-HEALTH-S02, S17].
- **Åland:** mainland 116117 coverage excludes Åland; national Health Care Act's general framework also excludes Åland. Consult Åland's own healthcare service for local route [FI-HEALTH-S22, S25].
- **Legacy Ukraine guidance:** a 2022 Migri article on the temporary municipal accommodation model is historical; that model ended 2023-12-31 and is not the current healthcare route [FI-HEALTH-S05, S28]. Kela's current Ukraine page contains an old 2023 earnings threshold; do not reproduce it as current [FI-HEALTH-S14].

## Current versus legacy guidance

- **Current national rule:** Reception Act §§26–27 and Health Care Act §50; current Migri healthcare/accommodation pages; current Kela entitlement and Kela-card pages [FI-HEALTH-S01–S05, S11–S17].
- **Current, recently changed:** Reception services terminate under the 2026-09 rule; use current Migri's duration page and Act 481/2026 rather than pre-September descriptions [FI-HEALTH-S06–S09].
- **Current but Ukraine-context:** Migri's TP FAQ and Kela's Ukraine page provide useful official operational answers for displaced Ukrainians. Read Kela's printed work-income threshold as dated 2023, not current 2026 guidance [FI-HEALTH-S01, S14].
- **Legacy:** Migri's 2022 municipal accommodation model announcement describes exceptional services/contracting during 2022–2023; Migri says the model contracts ended 2023-12-31. Do not reuse it as today's ordinary county responsibility [FI-HEALTH-S28].

## Editorial exclusions

Future article must not say, without new evidence:

- “Temporary protection gives full public healthcare immediately from the moment of application.”
- “You need kotikunta before you can receive any healthcare.”
- “A pending adult has all the same healthcare as a resident child.”
- “A TP permit card automatically transfers the person to county healthcare.”
- “Reception services continue until kotikunta is actually granted.”
- “Reception services stopping means the legal TP healthcare entitlement under §26 disappears.”
- “A reception centre client must live at the centre.”
- “Kela card is required for public healthcare.”
- “A residence permit card, personal identity code, kotikunta, or Kela card all mean the same healthcare/insurance status.”
- “Everyone under TP automatically receives Kela insurance, Kela card, medicine reimbursement or EHIC.”
- “Healthcare, medicines, dental care or emergency treatment are always free.”
- “Children and adults have exactly the same reception-healthcare scope before TP grant.”
- “All counties use the same booking route or proof document.”
- “116117 works in Åland or replaces 112.”
- “An employer's occupational healthcare replaces public/reception healthcare.”
- “A 2022 Ukraine municipal-accommodation instruction or 2023 Kela income threshold is current 2026 guidance.”
- “Private provider care is always Kela-reimbursed.”
