# Finland — School Enrolment, Pupil Language Support and Higher Education Admission

Research checked: 2026-11-11

Scope: exactly `education-school-enrolment`, `education-pupil-language`, `education-higher-admission`.

Baseline: `2a336b88b3ee6c5a590125ddb56b41016ef541c5`.

This is an official-source research record, not applicant-facing content. “TP” means a residence permit granted on the basis of temporary protection under the Aliens Act. The legal/statutory and administrative access rules are distinct from practical service availability. Recheck any rule for later cohorts and study starts.

## Research verdict

**READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS.** The national route is clear enough for school access, newcomer language support, and higher-education applications, provided the article distinguishes access from allocation and individual admission. Children receiving temporary protection can access pre-primary/basic education while living in a municipality even without a statutory municipality of residence (`kotikunta`); EDUFI says the municipality’s arranging duty under Basic Education Act section 4 does not require the child’s place of stay to be their statutory `kotikunta`. Actual first contact is the local municipality education department, and procedures, capacity, and support arrangements vary. Temporary protection is an express tuition-fee exemption for degree studies in a language other than Finnish or Swedish, effective 1 December 2024, and an express application-fee exemption where the applicant held TP when studies began and the permit has not been revoked under the statutory provision. A TP application alone is not the same as a granted permit.

## School access and enrolment

### Compulsory-education boundary

The legal boundary needs careful wording. A child must participate in one year of pre-primary (or equivalent activity) in the year before compulsory education begins, normally the year they turn 6. The Compulsory Education Act obligation begins in the school year when the child turns 7; basic education is grades 1–9, normally ages 7–16. After basic education, compulsory education continues through an eligible post-basic education route. It ends at 18 or earlier after successful completion of a Finnish matriculation or vocational qualification or corresponding qualification abroad. A completed foreign qualification is a statutory early-completion category, not an automatic class placement or Finnish equivalence decision (S01, S02, S04, S07, S34). EDUFI uses the shorthand “6–18” for the overall compulsory school pathway; distinguish the pre-primary attendance requirement from the age-7 start specified in the Compulsory Education Act.

The obligation is for children permanently resident in Finland; the statute treats a child as permanently resident for this Act where they have a `kotikunta`. The TP family with no `kotikunta` is therefore not automatically subject to the same compulsory-education duty. This does **not** remove the separately stated right to pre-primary/basic education: EDUFI says a municipality must arrange basic education for compulsory-age children living in its area, and that a statutory `kotikunta` is not required for this organising duty in TP/asylum contexts (S01, S02, S05, S08). As the sources do not fully reconcile every case at the edge of age/status and 2026 amendments, use the local education department to confirm the individual route.

### Who arranges school

The first practical contact is the education department of the municipality/city where the child actually lives or stays. National EDUFI guidance specifically recommends that contact and says municipal/school procedures are decentralized (S05–S07). If the child has a statutory `kotikunta`, the municipality has the ordinary local organising and compulsory-education oversight role. If the child is a TP recipient staying in a locality without `kotikunta`, EDUFI says the municipality where the child lives still has the section 4 organising duty for basic and pre-primary education. Do not direct the family only to the reception centre: sources reviewed do not identify the reception centre as the school-place allocator. A reception centre may help the family identify local contacts, but this referral role is not established as a national legal duty here.

### Enrolment route

Use this safe national sequence: (1) contact the education department of the city/municipality where the child lives; (2) explain the child's age, current address/stay, language(s), prior schooling, and whether a `kotikunta` has been registered; (3) ask for the municipal newcomer-enrolment and assessment procedure and whether the first contact should be with a named school; (4) provide available identity/guardian/prior-school information and request assessment/placement; (5) agree start date and any preparatory or language-support plan with the provider. The first contact and decentralized provider role are nationally supported; exact forms, documents, assessment and start dates are local (S05–S07).

Children usually attend a nearby school. A family may be able to request another school, but school choice is subject to restrictions and availability; there is no general entitlement to a preferred school (S04, S07). Mid-year entry is compatible with a child arriving during the school year and EDUFI urges timely school access, but no nationwide source guarantees same-day start, a particular class, or a particular school (S05, S08).

### Documents

No national exhaustive newcomer checklist was found. Have available: child's passport or other identity evidence; guardian identity and contact details; address/current place of stay; any residence-permit decision/card and Finnish personal identity code if already issued; prior school reports, grade/year information and curricula; language and learning-support information; health/vaccination records where available; and custody/guardianship papers where relevant. Treat these as helpful evidence to support identification, placement and practical school preparation, not as a nationally proven list of prerequisites. Local providers may request material under their own procedures. EDUFI's right-to-education guidance does not make possession of a TP card, personal identity code or Ukrainian transcript a stated prerequisite for access (S05, S06, S08).

### Missing documents

Official national material reviewed establishes school access on residence and local municipal responsibility, but does not spell out an all-country alternate-document procedure for a child lacking passport, Finnish personal identity code, residence card, vaccine record or Ukrainian school reports. Nor does it state that such absences always block enrolment. Contact the local education department promptly, explain what is missing, provide whatever identity/education evidence exists, and ask what temporary identification and assessment process the provider uses. Do not assert that missing records never matter or that they automatically prevent attendance. The first day and precise document alternatives remain an open local gap (S05–S08).

### Placement

No national conversion table equating a Ukrainian grade number to a Finnish year group was found. Safe statement: the receiving provider/school assesses the pupil’s age, prior schooling and available evidence, current skills, and Finnish/Swedish proficiency to select an appropriate class/year and support route; request that assessment locally. The evidence reviewed supports provider/local curricula and assessment autonomy, but does not prescribe a single national newcomer-grade formula. Preferred school, class/year placement, and language-support placement are separate decisions (S02, S05–S07, S09).

### Preparatory education

`Perusopetukseen valmistava opetus` prepares a child/young person with an immigrant background and insufficient Finnish/Swedish for participation in pre-primary or basic education. It is not a mandatory offer in every municipality: the Basic Education Act says the municipality may arrange it, and the provider decides whether/how to start or extend provision. It can be group-based, individual, or inclusive with participation in ordinary basic-education classes; it is not necessarily a permanently separate class. The curriculum is one-year-equivalent; national minimum is 900 hours/year for ages 6–10 and 1,000 hours/year for older pupils where arranged (S02, S09–S12).

From 1 August 2026 providers may offer supplementary preparatory teaching after the preparatory course for up to one year under the amended Basic Education Act. This is optional provider provision; it is not a fixed extra year every newcomer receives (S03, S15). Older Ukraine emergency pages describing 2022 attendance/funding periods are dated operational context and do not replace the 2026 statutory framework (S05, S17).

### Finnish/Swedish language support

S2 (Finnish as a second language and literature) and the analogous R2 (Swedish as a second language and literature) are curriculum syllabi selected around the learner’s language proficiency and need to strengthen basic language skills. Nationality, Ukrainian origin, or TP status alone does not automatically determine S2/R2 placement. There is no nationally established personal weekly allotment. The provider/school determines teaching arrangements and may combine S2/R2, preparatory support, language-aware instruction across subjects, other learning support and participation in ordinary classes (S09, S13–S15, S32).

Education in the pupil’s own mother tongue may be offered if local group/teacher arrangements allow it. Current EDUFI Ukraine guidance refers to a minimum group of four Ukrainian-speaking pupils for a teacher and possible state funding, but does not establish a nationwide Ukrainian-language class or remote course guarantee. Ask the municipality/school directly (S05, S09, S33).

An official administrative interpreter/translation duty can apply in relevant integration-related authority interactions, including school-home cooperation, where language needs require it; the cited EDUFI guidance notes state reimbursement for those integration-related interpretation costs. This is not a promise of an interpreter at every routine school meeting. Confirm arrangements when scheduling an important discussion (S05).

### 16–18-year-olds

For a youth with `kotikunta` within compulsory education, the obligation continues until 18 or qualifying earlier completion, and must be fulfilled in an approved post-basic route rather than necessarily ordinary comprehensive school. Typical routes include general upper-secondary (`lukio`), vocational education and training, TUVA (preparatory education for programmes leading to an upper-secondary qualification), adult basic education in relevant circumstances, or another route recognized by law. Admission, language readiness and seat availability remain provider decisions (S01, S16–S18).

For TP youth without `kotikunta`, EDUFI’s Ukraine-specific guidance says they are outside the compulsory-education scope; they can still apply to education. Depending on age, completed prior education, language readiness and provider offer, routes may include TUVA, upper-secondary or VET programme application, adult basic/upper-secondary subject studies, or liberal adult education/integration language courses. Some adult subject/liberal education can be fee-based; a municipal integration plan may make an accepted Finnish/Swedish course free. The 2022–23 Ukraine pages are useful route maps but are dated and provider/eligibility details should be confirmed locally (S01, S16–S18).

### Ukrainian online schooling

Current-enough EDUFI Ukraine information (updated June 2024) says that if a family wants the child to participate in Ukrainian distance education **in addition to Finnish education**, it should discuss this with the Finnish school. It does not say Ukrainian online school replaces Finnish school access or satisfies a Finnish compulsory-education obligation. The official 2022 EDUFI publication of Ukrainian online resources is a resource list, not legal equivalency. Finding: simultaneous Ukrainian study may be discussed with the school; standalone substitution/equivalence is **not established** (S08; legacy resource page: [EDUFI Ukraine learning materials](https://www.oph.fi/en/internationalisation/materials-support-teaching-pupils-who-have-fled-ukraine)).

### Costs / meals / materials

Pre-primary and basic education are free, with learning materials and a daily school meal provided. School transport is free where the pupil lives far from school or the route is long/dangerous under the national overview; local application of eligibility and transport arrangements should be checked. This does not mean every incidental cost (optional activities, personal items, travel outside qualifying transport) is always covered. Upper-secondary student entitlements depend on the applicable compulsory-education/free-education rules and should not be generalized from basic school (S04, S07).

## Higher education

### Where to apply

Finland has universities and universities of applied sciences (UAS), with different programme profiles and potentially different selection criteria. Studyinfo is the national search/application service. It hosts the two spring joint applications described in current 2026 EDUFI guidance (English programmes and named arts programmes in the first; Finnish/Swedish programmes in the second) and many institution-specific separate applications. Some HEIs/programmes use other institution-run procedures; inspect the programme’s current Studyinfo page and institution admission page. Submitting an application is not admission; the HEI applies eligibility/selection criteria and issues the decision (S19, S22).

The person can study/apply while holding TP; EDUFI’s adult Ukraine information says applications are generally not restricted by nationality or residence status, while programme prerequisites still apply. This research does not determine residence-permit transition after TP; admission is not a student residence permit and the applicant should check Migri separately if their immigration basis changes (S17, S18; residence transition out of scope).

### Ukrainian qualification

A Ukrainian upper-secondary qualification may support general eligibility if it gives eligibility for higher-education study in the country where completed, but the particular programme, selection method, documents and language conditions decide. Institutions assess foreign qualification eligibility and make admission decisions. Expect programme-specific requests for the certificate, grade/transcript evidence, official translations and authenticity/verification by deadlines. No automatic Finnish equivalence or automatic admission is established (S19–S22).

### Prior study / credit recognition

Foreign university studies do not automatically create advanced-year admission, transfer admission, or Finnish ECTS credit. The HEI decides whether to admit, what application route applies (including transfer/separate application), and whether prior learning can be credited toward the Finnish degree. Provide transcripts, course descriptions/syllabi, learning outcomes and official translations where requested. No national TP-specific credit-transfer route was found (S20–S21).

### Missing academic documents

No uniform national alternative credential pathway for TP applicants with incomplete Ukrainian higher-education documents was established. Some programme-specific criteria allow refugee-like applicants who cannot provide ordinary qualification evidence to submit other official protection/asylum decisions or verification forms, but that is one provider/programme’s rule and may not cover a TP holder, another institution, or another intake. Ask each admissions service before the document deadline; do not promise the institution will waive evidence (S22).

### Language

The language of instruction and proof requirement are programme-specific. An English-taught programme may require an approved test, prior studies in English, or another accepted proof; Finnish/Swedish-taught programmes specify their own language evidence. Current sample Studyinfo criteria show that accepted tests/proof and minimum scores vary by selection. No TP-based automatic exemption from programme language standards was found (S19, S22). Check the selected programme’s current criteria; this source inventory is not an exhaustive test table.

### Tuition fees

For non-EU/EEA/Swiss applicants, Finnish law requires tuition in degree-leading Bachelor’s and Master’s programmes taught in a language other than Finnish or Swedish, subject to statutory exemptions. Since 1 December 2024, a person holding a Finnish residence permit based on temporary protection is expressly exempt from that tuition fee for the covered degree education; the permit must meet the statutory validity/non-revocation conditions. This is a national exemption, not merely an institution scholarship. A pending TP application alone does not satisfy wording requiring a granted permit. Tuition exemption does not create an admission right and does not guarantee scholarship or cover living expenses (S23–S25).

The fee rule discussed here concerns degree programmes taught in another language (commonly English). The law’s tuition-fee requirement does not apply in the same way to Finnish- or Swedish-language degree education; programme and other charges still need individual checking. Programme-specific special/joint/on-demand arrangements should be verified with the institution (S24–S25).

### Application fees

The current statutory application fee is €100 for covered Bachelor/Master degree applications by applicants from outside EU/EEA or equivalent groups, under the 2025–26 schedule; postgraduate-degree applications and other statutory exceptions differ. A person who held a Finnish TP permit **when the studies began** is expressly exempt from the application fee, unless the permit was subsequently revoked under Aliens Act section 58(4). This differs from the tuition rule and from the date of application: do not infer that a pending TP application qualifies, or that having a TP permit only after study start retroactively removes the fee. Confirm applicability through Studyinfo/EDUFI for the particular application (S24–S27).

### Scholarships

HEIs may offer programme-specific scholarships, tuition waivers or other support, with institution-defined criteria. A statutory TP tuition exemption is not a scholarship; it removes the covered fee by law. No separate nationwide 2026 TP-only higher-education scholarship or active national special admissions scheme was established. Institution-specific Ukrainian support can exist, but must be checked at that institution and intake (S19, S23–S25, S31).

### Kela student-aid boundary

Kela’s current Ukraine guidance says a person under temporary protection cannot receive student financial aid (`opintotuki`) on TP status alone. Other Kela benefits, residence/work/family grounds and changed circumstances may affect separate eligibility; Kela decides case by case. Higher-education admission and tuition exemption do not grant Kela student aid, housing benefit, or a student loan guarantee automatically (S29–S30).

### Ukraine-specific higher-education support — current vs legacy

The reviewed current national route is ordinary Studyinfo/HEI applications, with general education access and the 2024 statutory TP tuition/application-fee exemptions. No national 2026 blanket TP admission, automatic recognition, or reserved study place was found. EDUFI’s 2022 “information point” described HEIs offering individual separate study rights to Ukrainians whose prior university studies were interrupted; because no current 2026 national continuation was found, classify that scheme as **legacy**, not as an open present-day route. Individual HEIs may independently offer support (S19, S23–S25, S31).

## Required distinctions

- Compulsory education is not an entitlement to a preferred school.
- School access/enrolment is separate from the school’s class/year placement decision.
- TP is not `kotikunta`; absence of `kotikunta` removes the ordinary compulsory-education scope described in the sources, but does not itself erase the municipality’s basic/pre-primary education arranging duty for a child living in its area.
- `Kotikunta` is not a prerequisite to every education service: EDUFI explicitly describes basic/pre-primary access without it for TP/asylum contexts.
- A Finnish personal identity code is not shown in national guidance as a prerequisite to the right to education; the precise alternative ID process is not established.
- A residence-permit card is evidence of status, not the same as school admission; school access does not wait on a card according to no national rule found.
- Preparatory education is not a permanent separate track and is not required in every municipality.
- S2/R2 is not automatically assigned by nationality or TP status.
- Own-language teaching is not a guaranteed Ukrainian class everywhere.
- Compulsory education to 18 does not mean everyone must attend ordinary comprehensive school to 18.
- Studyinfo application is not admission.
- Higher-education admission is not a student residence permit.
- TP does not create an automatic university place.
- A foreign diploma is not automatically declared equivalent to a Finnish qualification.
- EDUFI recognition for statutory occupational eligibility is not the HEI’s admission decision.
- Prior studies do not automatically transfer as credits.
- TP is an express tuition exemption for covered degree studies, but not an automatic scholarship.
- Tuition fee and application fee are separate; TP exemption conditions differ.
- Admission does not establish Kela student aid.
- Free public basic education does not mean every ancillary/personal expense is covered.

## Readiness per aspect

### `education-school-enrolment`

**Status: Ready with qualification.**

**Evidence:** Current compulsory-education statute, Basic Education Act and EDUFI Ukraine guidance establish age boundaries, local municipality responsibility, practical first contact, and access without statutory `kotikunta` for a child living in the area (S01–S08).

**Qualification:** No nationwide missing-document checklist, immediate placement promise, uniform assessment/grade formula, or right to preferred school was established. Local municipality procedures control. The interaction between no-`kotikunta` compulsory status and education access should remain explicit.

### `education-pupil-language`

**Status: Ready with qualification.**

**Evidence:** EDUFI describes preparatory education, S2/R2, own-language teaching, integration into ordinary classes and an Aug 2026 optional supplementary-preparatory route (S09–S15, S32–S33).

**Qualification:** Preparatory and own-language provision are not guaranteed in each locality; S2/R2 timetable and placement are needs-/provider-based. Interpretation is not guaranteed at every routine meeting. Ukrainian distance-learning equivalence is not established.

### `education-higher-admission`

**Status: Ready with qualification.**

**Evidence:** Current Studyinfo/EDUFI application guidance, EDUFI academic-recognition guidance, 2024 Acts 658/2024 and 659/2024, current fee decree and Kela Ukraine guidance establish separate application, admission, qualification, credit, fee and aid decisions (S19–S30).

**Qualification:** HEI decides programme admission, language evidence, recognition of prior studies and any incomplete-document pathway. TP is an explicit national exemption from covered tuition and application fees subject to distinct statutory conditions; scholarships and Kela aid remain separate. The 2022 special study-right scheme is legacy.

## Editorial exclusions

Future content must not claim without further evidence that:

- a child needs `kotikunta`, a residence card or a Finnish personal identity code before attending school;
- no `kotikunta` means no school access;
- a family can choose any school, receives an immediate preferred-school place, or has a guaranteed first-day start;
- missing Ukrainian records always block school enrolment or never matter;
- a Ukrainian class number converts automatically to a Finnish year;
- every municipality must offer preparatory education or every child is automatically placed in it;
- every pupil receives a fixed number of Finnish/Swedish lessons or stays in a separate preparatory class;
- every municipality provides Ukrainian-language teaching or every school meeting has an interpreter;
- Ukrainian online schooling by itself satisfies Finnish compulsory education;
- all TP youth are within compulsory education regardless of `kotikunta`, or everyone must attend ordinary school until 18;
- an application through Studyinfo guarantees admission;
- TP automatically grants a university place, waives admission language rules, or substitutes a study residence permit;
- a Ukrainian diploma is automatically equivalent to a Finnish one or prior credits automatically transfer;
- EDUFI recognition is required for every academic admission;
- TP tuition exemption equals a scholarship or covers living expenses;
- the tuition-fee and application-fee exemptions have identical rules or timing;
- every TP applicant automatically meets the application-fee exemption, regardless of whether the permit was granted by the statutory relevant date;
- admission automatically gives Kela student aid;
- the 2022 separate Ukrainian HE study-right initiative is active nationally in 2026 without new confirmation.
