# Finland — Housing Under Temporary Protection

Research checked: 2026-11-11

Scope: exactly `housing-initial`, `housing-private-rental`, and `housing-financial-support`.

## Research verdict

**READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS.** Official guidance establishes the main accommodation and ordinary-rental routes, the fact that reception services do not reimburse private rent, the September 2026 service-end rule, an emergency social-welfare route, and the separate Kela decision pathways. The account must be careful about individual placement and benefits, and must not promise a dwelling, private-rent reimbursement, a Kela benefit, or permanent housing after reception exit.

The central post-reception finding is more specific than “no route is known”: Social Welfare Act §§12 and 21 provide a route to urgent, individually assessed temporary social housing. Suomi.fi directs someone with nowhere to stay tonight to the county social-welfare emergency service, and Kela says county social services help find emergency lodging even for a person temporarily resident in Finland. Section 12a separately provides state reimbursement to the county for specified urgent housing/help after specified reception-service endings, but its text refers to a foreigner who applied for international protection; whether that reimbursement clause applies to a TP beneficiary whose reception services end under the new kotikunta-eligibility rule is **not established** in reviewed guidance. This is short-term, assessed help, not guaranteed reception-centre accommodation, ordinary social assistance, or a long-term home. Ordinary non-urgent support for a person without kotikunta remains a case-specific gap.

On 1 September 2026, the new timing rule took effect: for TP clients, reception services end three months after eligibility to apply for kotikunta, even without actually applying or registering; unaccompanied minors are excepted, and a reception-centre director may extend services for a reasonable time for exceptionally weighty grounds. Reception accommodation is itself a reception service, so the person cannot assume that centre-arranged accommodation continues after the client’s reception services end. The reviewed national sources do not set a separate uniform move-out notice period or guarantee alternative housing at that point. The person should obtain their individual end date and written move-out/contact instructions from the reception centre and contact local social services before that date if they have nowhere to go.

## Reception housing, private accommodation and fees

- **Right/route:** accommodation is listed among reception services, and Migri directs a registered applicant who needs a place to the reception centre. A specific city, apartment type or immediate placement time is not guaranteed in the reviewed sources. [FI-HOUSING-S01–S05]
- **Positive decision:** TP grant alone does not terminate reception services. Since 2026-09-01, the end date for a TP recipient is keyed to kotikunta eligibility, normally three months later, subject to the statutory exceptions/extension. [FI-HOUSING-S06–S08]
- **Private housing:** a client may stay with friends/relatives or arrange a rental. They remain a reception client while entitled to services and should maintain contact with their assigned centre. [FI-HOUSING-S02, S04–S05]
- **Private rent:** Migri explicitly says reception system will not reimburse rent, deposit, electricity or water costs and does not financially support arranging private accommodation. Reception allowance is not rent reimbursement. [FI-HOUSING-S04, S11]
- **Reception fee:** from 2026-10-16, Government Decree 904/2026 sets a maximum reception-services fee of €15/day; where meals are provided, meal charges can be up to €3 per meal. It is a legal ceiling, not an automatic bill or a specific accommodation rent. The fee must be reduced/waived where the law’s minimum subsistence conditions require it. [FI-HOUSING-S01, S05, S33]
- **Allowance:** Migri lists 2026 monthly reception-allowance amounts by household/meal arrangement, but the amount is assessed against actual needs, income and funds. Do not represent allowance as universal or a rent grant. [FI-HOUSING-S01, S05]

## Ordinary private rental

### Lease

Finnish residential leases can be oral or written, but a written agreement is safer and easier to evidence. An oral agreement is generally treated as indefinite-term; a fixed-term agreement must be made in writing. From 2026-10-01, Act 531/2026 changed several lease rules, so the agreement date matters. Tenant must pay agreed rent, care for the dwelling and return it in a reasonably tidy state; tenant is liable for damage caused intentionally or through negligence/carelessness, not ordinary wear. [FI-HOUSING-S21–S23, S26–S27]

### Deposit and advance rent

Under the current KKV summary, a typical security deposit is one to three months’ rent. The statutory maximum is three months’ rent. Deposit is security for agreed lease obligations (for example unpaid rent, agreed cleaning or damage beyond ordinary wear) and must not be confused with rent. From 2026-10-01, the amended §8 applies with transitional treatment for earlier leases; check the lease date and exact terms. If retaining any deposit, the landlord must give the required notice and basis; return is due promptly under the applicable law. Advance rent is distinct: current KKV guidance says it may be required only for a special reason and up to three months. [FI-HOUSING-S22–S25]

### Application documents and identity

There is no national mandatory rental-document checklist in the reviewed official sources. A landlord may ask for identity and relevant evidence of income/means or credit history; requested data must be reasonably related to the rental process and privacy respected. A residence permit and passport may be requested in practice; state-subsidised housing has separate proof-of-residence-right rules. A Finnish personal identity code is not shown as a general statutory prerequisite to make a private lease, but a landlord may require one in practice and many check Finnish credit information. Missing Finnish credit history is not itself a statutory bar; acceptance remains landlord-specific. Bring available passport/permit, income or benefit decision, references if available, and a way to establish identity, but treat these as useful examples, not mandatory national requirements. [FI-HOUSING-S19, S21, S28]

### Rent, increases, insurance, utilities, discrimination

Private rent is agreed in the lease. A landlord cannot unilaterally raise it without an agreed increase clause or later agreement; a stated increase basis and the applicable 2026 notice rules matter. State-subsidised housing has cost-based and resident-selection rules rather than one universal national rent cap. Citizenship, nationality, origin and language are protected grounds; a landlord may assess objectively relevant ability to pay, but must not discriminate on a protected ground. Home insurance is not generally compulsory by statute; it may be required as a lease condition. Electricity, water, heating and internet may be included or separately charged according to the dwelling and contract; check the lease and arrange an electricity contract if it is not included. [FI-HOUSING-S20, S22–S23, S25, S28–S30]

## Financial support and social/municipal housing

### Kela general housing allowance

TP status by itself does not automatically award housing allowance. Kela’s current Ukraine-specific page says a TP applicant/holder who pays private rent may apply if Kela determines they live permanently in Finland; Kela usually treats a year’s residence as permanent, but the assessment is individual and other connections/employment can matter. Kotikunta is not itself a prerequisite for residence-based Kela benefits, and does not itself prove eligibility. Kela assesses household, income, assets, rent/location and acceptable housing-cost limits; award does not cover every rent or every utility. Apply after signing the lease and send the written lease. [FI-HOUSING-S11–S13]

### Basic social assistance and rental deposit

People receiving reception services cannot receive Kela basic social assistance for the same subsistence need. Kela’s Ukraine page says ordinary social-assistance applications should generally follow kotikunta/transition to a home and requires the person to apply for primary benefits first; broader current Kela guidance also allows emergency help for temporary residents and says local social services help arrange temporary emergency lodging. Kela can, in qualifying basic-social-assistance cases, grant a rental-deposit voucher/commitment and reasonable moving costs; this is discretionary, based on means, acceptable rent and a recognized reason, and normally must be applied for before signing/paying. Do not promise Kela will pay every deposit or that its ordinary basic-benefit pathway resolves the post-reception/no-kotikunta gap. [FI-HOUSING-S11, S14–S16]

### Municipal and state-subsidised rental housing

Ordinary municipal housing is applied for through the relevant municipality or its housing company. State-subsidised rentals are applied for directly from the owner; Varke is the current state housing authority (ARA ended as an independent agency in February 2025), and Varke does not allocate individual dwellings. Selection generally considers housing need, household income and assets, statutory residence-status scope and local stock. Local-authority-owned providers may prioritize local residents among similarly situated applicants where law allows. Homelessness/urgent housing need can be relevant to priority, but TP status alone does not create automatic priority and application does not guarantee allocation. The reviewed national sources do not conclusively establish that every temporary-protection permit qualifies for every state-subsidised housing category; the exact permit validity/category and owner criteria should be confirmed with the provider. [FI-HOUSING-S17–S20]

### Housing counselling and local support

There is no single national housing-counselling intake route established here. Ask the municipality/housing company for its housing-advice service and contact the wellbeing services county for social-work/homelessness support. County-level channels and opening hours vary. [FI-HOUSING-S09–S10, S15]

## Required distinctions

- TP application/status **≠** reception-services client **≠** reception-centre accommodation. [FI-HOUSING-S01–S05]
- TP application **≠** guaranteed preferred apartment/city or immediate location choice. [FI-HOUSING-S02–S05]
- Private accommodation **≠** loss of all reception services while entitlement remains active. [FI-HOUSING-S04–S05]
- Reception allowance **≠** private-rent subsidy; Migri says private rent/utilities are not reimbursed. [FI-HOUSING-S04–S05, S11]
- Reception-service fee **≠** market rent; €15/day is the maximum service fee, not an automatic individual accommodation bill. [FI-HOUSING-S01, S05]
- Positive TP decision **≠** automatic immediate exit from reception. [FI-HOUSING-S06–S08]
- Right to apply for kotikunta **≠** kotikunta actually granted; from 2026-09-01 the reception end date follows eligibility plus three months. [FI-HOUSING-S06–S08]
- Reception-service end **≠** automatic eligibility for ordinary Kela benefits or a municipal home. [FI-HOUSING-S11–S20]
- Emergency temporary housing **≠** reception accommodation **≠** ordinary/long-term rental. [FI-HOUSING-S01, S09–S10]
- Kotikunta **≠** Kela housing-allowance decision; housing allowance **≠** guaranteed full rent coverage. [FI-HOUSING-S11–S15]
- Security deposit **≠** advance rent; deposit **≠** Kela commitment. [FI-HOUSING-S22–S25, S15]
- Personal identity code **≠** right to rent **≠** kotikunta **≠** Kela housing allowance. [FI-HOUSING-S11, S21–S22, S28]
- Social/municipal rental application **≠** allocation; municipal ownership **≠** free housing. [FI-HOUSING-S18–S20]
- 2022–23 Ukraine municipal accommodation model **≠** a current national host scheme. [FI-HOUSING-S31]

## Readiness per aspect

### `housing-initial`

Status: **Ready with qualification**

Evidence: Reception accommodation is a reception service; Migri describes referral and a place at a centre with room; private living and rent responsibility are explicit; three-month 2026 termination clock and exceptions are official; urgent temporary accommodation is supported by national law and Suomi.fi instructions. [FI-HOUSING-S01–S10]

Qualification: No guaranteed chosen location/immediate placement, uniform move-out notice period, post-exit long-term housing entitlement, or nationwide ordinary non-urgent no-kotikunta route established. Contact assigned centre/local social services for individual instructions.

### `housing-private-rental`

Status: **Ready with qualification**

Evidence: Official rental contract, deposit, advance rent, requested documents, privacy, discrimination, home-insurance and rent-increase guidance, including 2026-10-01 amendment. [FI-HOUSING-S21–S30]

Qualification: No mandatory national landlord-document list; a Finnish personal identity code may be requested by a landlord; actual screening and utility terms vary by provider/contract. Apply transitional rules based on lease date.

### `housing-financial-support`

Status: **Ready with qualification**

Evidence: Current Kela TP-specific/general housing allowance criteria, Kela social-assistance and deposit rules, reception allowance and private-rent exclusion, Varke/municipal housing selection structure. [FI-HOUSING-S04–S05, S11–S20]

Qualification: Kela decides individual residence/benefit eligibility; ordinary non-urgent support and rent/deposit help for a person after reception exit but before kotikunta are not established as a uniform national entitlement. Varke-owner guidance does not establish that every TP permit meets every state-subsidised housing category.

## Open gaps

- Whether each applicant can obtain a same-day reception bed and the precise local transfer/placement workflow.
- Whether any family/medical/vulnerability feature produces an accommodation preference at a particular centre; sources establish needs are relevant generally but not a complete public allocation rubric.
- Individual move-out notice format and exact move-out time from a centre when §14a service entitlement ends.
- Whether a centre offers any individualized relocation/lease-finding assistance at service end beyond general advice; no guaranteed transition accommodation.
- Routine non-urgent social services and basic social assistance without kotikunta after reception ends; emergency short-term assistance is the established route.
- Exact Kela housing-allowance eligibility in every TP chronology/employment/family scenario; benefit decisions are individual, and current Ukraine FAQ contains stale explicitly dated 2023 threshold examples.
- Whether each specific state-subsidised rental owner accepts each TP permit validity/category; confirm directly with the owner and applicable statute.
- Current national host-family payment programme: none established; 2022–23 municipal model is legacy.
- National rental-application document checklist and treatment of a foreign/empty Finnish credit record: no uniform list found.
- City/county-level emergency accommodation capacity, hours, charge and intake details vary locally.
- A national housing-advice contact point for all people in Finland was not established.
