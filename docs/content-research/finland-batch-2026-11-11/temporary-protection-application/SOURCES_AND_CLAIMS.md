# Finland — Temporary Protection: Eligibility, Application and Duration

Research checked: 2026-11-11

Scope: exactly `protection-scheme`, `protection-application`, `protection-dates`.

This is an evidence record, not applicant-facing copy. It separates the EU framework from Finland’s national implementation and central rules from local police intake arrangements. “Not established” means no sufficiently clear, current Finnish official instruction was identified in this review.

## Research verdict

**READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS**

A practical article can safely explain Finland’s listed eligible groups, in-person first-registration route, division between police/Border Guard and Migri, ordinary evidence examples, and the current EU scheme endpoint. It must qualify the military-condition start date and leave individual Finnish permit/card validity through 2028 unresolved: Migri’s own current pages conflict, and its card page still says 4 March 2027. The article must not tell a person that their card or Finnish permit is automatically valid to 2028 without a current individualized Finnish notice or clarification.

## Required distinctions

- EU TP duration through 4 March 2028 **≠** a confirmed expiry date for each Finnish permit or physical card. [FI-TP-S01–S05, FI-TP-S15]
- Application registration **≠** final grant; police/Border Guard registers and Migri decides. [FI-TP-S01]
- Pending-application certificate **≠** residence permit card. The certificate shows a pending application; card is ordered after grant. [FI-TP-S03, FI-TP-S08]
- TP **≠** asylum. A pending asylum process may now proceed concurrently; older Migri suspension text is superseded. [FI-TP-S16–S17]
- TP **≠** municipality of residence or a personal identity code. Migri says identity code is issued/indicated on the granted permit card; DVV registration and municipality are separate matters. [FI-TP-S01]
- Application **≠** guaranteed accommodation. Reception-centre and group-home pathways are distinct arrangements. [FI-TP-S07, FI-TP-S19]
- Reception centre **≠** immigration decision-maker; Migri decides. [FI-TP-S01]
- Police/Border Guard registration **≠** Migri decision. [FI-TP-S01]
- Finnish TP **≠** simultaneous entitlement to TP in several Member States; a prior/other-state permit may affect a Finnish decision. [FI-TP-S01]
- Earlier automatic-extension guidance through 2027 **≠** confirmed 2028 permit/card instructions. [FI-TP-S02–S05]

## Practical application

### Who can apply

Use the Finnish groups listed in FI-TP-S01 and the scheme/exclusion qualifications in the `protection-scheme` matrix. Do not add every EU optional category. A Ukrainian applicant applying on or after 5 August 2026 must also address Migri’s authorized-departure/military-obligation condition, subject to the same-Member-State continuous-status exception in FI-TP-S15 and the Finnish effective-date point recorded above.

### Where to apply

After arriving in Finland, tell a police officer or border-control official in person that you want temporary protection and ask them to register the application. Police and Border Guard are the registration authorities named by Migri; Migri makes the decision. Local police departments publish their own offices, hours, and contact/appointment steps. Check the specific local police page before travelling; no national walk-in or appointment rule is established. No online first application through Enter Finland was identified in the reviewed official sources. [FI-TP-S01, FI-TP-S06]

### What happens at registration

The police or Border Guard records identity/contact/residence, entry and travel route, family details, then takes a photograph, fingerprints, and signature. They register the application and may provide a pending-application certificate. Migri can request supplementary information and makes the decision. [FI-TP-S01, FI-TP-S08]

### Documents

Bring available identity evidence (for example, national passport or Ukrainian internal passport), children’s birth certificates, and the evidence relevant to the claimed cohort: family relationship, Ukrainian international-protection status, or permanent residence. A Ukrainian applicant subject to the 2026 condition should bring the passport exit stamp or, if absent, an official document showing compliance or exemption, as Migri describes. These are source-backed examples; there is no single exhaustive nationwide checklist for every cohort in the reviewed materials. [FI-TP-S01]

### If documents are missing

Migri says an identity document is required for a final decision and advises a person without one to contact the Ukrainian Embassy in Finland to obtain one. Its procedure distinguishes initial police/Border Guard registration from Migri’s decision-stage identity-document requirement. If asylum is also applied for, Migri says obtaining identity papers from home authorities is not required. The sources do not establish a complete alternative-evidence route when identity/status documents are unavailable, or promise that no-document applicants can complete each step. [FI-TP-S01]

### Children

A child tells police or a border-control official; the authority registers the application. Children arriving with a parent/guardian can remain with them; an unaccompanied child is placed in a group home and a district court appoints a representative to handle official matters. Use only these high-level steps; the reviewed sources do not set out a complete consent, attendance, or guardian-document checklist. [FI-TP-S07]

### After registration

Migri’s current page says the right to work starts upon registration, but the pending-application certificate’s stated function is to prove that the application is pending. A 2022 Migri bulletin described a printed certificate as proof of work right; treat the format as historical confirmation and do not infer all rights from possession of the current pending certificate. [FI-TP-S01, FI-TP-S08, FI-TP-S18]

The current approximate decision time is one week if further information is not needed; postal delivery is estimated at about one week. These are estimates, not legal guarantees. [FI-TP-S01]

### Decision and residence permit card

Migri sends the decision by post to the reception centre or the Finnish address supplied; police may notify the person if postal service is not used. After a positive decision Migri orders a residence-permit card, normally to a collection point; Migri currently says delivery within three weeks of the decision. A personal identity code is indicated on the card; any later DVV step or replacement card is separate. [FI-TP-S01, FI-TP-S03]

### If you had TP elsewhere

Migri says only one EU Member State grants TP to a person at a time and a Finnish application may be refused based on a TP permit held in another Member State. It directs a person required to leave Finland while holding another state’s valid permit to return there. Its guidance does not establish a self-service transfer/cancellation process; expired/historical status cases remain fact-specific and unresolved. [FI-TP-S01]

## Duration

### EU end date

EU temporary protection is extended through **4 March 2028**. The Article 2 military condition applies from **5 August 2026**, subject to its corrected exception for a person protected in the same Member State by/on **4 August 2026** who continuously retains that status there. [FI-TP-S09]

### Finnish permit and card mechanics

Migri’s live central page and card page still state **4 March 2027** for Finnish residence permits/cards, with the printed-date protection for cards dated 2023–2026 conditional on the underlying permit remaining valid. Migri’s information bank says permits will automatically be extended to 2028 without an application; the 5 August press release says further 2028 instructions would be issued when the extension begins. The pages do not explain which individual permit records or card cohorts have been updated. [FI-TP-S01–S05]

### Renewal / holder action

For the previous extension to 2027, Migri explains the applicable permit update and that no replacement card was needed. For 2028, there is no reconciled current Finnish instruction in the reviewed material on personal action, card validity, replacement cards, or evidence to carry. Until clarified by current Migri instructions or the holder’s individual case record, do not state either that all permits/cards expire in 2027 or that all are automatically valid through 2028. [FI-TP-S02–S05]

### Legacy extension information

Older 2022–2025 notices explain earlier extensions and card letters. These remain evidence of historical process only. The current 2027 card instruction is itself not proof of the 2028 mechanics. Label dated extension notices as legacy and never use them as the sole current instruction. [FI-TP-S02, FI-TP-S03, FI-TP-S18]

## Editorial exclusions

- every Ukrainian automatically qualifies or a Ukrainian passport alone establishes eligibility;
- all EU optional categories are accepted by Finland;
- police, Border Guard and Migri have interchangeable roles;
- every police station or border crossing accepts applications, or one nationwide appointment/online route exists;
- Enter Finland accepts an initial temporary-protection application;
- a reception centre grants temporary protection;
- a passport is always an absolute precondition to registration, or no documents make an application impossible;
- all applicants must submit the same combined document checklist;
- a pending application certificate is the residence permit card or automatically proves every right;
- a pending application guarantees accommodation or a specific reception-centre place;
- asylum must remain suspended during temporary protection;
- current status in one Member State can simply be transferred or simultaneously held in Finland;
- EU extension to 4 March 2028 makes every Finnish permit or physical card automatically valid to that date;
- holders universally need no action, or universally need a new card, for the 2028 extension;
- older automatic-extension or letter instructions are current for 2028;
- a personal identity code or municipality registration is the temporary-protection status itself.
