# Finland — Income Support, Applications and Child/Family Benefits

Research checked: 2026-11-11

## Research verdict

**READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS.** A practical account can explain the reception-centre route and current 2026 rates, the September 2026 reception-service end rule, urgent social support, the distinct Kela and employment-service processes, the May 2026 unemployment-benefit replacement, and the major child-benefit routes. It must not promise a seamless transition to Kela or an award based only on temporary protection. The ordinary support position after reception services end without `kotikunta`, current child-benefit eligibility for all TP households, Kela processing for an applicant lacking Finnish identity/bank credentials, and some work-based family-benefit cases need individual confirmation.

Research only. No content drafting, catalog integration, comparison-data change, or runtime change.

## Critical distinctions

- Temporary-protection status is not itself a cash award [FI-SOC-S01–S04, S08–S10].
- TP permit, reception-services client status, reception allowance, Kela coverage, and entitlement to a particular Kela benefit are separate decisions/bases [FI-SOC-S01–S04, S08–S10].
- Reception allowance is not Kela basic social assistance, housing allowance, or unemployment benefit [FI-SOC-S01–S04, S08–S10, S16–S21].
- Ending reception services does not automatically start Kela payments; `kotikunta` is not a universal Kela award, and a Finnish personal identity code or Kela card is not a cash-benefit entitlement [FI-SOC-S03–S04, S08–S10, S13–S15].
- Jobseeker registration, the employment authority/KEHA labour-policy statement, and a Kela or unemployment-fund payment decision are separate steps [FI-SOC-S16–S21].
- Basic social assistance and county supplementary/preventive assistance are distinct; housing allowance is not subsistence assistance; child benefit is not a blanket family-benefit award [FI-SOC-S11–S15, S22–S28].
- Work authorization does not establish benefit eligibility. Salary does not automatically terminate every benefit, but must be disclosed and may affect means-tested support [FI-SOC-S01–S02, S11–S12, S16–S18].

## Reception allowance

### Who can receive it

A person who has applied for or been granted temporary protection may apply while they remain entitled to reception services. The reception centre assesses the household's immediate means and decides the individual allowance; TP status or permit alone does not guarantee payment [FI-SOC-S01–S02]. Reception-centre clients ordinarily use this reception system for basic daily needs; Kela says people receiving reception services cannot receive Kela basic social assistance for the same supported subsistence route [FI-SOC-S08–S09].

### Amount / household structure

Migri's 2026 monthly schedule is [FI-SOC-S01, S30]:

| Household category | No meals provided | Meals provided by centre |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Person living alone | €300 | €88 |
| Other adult | €254 | €72 |
| Single parent | €336 | €97 |
| Child aged 10–17 living with family | €210 | €60 |
| Child under 10 living with family | €191 | €57 |

Unaccompanied minors in full-board minor units instead receive spending allowance (€26 under 16; €47 at 16–17); the unit provides necessary living costs. An unaccompanied minor in private/support housing can receive the adult-alone rate. These are published rates, not a personal award calculation [FI-SOC-S01, S30].

### Income and resources

Income and available resources of the applicant and family, including wages, other benefits, foreign income, savings and assets available in Finland, are considered. Recipients must report income/resources and changes to their reception centre. Work does not by itself establish an automatic termination rule, but earned income and allowances affect the calculated amount; an unreported change may produce a recalculation/recovery. The exact calculation is individual [FI-SOC-S01–S02, S05, S07].

### Private accommodation

Private living does not turn reception allowance into a housing benefit. Migri states that private rent and utilities are not reimbursed by the reception allowance; the payment is for essential everyday personal needs, with the applicable rate depending on meal provision and household category. No general promise that rent or deposit is paid from supplementary reception allowance is supported [FI-SOC-S01, S06].

### Supplementary reception allowance

The person may apply to the reception centre for supplementary reception allowance for necessary special expenses; the centre makes an individual assessment. This is not automatic reimbursement of every healthcare, child, rent or other expense. Evidence and the route should be confirmed with the person's centre [FI-SOC-S01–S02].

### When it ends

For TP beneficiaries who meet the legal conditions for `kotikunta`, reception services are provided for the transition period, at most three months after the date they became eligible to obtain `kotikunta`; the actual later application/registration is not the trigger. The amended rule applies from 1 September 2026. Reception allowance is part of reception services, so it ends when the person's reception-service entitlement ends; unaccompanied minors are excepted from this three-month rule and the director may extend services for exceptionally weighty grounds for a reasonable time [FI-SOC-S02–S05]. A TP permit, receiving `kotikunta`, income, or a job should not be described in isolation as the universal end event; ask the reception centre to confirm the person's recorded service end date [FI-SOC-S01–S04].

### Application / payment

Apply through the reception centre while a reception-services client; the centre assesses the immediate need and gives instructions. The allowance is primarily paid to a prepaid card or Finnish bank account. A centre can explain prepaid-card setup. Do not assume that a Kela application, kotikunta registration, or jobseeker registration substitutes for this application [FI-SOC-S01–S02].

## Ordinary Kela support

### When Kela can become relevant

Kela decides its own benefits. It assesses permanent residence individually using factors such as duration, family ties, work and home; some benefits also have a work-based basis. Kela's Ukraine guidance directs reception-centre residents to seek ordinary basic assistance after the family has `kotikunta` and a place to live. A valid TP permit and `kotikunta` may be relevant evidence, but neither automatically awards every Kela benefit [FI-SOC-S08–S10, S28].

### Basic social assistance

Basic social assistance (`perustoimeentulotuki`) is a last-resort Kela benefit based on the applicant/household's income, assets, accepted expenses and primary benefits. Reception clients receiving reception services are ordinarily supported through that system instead and are not automatically Kela basic-assistance clients. For a TP family after reception services end and with `kotikunta`, Kela's Ukraine-specific guidance identifies Kela basic assistance as an application route if income, assets and other benefits do not cover necessary daily expenses; Kela still decides residence, means and accepted-expense issues [FI-SOC-S08–S11].

The 2026 reform tightened primary-benefit/jobseeking obligations in stages. From 1 February, failing to apply for primary benefits or meet job-search requirements can expose the basic amount to reduction; the reformed calculation and income rules largely apply to assistance for periods from 1 March. A 50% reduction can apply in specified cases. Exact exemptions, notice and individual decision matter; do not reduce this to “all unemployed people lose half” [FI-SOC-S07–S08, S11, S29].

### Unemployment support

From 1 May 2026, Kela's general social security benefit (`yleistuki`) replaced Kela-paid labour market subsidy and basic unemployment allowance. A TP residence permit alone does not qualify a person: Kela's current guidance says holders of a temporary residence permit need to satisfy the statutory employment condition for the current full benefit route. A future government proposal to provide a special route to TP holders without the work condition is not the current rule as of the research date [FI-SOC-S16–S18].

The claimant must register as a jobseeker and remain active under employment-service requirements; registration alone does not confer payment. From 2025, the local employment authority handles jobseeker services and supplies the labour-policy statement (with KEHA functions where applicable); Kela decides and pays yleistuki. An unemployment fund, not Kela, decides and pays earnings-related allowance to members meeting its separate conditions [FI-SOC-S16–S21]. The earlier Finland job-research package further documents third-country permit/registration requirements [FI-JOB-S02, S14, S27, S35].

### Housing allowance boundary

General housing allowance is a separate Kela benefit, applied for and decided separately. TP status or rent alone does not create entitlement, and housing allowance is not general subsistence support. Detailed residence, household and housing-cost calculations are in the Finland housing research package, not duplicated here [FI-SOC-S10, S22; housing `FI-HOUSING` package].

### No-kotikunta transition

#### Reception services active, no kotikunta

Use the reception centre for reception allowance and reception social support; Kela says an active reception-services client ordinarily does not receive Kela basic social assistance. Supplementary reception allowance is individually assessed by the centre [FI-SOC-S01–S02, S08].

#### Reception services ended, kotikunta exists

Apply separately to Kela for any potentially relevant benefit. Kela's Ukraine guidance specifically points to basic social assistance once the household has `kotikunta` and a place to live; eligibility and amount remain individual. Consider jobseeker registration for unemployment support, and apply separately for family/housing benefits if their own rules may be met [FI-SOC-S08–S10, S16–S23].

#### Reception services ended, no kotikunta

**National ordinary income-support route is not established.** The Ukraine-specific Kela guidance ties its ordinary basic-assistance route to `kotikunta`; generic Kela guidance says anyone physically in Finland, even temporarily, has a right to urgent support to secure essential subsistence and care. Kela may provide urgent support, for example a voucher for essential food or necessary prescription medicine; the wellbeing services county is the route for urgent social services and emergency lodging. These urgent fallbacks do not establish regular monthly Kela basic assistance, an automatic handoff, or a guaranteed cash amount [FI-SOC-S08–S09, S15]. Contact Kela and the county emergency social services immediately, explain reception services have ended and `kotikunta` is absent, and ask for an individual assessment [FI-SOC-S08, S15].

## Child and family support

### Child benefit

Child benefit is a separate Kela benefit for eligible children under 17; Kela decides the applicable residence or work-based coverage and child's residence conditions. Current Ukraine-specific Kela guidance and its UA1e form describe a special route for TP families after at least one year in Finland, with child benefit application even if `kotikunta` has not yet been assigned. The form is dated 2023 and should be checked with current Kela before being relied on; do not state that TP alone, `kotikunta` alone, or every child automatically produces an award [FI-SOC-S09, S23–S24].

If the child is outside Finland, national current TP-specific entitlement was not established by the reviewed material. Do not extrapolate EU/EEA coordination or a general child's temporary absence rule to a child living in Ukraine [FI-SOC-S23–S24].

### Other major family benefits

Parental/pregnancy allowance has its own coverage, parent/child and timing criteria; a TP permit does not automatically establish entitlement. Child home care allowance may be relevant for a child under three who is not in municipal early-childhood education and is cared for at home, but Kela assesses coverage and the child's situation. Kela has announced a new three-year Finland/EU/EEA/Swiss residence condition for child home care allowance from 1 January 2027; it is future law on this research date, not a current 2026 condition [FI-SOC-S25–S27].

The reviewed national sources did not establish a TP-specific national school-start cash grant. Do not present free school meals or education services as cash benefits. Disability benefits are separate individually assessed schemes and are outside this research [FI-SOC-S27, S29].

### Child residence/status and application

Use the benefit's own Kela channel and evidence requirements. Kela's Ukraine form UA1e is a distinct paper application for Kela card/child benefit; general Kela benefits may be applied for in OmaKela or by the appropriate paper form. A Kela card concerns health-insurance reimbursements and is not proof of entitlement to all Kela cash benefits [FI-SOC-S09–S10, S12, S23–S25].

### What TP does not guarantee

TP does not guarantee child benefit, parental allowance, child home care allowance, housing allowance, basic social assistance or yleistuki. Each has distinct coverage, residence, child, work-history, means or jobseeking conditions and needs a separate decision [FI-SOC-S08–S10, S16–S18, S22–S28].

### No Finnish identity code, e-ID or bank account

Kela basic-assistance application is not online-only: Kela identifies online, paper and service-point options and allows verbal help/application in specified circumstances. Do not claim that a Finnish bank-ID/e-ID is mandatory for every application. However, the reviewed national material does not settle whether all Kela claims can be processed without a Finnish personal identity code, or a dependable payment alternative if the applicant has no bank account; contact Kela for the specific case. Reception allowance has a separate prepaid-card route [FI-SOC-S01, S12–S14]. A personal identity code helps identify a person but does not create benefit eligibility [FI-SOC-S10].

### Reporting changes and overpayments

Report changes separately to each relevant authority: reception centre for reception allowance; Kela for Kela benefits; employment authority for jobseeker status; and county social services for an open county-assistance matter. There is no evidence that one notice updates all systems. Income, resources, household, address, child residence, work and departure can matter under different benefit rules. Failure to disclose may change/reduce an award or lead to recovery under the relevant decision; confirm the process with that authority [FI-SOC-S01–S02, S08, S11, S16–S20, S23–S27].

## Urgent need and social-work roles

For an urgent lack of food, essential medicine or care, contact Kela and explicitly request urgent assessment; current guidance describes vouchers for essential food/necessary prescription medication. For immediate safety, emergency lodging, or urgent social-service need, contact the wellbeing services county's social services/emergency service. County lodging is not a guaranteed cash payment. While reception services are active, the reception centre's social workers and allowance process are the relevant first contact. Kela decides Kela benefits; the county decides its supplementary/preventive assistance and social services [FI-SOC-S08, S12, S15].

## Readiness by comparison aspect

### `social-subsistence`

**Ready with qualification.** Current reception rates, income/resource treatment, private-accommodation boundary, September 2026 end rule, Kela assistance basics, unemployment reform and urgent fallback are sufficiently supported. The no-`kotikunta` post-reception regular-support gap and the individual's actual end date must remain explicit.

### `social-application`

**Ready with qualification.** Authority split, Kela non-online routes, reception-centre application, common evidence and jobseeker/payment sequence are supported. No universal process is established for a Kela claim with neither Finnish personal identity code nor usable e-ID, nor a general alternative Kela payment route without a bank account.

### `social-child-benefits`

**Ready with qualification.** Kela child benefit and selected family benefits can be described with separate coverage/child conditions and a specific TP one-year application route. The UA1e form is dated; child-abroad TP cases and individual family-benefit coverage need Kela confirmation. Do not present old examples/amounts as current.

## Open gaps

- Individual reception-centre calculation, evidence checklist, payment schedule and exact termination date; confirm directly with the responsible centre [FI-SOC-S01–S04].
- How ordinary Kela basic assistance is decided where reception ends before `kotikunta` is recorded; public Ukraine-specific guidance does not establish this case [FI-SOC-S08–S10].
- Kela permanent-residence assessment and decision timing for an individual TP family [FI-SOC-S09–S10].
- Application without Finnish personal identity code and payment without a Finnish bank account; no general alternate procedure confirmed [FI-SOC-S12–S14].
- TP eligibility for yleistuki depends on individual work history; no-work-condition reform remains a proposal on this date [FI-SOC-S16–S18].
- Current TP-specific child-benefit treatment is partly in a 2023 form; child outside Finland and particular temporary-absence situations remain unresolved [FI-SOC-S09, S23–S24].
- TP-specific assessment for parental/pregnancy allowance and child home care allowance beyond general Kela rules [FI-SOC-S25–S28].
- Local county supplementary/preventive assistance and emergency-social-service procedures vary; do not promise local cash/amount [FI-SOC-S12, S15].
- Current Ukraine Kela page contains older examples and amounts; only use individual statements corroborated by live general pages or statute [FI-SOC-S09, S23–S29].

## Editorial exclusions

Do not say that every TP holder gets monthly Kela money; a TP permit automatically pays reception allowance; reception allowance reimburses private rent; a job automatically ends every benefit; reception services continue until actual `kotikunta` registration; Kela automatically takes over when reception ends; `kotikunta`, a personal identity code, or a Kela card guarantees benefits; jobseeker registration guarantees unemployment payment; all unemployed TP holders qualify for the same yleistuki; reception clients automatically get Kela basic assistance; Kela always pays rent/deposit; every TP family/child qualifies for the same family benefits; a child in Ukraine automatically qualifies; no bank account means no benefit; Kela is online-only; or any old 2023 Ukraine threshold/amount or pre-2026 unemployment/reception rule remains current without rechecking. County emergency services do not guarantee a national cash amount.
