# Finland — Residence Options During and After Temporary Protection

Research checked: 2026-11-11  
Baseline: `e25fe0e7661b5d5be078867b68d854eaae7b8535`  
Scope: exactly `residence-routes`, `residence-conditions`, `residence-filing`.

This is an evidence record, not applicant-facing article text. “TP holder” means a person holding a Finnish residence permit issued on the basis of temporary protection. The terms **first residence permit** and **extended permit** are used as Migri uses them; they are not interchangeable.

## Research verdict

**READY WITH QUALIFICATION** for a bounded explanation of ordinary residence options available to a current Finnish TP holder. Migri expressly advises a TP permit holder who wants to remain in Finland to apply for an **extended permit** on another ground, including work, family ties, or studies, and says the applicant must satisfy that permit’s requirements [FI-RES-S01]. This is an ordinary, fee-bearing application route, not an automatic conversion or a blanket waiver of substantive rules.

The central filing distinction is decisive: a TP holder is already in Finland under a residence permit and may apply there for an extended permit on new grounds; that does **not** mean that temporary protection is a general exception allowing a person to apply in Finland for any **first** residence permit. A first permit is normally applied for abroad. The listed in-country first-permit exceptions are limited; temporary protection is an exception only for applying for temporary protection itself. A person who applies for an ordinary first permit in Finland outside an exception will usually receive a negative decision [FI-RES-S02–S04].

For current TP holders, Migri’s specific extended-permit guidance supports in-country applications on the ordinary work, study, family, and entrepreneur routes, subject to each route’s conditions [FI-RES-S01, FI-RES-S05–S11]. The EU Blue Card is materially less certain: the Blue Card Directive excludes beneficiaries of temporary protection from its scope, while a later Council Recommendation encourages Member States to allow qualifying TP holders to apply for certain EU-law authorisations if the statuses will not coexist. The reviewed Finnish sources do not explain how Finland applies that recommendation to a TP holder seeking a Blue Card [FI-RES-S12–S14].

If an extended-permit application is made while the current permit is valid, Migri says the applicant may remain in Finland while it is processed. Migri also says work rights during a timely extended-permit application usually continue under the current permit. But its TP-specific instruction says the TP work right ends when the TP permit expires. The reviewed guidance does not explicitly reconcile those statements for a TP holder whose permit expires while an ordinary extended-permit application is pending. Do not promise a continuing TP-based work right in that scenario [FI-RES-S01, FI-RES-S15–S18].

No special automatic national TP-to-ordinary conversion or Finnish post-4 March 2028 transition programme was identified in the reviewed official sources. Migri currently points holders to ordinary extended permits; EU-level transition recommendations are not themselves Finnish residence permits [FI-RES-S01, FI-RES-S13, FI-RES-S19–S20]. Individual Finnish TP permit/card validity for the period to 2028 remains a separate unresolved issue: the reviewed Migri pages give conflicting 2027/2028 mechanics. This file does not infer an individual permit’s end date from the EU scheme endpoint [FI-RES-S19–S21].

## Required distinctions

- TP work right **≠** ordinary work residence permit. [FI-RES-S01, S06–S08, S17]
- Job offer/contract **≠** residence permit or satisfaction of all permit criteria. [FI-RES-S07–S09, S28]
- Study admission **≠** study residence permit. [FI-RES-S11]
- Family relationship **≠** automatic residence permit. [FI-RES-S14]
- Enter Finland submission **≠** legal entitlement to file a first permit from Finland. [FI-RES-S02, S05]
- First permit **≠** extended permit. [FI-RES-S02–S05]
- Pending application **≠** automatic extension of temporary protection. [FI-RES-S01, S15, S17]
- Lawful stay while an extended permit is pending **≠** work right while it is pending. [FI-RES-S05–S06, S15–S18]
- Finnish permanent residence P **≠** EU long-term residence P-EU. [FI-RES-S22–S25]
- Legal residence **≠** qualifying residence time for P or P-EU. [FI-RES-S22–S25]
- Municipality of residence **≠** residence permit. Municipality registration is a separate administrative matter and is not examined further here. [FI-RES-S10, S23]
- Ordinary permit route **≠** automatic post-TP conversion. [FI-RES-S01, S03–S04, S19–S21]
- EU recommendation **≠** binding Finnish permit entitlement. [FI-RES-S12–S13]
- Asylum **≠** ordinary immigration permit. [FI-RES-S27]

## Special findings

### Employment transition

Migri expressly identifies a job as a possible new ground for a TP holder’s extended permit [FI-RES-S01]. The employed-person route requires qualifying employment terms and sufficient income; in 2026 the gross pay floor is €1,600/month and applicable collective-agreement pay must also be met. Some applications may undergo labour-market testing. The employer supplies employment terms for the application [FI-RES-S07–S08, S28].

A specialist route requires a confirmed expert job, relevant qualification or equivalent expertise, and at least €3,937 gross monthly salary in 2026. The first permit is filed abroad; only an extended permit is filed in Finland. Fast track and D visa information concern first-permit processing and do not create a TP-specific right to file a first permit in Finland [FI-RES-S09]. A contract or present TP work right does not itself change residence status [FI-RES-S01, S07–S09, S17].

### Study transition

Migri expressly names studies as a possible extended-permit basis for a TP holder [FI-RES-S01]. The study route requires acceptance to qualifying Finnish studies, sufficient funds (generally €800/month), and prescribed insurance or accepted health-cover evidence. First study permits are normally filed abroad; extended permits are filed in Finland [FI-RES-S02, S05, S11]. Admission alone does not confer study-permit status. The reviewed material supports a study-based extended application during TP but does not establish study-permit rights while pending beyond rights attached to the still-valid TP permit [FI-RES-S06, S11, S17].

### Family transition

Migri expressly names family ties as a possible new ground for an extended permit by a TP holder [FI-RES-S01]. The Finnish core family definition covers spouses, registered partners, qualifying cohabiting partners, custodians of minor children, and unmarried minor children in a sponsor’s custody. Cohabitation generally requires two continuous years unless an exception applies. Income and other route-specific criteria can apply, with different rules for some Finnish-citizen sponsors [FI-RES-S14].

An ordinary first application based on family ties can be submitted in Finland only in the identified cases of a Finnish-citizen family member or a child born in Finland. A TP holder relying on the TP-specific extended-permit route should not confuse that with a general first-permit exception [FI-RES-S02, S14].

### Entrepreneur transition

Migri’s TP-specific advice extends to new grounds generally; entrepreneur guidance confirms that extended entrepreneur permits are filed in Finland [FI-RES-S01, S26]. The route requires an eligible business role and viable business/income evidence; ownership alone is insufficient. The Economic Development Centre makes a partial decision before Migri. First entrepreneur permits are normally filed abroad. Light entrepreneurship through an invoicing service is not eligible for this permit [FI-RES-S26].

### EU Blue Card

The 2026 Finnish salary threshold is €3,937 gross/month; the route is for highly qualified employment [FI-RES-S08, S10]. Directive 2021/1883 excludes TP beneficiaries from its scope. The Council’s 2025 Recommendation asks Member States to allow otherwise-qualified TP beneficiaries to apply for certain EU-law authorisations, including Blue Cards, if TP and the new authorization are not held concurrently. The Finnish sources reviewed do not establish how Finland implements this recommendation for a person who still holds Finnish TP. Do not present Blue Card transition eligibility as confirmed [FI-RES-S12–S13].

### Permanent residence

Migri’s current guidance, effective for applications from 8 January 2026, requires an eligible continuous A-permit ground and provides several application paths with different residence, work, language, income, or degree conditions. Migri specifically says a person living in Finland only under temporary protection cannot apply for Finnish permanent residence because the required qualifying residence is under a continuous A permit [FI-RES-S01, S22–S23]. TP time therefore does not count toward the Finnish P qualifying residence period under the reviewed rule. A later ordinary permit counts only if its classification and the applicable calculation rules qualify; do not assume all new permits are A.

Migri’s TP-specific guidance also says a person living only under TP cannot apply for Finnish citizenship because the relevant residence requirement is under an A permit [FI-RES-S01]. This is only a high-level boundary; citizenship residence-time calculation and other nationality requirements are outside scope.

### EU long-term residence

Finland’s P-EU permit is a separate status. Migri requires five years under listed categories, including A or P permits, an EU-family residence card, or Brexit residence rights. Directive 2003/109 excludes people authorized to reside under temporary protection. TP residence therefore is not a qualifying basis for this five-year P-EU period [FI-RES-S24–S25]. Do not conflate P-EU with Finnish P.

### International protection

Asylum is a separate protection procedure. A current Migri notice dated 9 November 2026 says asylum applications by TP recipients will be processed; older wording on Migri’s general TP page about suspension is superseded [FI-RES-S17, S27]. This topic does not assess asylum eligibility or procedure. A possible asylum application is not a special automatic TP-to-ordinary residence conversion.

### Filing channel and identification

For a qualifying extended permit, the applicant is in Finland and can use Enter Finland or paper submission; an online submission does not change legal filing eligibility. Migri generally requires identity verification at a Migri service point after online filing, commonly within three months; first applications normally require personal identity verification at a Finnish mission abroad. A pending-application certificate can show authorities that an application is pending, but is not an entry permit or residence permit [FI-RES-S02, S05, S15]. Route-specific exceptions to an in-person identity visit can apply where Migri’s current instructions permit them; verify the selected application’s instructions.

For first permits that must be filed abroad, Migri names a Finnish mission abroad as the identity-verification location; the applicant normally waits abroad for the decision. The reviewed sources do not establish which other mission will accept a specific applicant in every country. No travel, exit, or re-entry recommendation is made here [FI-RES-S02, S14].

### Pending application / bridging rights

**Stay:** while a TP permit remains valid, the applicant’s lawful stay derives from TP, not from Enter Finland or the pending ordinary application. If a holder submits an extended-permit application in Finland before the current permit expires, Migri says they may remain while awaiting the decision [FI-RES-S05]. Migri says a first-permit applicant may wait in Finland only if legally resident when applying; a first permit is normally filed and awaited abroad, and in-country applications outside the listed exceptions usually receive a negative decision [FI-RES-S02]. The reviewed guidance does not establish a TP-specific rule for every case where the TP permit expires while a first-permit application filed during lawful TP remains pending.

**Work:** Migri says right to work under a current permit usually continues during processing if an extended-permit application is submitted before expiry; an application filed late does not preserve the old permit’s work right [FI-RES-S06, S16]. Migri separately says TP-based work right ends when the TP permit expires [FI-RES-S01]. No reviewed official source explicitly resolves whether a timely application to change from TP to an ordinary ground preserves the TP work right after the TP permit itself expires. Accordingly, the evidence supports work while the TP permit/right remains in force, but does not support a blanket promise of work after its expiry while the new application remains undecided.

**Proof:** Migri can issue a pending-application certificate, including through Enter Finland for ordinary applications. It evidences a pending case; it does not itself create a permit, entry entitlement, or independent work right [FI-RES-S15–S16].

**Refusal:** a refused ordinary application is not a grant of a replacement permit under §59. The reviewed guidance does not state a blanket TP continuation rule for all refusal/expiry timelines. If the TP permit remains valid, no source reviewed says a refusal alone automatically replaces it; if the TP permit has expired or a removal decision applies, do not promise continued stay or work [FI-RES-S01–S06, S17].

### When an ordinary permit is granted

Under current §59, if the residence purpose changes and a new fixed-term or permanent permit (or specified Chapter 10 residence right) is granted, the prior permit expires. Migri states that a person may hold only one valid residence permit or other residence right at a time and that TP work and reception-service rights end when the TP permit expires; the new permit determines future work rights [FI-RES-S01, S03–S04]. This is automatic expiry by law, not an automatic conversion. No separate cancellation decision is issued for the TP permit under this rule [FI-RES-S01].

### Post-TP / post-2028 transition scheme

The EU scheme is extended to 4 March 2028, but that EU date does not itself issue or convert an individual Finnish residence permit [FI-RES-S19, S21]. Migri’s current national advice is to apply for an ordinary extended permit on a qualifying ground [FI-RES-S01]. The Council Recommendation encourages Member States to facilitate transitions to national or specified EU-law permits while avoiding concurrent TP and new status; it is not binding national law [FI-RES-S13]. No automatic Finnish conversion or specific national post-2028 ordinary-permit scheme was identified in the reviewed sources as of 2026-11-11.

The separate individual-validity issue is unresolved: Migri’s main TP page and August 2026 release refer to permit/card validity through 4 March 2027 and promise further 2028 instructions; the Information Bank says permits are automatically extended to 4 March 2028. The sources reviewed do not reconcile the statement or establish which individual electronic permit record/card is valid. Do not use the EU endpoint or the pending ordinary application to infer that an individual TP permit has been extended [FI-RES-S17, S19–S21].
