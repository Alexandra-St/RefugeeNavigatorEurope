# Finland — Employment Rights During Temporary Protection

This evidence record is not applicant-facing article text. It separates immigration permission from ordinary Finnish employment rights and payroll obligations. It does not provide individual legal advice, tax calculations, benefit eligibility, or sector-specific wage tables.

## Research verdict

**READY WITH QUALIFICATIONS** for a practical article about TP work permission, core employer duties and Finland's minimum-pay system.

Current Migri guidance confirms that the right to work begins when the TP application is registered by police or a border control authority. Migri now expressly says the applicant receives a printed certificate of pending application that proves the right to work; if the applicant applied but did not receive it, they can request it from Migri. The right is unrestricted by field, and a granted TP permit supports entrepreneurship/freelance activity. This is distinct from arrival, a final decision, or receipt of the residence permit card [FI-EMP-S01–S04].

The Aliens Act obliges an employer to verify the right to work for foreign employees and retain workplace information about the employee and basis of work right, accessible to occupational safety and health inspectors; the information must be kept for two years after the foreign employee's employment ends. A recently effective amendment (27 October 2026) requires notification to Migri within seven days for hiring a non-EU/EEA-equivalent employee and includes employment duration, salary, hours and applicable collective agreement. Early-termination/no-start notification is separately phrased for permits granted on the basis of work; its application to TP residence permits is not established [FI-EMP-S05–S08].

Finland does not have a statutory national minimum wage [FI-EMP-S35]. The applicable generally binding collective agreement (if any) sets sectoral floors, even for an employer outside the signatory employer federation. If no binding or generally binding agreement applies and the parties have not agreed remuneration, the Employment Contracts Act requires usual and reasonable pay. No universal euro figure follows from that rule. Ordinary labour statutes apply to employment relationships without a TP-specific lower rate identified in the reviewed sources [FI-EMP-S09–S12].

The individual expiry/extension mechanics for TP residence permits/cards remain unresolved across Migri sources reviewed for the prior Finland batch. This record does not determine the 2027/2028 discrepancy or advise an employer to rely on an expired printed card plus a general extension notice. Verify current individual status through Migri before hiring/continuing where the card or permit status is unclear [FI-EMP-S03, S13–S14].

## Employer checklist

### Before hiring

- Check that the prospective worker has a current Finnish right to work. For a TP applicant with no residence card yet, current Migri guidance names the certificate of pending application as proof [FI-EMP-S01, S05–S07].
- Check the right again for a current foreign employee as required; do not assume a tax card, personal identity code or payroll registration is immigration authorization [FI-EMP-S05–S07, S21–S22].
- Determine the employment relationship, job, hours, pay and applicable collective agreement. For regulated professions, check the competent professional authority separately [FI-EMP-S03, S09, S11–S12].

### At start of employment

- Keep a copy of the foreign worker's documents and the basis of work right available at the workplace for inspection; keep the required information until two years after the foreign worker's employment ends [FI-EMP-S05, S07].
- For a non-EU/EEA-equivalent worker, submit the Migri notice with employment duration, salary, hours and applicable collective agreement within seven days of hiring; a special approved form route has a ten-day deadline [FI-EMP-S06–S07, S34].
- Inform the workplace shop steward, elected representative and occupational safety and health representative of the foreign worker's name and applicable collective agreement as §82 provides [FI-EMP-S07].
- Agree essential terms. The contract itself may be oral, written or electronic, but if the essential terms are not in writing, supply the statutory written statement within the §2:4 deadline [FI-EMP-S09, S28].

### During employment

- Ensure the right to work continues and the terms follow applicable Finnish law and collective agreement [FI-EMP-S05–S07].
- Pay on the applicable pay period and provide a calculation/payslip showing amount and basis; withhold tax according to the employee's tax card [FI-EMP-S20–S22, S27].
- Report wages and meet ordinary employer social-insurance/payroll obligations as applicable [FI-EMP-S23–S27].
- Provide safe work, risk information and sufficient induction, taking account of the worker's competence and experience [FI-EMP-S19].
- Notify Migri within 14 days only in the no-start/early-ending circumstance specified in the current Aliens Act. Its phrasing links early end to a permit granted on the basis of work; the application to a TP-based permit is not established here [FI-EMP-S06–S07, S34].

## Minimum-pay system

### Is there a national minimum wage?

**There is no statutory minimum wage in Finland.** Statutory rules instead require following the applicable binding collective agreement, including a generally binding agreement in the sector; if no applicable agreement and no remuneration was agreed, the Employment Contracts Act provides a “usual and reasonable” remuneration fallback [FI-EMP-S09, S11–S12, S35]. No euro minimum is asserted.

### How minimum pay is set

First identify the work/sector and the collective agreement that binds the employer. The agreement can determine minimum pay and related additions as well as hours, overtime, leave and other terms. A generally binding agreement applies at least to its covered work even if the employer is not a member of the signatory employer association [FI-EMP-S09, S11–S12].

### Generally binding collective agreements

The Ministry of Social Affairs and Health's General Applicability Confirmation Board confirms general applicability. The official Finlex register publishes current generally binding agreements and decisions [FI-EMP-S11–S12]. There is no single agreement for every job. The applicable agreement depends on the employer's activity and the employee's work; ask the employer which agreement it applies and verify it in the register. If none is claimed, check with the Occupational Safety and Health Administration advisory service [FI-EMP-S07, S11–S12, S32].

### If no collective agreement applies

If neither a normally binding agreement under the Collective Agreements Act nor a generally binding agreement applies, and employer and employee have not agreed on remuneration, the employee must receive **usual and reasonable pay** for the work [FI-EMP-S09]. This is a statutory fallback, not a published universal euro minimum. The statutory wording is conditional on no remuneration being agreed; this research does not convert it into a numeric minimum or decide every dispute over an agreed rate.

### Overtime / supplements

Statutory working-time and overtime entitlements apply; the applicable collective agreement can specify minimum rates, supplements, Sunday pay and more favourable arrangements [FI-EMP-S09, S11–S12, S16]. A collective-agreement wage is gross unless its terms state otherwise; net pay is affected by tax withholding and other payroll deductions [FI-EMP-S20–S25].

### How to verify applicable minimum pay

1. Ask the employer for the name of the collective agreement and the job classification/pay group it applies.
2. Check whether the agreement is listed as generally binding on Finlex and whether its scope covers this employer/work.
3. Read the agreement's gross wage floor and applicable supplements, hours and experience classifications.
4. If no agreement is identified or the answer is unclear, ask the OSH authority's employment-conditions advisory service. Do not compare a gross collective rate with net take-home pay [FI-EMP-S07, S11–S12, S32].

## Required distinctions

- TP work right ≠ residence-permit decision or residence-permit card [FI-EMP-S01–S03].
- TP application registration ≠ final TP decision [FI-EMP-S01–S03].
- Current Migri TP certificate of pending application **does** prove work right according to Migri; that does not mean any pending-application certificate automatically does [FI-EMP-S01].
- TP permit/card expiry dates remain unclear across reviewed extension sources; an expired printed card alone is not established as proof of current right [FI-EMP-S02–S03, S13–S14].
- Tax card or Finnish personal identity code ≠ immigration authorization [FI-EMP-S20–S25].
- Employer payroll notification ≠ permission to work; both are separate obligations [FI-EMP-S03, S06–S07].
- Employee ≠ self-employed entrepreneur; employee protections apply to an employment relationship as legally defined [FI-EMP-S04, S26, S33].
- Regular working time (40 hours/week general rule) ≠ maximum working time (48-hour weekly average including overtime) [FI-EMP-S15–S17].
- Statutory labour law ≠ additional collective-agreement terms [FI-EMP-S09, S11–S12].
- Gross pay ≠ net pay [FI-EMP-S20–S25].
- No national statutory minimum wage ≠ an employer may pay any amount: a binding/general collective agreement or, in the stated no-agreement/no-rate case, the statutory usual-and-reasonable-pay fallback applies [FI-EMP-S09, S11–S12].
- A collective-agreement floor is sector/work-specific, not one amount across Finland [FI-EMP-S11–S12].
- TP permission to work ≠ regulated-profession licence [FI-EMP-S03].
- Jobseeker registration ≠ permission to work; the Finland finding-a-job record addresses registration separately [FI-EMP-S03; local record: `docs/content-research/finland-batch-2026-11-11/finding-a-job/SOURCES_AND_CLAIMS.md`].
- Employment contract ≠ residence permit [FI-EMP-S01–S03, S09].

## Open gaps

- **Individual TP expiry:** Migri's official individual-permit/card/extension texts have a 2027/2028 discrepancy documented in the prior Finland TP research. This package does not reconcile it [FI-EMP-S13–S14].
- **Expired card:** no reviewed current source establishes that employer may rely on an expired printed card plus a general extension notice/database check in every case. Ask Migri to verify individual current status [FI-EMP-S02–S03, S13–S14].
- **Early end/no-start Migri notice:** current wording is tied to an employment-based permit's validity; whether it applies to a TP-based permit is not established [FI-EMP-S06–S07, S34].
- **Contract languages:** no universal language/translation duty for all terms was established. Safety induction must be sufficient; individual conditions and applicable agreements may add detail [FI-EMP-S19, S29].
- **Collective-agreement identification:** official register and statutory rule are clear; borderline scope/classification for an individual company/job requires the agreement and possibly OSH advice [FI-EMP-S11–S12, S32].
- **Collective agreement absent:** statutory fallback is “usual and reasonable” only where no binding agreement applies and no remuneration was agreed. No single benchmark/euro formula was identified [FI-EMP-S09].
- **Working time and holidays:** statutory baseline is clear, but sector/shift/transport variations and agreement-based improvements are not mapped [FI-EMP-S11–S12, S16–S18].
- **Public holiday pay:** no universal same-rate national rule established; agreement-specific terms need checking [FI-EMP-S11–S12].
- **TP-specific labour differences:** no TP-specific lower labour standard located; this is not a finding that every cross-border social-insurance/benefit rule is identical [FI-EMP-S18–S27, S33].
- **Regulated professions:** exact recognition/licensing remains profession-specific and outside scope [FI-EMP-S03].
