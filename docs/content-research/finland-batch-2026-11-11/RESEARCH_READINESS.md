## 3. Temporary Protection consistency audit

### Eligibility and military-obligation rule

The focused file supports a qualified explanation rather than a blocker: EU Decision 2026/1912 and Migri state 5 August 2026; Finnish Government Decision 751/2026 entered into force on 13 August and describes application to persons expressing intent after its entry into force. The source package explicitly says official sources do not fully reconcile the date/scope interval. Proof examples include an exit stamp and official compliance/exemption material (including Reserv+), but a complete nationwide evidence checklist, accepted formats and treatment where proof is unavailable are not established. Preserve both dates and the gap; do not silently select one date as the Finnish rule. This qualification belongs to `protection-scheme`, not a new blocker.

### Application route

The packages are consistent: the person contacts police or Border Guard in person; that authority registers the application and may issue the pending-application certificate; Migri decides, requests any further information and arranges the permit card after grant. No initial Enter Finland filing route is established. Local appointment/intake varies. The package distinguishes missing-ID registration from the ID requirement for a final decision; available documents are examples rather than a complete national checklist. No cross-topic conflict found.

### `protection-dates` — Blocked — material evidence gap

**Known:** The EU temporary-protection scheme endpoint is 4 March 2028. Migri's extension/information-bank pages state automatic extension to 2028. Other current Migri permit and card pages state 4 March 2027; prior 2027 cohort mechanics explain certain earlier extensions but do not settle current 2028 individual mechanics [Temporary Protection package, `protection-dates` matrix].

**Missing:** Reconciled current Finnish rule for the individual permit record and physical card during 2027–2028, whether a holder must take any action or obtain updated proof/card, and how conflicting Migri instructions apply to a particular cohort.

**Official evidence needed:** Current written Migri guidance or an authoritative Finnish legal/administrative clarification explaining which individual permits are extended, the effective date and cohort, card usability/replacement, holder action, and how to verify the status.

**Scope blocked:** Any unqualified assertion of an individual's Finnish residence-permit expiry, that all physical cards remain usable through 4 March 2028, that everyone must apply for a new card, or that no holder action is needed. The EU scheme endpoint must not be substituted for any of these individual facts.

**Drafting still possible:** Explain EU scheme extension through 4 March 2028, explicitly distinguish it from individual Finnish permit and card validity, disclose that current Migri pages conflict, and direct each reader to verify their own permit/card status with Migri. This supports a constrained TP article, not full comparison readiness.

## 4. Residence consistency audit

All three residence aspects remain supported with qualification. A TP holder may apply for an ordinary extended permit in Finland on another qualifying basis; it is not automatic conversion. First-permit filing remains distinct, with ordinary first applications generally filed abroad except statutory exceptions. Work, study, family, entrepreneur, P/P-EU and EU long-term-residence routes each have separate requirements; the reviewed package says TP time does not count for permanent residence where a qualifying continuous A permit is required. Blue Card availability during TP is unresolved. Timely extended-permit filing may allow waiting in Finland, but pending work rights after TP expiry are unresolved. No special automatic Finnish post-2028 conversion scheme was identified. No direct contradiction between the residence package and Temporary Protection package was found; the `protection-dates` uncertainty remains a boundary for individual timing claims.

## 5. Employment and job-search consistency

Finding-a-Job and Employment-Rights packages agree on the division of roles and timing:

- TE Offices closed on 1 January 2025; local municipal employment areas now provide services. Job Market Finland is a national platform; KEHA has distinct national functions. A person without e-ID can contact local authority, but the responsible area without `kotikunta` is not established universally.
- TP work right begins at police/Border Guard registration. Current Migri material describes a pending-application certificate as evidence; a permit card is not required before the right starts. This right does not itself make a pending applicant eligible to register as jobseeker under the ordinary route.
- Employer verification and reporting duties are separate from jobseeker registration and from the worker's permit status. 2026 employer notice rules replace old TE-office instructions; early-ending notice application to TP workers remains qualified.
- Jobseeker registration does not award unemployment benefit. Employment authority/KEHA supplies the labour-policy statement; Kela decides/pays `yleistuki`, while a fund handles earnings-related allowance under separate conditions.
- Finland has no universal statutory national minimum wage in the reviewed packages. The applicable collective agreement may set minimum pay; a generally binding agreement can apply beyond employer membership. Where no agreement applies and no wage was agreed, usual/reasonable remuneration is the bounded fallback. No universal euro minimum is supported.

No direct cross-topic contradiction found. Qualifications remain: no-kotikunta employment-area assignment, current evidence for pending TP work right, and exact TP application of the early-ending employer notification rule.

## 6. Healthcare consistency

Healthcare access and proof remain **Ready with qualification**; urgent healthcare remains **Ready**. Before a TP decision, pending adults receive urgent/essential care through reception, while minors have a broader basis. After grant, Reception Act §26 gives a TP beneficiary resident-basis healthcare; the reception centre organises it while the person remains a reception client. On reception exit, Migri operationally says centre healthcare stops, but the statute's TP/no-kotikunta healthcare basis remains. The source package does not document a uniform local provider/referral/proof/billing handoff after reception services end without `kotikunta`.

Therefore do not write that the statutory healthcare right disappears, or invent an automatic county transfer. Also do not imply that the pending certificate, residence card, personal identity code, Kela card or Kela entitlement certificate are interchangeable. Urgent-care access remains nationally established regardless of residence; it does not mean free care.

## 7. Housing consistency

The housing and social-assistance packages agree that reception accommodation is assigned subject to capacity and ends with reception-service entitlement; private accommodation is allowed but rent, deposit and utilities are not reimbursed by the reception system. Reception allowance is for immediate basic needs, not rent subsidy. No uniform separate move-out deadline was found. The housing package identifies an urgent temporary-housing route through wellbeing-services-county social emergency services; Social Welfare Act temporary housing is individually assessed and short-term, not a guarantee of long-term housing.

This is consistent with ordinary non-urgent support remaining unclear after reception exit without `kotikunta`: **urgent emergency route exists** does not mean **regular housing/income support is established**. Kela housing allowance, rental-deposit assistance, and social/state-subsidised housing each need separate criteria/application/decision. Housing applications do not guarantee allocation; Varke replaced ARA as current authority in the focused package. Rental details remain bounded by lease date for the October 2026 deposit amendment, distinguish deposit from advance rent, and do not claim a universal Finnish personal-code prerequisite or statutory home-insurance requirement.

## 8. Education consistency

The education package supports pre-primary normally at age 6 and compulsory education beginning in the school year the child turns 7, ending at 18 or earlier on qualifying completion. A TP child living in a municipality can access pre-primary/basic education without `kotikunta`; the municipality education department is the practical first contact. There is no national missing-document checklist, preferred-school or immediate-placement guarantee. Preparatory education may be offered but is not mandatory in every municipality; S2/R2 is need-based, and Ukrainian-language provision and online-school equivalence are qualified/local.

Studyinfo is a search/application route, not admission. HEIs decide foreign qualification, credits, language and incomplete-document cases. The package distinguishes granted TP tuition-fee exemption for covered foreign-language bachelor/master education from the application-fee exemption, which has a separate TP timing condition. Pending TP is not the same as granted permit; tuition exemption is not scholarship, and TP alone is not Kela student aid. No conflict found between the two fee timing rules in the focused file; preserve their separate statutory tests.

## 9. Social-assistance consistency

The social package supports Migri's 2026 household/meal reception rates, centre application, individual resource assessment, income reporting, private-rent exclusion and individually assessed supplementary reception allowance. Work/income can affect amount but work alone is not established as automatic termination. Reception allowance ends with reception services under the eligibility-based three-month clock; actual `kotikunta` registration is not the trigger, with the package's minor/extension exceptions retained.

After reception ends with `kotikunta`, Kela basic assistance may be applied for subject to its own decision. Without `kotikunta`, regular recurring support is not established in the package; urgent Kela/county support exists. This is a qualification, not a blocker for `social-subsistence`, because its practical route and limits are supported. `Yleistuki` has been current since 1 May 2026; old Kela labour market subsidy/basic unemployment allowance are legacy current-scheme labels. Jobseeker registration, labour-policy statement, and Kela/fund decision remain separate. TP work-condition boundary applies. Child benefit and other family benefits are individual; one-year TP child-benefit route is supported but partly grounded in a dated form, the child-abroad case is unresolved, and the three-year home-care rule starts in 2027.

No direct contradiction with housing or finding-a-job packages found. In particular, county urgent support should not be represented as ordinary monthly Kela support or guaranteed cash.

## 13. Drafting constraints

### Safe to state

- Current Finnish TP scheme eligibility/application channels as bounded in the focused package; in-person police/Border Guard registration and Migri decision.
- EU TP scheme endpoint 4 March 2028, provided it is explicitly separated from individual Finnish permit/card validity.
- TP holder's ability to seek ordinary extended residence permit on another qualifying basis, while distinguishing route conditions and first-permit filing.
- Local employment-service structure, JMF search, conditional job-search assistance, TP work-right start, employer duties and Finland's collective-agreement-based pay floor—within the qualifications in the matrix.
- Healthcare status routes and urgent contacts, with statutory entitlement kept separate from reception-centre operational arrangements, Kela coverage and fees.
- Reception accommodation/allowance, private-rent boundary, current 2026 rates, and the eligibility-based reception-service end clock with minor/extension exceptions.
- TP children's access to pre-primary/basic education without `kotikunta`, local application path, needs-based language support, and separate higher-education admission/fee rules.
- Kela's separate means-tested/coverage decisions, current `yleistuki` system, and urgent county/Kela fallback, without promising a regular no-kotikunta post-reception award.

### Require qualification

- Military-obligation condition: retain 5 August EU/Migri and 13 August Finnish decision dates and unresolved evidence/scope interval.
- Individual TP permit/card dates, pending TP work-right documentation, Blue Card interaction, and any work right after TP expiry.
- Employment-service area for a person without `kotikunta`, local manual process/interpreter/service availability, and employer early-ending notice application.
- Healthcare after reception exit without `kotikunta`: §26 basis remains, but no uniform local referral/proof/billing route.
- Housing placement/capacity, move-out instructions, emergency temporary accommodation, Kela housing/deposit support, and county local assistance.
- Local preparatory/Ukrainian-language education; HEI admission/credential/language/incomplete-document outcomes and distinct tuition/application fee tests.
- Kela basic assistance after reception exit, especially absent `kotikunta`; Kela without identity code/e-ID/bank account; TP unemployment work condition; TP child-benefit one-year route and child-abroad/family-benefit cases; dated Ukraine-specific guidance.

### Do not assert

- All Finnish TP permits/cards are valid through 4 March 2028, every holder's personal expiry date, or that no holder action is needed.
- Military-obligation date/proof is fully reconciled nationwide; the government decision itself took effect on 5 August; or a complete accepted-document list exists.
- A TP permit automatically converts to another residence permit or that a national post-2028 Finnish scheme has been announced.
- Pending TP applicant is automatically eligible for ordinary jobseeker registration; jobseeker registration guarantees unemployment payment; employer reporting is the worker's jobseeker process; or Finland has a universal statutory euro minimum wage.
- Healthcare is free, the statutory no-kotikunta TP healthcare basis ends with reception, or every county has the same post-exit referral/proof route.
- A reception bed/location, private rent/deposit reimbursement, long-term housing, or Kela housing award is guaranteed.
- `Kotikunta`, identity code, Kela card or TP alone automatically gives every Kela benefit; Kela automatically takes over at reception exit; or ordinary recurring support without `kotikunta` after exit is established.
- Every municipality offers preparatory/Ukrainian-language teaching, immediate or preferred school placement is guaranteed, Studyinfo admission is automatic, tuition exemption means scholarship, or TP alone grants student aid.
- Every TP family receives child/family benefit; a child in Ukraine automatically qualifies; old Kela Ukraine thresholds/amounts remain current; or the 2027 child-home-care rule already applies in 2026.

## 14. Blocking gaps

### `protection-dates` — Blocked — material evidence gap

The conflict is between **EU scheme endpoint**, **individual Finnish residence-permit validity**, **physical permit-card validity**, and **automatic extension mechanics**. The focused research establishes only the first unambiguously: EU scheme to 4 March 2028. Migri's live official materials in the package make inconsistent 2027 versus 2028 statements, and prior cohort mechanics do not resolve the current holder's status.

Before an unqualified individual-date instruction or full 24-aspect Compare use, obtain an authoritative current Migri clarification of affected permit records/cards, card use/replacement and any holder action. Until then, only the narrow EU endpoint plus explicit individual-verification warning is supportable. Other aspects with ordinary qualifications are not promoted to blockers merely because their packages list open questions.
