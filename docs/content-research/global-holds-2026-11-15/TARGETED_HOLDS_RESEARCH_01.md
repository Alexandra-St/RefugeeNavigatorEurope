## Netherlands — NL-TP-01

**Country:** Netherlands<br>
**Article:** `NL-TP-01` (`244b1055-4d8c-55b2-820e-60ff018c3b1f`)<br>
**Compare aspects:** `protection-scheme`, `protection-application`<br>
**Questions:** What military-related condition applies, to whom and from when? What happens after a negative municipal/IND triage, and what challenge route exists?

### Military scope

The Council’s amended EU decision states that, from **5 August 2026**, temporary protection may be granted only to people who satisfy their military obligations in Ukraine, with proof where applicable. The official corrigendum says the condition does not apply to people who enjoyed temporary protection in a given Member State before or on 4 August 2026 and continuously retained it **in that same Member State** afterward. [Consolidated official decision, including the 5 August corrigendum](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=CELEX%3A02026D1912-20260804) (**CURRENT**; applies from 5 August 2026 for Article 2).

IND’s current public guidance is narrower and operational: Ukrainian men aged **23–60** who are liable for military service or registered as reservists must show that they are exempt or have completed service. It describes acceptable evidence, including official electronic documents; screenshots and photographs are not accepted. IND says this new requirement is for applications from 5 August 2026, with exceptions for people who already held temporary protection in an EU country before that date and for people joining a family member who already has it. [IND temporary protection page](https://ind.nl/en/ukraine/temporary-protection-directive-ukraine) (updated 6 August 2026; **CURRENT**) and [IND announcement](https://ind.nl/en/news/temporary-protection-directive-for-ukraine-extended-with-new-condition-for-new-applications) (updated 31 August 2026; **CURRENT**).

**Official-source conflict:** the corrected Council text limits its continuity exception to protection retained in the *same Member State*. IND’s public wording describes prior protection in an EU country more broadly. The checked sources do not resolve whether IND is describing a broader national exception or simplifying the Council rule. The corrected decision does not establish a blanket exclusion of serving or former armed-forces members, conscripts, or everyone connected with the armed forces. IND’s public page does not exhaustively explain how each such status is assessed. Do not infer an automatic exclusion from military service history or membership alone.

**Safe assertion:** For new Dutch applications from 5 August 2026, IND says Ukrainian men aged 23–60 who are liable for military service or registered as reservists must provide accepted proof of completed military obligations or exemption, subject to IND’s stated exceptions. Applicants should confirm which exception applies to their own history and documents directly with IND.

**Must NOT assert:** that all men, all serving personnel, all former military personnel, all conscripts, or all people connected to the armed forces are automatically excluded; that IND’s broader exception wording is legally identical to the corrected same-Member-State exception; or that an exception is assured without IND’s individual assessment.

**Evidence ceiling:** the official sources establish the date, IND’s 23–60 category, evidence examples and the wording conflict. They do not settle the conflict or publish an exhaustive treatment of serving/former military, conscripts and other armed-forces connections. The safe assertion above avoids those unsupported conclusions.

### Negative triage / challenge procedure

The Dutch municipalities’ official handbook says it is valid from **15 June 2026**, and describes a triage interview by IND before BRP registration. A municipality may refer a case where the person may not meet Temporary Protection Directive conditions or there is doubt. A negative triage means the applicant need not be registered in the BRP and, under that route, has no right to municipal reception or the other municipal Temporary Protection facilities. The handbook says IND issues a formal written decision (*beschikking*) after negative triage. [Official municipal triage handbook](https://open.overheid.nl/documenten/ce194a40-fe32-404c-a51b-4b8cdf1bed8e/file) (**UNCLEAR** as of this report: the handbook says this method had a six-week pilot with 30 municipalities beginning 15 June; evidence checked does not confirm whether the pilot continued or became national procedure after that period).

IND’s administrative-challenge page, updated **10 August 2026**, expressly includes decisions under the Temporary Protection Directive among decisions that can be challenged by *bezwaar* (administrative objection). The person should send the objection to IND using the filing route in the individual decision; that decision sets the deadline. It also states that the decision says whether the person may await the objection in the Netherlands. If it says they may not, they may ask the court for an interim measure within **24 hours** of receiving the decision. If IND rejects the objection, the person may appeal to court; the IND decision also states whether they may await that appeal in the Netherlands, with a 24-hour interim-measure route if it says they may not. Legal help can be requested from a legal-aid provider or the Juridisch Loket; free representation is not promised. [IND objection and appeal guidance](https://ind.nl/nl/na-uw-aanvraag/bezwaar-maken-of-in-beroep-gaan) (**CURRENT**, updated 10 August 2026).

The IND page describes challenge of an IND decision generally and does not expressly say in a triage-specific sentence that every negative-triage beschikking follows that route. The handbook and IND page together support the bounded wording that a negative triage produces an IND beschikking and IND identifies Temporary Protection Directive decisions as objectionable; the specific decision must be followed for filing and the deadline. An official court ruling confirms that one applicant used objection and obtained interim relief during a pilot-related case, but the court did not decide the pilot’s legality and this individual result is not a general entitlement. [Official Rechtspraak ruling, ECLI:NL:RBDHA:2026:22484](https://uitspraken.rechtspraak.nl/details?id=ECLI%3ANL%3ARBDHA%3A2026%3A22484&idx=4&showbutton=true) (decision 30 July 2026, published 27 August 2026; **CURRENT** only as an example of that individual case).

The current 2026 sources do **not** establish work rights or municipal-reception rights while an objection or appeal is pending. The triage handbook says the municipality provides accommodation at least until the triage advice, but after a negative triage says no right to municipal reception or TP facilities under that route. An official municipal handbook dated 10 July 2024 describes an older post-registration process; because the 2026 handbook describes a different pre-registration triage and the 2024 document is not current evidence for it, do not use that older procedure to promise pending reception or work rights. [Legacy 2024 municipal guidance](https://open.overheid.nl/documenten/d8beb746-55ce-4c8e-80ca-c8dba13cc9be/file) (**LEGACY** for the 2026 triage process; reviewed only as a historical comparator, not evidence for the current procedure).

**Safe assertion:** If a municipality uses the IND triage procedure described in the June 2026 handbook, a negative triage results in an IND written decision and may prevent BRP registration and municipal reception/TP facilities under that route. IND’s general guidance lists Temporary Protection Directive decisions as objectionable; the individual decision controls the filing deadline and whether the person may remain during the challenge. Do not promise work, registration, reception, or residence while a challenge is pending.

**Must NOT assert:** that triage is the same as a final refusal or termination/revocation of an existing permit; that the described pilot remains active nationwide; that an objection automatically suspends the decision; that reception, work or residence continues during the challenge; or a general objection deadline not stated in the individual decision.

**Evidence ceiling:** the handbook establishes the process and consequences while it is used, but its six-week pilot note leaves current national operation unclear. IND establishes a general objection and appeal route and individualised stay decision, not a triage-specific guarantee of pending rights. A negative pre-registration triage must be kept distinct from a substantive refusal after registration and from later termination/revocation.

The Government.nl page’s 2027 horizon wording is superseded for the scheme horizon by the 2026 Council decision. The appointment, extension-card and Rijksoverheid pages were checked as owner sources but do not answer the military-scope or negative-triage questions.

## Spain — housing support

**Country:** Spain<br>
**Aspect/article:** `housing-financial-support` / `ES-HOUSING-01` (`bd08644f-dd27-412c-9bfd-888de1fe304d`)<br>
**Question:** Which rental supports exist, who may qualify, how are they administered, and does temporary-protection status alone qualify?

**Finding:** A national framework for rental assistance exists. Royal Decree 326/2026 regulates the 2026–2030 State Housing Plan and took effect on publication, **23 April 2026**. For its general rent aid, a non-EU applicant must have legal residence in Spain. The rent aid also requires an eligible rental/assignment agreement, the home or room to be the person’s habitual and permanent residence, registration evidence, and income within the statutory limit (generally household income no more than five times IPREM for a dwelling; regional authorities may set additional requirements). The decree sets rent ceilings and exclusions, and the Autonomous Communities and Ceuta/Melilla issue public calls and administer awards. An application or temporary-protection status by itself does not ensure payment. [Royal Decree 326/2026, State Housing Plan 2026–2030](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2026/04/22/326/con/20260423) (**CURRENT**, effective 23 April 2026; especially Articles 8, 115–122).

Separately, the Ministry’s Techo page describes rent assistance and accompaniment for displaced families, including Ukrainians, **incorporated into the International/Temporary Protection Reception System**. The Ministry says NGOs validate and support the housing arrangement, families contract directly with owners, and assistance may cover rent and in some cases deposit, insurance or agency costs. The page gives no current funding, intake, individual eligibility decision or guarantee of a place/payment. [Ministry Techo programme](https://expinterweb.inclusion.gob.es/techo/propietarios/) (**UNCLEAR**: live official page, but no publication/update date or confirmation of current capacity was found).

The owner’s existing Ministry sources describe entry into the reception system and temporary-protection status, but do not establish universal rent-aid entitlement. [Existing reception-system eligibility page](https://www.inclusion.gob.es/web/migraciones/personas-destinatarias) and [existing Ukraine temporary-protection page](https://www.inclusion.gob.es/web/ucrania-urgente/proteccion-temporal1). The latter is used only to identify the status/program context, not to assert benefit eligibility.

**Safe assertion:** Spain has regional-authority rental-aid calls under the State Housing Plan, with legal-residence, tenancy, habitual-residence/registration, income and other conditions. Temporary protection does not by itself guarantee an award. A separate Ministry/NGO-supported Techo route is described for displaced people admitted to the reception system, subject to programme assessment and availability; applicants should ask their reception-system contact and the housing authority in their Autonomous Community.

**Must NOT assert:** that Spain has no national housing assistance; that every Ukrainian temporary-protection holder qualifies; that aid is available in every region at all times; that a request guarantees money or housing; or that Techo support is available outside its reception-system/programme conditions.

**Evidence ceiling:** the State Plan establishes a national policy framework and regional calls, not a guaranteed national payment or uniform regional application window. The Techo page establishes a targeted programme description but does not establish current capacity or a universal individual entitlement.

**New official sources:** State Housing Plan (BOE) and Ministry Techo page. Neither exact URL is in the ES-HOUSING-01 registry; owner-specific source records would be needed before production citation.

**Decision: READY FOR TARGETED REVIEW.** A useful national-plus-programme statement is supported if regional administration and the non-automatic conditions are retained.

## Germany — pupil language support

**Country:** Germany<br>
**Aspect/article:** `education-pupil-language` / `DE-EDU-01` (`f5e0f499-0f60-5465-85f8-de6345029b59`)<br>
**Question:** What pupil German-language support exists, who provides it, and is it uniform or guaranteed nationwide?

**Finding:** School policy is the responsibility of Germany’s **16 Länder**. The current federal Germany4Ukraine school page directs Ukrainian families to the KMK page for school contacts and information in the Land where they live. [Germany4Ukraine school page](https://www.germany4ukraine.de/EN/bildung-und-forschung/schule/seite_node.html) and [KMK Ukraine school contacts](https://www.kmk.org/en/downloads-dokumente/statistik/schulstatistik/gefluechtete-kinder/jugendliche-aus-der-ukraine.html) (**CURRENT** official guidance; the pages do not publish one nationwide pupil-language application or guaranteed service).

KMK’s resolution of **23 June 2022** for the 2022/23 school year said Länder would use their existing Land-specific programmes for newly arrived and protection-seeking pupils and provide systematic German-as-a-second-language offers; it also said school-attendance rules follow Land requirements. [KMK resolution on schooling for children and young people from Ukraine](https://www.kmk.org/fileadmin/veroeffentlichungen_beschluesse/2022/2022_06_23-Beschulung-Kinder-Ukraine-SJ-22-23_01.pdf) (**LEGACY** for current availability: it expressly covers school year 2022/23). The owner’s current adult language-course/integration-course sources are not evidence of school-age language support and are not used for this claim.

**Safe assertion:** German-school language support and the route to access it are handled under Land-specific school arrangements. Ukrainian families should contact the responsible school authority in their Land; some Länder’s official 2022/23 framework used German-as-a-second-language and existing support programmes, but current local provision must be checked with that Land.

**Must NOT assert:** that one nationwide welcome/preparatory class exists, that every school-age child is guaranteed a specific language course or timetable, or that adult BAMF integration-course eligibility applies to pupils.

**Evidence ceiling:** current federal guidance confirms Länder responsibility and gives a current contact route. The named KMK language-support framework is dated 2022/23; these sources do not establish which particular class or language-support service is currently available in each Land. This is sufficient for a Land-specific editorial statement, not a national service guarantee.

**Existing official sources reused:** Germany4Ukraine school page and KMK Ukraine contacts (both in DE-EDU-01 registry).<br>
**New official source:** KMK 2022/23 resolution; not in the owner registry and should receive an owner-specific record if cited in future production content.

**Decision: READY FOR TARGETED REVIEW.** The Land-specific route is supported; keep current local availability qualified.

## Spain — child benefits

**Country:** Spain<br>
**Aspect/article:** `social-child-benefits` / `ES-SOC-01` (`d33628da-cb49-44b5-a7aa-ae6c6ac8f389`)<br>
**Question:** Which national child/family payments may apply, and what status, residence, household and means requirements apply?

**Finding:** The principal broadly relevant national route established in the checked sources is the **Complemento de Ayuda para la Infancia (CAPI)**, the child supplement within the Income Minimum Vital (IMV). A household with minors may qualify with IMV or, in certain cases, for CAPI without the IMV income-vulnerability threshold; the latter still requires the IMV’s other conditions and its separate income, net-asset and non-company-asset limits. A beneficiary and each household member generally need legal and effective residence in Spain continuously for at least the year before application, subject to statutory exceptions. Foreign nationals must show a qualifying residence authorisation. Household composition and income/assets are assessed; meeting the stated criteria does not itself constitute a prior award. Application is through the INSS/IMV application route. [Consolidated Law 19/2021](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2021-21007&lang=es&p=20261003&tn=0) (**CURRENT**, consolidated through 3 October 2026; Articles 10–13 and 20) and [Social Security IMV/CAPI FAQ](https://www.seg-social.es/wps/portal/wss/internet/FAQ/044f87eb-644b-41e1-a888-9c2cfafa78b8/42b9f3e8-8eea-4106-864e-2b5bdad27269/imv12nuevo) (**CURRENT**, official application/eligibility guidance).

The separate Social Security family payment for a dependent child is currently relevant to new claims principally where the child under 18 has a disability of at least 33%, or a child over 18 has a disability of at least 65%, subject to its own conditions. New claims for a non-disabled child (or disability below 33%) were closed from 1 June 2020; existing transitional recipients may continue while eligible. [Social Security family benefit guidance](https://www.seg-social.es/wps/portal/wss/internet/Trabajadores/PrestacionesPensionesTrabajadores/10967/27924/) (**CURRENT** page; the closure date is 1 June 2020).

The owner’s Ukraine page says temporary protection grants a residence authorisation; the national IMV statute accepts a residence authorisation as proof for non-EU nationals. That means a person with the required valid residence documentation can present it for the residence test, but **temporary-protection status alone does not create entitlement** and the sources do not promise an automatic qualifying start date for every individual. [Existing Ukraine protection information](https://www.inclusion.gob.es/web/ucrania-urgente/proteccion-temporal1) (**UNCLEAR** for broader current programme details; used only for the residence-authorisation context).

**Safe assertion:** Families with minors may apply to the INSS for IMV and its CAPI, or potentially for CAPI alone, if they meet the applicable residence, household, income and asset rules. A temporary-protection residence authorisation may be relevant evidence of legal residence, but it does not guarantee a payment; the INSS determines eligibility. The separate family allowance for a dependent child is not a general new-claim payment for non-disabled children; its current new-claim categories concern qualifying disability. Direct users to INSS for their documents and circumstances.

**Must NOT assert:** that all temporary-protection holders receive CAPI; that the child supplement is a universal benefit separate from IMV’s eligibility framework; that a new general non-disabled-child allowance is open; that the disability-based payment is awarded automatically; or that regional/emergency social assistance is the same as a national Social Security benefit.

**Evidence ceiling:** national law and Social Security guidance establish routes and conditions, but no TP-specific INSS circular was found guaranteeing that a particular person’s permit dates or household evidence satisfy the one-year test. Do not calculate an individual’s qualifying period or imply that application equals award.

**Existing official sources reused:** owner-registered Law 19/2021, IMV/CAPI FAQ and Ukraine-protection page.<br>
**New official source:** Social Security family-benefit guidance URL above; not in ES-SOC-01 registry and should receive an owner-specific record before production citation.

**Decision: READY FOR TARGETED REVIEW.** The CAPI route and disability-based family benefit can be described with the statutory conditions and no status-based promise.

## Denmark — employment assistance

**Country:** Denmark<br>
**Aspect/article:** `employment-assistance-conditions` / finding-jobs article (`c2356631-3fa2-4553-8fda-50a4419262c0`)<br>
**Question:** What official job-search help exists, who provides it, and what conditions or obligations apply to displaced Ukrainians?

**Finding:** Workindenmark, a national public employment service under the Danish Agency for Labour Market and Recruitment, offers international jobseekers access to English-language vacancies, information and guidance on job search and settlement, and contact with specialists by telephone, email and chat. The page does not make this service specific to temporary-protection holders or promise a job, benefit, or individual service outcome. [Workindenmark services for jobseekers](https://www.workindenmark.dk/for-job-seekers/) (updated 19 January 2026; **CURRENT**). Life in Denmark also says the person’s municipality may offer job-search help, but does not promise identical services in each municipality. [Life in Denmark, finding and taking a job](https://lifeindenmark.borger.dk/working/finding-a-job/finding-and-taking-a-job) (**CURRENT** official guidance; page refreshed within 2026).

For a person granted residence under the Ukraine Special Act, the Immigration Service assigns a municipality, which is responsible for integration, including employment efforts. [New to Denmark: after a positive decision](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB/You-have-received-an-answer/Ukraine/Ja-Ukraine/?anchor=971832788A114D1A8BAD78CBF5142E22&callbackAnchor=F5EF68CA41B148B4BCDBBD8B4B3C8B9C971832788A114D1A8BAD78CBF5142E22&callbackItem=FE525DC60F8E41D2B12EFD5F3D841817) (**CURRENT** official guidance; page undated). The current Integration Act text provides for a municipal programme for covered adults and employment-oriented offers in specified circumstances. The official legislative explanation states that holders under the Ukraine Special Act were not included in the 37-hour work obligation introduced for certain cash-assistance recipients; they remained subject to the existing integration-programme or employment measures relevant to their case. [Integration Act, Consolidated Act No. 453/2026](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2026/453/pdf) (**CURRENT**, 16 April 2026) and [Act No. 1654/2024](https://www.retsinformation.dk/eli/lta/2024/1654/pdf) (**CURRENT amendment, effective 1 January 2025 for the relevant provision).

As of this report date, Folketinget’s **L 43 (2026–27)** proposal to change some special conditions, including work-obligation coverage, was before Parliament and had reached first reading; it is a proposal, not current law. [Folketinget bill L 43 status](https://www.ft.dk/samling/20261/lovforslag/L43/index.htm) (**CURRENT as a legislative-status page**, but the bill itself is not law). Do not present its proposed future rules as already effective.

**Safe assertion:** International jobseekers can use Workindenmark’s public job-search information and guidance. After a Ukraine Special Act residence permit is granted, the assigned municipality is responsible for integration and employment efforts; the actual measures and any participation requirements depend on the person’s programme and circumstances. As of 15 November 2026, do not describe the proposed L 43 work-obligation changes as in force.

**Must NOT assert:** a guaranteed municipal job-search service or outcome; that Workindenmark’s services are a Ukraine-only entitlement; that every displaced person must complete a 37-hour work obligation; that receiving any benefit automatically triggers identical duties; or that pending legislation is effective law.

**Evidence ceiling:** national sources establish a general service for international jobseekers and municipal responsibility for integration/employment efforts after a Special Act permit. They do not set out a uniform service package across municipalities or all individual benefit-related participation duties. L 43 is pending legislation on the report date; future changes require a later freshness check.

**Existing official sources reused:** Workindenmark jobseeker page and Life in Denmark job-search page.<br>
**New official sources:** New to Denmark post-grant guidance, current Integration Act, Act No. 1654/2024, and Folketinget L 43 status page. These URLs are not in the finding-jobs owner registry; owner-specific records would be needed before production citation.

**Decision: READY FOR TARGETED REVIEW.** The statement can distinguish general public guidance from municipality-led, individual employment measures and expressly exclude unpassed proposals.
