# Temporary protection in Denmark: how to apply and extend your permit

**Summary:** Denmark’s Ukraine Special Act is a national temporary-residence scheme with specific eligibility and application rules. This guide explains the application steps, conditional rights while waiting, practical steps after approval, and an unresolved conflict between official extension dates. Checked 15 October 2026; draft only.

## Who can apply

Denmark’s Ukraine Special Act is a national scheme. Denmark does not participate in the EU Temporary Protection Directive. A protection permit or status from another EU country does not by itself establish eligibility under Danish law.

The Danish Immigration Service says a person may qualify under section 1 if, for example, they are a Ukrainian citizen who left Ukraine on or after 1 February 2022 and had residence there on or after that date; a person recognised as a refugee in Ukraine on 24 February 2022 who left Ukraine on or after 1 February 2022; or a Ukrainian citizen or such a recognised refugee who was in Denmark or held Danish residence permission on 24 February 2022. The exact dates and evidence matter. The agency says some people who lived elsewhere and did not return to Ukraine before leaving may normally not meet the section 1 departure condition.

A non-Ukrainian may have a route if they were recognised as a refugee in Ukraine on the relevant date, or may qualify as a close family member under section 2. Other citizenship or a residence permit in another country can affect the assessment. These rules are fact-specific; the list is not a decision about any reader’s eligibility.

Special rules apply to Ukrainian men aged 23–59 who are subject to Ukraine’s mobilisation rules. The Immigration Service says they must document that they are exempt from military service and have fulfilled their military obligations. Some permits for people under 23 may be limited until they become subject to mobilisation rules. Check the current official criteria if this may apply to you.

## How to submit an application

For a section 1 application, the Immigration Service says you must be in Denmark. A qualifying section 2 family application may be submitted in Denmark or abroad. The Immigration Service decides applications under the Special Act.

The main applicant uses form SL1. The current official page offers a digital application and printable paper versions. The form says there is no application fee for a person applying as displaced from Ukraine. Applicants are asked for identity documents; depending on the case, evidence of refugee status in Ukraine, children’s documents, or a family relationship may also be needed. Family members who use section 2 have a separate SL2 form and requirements.

After submitting, book the required appointment for an identity check and biometrics. The current page gives a four-week deadline. It describes an exception to repeating biometrics for some people who already hold an asylum or accommodation card. Children applying with an adult must attend when the Immigration Service requires an identity check. Follow the live page and your appointment instructions, since the procedure can change.

The agency says an application is received when it reaches the Immigration Service. It currently gives an average maximum processing-time target of 35 days; this is not a promise, and some cases take longer. The agency will contact you if it needs more information.

## While waiting for a decision

A submitted application is not an approved residence permit. The Immigration Service says an applicant under the Special Act may stay in Denmark while the case is processed. An asylum centre may be available, but staying there is not required; private accommodation with family or friends is also described. Do not assume that an application gives a right to re-enter Denmark after travel. Check the current travel and re-entry rules before leaving.

The right to work while waiting is conditional. The Immigration Service says it begins after your fingerprints and facial image have been recorded, and does not apply if you obviously cannot qualify under section 1 or 2. If you or an employer is unsure whether this exclusion applies, the agency says not to start work before a decision.

The Immigration Service describes access to certain healthcare services during processing if you can show proof of your application. The scope is separate from healthcare access after approval; see the Denmark Healthcare guide and current official health guidance for details.

## What happens after approval

If your application is approved, read the decision and the documents issued to you. The Immigration Service says a Special Act permit allows work in Denmark. It may also assign a municipality responsible for integration and housing. A requested municipality is not guaranteed, and people who already have another Danish residence permit may be treated differently.

Ask the assigned municipality or its Citizen Service how to register your Danish address and CPR. The steps and documents depend on your residence basis, address, and municipality. A residence permit, CPR registration, and any residence card are separate records; one does not replace the others. For practical healthcare, housing, and financial-support information, use the existing Denmark guides and confirm local instructions with your municipality.

If a family member has their own application or permit, check their decision and conditions separately. Do not assume that one person’s approval automatically grants a permit or identical rights to relatives.

## Extension and permit validity

You do not normally submit an individual extension application under the current extension regulation. BEK no. 828/2026, effective 1 October 2026, says a Special Act permit may be extended to 17 March 2028 unless grounds for revocation under section 10 apply. It says the Immigration Service decides on its own initiative and that the case has suspensive effect while processed. This is a possible extension date under the regulation, not a guarantee that every permit holder has that personal expiry date.

There is an unresolved conflict in official public information. The regulation states 17 March 2028, while the Immigration Service’s application and family pages still state 17 March 2027. Its extension guidance also contains old March 2026 processing dates. The sources have not reconciled these statements. Do not infer your own permit’s validity from either general page: check your decision and the latest written message from the Immigration Service, and ask the service which date applies to your case.

As of 15 October 2026, bill L 43 had been introduced but had not been enacted. It proposes possible restrictions for some new applicants from less war-affected areas of Ukraine and changes to other Special Act rules. These proposals are not current law. Check the bill’s status and any final enacted text before relying on future guidance.

The rule may also differ because of individual circumstances, including the recent military-status provisions and section 10 revocation grounds. Recheck current official guidance before relying on an extension date.

## Family members and special circumstances

Section 2 may cover specified close family members of a person with a section 1 permit, including a cohabiting spouse or partner, an unmarried child under 18, and certain dependent or otherwise qualifying close relatives. The agency says the relevant family life must have existed on 24 February 2022. Some children may apply with a parent; other family members may need their own SL2 application. Each relationship and set of documents is assessed under its own conditions.

If an application is refused, read the decision immediately. The Immigration Service’s public page describes an appeal to the Immigration Appeals Board, an eight-week appeal deadline, and a separate seven-day condition for the stated right to remain while an appeal is processed. That page was last updated in 2023, so verify the current deadline and effect in the decision and current official instructions. Do not assume that filing an appeal automatically permits a person to stay.

Tell the Immigration Service if it requests more information, and use the contact channel for “Displaced from Ukraine.” A permit holder should also check requirements concerning residence in Denmark, travel, and changes in personal circumstances. This guide does not determine how a rule applies to an individual family.

## Where to get help

- For current eligibility, application forms, appointments, and case questions, use the Danish Immigration Service’s “New to Denmark” Ukraine pages.
- For a section 2 family application, use the official family-member page and SL2 instructions.
- For address registration and CPR, ask the municipality or International Citizen Service that handles your address, and consult Life in Denmark.
- For healthcare, housing, and social assistance after approval, see the separate Denmark guides; confirm case-specific requirements with the responsible authority.
- Before relying on a general extension date, ask the Danish Immigration Service to confirm the date in your own decision or written case communication.

This draft is general information, not individual immigration or legal advice. It does not recommend which application a person should file.
