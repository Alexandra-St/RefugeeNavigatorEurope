## Research findings with release impact

- The Act of 23 January 2026 (Journal of Laws 2026 item 203) took effect for the relevant transition on 5 March 2026. The former PESEL UKR / certificate arrangements and separate residence routes must not be treated as interchangeable.
- The 31 August 2026 identity-document deadline applied to the defined group whose PESEL UKR record was based only on a declaration without presenting a valid travel document. It has passed. UdSC says affected people who did not comply lost UKR status and Polish temporary protection from 1 September 2026. It is not a universal deadline for all PESEL UKR holders.
- UdSC says CUKR has been available since 4 May 2026, is a three-year temporary permit, requires four cumulative conditions for the general group, has separate child rules, and must be applied for online through MOS by 4 March 2027.
- Council Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/1912, directly read in the Official Journal PDF, extends EU temporary protection until 4 March 2028 and applies the extension from 5 March 2027. MRPiPS guidance says PESEL UKR lawful stay runs to 4 March 2027. The drafts distinguish EU and Polish legal levels and leave the effect on individual Polish records unresolved; the EUR-Lex HTML remains blocked, but the operative Official Journal PDF was accessible.
- From 5 March 2026, health access described by NFZ is limited by category unless a separate compulsory-insurance title applies. PESEL UKR does not promise universal public care.
- The 2026 800+ rules include a work-activity condition for UKR recipients from 1 February and a Polish-education condition for school-age children, with stated exceptions and limited cases for registered unemployed parents.
- Official sources frequently provide national law alongside local execution. Housing, job-office services, preschool access and language learning must be checked with the responsible local authority.
