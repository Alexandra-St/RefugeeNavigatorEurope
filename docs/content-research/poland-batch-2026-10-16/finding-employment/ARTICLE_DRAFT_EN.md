# Finding a Job in Poland

> Research draft — unpublished; pending human editorial review. No content in this draft is approved or marked Verified.

**Summary:** Start with the public ePraca listings and your local powiat employment office. Access to particular services depends on registration, status and location.


## RULES

ePraca is the public job database run by the Ministry of Family, Labour and Social Policy. It lists vacancies, internships and work placements, and information about job fairs, events and training. A listing does not confirm the hiring conditions or your authorization for a particular job.

## TASKS

Prepare a concise CV in Polish or another language, with contact details and a clear account of your experience, education and skills. Check language requirements, schedule, work location and contract type. If the occupation is regulated or an employer asks for recognition of a qualification, confirm the process with the relevant authority or institution before relying on that route.

## PLACES

Search ePraca and contact your local powiat employment office (PUP) to ask about counselling, training or job-matching services. The Ministry also describes free support through National Integration Centres (KBI), including administrative, legal and employment guidance and Polish-course enrolment; KBI do not provide housing or cash. Eligibility to register as unemployed or a jobseeker, and access to each service, depend on current rules and your circumstances; 2026 changes affected some registration categories.

## NEXT STEPS

Ask your PUP which documents it needs for registration or advice. These may include identity, education and work-history records and, where relevant, proof of lawful stay. Confirm the list locally. You can use ePraca to look for vacancies without assuming that PUP registration gives you access to support payments, insurance or a particular service.
