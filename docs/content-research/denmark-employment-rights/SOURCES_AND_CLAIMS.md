# Denmark employment rights and conditions — research register

**Research checked:** 15 October 2026 (Europe/Copenhagen). Exact pages below were opened directly on that date unless a source-specific note says otherwise. This is research and draft material, not individualized legal advice or a published article. The subject is time-sensitive; recheck before publication.

## Scope and method

This research is for people displaced from Ukraine who are considering or doing paid work in Denmark. It prioritizes official Danish authorities and public information services. It does not repeat the existing Denmark × Employment article’s job-search instructions, the existing temporary-protection application guide, or detailed social-assistance guidance. The existing content workflow was followed: each user-facing statement is tied to a direct official page, conditions are retained, and uncertainty is disclosed rather than resolved by assumption.

The exact L 43 parliamentary overview was reopened on 15 October 2026. The overview page showed “Status: Fremsat” (introduced), introduced 8 October 2026, with first reading scheduled for 13 November 2026. It was not enacted on the check date. The bill’s full proposed text could not be reopened in this research session, so no proposed operative clause or commencement date is summarized here. The proposal is not current law.

## Key legal distinctions and editorial exclusions

- **Application, biometrics, permit and work:** S01 makes biometrics plus the specific “not obviously unable to qualify” limitation material while a section 1 or 2 application is pending. A submitted application alone is not an unconditional permission. A permit grant is a separate event. The drafts preserve this order.
- **Work basis and job change:** the reviewed Special Act pages confirm work for permit holders but do not set out a complete rule for every job change or status transition. No blanket “change employers requires a new permit” statement is made. Individuals should check their own decision and ask the Immigration Service/SIRI if the basis changes, expires, is revoked or is unclear.
- **Employment terms:** Act no. 501/2023 §1 generally covers employees over the three-hours/four-consecutive-weeks threshold and also covers work without a predetermined guaranteed amount of paid work. §3 deadlines are for delivering different categories of information after work starts, not a minimum job duration. Statutory seafarer and qualifying collective-agreement exceptions apply. S08 remains a live official page but its one-month/eight-hours threshold is obsolete; the statute, not S08, supports the corrected draft.
- **Wages:** no statutory minimum wage does not mean there is no enforceable wage. Agreed/collective terms still matter. The drafts do not set a salary or overtime multiplier.
- **Sick leave and holiday:** neither a universal full-salary sick-leave promise nor a guaranteed holiday payout is supported. Employment category, accrual, contract/agreement and statutory criteria matter.
- **Tax versus status:** tax-card/CPR procedures do not create or replace permission to work. SKAT’s route depends on the person’s documentation and registration.
- **A-kasse versus municipality:** unemployment insurance is voluntary and administered by a-kasse with separate eligibility conditions. Municipal financial assistance is a distinct topic covered in the existing social-assistance article and is not repeated here.
- **Safety versus pay complaints:** Arbejdstilsynet is the authority for workplace environment and safety, not a general wage tribunal. Trade unions may advise members; membership is voluntary and does not guarantee an outcome.
- **Work-injury reporting as of 15 Oct 2026:** for reports made on/after 1 Jul 2026, current Act no. 667/2026 §31 (as amended by Act no. 1626/2025) and the current Danish Arbejdstilsynet/AES pages set a 3-day absence ground plus a separate likely-benefits ground. Employer deadlines are generally 14 days, with the start date depending on the ground; an injured person/survivor can self-report within one year if the employer has not reported, subject to the rules.
