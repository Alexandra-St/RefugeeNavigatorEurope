# Romania Healthcare — Sources and Claims

**Research checked:** 2026-11-05. **Scope:** focused evidence for future Romania healthcare content and the `healthcare-access`, `healthcare-proof`, and `healthcare-urgent` comparison aspects. This is research, not individual medical, insurance, or legal advice, and not publication approval. Romania still has no production catalog records. No catalog, source, article, fact, code, test, visibility, or status files were changed.

## Research verdict

**Ready** — current Romanian law and CNAS guidance support a bounded explanation of healthcare access for people covered as temporary-protection beneficiaries or previous beneficiaries under OUG 96/2024, the provider-side registration/proof process, the separate contribution rule for income subject to CASS, and the emergency route. Future copy must keep the statutory cohort and service-package limits explicit; it must not equate access under OUG 96/2024 with a blanket finding that someone has ordinary insured status.

## Unresolved boundaries

- **CNAS registration mechanics:** legislation assigns the registration duty to contracted providers and requires retention of identity/TP documents. The CNAS guide describes identifier assignment and reuse, but local provider workflows, system outages, unusual documents, and case-specific proof can vary. Do not publish a universal checklist or tell everyone to complete self-registration first.
- **CNP / no identity document:** the older CNAS application manual documents a no-CNP path; the 2025 guide and law do not provide an exhaustive public-facing checklist for every edge case. The law allows provider medical records where a person lacks identity documents. Current operational handling should be confirmed with the provider/CNAS if this becomes a specific article claim.
- **Employment and CASS:** OUG 96/2024 Art. 4(8)–(9) makes the payroll/contribution boundary explicit from income earned 2025-01-01 onward. The reviewed materials do not say that contribution payment cancels Art. 4 access or automatically changes the person’s ordinary insured-status record. Do not infer either outcome.
- **Local availability:** statutory entitlement does not promise a local family doctor, appointment, contracted provider, hospital capacity, or language support. CNAS guidance says a family doctor can provide package services without adding the person to the doctor's own list; that is not a guarantee of a particular doctor.
- **Medicines and devices:** law provides package/programme-covered items and waives the specified ambulatory personal contribution/copayment, but prescriptions, approved lists, protocols, programme inclusion and contracted pharmacies/providers govern. Do not say all medicines are free or guaranteed.
- **Dental and mental health:** no separate TP-specific entitlement beyond applicable package/programme rules was established for broad article claims. Keep these out unless a precise, current service claim is separately evidenced.
- **Children, pregnancy, chronic conditions and vulnerability:** the general package and CNAS guide identify relevant services, but no distinct universal entitlement or priority pathway for the TP cohort was established. A specific service, eligibility rule, fee, or priority claim needs its own source.
- **Emergency vs. urgent / routine care:** 112 and emergency response are distinct from routine care and non-emergency advice. The reviewed sources do not establish a nationwide non-emergency medical advice line; do not invent one or make routine care sound unavailable.
- **Cohort boundaries and later status changes:** apply the OUG 96 definitions of beneficiary/previous beneficiary. Do not assume a person's TP document, employment, expiry, asylum application, or other status resolves every healthcare/insurance question without checking the applicable statutory category.

## Drafting constraints

1. Keep entitlement under OUG 96/2024 separate from ordinary CNAS insured-status classification and from contribution liability.
2. State that covered beneficiaries/previous beneficiaries have the statutory package without paying CASS for that entitlement; separately explain that income subject to CASS carries a contribution obligation from 2025-01-01.
3. Do not say all healthcare is free. Name the basic package/programmes and retain prescription, package, provider-contract and clinical conditions.
4. Do not describe TP healthcare as “emergency only.” Conversely, do not imply every routine service is immediately available everywhere.
5. Describe CNAS registration as provider-side at first request; do not require a person to self-register with CNAS or to have a CNP unless current official instructions for that situation explicitly say so.
6. Mention the no-referral rule only for specialist clinical outpatient specialties within the basic package.
7. Keep children/pregnancy/chronic-care statements within the same verified package provisions; do not promise special priority or a particular service.
8. Use 112 only for an emergency requiring response, not for routine care or general health advice.
9. Do not promise coverage of every medicine, dental treatment, mental-health service, a named doctor, appointment, or treatment outcome.
10. Before publication, recheck the consolidated OUG 96/2024 text, current CNAS operational instructions, and the current service-package rules; this research reflects the official materials checked on 2026-11-05.
