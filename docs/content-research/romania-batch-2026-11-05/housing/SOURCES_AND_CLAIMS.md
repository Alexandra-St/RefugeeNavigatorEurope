# Romania Housing — Sources and Claims

Research checked **5 November 2026**. This is an evidence matrix for future drafting, not article copy, individualized legal advice, or a promise of accommodation or financial support. The reviewed rules distinguish statutory emergency accommodation, ordinary private renting, and a time-limited accommodation sum. Local availability and individual outcomes are not established by national legislation alone.

## 1. Research verdict

**Ready** for carefully bounded drafting of `housing-initial`, `housing-private-rental`, and `housing-financial-support`.

The evidence supports describing a narrow temporary-camp route for a specified first-arrival cohort; ordinary written rental agreements and key contract terms; landlord-side tax registration; a current, limited three-month accommodation sum with procedural and later-month conditions; and locally allocated social housing. It does **not** establish that a particular reception site is operating or has a place, that a temporary-protection holder qualifies for social housing, or that there is a general continuing rent subsidy. The exact application route for current accommodation locations should be checked with the competent local authority/IGSU before giving operational directions.

## 6. Unresolved boundaries

- **Local accommodation in practice:** The statutes establish the mechanism, but the reviewed sources do not establish which OUG 96 temporary camps or designated locations accept referrals on 5 November 2026, current capacity, or referral contacts. IGI’s May 2026 centre-occupancy report is not a site list or vacancy guarantee.
- **Location and duration:** No chosen city, facility, accommodation type, or indefinite stay is supported. Keep each statutory time boundary attached to its specific mechanism; do not infer an automatic exit date from a short news item.
- **Expired Ukraine programmes:** OUG 15/2022 was repealed. OUG 96/2024 includes transitional provisions for prior beneficiaries, but these do not establish continuation of the prior general 50/20 programme in November 2026.
- **Address documents:** The previously reviewed TP research supports only the declared-locality fallback in specified circumstances. A complete current IGI checklist for address/residence evidence, and every acceptable landlord declaration/lease/title combination, was not rechecked in this housing phase.
- **Deposit rules:** OUG 40/1999 includes a three-month limit, but its application to ordinary new market rentals was not sufficiently established. Do not state a universal cap pending a focused legal scope check.
- **Rent caps:** No general cap for ordinary private-market rent was established. Statutory limits for specified state/local or special categories must not be applied to all private leases.
- **Utilities and charges:** The written contract should set out what parties agree, but the reviewed sources do not establish a universal utility-cost allocation.
- **Termination and disputes:** Housing Law and OUG 40/1999 contain relevant provisions, but tenancy type, contract terms, and the scope of special regimes matter. Avoid giving categorical notice, eviction, or court-route guidance without confirming the applicable route and current procedure.
- **Social/public housing:** Local annual allocation, income criteria, eligibility evidence, waiting lists, and capacity are municipality-specific. TP status alone was not shown to qualify an applicant.
- **Indirect social assistance:** OUG 96/2024 Art 17 allows an application for minimum inclusion income after the three-month sum period, subject to the separate governing law. Detailed current eligibility, amount, and whether a payment can meet a particular household’s rent were not researched.
- **Current support availability:** The law and implementation text remain in force in the accessed versions, but this review did not verify local application-system uptime, current budget execution, individual eligibility decisions, or processing time.

## 7. Drafting constraints

- Separate legal access to a first-response camp from actual referral, local site operation, and available capacity.
- Do not promise state accommodation, a particular city, location, room type, waiting period, or housing for an unspecified duration.
- Keep private rental separate from reception accommodation and temporary designated locations.
- Describe the accommodation sum as a narrow, time-limited statutory measure. Include the beneficiary cohort, three-month limit, application, later-month conditions, and exclusions at a level appropriate to the article.
- Do not use the historic “50/20” support scheme or describe the OUG 96 transitional sums of 500/1,500 lei as the ordinary current scheme.
- Do not state that there is no rent support; say that the reviewed sources establish the specific three-month sum and do not establish a general continuing rent subsidy.
- Do not promise reimbursement of actual rent or payment to a private host. Explain provider reimbursement only for the designated-location path if it is useful.
- Present minimum inclusion income as a separate apply-for benefit, not a housing allowance; detailed eligibility needs its own research.
- Distinguish address details recorded on a TP permit from a housing right, landlord consent, and accommodation placement. A missing-address-proof fallback is not a general waiver of identity/eligibility documents.
- Tell renters to use a written agreement and clarify rent, duration, occupants, utilities, maintenance, and payment arrangements. State that the qualifying lessor has the 30-day ANAF registration duty; do not shift it to the tenant.
- Do not announce a universal three-month deposit cap or a universal rent cap until the applicable statutory scope is confirmed.
- Keep social-housing statements conditional on local criteria, income and priority rules, and available stock; do not imply TP beneficiaries automatically qualify.
