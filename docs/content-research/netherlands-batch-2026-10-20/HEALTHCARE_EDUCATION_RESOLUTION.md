# Netherlands Phase 18.1E — Healthcare and Adult Dutch-Language Evidence Resolution

**Research date:** 20 October 2026 (UTC; exact check time not recorded)
**Scope:** Targeted evidence review of healthcare insurance for working temporary-protection holders and adult Dutch-language learning. This is research only—not legal advice, an individual insurance/course decision, article drafting, publication approval or permission to integrate content.

## Executive result

- **Healthcare insurance remains CONFLICTING.** The targeted review clarifies why official pages differ: parliamentary and ministerial explanations describe employed displaced people as formally liable under the Zorgverzekeringswet (Zvw), while the government chose not to enforce individual policy-taking while those people remain under the Regeling medische zorg ontheemden uit Oekraïne (RMO). Current TPD-specific Rijksoverheid guidance says a Dutch policy is not needed, but UWV's general page still says workers must take one out. The current consolidated Zvw text was not directly retrievable, and the exact work/status triggers—including self-employment—remain unresolved. Do not advise a person to take out, retain, cancel or change insurance.
- **Adult Dutch-course navigation is conditionally supported.** Current municipal guidance documents routes for the specified BRP-registered Ukrainian TPD adult cohort, but municipalities decide whether to offer courses. No national course/place, free course, current 2026 funding amount or online option is established. This supports a bounded article draft that signposts readers to their municipality or local adult-education provider and excludes promises.
- All **38 Claim IDs** and **24 comparison aspect IDs** remain unchanged. No claim verdict changed. `NL-HEALTH01-01` remains `CONFLICTING`; `NL-EDU01-03` remains `CONDITIONAL` with expanded evidence boundaries. Education readiness changes to **READY FOR BOUNDED DRAFT**; healthcare remains **REQUIRES TARGETED RESEARCH** for the complete scope, although a narrower draft may omit insurance advice.

## 1. Healthcare — ordinary insurance and RMO

### What the directly reviewed official material says

1. **Statutory scheme basis.** Article 2(2) of the *Tijdelijke wet opvang ontheemden Oekraïne* assigns the Minister responsibility for covering displaced people's medical-care costs through a designated health-cost scheme and ensuring care is available. The article establishes the RMO scheme basis; it does **not** itself state how Zvw insurance duties apply to employed people. The statute was promulgated 19 June 2024 and published in *Staatsblad* 2024, 190 on 27 June 2024.

2. **Formal position described for employees.** A State Secretary's 4 June 2024 parliamentary letter says displaced people under temporary protection—except workers—are outside Dutch social security, not insured under the national-insurance schemes and have no Zvw insurance obligation; it says non-working people may not take out Dutch insurance. It separately says workers are treated as insurance-liable because of work, pay the income-related Zvw contribution and may take out a policy, but remain under RMO to avoid implementation problems. A second State Secretary's 5 March 2024 letter states that the government decided **not to enforce** the policy-taking requirement for working displaced people while all displaced people remained under RMO; the stated reason is the operational burden when work alternates and coverage/premium administration would otherwise switch back and forth.

3. **Later ministerial explanation.** A 1 July 2025 Ministry of Asylum and Migration decision memorandum repeats the distinction: workers are described as insurance-liable but remain under RMO to avoid implementation problems; transition into Zvw was not feasible short term and was being explored long term. This is a policy explanation, not an amendment repealing the Zvw rule.

4. **Current public guidance still differs.** Rijksoverheid's current provider guidance says workers may take out an ordinary policy, says Ukrainian refugees do not need to do so, and identifies RMO coverage for people with a BSN and title code 46; the page says RMO continues at least until March 2028. CAK's provider FAQ says working Ukrainians can choose to retain insurance. UWV's current page still says TPD-covered people “must take out” Dutch health insurance. The UWV page shows no update date in the inspected view. Provider-facing reimbursement information is not an individual coverage determination.

5. **Cohorts and separate routes.** The current RMO guidance defines its route using BRP/BSN and title code 46 for the described temporary-protection cohort. The sources do not support treating every Ukrainian, every third-country national or every person with a different Dutch status as the same cohort. RMO, RMA for asylum applicants, CAK's SOV provider reimbursement for qualifying care without BSN, OVV for specified uninsurable-foreigner cases, and ordinary Dutch insurance are separate arrangements. This review does not establish patient-level coverage under any of them.

### What remains unresolved

- The distinction between a **formal Zvw liability** and the decision **not to enforce policy-taking while the person remains under RMO** explains much of the apparent conflict, but it does not provide a safe categorical answer for every individual. The current consolidated Zvw text could not be directly fetched from the official legislation portal in this review; the Wetten.nl link returned a cache-miss error. The enacted Temporary Act was directly read, but it establishes the separate medical-scheme authority rather than resolving the Zvw rule.
- The parliamentary explanation focuses on employed people. No source reviewed here establishes whether or when self-employment creates the same Zvw position for a person under TPD. Work authorization and insurance classification must not be conflated.
- The letters explain the practical problem of workers moving between work and non-work periods, but do not establish precise individual start/stop dates or a universal notice/action rule for employment, self-employment, a code/status change or the end of TPD.
- Current public Rijksoverheid guidance says special provisions, including RMO, are intended to stop when TPD ends and that people with a residence permit or transit document can then obtain ordinary insurance. The transit-document details remain government planning in the existing research record; this is not an operational transition procedure. Do not infer a person's individual insurance start date from the EU-wide TPD horizon or a public planning date.

### Healthcare verdict and drafting boundary

`NL-HEALTH01-01` remains **CONFLICTING** and `healthcare-access` remains **CONFLICTING**. No status change is recorded. The current matrix now documents the formal-liability/non-enforcement distinction and the remaining public-page disagreement. A complete article remains **REQUIRES TARGETED RESEARCH**. A narrower healthcare draft is possible if it omits advice or conclusions about whether a working person should take out, keep, cancel or change ordinary insurance; it may describe only the bounded RMO route, service conditions and emergency/GP navigation already supported by the matrix. RMO coverage is not a claim that all care is free.

## 2. Adult Dutch-language learning

### Verified route and limits

The January 2026 version 8.3 of the inter-ministerial/VNG *Naslagwerk Gemeentelijke Opvang Oekraïners* (GOO), Part D, section 3, says Ukrainian displaced adults aged 18+ who are registered in the BRP can access adult education, including existing municipal courses funded under the Wet Educatie en Beroepsonderwijs (WEB). It says municipalities may offer Dutch-language courses and decide whether to make an offer to this group using WEB funds. For people living in municipal reception (GOO), municipalities may also provide optional educational activities, including Dutch lessons, as activities in the reception setting. This is a local, discretionary route, not a nationwide course guarantee.

Current Rijksoverheid public guidance says the described temporary-protection stay does not create a civic-integration duty because it is temporary. Course participation is therefore not a mandatory integration programme under that guidance. Do not generalize this rule to people on another residence basis.

A 1 July 2025 ministerial decision memorandum records €10 million through WEB for Dutch-language learning by displaced people **in 2025**. This is historical funding evidence only; it does not establish an available 2026 amount. DUO's 2026/27 course-fee remission is a separate conditional MBO course-fee route and is not a right to free Dutch courses.

### Practical access and exclusions

- A bounded navigation statement is: an adult in the specified cohort can ask their municipality or local adult-education provider whether a current Dutch course is offered, what registration and residence/BRP requirements apply, and whether there is a fee. The source set does not specify a national application form or a guaranteed place.
- No current source verified a nationwide free programme, current 2026 funding amount, universal eligibility beyond the stated route, online course offer, or uniform regional registration process. Do not claim any of those.
- Keep school-age newcomer lessons separate from adult learning; keep civic-integration duties separate from voluntary courses; and keep DUO MBO fee remission separate from language-course pricing.

`NL-EDU01-03` remains **CONDITIONAL**; its evidence text now records both the child-language boundary and the conditional adult municipal route. `education-pupil-language` remains **CONDITIONAL** and is explicitly limited to pupil support. NL-EDU-01 is now **READY FOR BOUNDED DRAFT** if adult learning is presented only as municipal/provider navigation, with local variation and no course/price/placement promise.
