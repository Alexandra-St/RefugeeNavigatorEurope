## Research verdict

**READY FOR CONTENT DRAFTING WITH QUALIFICATIONS.** Official Italian law and EU primary law establish the principal scope of the Ukraine temporary-protection scheme, the Questore’s role, direct filing at the competent Questura, a free electronic permit under the 2022 Italian implementing decree, and the EU end date of 4 March 2028. The decree’s original one-year permit duration and six-month automatic extensions are historical framework text and must not be repeated as the current end date. The EU military-obligation rule applies from 5 August 2026, subject to a continuity exception corrected to 4 August 2026. Italy-specific nationwide evidence-checking instructions under that new rule are **not established** in the reviewed sources: one Questura page is province-specific and still states the superseded 31 July cutoff. Do not use that local page as a national checklist or state its cutoff as current.

## Italian eligibility and filing boundaries

### Covered population

The safe starting point is the Italian implementing decree’s categories, read with the EU decision: qualifying Ukrainian nationals resident in Ukraine before 24 February 2022 and displaced on/after that date; qualifying stateless and third-country nationals who had international or equivalent national protection in Ukraine; the family categories and documentary/residence conditions defined by DPCM Art. 1; and the additional permanent-residence route in Art. 1(3) for persons unable to return safely and durably. Do not turn these into a blanket right for every Ukrainian national or every former resident of Ukraine. (S02–S03)

### Who receives the request

The DPCM directs a person to submit the request directly to the Questura and places issuance with the Questore for the place of domicile. The permit described in the DPCM is an electronic residence permit issued free of charge. These statutory points can be stated nationally. The appointment system, location, and local supporting-document instructions can differ and should be checked with the competent Questura. (S03)

No reviewed national primary source establishes a single mandatory nationwide appointment system, a complete universal document checklist, a proxy or online filing option, or a fixed processing time. Do not tell a person to use a particular city’s procedure unless the content is explicitly about that province. (S03, S10)

### Military-obligation verification

The EU condition is expressed as compliance with Ukrainian military obligations and submission of proof where applicable, subject to EU law and fundamental rights. The corrected commencement date is 5 August 2026. People already enjoying TP in a given Member State by 4 August 2026 and continuously retaining it in that same State fall within the corrected exception. (S06–S08)

The Commission’s 18 August 2026 FAQ discusses proof and verification at EU level but is non-binding guidance. The Italian Questura of Arezzo page is a local administrative example; its linked first-issue notice still says the rule began 31 July 2026, which predates the corrigendum. **The Italy-wide evidence format, review authority workflow, fallback process for unavailable documents, and individual consequences are not established in the reviewed sources.** Do not prescribe an exit stamp, Reserv+ record, consular certificate, age threshold, sex-specific rule, or automatic denial as an Italian national requirement based on these materials. (S06–S10)

## Date, renewal, and document boundaries

Keep four separate concepts clear:

1. **EU scheme duration:** temporary protection for the covered displaced people continues through **4 March 2028** under Decision 2026/1912. (S06–S07)
2. **Application of the extension:** Article 1 applies from **5 March 2027**; this does not move the scheme’s stated end date. (S06–S07)
3. **New military condition:** Article 2 applies from **5 August 2026**, with the corrected 4 August continuous-status exception. (S06–S08)
4. **Individual Italian permit card and procedure:** the old DPCM describes the initial 2022 permit as annual and its original automatic six-month renewal mechanism. Those historical words do not override later EU extensions. The reviewed sources do not establish that every card is automatically reprinted, that each holder must take the same renewal step, or that one local Questura notice applies nationwide. (S03, S06–S10)

DL 201/2025, converted by Law 26/2026, has a separate clause for renewal through 4 March 2027 of certain `protezione speciale` permits held by Ukrainian citizens already in Italy before 24 February 2022. That is not the `protezione temporanea` route in this package. (S09)

The Italian Council of Ministers’ 28 October 2026 extension of the state of emergency is not, by itself, a temporary-protection permit extension or proof of a new application mechanism. Emergency status and EU temporary-protection duration are distinct legal matters. (S06–S07; compare S11 for publication context)

## Drafting boundaries for Italy content

### Safe to state

- The Italian measure is `protezione temporanea`; the Italian permit is a `permesso di soggiorno per protezione temporanea`.
- The DPCM’s covered cohorts, including its specific family and qualifying stateless/third-country routes, with their conditions.
- The request is submitted directly to the Questura competent for the applicant’s domicile; the Questore issues the electronic permit under the DPCM.
- The DPCM describes issuance as free; avoid turning a province’s renewal-related charges into a national rule.
- EU temporary protection currently extends through **4 March 2028**.
- The military-obligation condition applies from **5 August 2026**; the corrected continuity exception refers to same-Member-State status by **4 August 2026**.
- International protection and temporary protection are separate procedures; DPCM permits the requests to coexist procedurally, while deferring examination of the international-protection request if TP is granted.

### Require qualification

- Explain that national Italian family and third-country routes are bounded by the DPCM; do not imply that every optional EU cohort was adopted.
- Explain that the EU military requirement is qualified by “where applicable,” and that Commission FAQ is guidance, not Italy’s national checklist.
- Say that the local Questura controls appointments and may publish local document instructions; identify a specific province when giving local details.
- Distinguish the legal protection period, the printed permit-card date, and renewal/replacement arrangements.
- Label DPCM 2022 original duration text as historical when discussing current dates.
- Keep `protezione speciale` under DL 201/2025 separate from Ukraine temporary protection.

### Do not assert

- That every Ukrainian national, all former residents of Ukraine, or all third-country nationals qualify automatically.
- That Italy has one online appointment route, universal document checklist, fixed processing time, or identical local procedures.
- That every applicant must provide a specific military document, that a particular age/sex group alone is covered, or that the stale Arezzo 31 July date is current.
- That all existing permit cards have an identical printed expiry, are all reissued automatically, or require no local follow-up.
- That a residence permit for `protezione speciale` is the same as a permit for `protezione temporanea`.
- That Italy’s continued state of emergency, by itself, sets the TP status period or guarantees an individual permit.
