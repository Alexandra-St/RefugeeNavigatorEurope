## Research verdict

**READY FOR CONTENT DRAFTING WITH QUALIFICATIONS.** Official Italian law and EU primary law establish the principal scope of the Ukraine temporary-protection scheme, the Questore’s role, direct filing at the competent Questura, a free electronic permit under the 2022 Italian implementing decree, and the EU end date of 4 March 2028. The decree’s original one-year permit duration and six-month automatic extensions are historical framework text and must not be repeated as the current end date. The EU military-obligation rule applies from 5 August 2026, subject to a continuity exception corrected to 4 August 2026. Italy-specific nationwide evidence-checking instructions under that new rule are **not established** in the reviewed sources: one Questura page is province-specific and still states the superseded 31 July cutoff. Do not use that local page as a national checklist or state its cutoff as current.

## Compulsory categories, exclusions, and terminology boundaries

### Covered population

The safe starting point is the Italian implementing decree’s categories, read with the EU decision: qualifying Ukrainian nationals resident in Ukraine before 24 February 2022 and displaced on/after that date; qualifying stateless and third-country nationals who had international or equivalent national protection in Ukraine; the family categories and documentary/residence conditions defined by DPCM Art. 1; and Italy’s additional permanent-residence route in Art. 1(3) for persons unable to return safely and durably. Italy adopted this specific optional route; do not infer that it adopted every optional EU group. Do not turn the listed routes into a blanket right for every Ukrainian national or every former resident of Ukraine. (S02–S03)

The Italian name is `protezione temporanea`; the DPCM provides for a `permesso di soggiorno per protezione temporanea`. The permit is the individual residence document. The scheme duration is set at EU level and should not be conflated with a printed card date or local renewal/replacement process. (S03, S06–S07, S10)

The DPCM provides exclusions by reference to D.Lgs. 85/2003 Art. 5 and adds a State-security ground. The available sources do not justify a generalized discretionary exclusion beyond these legal grounds. (S03–S04)

### Who receives the request

The DPCM directs a person to submit the request directly to the Questura and places issuance with the Questore for the place of domicile. The permit described in the DPCM is an electronic residence permit issued free of charge. These statutory points can be stated nationally. The appointment system, location, and local supporting-document instructions can differ and should be checked with the competent Questura. (S03)

No reviewed national primary source establishes a single mandatory nationwide appointment system, a complete universal document checklist, a proxy or online filing option, or a fixed processing time. Do not tell a person to use a particular city’s procedure unless the content is explicitly about that province. (S03, S10)

### Military-obligation verification

The EU condition is expressed as compliance with Ukrainian military obligations and submission of proof where applicable, subject to EU law and fundamental rights. The corrected commencement date is 5 August 2026. People already enjoying TP in a given Member State by 4 August 2026 and continuously retaining it in that same State fall within the corrected exception. (S06–S08)

The Commission’s 18 August 2026 FAQ discusses proof and verification at EU level but is non-binding guidance. The Italian Questura of Arezzo page is a local administrative example; its linked first-issue notice still says the rule began 31 July 2026, which predates the corrigendum. **The Italy-wide evidence format, review authority workflow, fallback process for unavailable documents, and individual consequences are not established in the reviewed sources.** Do not prescribe an exit stamp, Reserv+ record, consular certificate, age threshold, sex-specific rule, or automatic denial as an Italian national requirement based on these materials. (S06–S10)

## Initial registration and document boundaries

The DPCM establishes that the request is presented directly to the Questura and that the Questore for the applicant’s domicile issues the electronic permit. The Ministry-supported booklet says the applicant is fingerprinted, presents a passport or other identity document, receives a receipt, and is later summoned to collect the permit. These are national-facing instructions in a 2025 booklet, but not a current 2026 checklist. A photo requirement, a universal appointment platform, remote/proxy filing, and a fixed processing time are not established. (S03, S12)

For missing documents, the booklet says the authorities may need confirmation from Ukrainian diplomatic/consular authorities; it also says a person who fears that contact would endanger them or relatives may raise that concern and seek international protection. This is qualified guidance, not a guarantee of eligibility or a complete legal procedure. The booklet does not address the 2026 military-proof requirement. The 2026 Italian military-document fallback is not established in the reviewed national sources. (S06–S08, S12)

The booklet says an accompanying minor under 18 should be reported promptly to the nearest public-security authority. The reviewed materials do not establish a full current guardian, unaccompanied-minor, or child-specific filing procedure. The DPCM uses domicile to identify the competent Questura; a separate municipal residence-registration prerequisite was not established. (S03, S12)

The booklet describes later collection after a Questura summons but gives no reliable current processing duration. Do not generalize provincial appointment, renewal, payment, or document lists. Arezzo’s renewal notice is a local example only. (S10, S12)

## EU and Italian date boundaries

Keep four separate concepts clear:

1. **EU scheme duration:** temporary protection for the covered displaced people continues through **4 March 2028** under Decision 2026/1912. (S06–S07)
2. **Application of the extension:** Article 1 applies from **5 March 2027**; this does not move the scheme’s stated end date. (S06–S07)
3. **New military condition:** Article 2 applies from **5 August 2026**, with the corrected 4 August continuous-status exception. (S06–S08)
4. **Individual Italian permit card and procedure:** the old DPCM describes the initial 2022 permit as annual and its original automatic six-month renewal mechanism. Those historical words do not override later EU extensions. The reviewed national sources do not establish that every card is automatically reprinted, that each holder must take the same renewal step, or that one local Questura notice applies nationwide. A current Arezzo notice says renewal is on request through 2028-03-04, but that is a provincial procedure example. (S03, S06–S10, S12–S13)

DL 201/2025, converted by Law 26/2026, has a separate clause for renewal through 4 March 2027 of certain `protezione speciale` permits held by Ukrainian citizens already in Italy before 24 February 2022. That is not the `protezione temporanea` route in this package. (S09)

The Italian Council of Ministers’ 28 October 2026 extension of the state of emergency is not, by itself, a temporary-protection permit extension or proof of a new application mechanism. Emergency status and EU temporary-protection duration are distinct legal matters. (S06–S07, S11)

## 2026 changes / unresolved issues

- **EU extension and military rule:** Decision (EU) 2026/1912 extends the EU scheme through 4 March 2028 and adds the military-obligation condition from 5 August 2026. The corrigendum changes the continuous-status cutoff to 4 August 2026. These are EU-level legal rules, not evidence of a specific Italian application form or office workflow. (S06–S08)
- **Italy’s national administrative implementation:** the reviewed nationwide Italian legislation and guidance do not establish a complete post-corrigendum military-proof checklist or decision workflow. The Questura of Arezzo’s first-issue page was updated on 27 August 2026 but still gives 31 July 2026 as the start date. That conflicts with the corrected EU date and must not be silently reconciled or used as a nationwide rule. (S06–S08, S10, S13)
- **Italian TP-specific legislation:** no separate 2026 Italian decree was found in the reviewed official sources that replaces the DPCM’s original category/filing structure with a new nationwide military-verification procedure. This means “not found in reviewed sources,” not that no such instrument exists. The EU decision is the current legal source for the scheme date. (S03, S06–S11)
- **Separate `protezione speciale` clause:** DL 201/2025, converted in 2026, concerns qualifying Ukrainian nationals already present in Italy before the invasion with a different `protezione speciale` permit. It is not a temporary-protection amendment. (S09)
- **Permit renewal / card validity:** Arezzo’s Questura currently states local renewal on request to 4 March 2028, while the nationwide DPCM and Ministry booklet retain original 2022 duration language. A uniform nationwide renewal, reissue, and printed-card rule was not established in the reviewed sources. (S03, S10, S12–S13)
- **Other 2026 requirements:** no other Italy-specific official amendment to TP eligibility or first-application document rules was established in the reviewed materials. The booklet’s dates and benefit details are stale; do not reuse them as current. (S03, S06–S12)

## Drafting boundaries for Phase 23 content

### Safe to state

- The Italian measure is `protezione temporanea`; the Italian permit is a `permesso di soggiorno per protezione temporanea`.
- The DPCM’s covered cohorts, including its specific family and qualifying stateless/third-country routes, with their conditions. Italy’s adopted additional group is the bounded permanent-residence route in Art. 1(3); do not imply adoption of every optional EU category.
- The request is submitted directly to the Questura competent for the applicant’s domicile; the Questore issues the electronic permit under the DPCM.
- The DPCM describes issuance as free; avoid turning a province’s renewal-related charges into a national rule.
- EU temporary protection currently extends through **4 March 2028**.
- The military-obligation condition applies from **5 August 2026**; the corrected continuity exception refers to same-Member-State status by **4 August 2026**.
- International protection and temporary protection are separate procedures; DPCM permits the requests to coexist procedurally, while deferring examination of the international-protection request if TP is granted.

### Require qualification

- Explain that national Italian family and third-country routes are bounded by the DPCM; do not imply that every optional EU cohort was adopted.
- Explain that the EU military requirement is qualified by “where applicable,” and that Commission FAQ is guidance, not Italy’s national checklist.
- Say that the local Questura controls appointments and may publish local document instructions; identify a specific province when giving local details.
- Distinguish the legal protection period, the printed permit-card date, and renewal/replacement arrangements.
- Label DPCM 2022 original duration text as historical when discussing current dates.
- Keep `protezione speciale` under DL 201/2025 separate from Ukraine temporary protection.

### Do not assert

- That every Ukrainian national, all former residents of Ukraine, or all third-country nationals qualify automatically.
- That Italy has one online appointment route, universal document checklist, fixed processing time, or identical local procedures.
- That every applicant must provide a specific military document, that a particular age/sex group alone is covered, or that the stale Arezzo 31 July date is current.
- That all existing permit cards have an identical printed expiry, are all reissued automatically, or require no local follow-up.
- That a residence permit for `protezione speciale` is the same as a permit for `protezione temporanea`.
- That Italy’s continued state of emergency, by itself, sets the TP status period or guarantees an individual permit.
