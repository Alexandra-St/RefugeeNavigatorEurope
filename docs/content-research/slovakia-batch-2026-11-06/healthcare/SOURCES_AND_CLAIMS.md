## Core legal distinction

Keep these states separate in any future fact:

1. **Temporary-refuge holder without Slovak public insurance:** temporary refuge does not, by itself, make the person a public-insurance policyholder. Under current Act 580/2004 §9h(2), the person is entitled to reimbursement within the scope under §9(8) if they have been granted temporary refuge in Slovakia, are not publicly insured in Slovakia, and are not insured in another EU Member State. Temporary refuge status alone is not enough where either insurance exclusion applies.
2. **Temporary-refuge holder who qualifies for Slovak public insurance:** when a separate statutory §3 basis begins, the person becomes a public-insurance policyholder. VšZP's role as the largest insurer for §9h reimbursement, or provider records used to bill VšZP, does not enroll the patient as a policyholder.
3. **Person insured in another EU Member State:** excluded from this §9h uninsured-person route; use the applicable EU coordination proof/route instead. The person's precise EHIC/S1 situation depends on the facts and is not researched as a separate entitlement here.

## Findings and evidence boundaries

- **Ministry determination:** “Určenie rozsahu potrebnej zdravotnej starostlivosti” remains publicly posted and is directly referenced by VšZP's current 2026 provider instructions. No expiry or successor was found. The detailed appendix could not be independently extracted through the Ministry page cache, so §9h scope is stated with a VšZP corroboration qualifier.
- **Adults/children:** current VšZP guidance says adults: Act 577 §§2–3; children/adolescents from birth through 18 years + 364 days: §§2, 3 and 7. This is not full public-insurance coverage. Verify any individual service—including vaccination, chronic care, pregnancy, mental health, dental work, rehabilitation and planned specialist care—against the appendix, applicable statutory section, age and copay.
- **Employment threshold:** the law uses qualifying §10b(1)(a) activity and agreed monthly income at least the amount in the Minimum Wage Act for a monthly-paid employee. 2026 amount: €915 gross monthly. Some types of agreement/income are statutorily excluded; do not use “any job” or a month-by-month actual-income test. Application: within eight days; VšZP asks for signed application, contract and identity proof. Recheck 2027 threshold before use beyond 2026.
- **Other insurance routes and children:** qualifying self-employment and some other statuses may establish public insurance independently. Do not infer that temporary refuge or a parent's insurance automatically enrolls each child. Assess each person's own §3 category, residence/status documents and EU-coordination facts.
- **After job end:** insurance on the non-permanent-resident employee basis ends when its statutory facts cease, subject to another qualifying insurance basis. Notify/confirm with insurer; §9h can apply again to a TP holder who meets both exclusions, but the practical data transition and any processing delay are not documented here.
- **Proof without a physical card:** current §9h(6) establishes specified document types, and provider instructions recognize models 4A/4B. No current official source reviewed establishes that identity alone plus database confirmation is enough for all services where the status document is pending. The old emergency-only page is kept only as legacy context.
- **Provider access / GP:** a person may seek an ongoing general-practice agreement. The statute bars refusal solely for workload for a TP holder, but other legal grounds remain; no source guarantees a particular GP. Current provider billing instructions route the covered cohort through contracted VšZP providers. Non-contracted billing and any possible advance payment remain unsettled.
- **Medicines:** do not call medicines, devices or dietetic foods universally free. VšZP's current provider page says the person pays a statutory listed copayment where one applies. Copay amount depends on the currently effective category list and the prescribed item.
- **Urgent, necessary, planned, full coverage:** urgent care is the legal immediate-risk category. §9h plus the Ministry determination adds a defined necessary-care package. Neither means all planned care or full public-insurance cover. 155 is emergency medical service; 112 is general integrated emergency response. Call emergency services when indicated regardless of paperwork uncertainty.
