# Slovakia Financial and Social Assistance — Sources and Claims

**Research date / `LastCheckedAt`:** 2026-11-06
**Repository baseline:** `bf7f26ca5b716cd48ade72b9535f70eb4d064bfb`
**Topic:** Financial and Social Assistance
**Exact comparison aspects:** `social-subsistence`, `social-application`, `social-child-benefits`
**Scope:** Temporary-refuge beneficiaries (`dočasné útočisko`, commonly `odídenec`) and closely related Slovak assistance schemes. This is research, not a benefit decision or a universal eligibility guarantee.

## Findings at a glance

- A temporary-refuge holder is within the statutory foreigner category that may apply for **material-need assistance** (`pomoc v hmotnej núdzi`). Status alone does not create an award: the household must meet the statutory income, need, cooperation and property tests.
- Regular material-need assistance is decided/administered by the competent **ÚPSVaR**. The **municipality** has the separate, discretionary one-off material-need benefit. One application does not cover every scheme in this document.
- Work does not automatically terminate material-need assistance. Countable earnings and applicable disregards are included in a household calculation; the award can decrease or end if the resulting statutory conditions are no longer met.
- Temporary refuge alone is not the ordinary temporary residence (`prechodný pobyt`) named in the child-allowance and parental-allowance statutes. Neither ordinary scheme has a general temporary-refuge exception in the current provisions reviewed. A separate EU coordination basis may apply in a cross-border case; employment by itself does not settle that question.
- Childcare allowance is different: Regulation 93/2022 provides a temporary-refuge/special-protection deeming rule for the residence condition of both applicant and child. Other requirements, including work or full-time study, child age and eligible care, still apply.
- Standard cash disability compensation under Act 447/2008 does not list temporary-refuge status as a general eligible foreigner category. A separate discretionary humanitarian subsidy may be requested under Act 544/2010; it is not statutory disability compensation and is not guaranteed.

## Claim status vocabulary

- **Supported:** directly established by current primary law or current competent-agency instruction.
- **Supported with qualification:** established only subject to the stated conditions, cohort, evidence or unresolved administrative detail.
- **Not established:** reviewed sources do not establish the proposition. This is not a finding that the benefit or route is legally prohibited.

## `social-subsistence`

| Claim | Status | Evidence and safe drafting boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A temporary-refuge holder can apply for regular material-need assistance as a foreigner lawfully staying under the special-law/treaty clause. | **Supported with qualification** | Act 417/2013 §1(2) covers a foreigner lawfully staying under a special act or international treaty. Current temporary refuge is a legally authorized stay; ÚPSVaR Ukraine guidance identifies the temporary-refuge application card or tolerated-residence document as evidence for this route. Say “can apply / can be assessed,” not “automatically receives.” |
| The household must be in material need and be unable to secure or increase income by work, property or other claims under the statutory test. | **Supported** | Act 417/2013 §§2–4. Eligibility is household-based, income-tested and subject to statutory cooperation and asset rules. The law also prevents a finding of material need where household members can secure basic living conditions from property; statutory exclusions apply to particular property. |
| “Household” always means everyone sharing a dwelling. | **Not established** | Act §3 defines assessed relationships, including a single person, spouses and dependent children, and specified parent/adult-child combinations. Shared address alone does not determine household membership. Apply statutory categories to the actual family and dependency facts. |
| The applicant must claim other legally available income/entitlements and disclose household income and property. | **Supported with qualification** | Act 417/2013 §§4–9 and current MPSVR guidance require relevant income/claims, cooperation and property assessment. Exact forms/evidence depend on the household and claim. Do not turn this into an unverified exhaustive document list. |
| A household receives a fixed payment simply because one member has temporary refuge. | **Not established** | Status permits consideration under the lawful-stay route but no refugee flat-rate award exists. Amount depends on statutory household categories, components, countable income, exclusions and eligibility. |
| The regular benefit’s 2026 base monthly amount is one fixed figure. | **Not established** | Effective 1 Jan 2026, the base benefit by household category is: single adult €89.70; single adult with 1–4 children €170.60; couple without children €155.90; couple with 1–4 children €233.20; single adult with more than 4 children €249.10; couple with more than 4 children €314.40. These are component rates before household-income deduction and other applicable components, not a guaranteed net award. |
| Eligible households may receive additional monthly components on top of the base benefit. | **Supported with qualification** | MPSVR’s 2026 table, read with Act 417/2013, lists: protection allowance €91.70 for each qualifying person; activation allowance €183.30 for qualifying gainful activity at least at the monthly minimum-wage level, €137.50 for specified education/retraining, or €91.70 for qualifying registered-jobseeker activation; dependent-child allowance €25.10 per eligible dependent child; and housing allowance by household size: €100.60 (1), €170.80 (2), €216.70 (3), €262.70 (4), €308.40 (5+). Each component has its own eligibility rules; amounts are monthly and effective 1 Jan 2026. |
| Housing allowance is automatically paid to a temporary-refuge holder, or is the same as the host accommodation contribution. | **Not established** | The material-need housing allowance is a component with statutory housing and household conditions; it is not the separate host accommodation contribution paid under displaced-person accommodation rules. Do not conflate them or imply automatic status-based payment. |
| Host accommodation contribution paid to a provider/host is countable cash income received by the refugee. | **Not established** | Current MPSVR material-need guidance and Act 417/2013 income exclusions treat the accommodation contribution for providing accommodation to a displaced person as excluded income. It is a host/provider-side contribution, not cash paid to the refugee. |
| Starting employment automatically ends material-need assistance. | **Not established** | Employment income enters the calculation subject to statutory exclusions. MPSVR states a six-calendar-month enhanced disregard for qualifying first employment arising while receiving assistance: 100% in months 1–2, 75% in months 3–4 and 50% in months 5–6, in addition to the general 25% exclusion of income from dependent work or equivalent foreign income. The enhanced rule has applied since 1 Sep 2025; it may be used again only for employment beginning after 24 calendar months from the employment for which it was first used. A remaining award depends on recalculated household entitlement and component rules. No temporary-refuge exclusion is stated, but each case must meet the conditions. |
| Every employed recipient qualifies for the activation allowance. | **Not established** | Activation has multiple distinct statutory routes and conditions. The €183.30 amount corresponds to qualifying work with at least the monthly minimum wage; other listed amounts concern specified education/retraining or registered-jobseeker activity. Do not infer qualification from any job alone. |
| One-off `jednorazová dávka v hmotnej núdzi` is a guaranteed national payment. | **Not established** | Act §17 and MPSVR guidance: it is a separate, discretionary municipality/city benefit for a household receiving regular material-need assistance, for evidenced extraordinary expenses such as clothing/footwear/linen, essential household equipment, extraordinary medical costs or school supplies. Annual aggregate ceiling is proven eligible expenses up to 3× the adult life minimum. With adult life minimum €295.22 from 1 Jul 2026, the ceiling is €885.66 in a year; actual amount can be lower or zero, subject to local decision/budget and expense proof. |
| The national sources establish a universal one-off municipal amount and operational access for every temporary-refuge address. | **Not established** | Amount is locally decided within the statutory ceiling. General MPSVR guidance describes territorial competence by permanent residence, but the reviewed current sources do not resolve which municipality handles a temporary-refuge holder who has no Slovak permanent residence. Do not invent an address workaround or promise municipal eligibility; confirm with the municipality/ÚPSVaR for the person’s documented situation. |

## `social-application`

| Claim | Status | Evidence and safe drafting boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regular material-need assistance and the one-off municipal benefit use the same decision-maker/application. | **Not established** | Regular assistance is decided/administered by ÚPSVaR under Act 417/2013; the one-off §17 benefit is decided by the municipality/city. Clearly distinguish the routes. |
| A temporary-refuge applicant submits the regular application to ÚPSVaR for the area where they are located. | **Supported with qualification** | Current ÚPSVaR Ukraine-specific guidance directs temporary-refuge holders to the ÚPSVaR office for the territory where the person is located. Ordinary applicants’ statutory competence may use residence/address criteria. The reviewed guidance does not explain every move, registration, or disputed territorial case; confirm with ÚPSVaR if office assignment is unclear. |
| The regular material-need application is made using the competent authority’s application form; a written filing route is available. | **Supported** | Act 417/2013 and ÚPSVaR’s current form page provide the application/template. Follow the version in force at filing. The source reviewed does not establish a universal electronic filing route for every applicant, so do not promise one. |
| The temporary-refuge proof can be the temporary-refuge application card or the tolerated-residence document bearing `ODÍDENEC` / `DOČASNÉ ÚTOČISKO`. | **Supported with qualification** | Ukraine-specific ÚPSVaR instruction names these proofs. Use the current document actually held; it does not establish that an expired, pending or identity-only record is sufficient. |
| There is one exhaustive document checklist that applies to every household. | **Not established** | Current ÚPSVaR templates collect information about applicant/household, income, assets/property, housing and children, and supporting evidence varies by household composition and claims. Present these as likely evidence categories, not an exhaustive universal list. Ask ÚPSVaR for case-specific missing documents. |
| The applicant should report all household members, income, property, housing and dependent children relevant to statutory assessment. | **Supported** | Required by Act 417/2013’s household, income and property tests and reflected in current application materials. Other available benefits/claims must also be disclosed and pursued where required. |
| Every listed material-need component is decided by a separate application. | **Not established** | The regular material-need calculation is made within that assistance claim, subject to each component’s facts; this does not mean one filing covers child allowance, parental allowance, childcare allowance, disability compensation or humanitarian subsidy. Those schemes have separate legal bases and application authorities/routes. |
| A decision/payment follows immediately after submission. | **Not established** | Statute sets the decision process and payment timing; the reviewed sources do not support an immediate-award promise. Assistance is paid monthly in arrears, no later than the end of the following calendar month under the statutory rule, once entitlement is established. Missing evidence or individual proceedings can affect timing. |
| A TP holder can use the one-off municipality route at the same office where regular assistance is claimed. | **Not established** | The one-off benefit belongs to the municipality/city, not ÚPSVaR. The exact competent municipality if the TP holder lacks permanent residence remains unresolved in reviewed official guidance. |
| A serious-disability-related humanitarian subsidy is filed through the regular material-need application. | **Not established** | Humanitarian subsidy is a separate MPSVR route under Act 544/2010 §9, using the Ministry’s form and supporting evidence. It is not an ÚPSVaR material-need component. |

### Application evidence boundary

For regular material-need assistance, safe public guidance can say to contact the territorially competent ÚPSVaR and prepare current status proof plus information/evidence relevant to household composition, income and other benefits, property/assets, accommodation, and dependent children. Do not assert that every item is mandatory for every person; ask the assigned office for its case-specific checklist. For a municipal one-off application, ask the municipality about its own form, local requirements and territorial competence. For humanitarian subsidy, use the current MPSVR application instructions and submit evidence of the exceptional situation and eligible expense.

## `social-child-benefits`

| Claim | Status | Evidence and safe drafting boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A family holding temporary refuge automatically receives Slovak child allowance, parental allowance and childcare allowance. | **Not established** | The schemes have different residence/status rules. Never write a blanket statement that temporary-refuge families receive all Slovak family benefits. Assess each scheme independently. |
| Child allowance (`prídavok na dieťa`) is available on temporary refuge alone, without qualifying ordinary residence or a covered EU coordination basis. | **Not established** | Act 600/2003 §7 requires the entitled person to have Slovak permanent residence or temporary residence, or to fall within the applicable EU coordination regulations. Temporary refuge under the Asylum Act is not the ordinary `prechodný pobyt` under Act 404/2011, and the current child-allowance act contains no general TP-only deeming exception. An old Ukraine-specific ÚPSVaR information page states TP holders do not meet the ordinary residence condition; it is historical (2022) and corroborative only, not controlling current evidence. |
| Temporary-refuge status is the same as ordinary temporary residence for child allowance. | **Not established** | Do not equate `dočasné útočisko` / `odídenec` with `prechodný pobyt`. A person with an independent qualifying Slovak residence status may meet the ordinary condition; an EU coordination basis can also matter in a genuine cross-border case. |
| Slovak employment by itself establishes a Slovak child-allowance right for every temporary-refuge family. | **Not established** | Act 600/2003 references EU social-security coordination. Regulations 883/2004 and 1231/2010 may cover some legally resident third-country nationals in cross-border situations. Which state is competent depends on the family’s employment/insurance and residence facts, the other parent and child circumstances, and EU priority rules. A job in Slovakia alone does not establish every condition or guarantee Slovakia is the paying state. |
| Child allowance has a child dependency/schooling test. | **Supported** | Act 600/2003 defines a dependent child through completion of compulsory schooling and, within statutory limits (generally to age 25), qualifying continuous study or specified long-term illness. Apply the precise statutory definition and exceptions; do not reduce it to “any child under 25.” |
| The child must always have the same Slovak residence status as the parent for child allowance. | **Not established** | The ordinary §7 residence condition is framed around the entitled person; where there are cross-border facts the EU coordination rules and child’s situation may affect competence. The reviewed sources do not support a universal identical-status rule for the child. |
| A parent/caregiver holding only temporary refuge qualifies for parental allowance (`rodičovský príspevok`) under the ordinary residence rule. | **Not established** | Act 571/2009 requires a qualifying parent/substitute caregiver and ordinarily Slovak permanent or temporary residence, or an applicable Regulation 883/2004 basis. The statute reviewed has no express general temporary-refuge exception, and TP is not ordinary temporary residence under Act 404/2011. An independent status or EU coordination basis may change the analysis. |
| Parental allowance has a child-age and care condition. | **Supported** | Generally through the child’s third birthday; to age six in specified cases, including long-term adverse health condition, substitute care within the statutory period, or non-admission to a capacity kindergarten under the statutory rules. Exact evidence and extension category matter. |
| 2026 parental allowance has one rate regardless of maternity history. | **Not established** | Effective 1 Jan 2026, monthly rate is €345.20, or €473.30 if maternity (or equivalent EU benefit) was paid for that child before the claim. Multiple simultaneous births increase the rate by 25% for each additional child; statutory reductions/coordination apply. The allowance is not paid in a month where a higher maternity benefit precludes it; where maternity is lower, statutory difference rules apply. Refresh values before later use. |
| Childcare allowance (`príspevok na starostlivosť o dieťa`) is available merely because a TP holder has a job. | **Not established** | Act 561/2008 generally requires qualifying gainful activity creating pension insurance or full-time study; an eligible child and qualifying care/provider in Slovakia; and the other statutory conditions. A job alone is insufficient. |
| The childcare allowance applies to every child under six. | **Not established** | The ordinary statutory age is under three; it may extend to under six for a child with a long-term adverse health condition. Do not confuse this allowance with other childcare or early-education support. |
| A temporary-refuge parent and child can never satisfy childcare-allowance residence requirements. | **Not established** | Regulation 93/2022 §2 defines persons with special protection and deems the ordinary residence condition for both applicant and child fulfilled where both fall within its special-protection rule. This covers qualifying temporary-refuge holders while the regulation’s crisis/temporal framework applies. It is a specific childcare-allowance exception; it does not extend automatically to child allowance or parental allowance. Verify both parent and child’s status. |
| Childcare allowance has a single monthly amount. | **Not established** | Current 2026 Act 561/2008 and MPSVR guidance set caps by eligible provider/payment type: documented provider fees up to €280/month; qualifying care by a self-employed provider/children’s group route up to €160/month; qualifying municipal/public provider in the Ministry network up to €80/month; and care by a parent or other non-business individual up to €41.10/month. Actual payment is limited by eligible actual costs and statutory conditions; verify provider classification and current cap at application. |
| Childcare allowance may be combined with parental allowance or maternity payment for the same month. | **Not established** | Act 561/2008 contains a non-duplication rule for months in which parental allowance or maternity is paid, subject to its statutory exceptions. Check the applicant’s month and any birth-related exception with ÚPSVaR; do not promise two simultaneous full payments. |
| One family-benefit application establishes all child/family benefits. | **Not established** | Child allowance and parental/childcare allowances have separate statutory bases, forms and qualifying conditions. Apply to ÚPSVaR using the relevant current form and disclose status, child identity/dependency/school evidence, residence, childcare provider, work/study and maternity facts as relevant. |
| Birth grant, substitute maintenance, school-meal or school-supplies subsidies create a general temporary-refuge cash route. | **Not established** | These schemes were not expanded because reviewed evidence did not establish a clear general temporary-refuge-specific route material to the three requested aspects. Assess a separate scheme only against its own current law and facts; this is not a finding that no person could qualify through another status. |

## Disability support and humanitarian subsidy

| Claim | Status | Evidence and safe drafting boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A temporary-refuge holder qualifies for standard cash compensation for severe disability under Act 447/2008 solely by holding TP and meeting the disability assessment. | **Not established** | Current Act §3 enumerates eligible foreigner categories (including specified treaty-covered persons, family members of Slovak/EU citizens and people granted asylum or subsidiary protection); temporary-refuge beneficiaries are not a general listed category. Do not claim ordinary `ŤZP` cash compensation on TP status alone. A narrow §41(10) route concerning returned used functional aids is not cash disability-compensation eligibility. |
| A temporary-refuge holder with serious disability may request humanitarian subsidy. | **Supported with qualification** | Act 544/2010 §9 permits a discretionary subsidy to a natural person in crisis or exceptionally adverse social circumstances, subject to statutory conditions and Ministry assessment. The person may apply; the reviewed law/guidance does not create automatic TP eligibility or a Ukraine/`odídenec` priority. The MPSVR lists certain extraordinary health expenses not covered by insurance/other disability compensation among relevant situations. |
| Humanitarian subsidy is a regular, means-tested monthly disability pension or entitlement. | **Not established** | It is a separate discretionary grant, normally assessed against a particular exceptional situation and evidence. MPSVR describes a maximum of €1,500 in a calendar year for the relevant personal humanitarian subsidy; actual award can be lower or refused. Separate emergency/disaster provisions have their own conditions and ceilings. It is not a recurring benefit or a substitute for statutory disability compensation. |
| Humanitarian subsidy applications are made to ÚPSVaR. | **Not established** | The current personal humanitarian-subsidy route is administered by MPSVR using its official form and supporting documents. Keep separate from ÚPSVaR material-need/family-benefit applications and municipality one-off assistance. |
| A humanitarian-subsidy recipient with severe disability may fit the vulnerable-person wording in Asylum Act §69 for host accommodation arrangements. | **Supported with qualification** | Act 69/2026 §69’s specified vulnerable-person definition includes a person with severe disability who receives the MPSVR humanitarian subsidy. This is a housing/provider-side accommodation contribution condition; it does not itself pay the refugee or establish standard disability compensation. See Phase 20.7 Housing research for the accommodation scheme; no housing rules are re-researched here. |

**Calculation caution:** material-need assistance is calculated from applicable household components and statutory income treatment; the published component values are not additive guaranteed cash. The final amount is rounded/calculated under the Act and may be reduced to zero. The housing allowance in this system is distinct from the contribution for accommodation provided to a displaced person.

## Evidence boundaries and unresolved details

- **Municipal one-off territorial competence:** national guidance uses permanent-residence language; no current official source reviewed resolved the competent municipality for a temporary-refuge holder without Slovak permanent residence. Confirm directly for the case; do not assert a workaround.
- **Exact individual application attachments:** current forms identify relevant categories, but the official sources do not establish one exhaustive checklist for every household/status. Current status document, household facts, income, assets, housing, children and other claims may be relevant; the assigned office determines case-specific proof.
- **Electronic filing for every TP applicant:** written/form filing is established; universal availability of a qualified electronic route for every applicant was not established.
- **EU coordination:** a qualifying cross-border basis may alter child/family benefit entitlement and competent state. Temporary refuge, employment or a child’s status alone do not resolve the full coordination analysis. Case-specific facts (parent(s), work/insurance, residence and family members) are needed.
- **TP-only child allowance and parental allowance:** no general TP-only exception was found in current Acts 600/2003 or 571/2009. “Not established” here means do not assert eligibility on TP alone; it is not a ruling on an independent temporary-residence status or applicable EU coordination route.
- **Childcare special rule:** the Regulation 93/2022 exception requires both applicant and child to qualify as persons with special protection and remains subject to the regulation’s temporal framework. Recheck if the protection/crisis rules change.
- **Municipal one-off local amounts:** no universal local award amount; local decision/budget and actual eligible costs matter.
- **Disability compensation:** TP alone is not a general foreigner eligibility category under Act 447/2008. The limited returned-aid provision does not establish cash compensation.
- **Humanitarian subsidy:** discretionary; current sources establish application eligibility/conditions and a cap but no TP-specific priority or guaranteed result. It is separate from material-need, disability and accommodation contributions.
- **Benefit indexation:** all amounts are dated to their cited 2026 effective source. Refresh the laws and official agency pages immediately before drafting/publishing later guidance.
- **Employment earnings:** Ministry’s 6-month disregard mechanics are current as checked on 6 Nov 2026. Confirm qualifying first employment, covered income, calculation and the 24-month repeat limit for an individual case; no automatic loss on hiring should be stated.
