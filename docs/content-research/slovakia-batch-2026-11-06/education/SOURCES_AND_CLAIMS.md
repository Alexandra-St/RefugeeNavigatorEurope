## Current legal and practical distinctions

- **Compulsory education and foreign-child access are different questions.** School Act §146(1)–(2) gives equal educational access/conditions to its defined foreign-child groups, a wider group than Ukrainian temporary-refuge beneficiaries. Section 161r(3)–(6) separately imposes Ukraine-specific compulsory-education enrolment and transition duties on children who are Ukrainian citizens and whose temporary-protection proceeding/status falls within the specified categories. Do not extend §161r's special rule to every foreign child, or describe §146 access as itself the new universal compulsion.
- **Exact Ukraine cohorts (§161r):** (3) for a qualifying proceeding started after 31 Aug 2025, the guardian applies within three months of starting the temporary-protection proceeding; (4) where the proceeding started/status was granted 1 Jan–31 Aug 2025, apply within three months after 1 Sep 2025; (5) the pre-2025 cohort must take all necessary steps for schooling to begin 1 Sep 2025. Eligible age on 31 Aug of the relevant calendar year: age 5+ for compulsory pre-primary education; age 6+ and under 16 for primary or secondary education. The transition has cohort-specific wording; don't reduce it to time since arrival.
- **Initial school application:** apply directly to the chosen school director. The Ministry's practical page asks for proof that temporary protection/asylum proceedings have started or status granted; prepare the child's passport/ID, birth certificate, available last report card or school record, and residence proof if requested. The Director makes the decision. Previous-school evidence helps assess placement; missing records do not prevent the school from investigating/placing the child and do not require a formal diploma-recognition decision just to begin ordinary enrolment.
- **Grade is not set by age alone.** Under §146(4), the director determines the grade promptly after application based on level of prior education and instruction-language proficiency. Language difficulty allows conditional placement in the age-appropriate grade. Placement one grade lower needs informed parental/guardian consent and an opinion from a counselling and prevention facility. The school can request and discuss available records; the Ministry says it helps identify a suitable grade where Ukrainian records are absent.
- **Capacity and chosen school:** if an eligible Ukraine child cannot be placed in the selected kindergarten/basic/secondary school under §161r(3)–(5), that director must notify the guardian, municipality where the child resides/has temporary-refuge address, and the competent regional state school-administration office (RÚŠS); that authority then determines a school where the child will be educated (§161r(6)). This is a fallback assignment mechanism, not a guarantee of the family's chosen school. It is not a statement that no school elsewhere has a place.
- **District priority is current and purpose-specific.** For municipal/regional basic schools, §20(5) allows fulfilment in the public school district where the pupil has permanent or usual residence unless the guardian chooses another school that admits them. §20(6)'s priority order, up to class maximum, is: (a) permanent residence in district; (b) court-placed pupil in a facility within district; (c) usual residence in district. For compulsory pre-primary education, §59a(1) gives the catchment kindergarten unless the guardian chooses another that admits the child; §59a(2) lists permanent-residence children, then court-placed children. The Ministry's Ukraine guidance notes no legally specified priority between its asylum-seeking-child and displaced-child categories where §146(4) applies. No special blanket “temporary refuge first” priority is established. Municipalities define/coordinate public districts under Act 321/2025 §44; RÚŠS intervenes where the district/access arrangement fails under the Act.
- **Compulsory preschool is not daycare.** Ordinary compulsory pre-primary education is one school year for children who reach age five by 31 Aug (§28a). The Ukraine transition expressly includes eligible age-five children in compulsory pre-primary education. It is an education-program obligation in a kindergarten/qualifying institution, not a promise of a daycare place. The §161r capacity fallback also applies to kindergarten.
- **Leaving:** a Ukrainian citizen with temporary refuge who permanently leaves Slovakia should notify the school director in writing (§161r(2)); a parent/guardian does so for a minor. Without notice and with unexcused non-attendance, the child ceases to be registered after 30 days from last participation. Do not treat this registry rule as permission to replace Slovak school with online Ukrainian study.
- **Language and adaptation support:** §146(3) provides for state-language courses to remove foreign pupils' language barriers; §146(6) provides adjustments to content/assessment plus adaptation and psychosocial support in line with instruction-language proficiency and risk. Current Ministry language-course measure is based on language diagnosis and public funding, but does not establish that a course is automatically available at every school. Adaptation classes are discretionary (may be established with founder consent), not an automatic placement: director, prescribed teacher/support/counselling pathway and prior informed parental consent apply. Elementary-school adaptation class: current §29(14)–(15), normally up to 12 months, not repeatable; SŠ adaptation class: §33(12)–(14), up to one school year, not repeatable. The former page saying elementary-only, one school-year maximum is outdated as a complete 2026 description. There is no permanent exemption from Slovak-language learning/assessment in the current law reviewed.
- **Ukrainian online/distance study:** the Ukrainian component is presented as additional study alongside attendance in the host-country school. No current Slovak law or Ministry material found says that an ordinary Ukrainian online school by itself satisfies §161r compulsory enrolment/education. School Act §23 does recognize specific forms of fulfilment abroad/individual study; their eligibility and approval are separate. Do not claim the Ukrainian online component substitutes for the Slovak school without an individual statutory route confirmed by the responsible school/authority.
- **Completed school credentials:** for routine initial grade placement under §146(4), the school assesses prior learning; formal recognition is not established as a universal precondition. For university admission the baseline first-cycle qualification is completed upper-secondary general or vocational education under Higher Education Act §68(a). Ukrainian students may use SUDV's electronic recognition clause for selected listed qualifications and submit it with the qualification directly to the university. For other credentials, recognition of secondary education is handled by RÚŠS; the current SUDV navigator should be checked for the exact qualification and purpose. This is not a single recognition channel for every Ukrainian credential.
- **Missing recognition documents / EDBO:** submit any available copies, scans or photos and explain by signed declaration which document cannot be supplied due to displacement. SUDV says cases are examined individually. For newer Ukrainian records, the holder may obtain supporting confirmation from EDBO (roughly records from 2000 onward); it is evidence, not an automatic recognition decision. Where a person with international protection completed upper-secondary education but cannot prove it, SUDV describes a special pathway involving a sworn statement and general study-aptitude test under the receiving university's rules; missing originals do not guarantee recognition or admission.
- **Unfinished university study:** SUDV directs a person seeking placement based on partial study/credit recognition to the receiving Slovak university; the receiving university/faculty evaluates courses/credits and year placement. No central guaranteed transfer, acceptance of all credits, or continuation entitlement was found.
- **Higher-education admission:** use Act 300/2025 effective 1 Sep 2026. First-cycle / integrated first-plus-second-cycle minimum is completed upper-secondary education; second-cycle minimum is relevant first-cycle degree (subject to statutory credit-total/health-field rules). University/faculty can set additional academic conditions and examinations (§69), must publish its own deadline, conditions, exam outline and applicant limit at least two months before that deadline; applications are filed through the central electronic admission system and the university/faculty decides (§70). There is no single national closing date or general admission guarantee. Statutory minimum does not replace credential proof or a particular faculty's requirements.
- **Tuition/fees and aid:** Act 300/2025 §96(14)(b), in the rules for public and state universities, states that tuition does not arise where a student has temporary refuge (also asylum or subsidiary protection). This is an express current exemption at those institutions, not a universal exemption across all university types: §99 lets private universities set tuition and study fees in their internal rules and does not state a parallel temporary-refuge exemption. The public/state exemption does not automatically waive the separately allowed admissions/material-administration fee under §97, nor does it establish that every incidental charge is tuition. 2026/27 talent scholarships are current but competitive, annual and eligibility/program-limited; social scholarship may be available to a daily student with temporary-refuge status subject to means/evidence and university/faculty decision. No universal Ukraine-specific scholarship or guaranteed funded place was found.

## Evidence boundaries / unresolved

- How a particular pupil's prior learning is assessed in practice, especially without any Ukrainian records; the school decides placement and Ministry says it helps identify a suitable grade, but no national grade-assignment table for each Ukrainian school level was established.
- Whether a particular selected school has a place, whether the family prefers it, and the assigned alternative school's location; §161r sets an alternative-school determination process, not a choice guarantee.
- Exact acceptance/recognition of a particular Ukrainian online school or program for Slovakia's §23 foreign-study routes. Ordinary online Ukrainian study does not automatically replace the Slovak school duty.
- Availability, staffing and duration of a language course or adaptation class at a specific school; law enables/supports measures but does not guarantee a specific course/class in each location.
- Whether a formal recognition clause is required for a specific Ukrainian upper-secondary qualification and target faculty; use the current SUDV selected-qualification list and that university's published admissions terms.
- Whether an individual student's Ukrainian university credits transfer and which Slovak year/subjects they count toward; receiving institution decision only.
- Individual tuition billing at a private university, or where separate non-tuition fees apply; §96(14)(b) expressly exempts temporary-refuge students from tuition at public/state universities, while private-university fees are set under §99. Confirm individual billing and status timing with the institution.
- Which annual scholarship round is open, individual eligibility, dates, competition and amount; the 2026/27 scholarship call is current but not a permanent entitlement.
