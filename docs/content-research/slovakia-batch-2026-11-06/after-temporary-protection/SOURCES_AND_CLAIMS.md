# Slovakia — Residence Options for People with Temporary Refuge

**Research checked:** 2026-11-06
**Baseline:** `2297c70b288e2db5d8ee18a0480bad65dded2e14`
**Scope:** focused legal and procedural evidence review for exactly `residence-routes`, `residence-conditions`, and `residence-filing`. This is not an article, legal opinion, or individual eligibility decision.

## Verdict

**READY FOR CONTENT DRAFTING**, with the qualifications and unresolved points below preserved. The central route is established as legally usable before the general end of Slovak temporary refuge: §131o(14) expressly permits a current beneficiary to apply from 15 July 2026 until refuge ends. This is an application route, not an automatic conversion or grant. The cross-reference in §131o(14) to subsections (4)–(13), particularly subsection (4)'s continuous-activity wording tied to the eventual end of refuge, creates a genuine temporal ambiguity about what exact continuity test is administered to a pre-end applicant. The statute's own subsection (14), the Ministry's present-tense operational notice, and the separate post-end rule support present availability; they do not fully explain how Police applies the cross-reference in an individual case. Draft the pre-end and post-end tests separately and advise applicants to confirm current evidence/interpretation with Foreign Police before relying on the special route.

## §131o: exact route and temporal issue

### Two distinct filing windows

1. **Before Slovak temporary refuge ends — §131o(14):** a person currently granted temporary refuge in Slovakia may apply between **15 July 2026 and the termination of Slovak temporary refuge** for a residence document under one of §21(1)(a)–(f), if they performed that same purpose for at least six months immediately before filing. The Ministry notice confirms this is a current simplified transition and gives an appointment path. It is not merely future preparation and does not require waiting for the EU/Slovak endpoint.
2. **After the general termination — §131o(1), (4):** the cohort is a third-country national who still held Slovak temporary refuge on the day immediately before its general termination. For up to one year after that termination they continue to be treated as `odídenec`, subject to exclusions below. During that framework §131o(4) permits a purpose-specific application for an activity carried on for at least six months immediately before refuge ended and continuously carried on through the application date.

The text uses different reference dates. Do not replace §131o(14)'s “six months immediately before application” with the post-end §131o(4) lookback, or tell a person applying pre-end that a new purpose started after refuge ends qualifies. For subsection (14), the ordinary wording clearly requires six months of the same purpose immediately before filing. However, subsection (14) also says subsections (4)–(13) apply to issuance; subsection (4) additionally requires the purpose to have been performed continuously “also on the date of application” and describes the six months by reference to the future end of refuge. The statute does not expressly state whether subsection (4)'s end-date continuity is imported for a subsection (14) application. Ministry guidance confirms the route is presently processed but does not clarify that interaction. **Evidence conflict/interpretive boundary remains:** current availability is established; the precise extra continuity test applied to the pre-end cohort is not fully established. Verify with Foreign Police in a specific case.

### Covered purposes, exact §21(1)(a)–(f)

§131o covers only these ordinary temporary-residence purposes:

| §21(1) | Purpose | §131o coverage and evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (a) | Business (`podnikanie`) | Covered. Purpose proof is waived in §131o(5)(a) only for the specified person already recorded in the commercial, trade, legal-person/public-authority or similar register, or recorded in the commercial register as authorised to act for a company/cooperative. No general waiver for an unregistered planned business. |
| (b) | Employment (`zamestnanie`) | Covered. Special §131o(6)(a) proof: Social Insurance Agency certificate that the applicant is registered as an employee in the employee and old-age pension savings register, **not older than 30 days**. This proof requirement is distinct from the six-month activity criterion. |
| (c) | Study (`štúdium`) | Covered, with purpose evidence specified through §32(5)(c)–(n). No special student-route shortcut beyond §131o is established. |
| (d) | Special activity (`osobitná činnosť`) | Covered, with §32(5)(c)–(n) purpose evidence as relevant. This is a statutory category with enumerated activity types, not a general “other reason” route. |
| (e) | Research and development (`výskum a vývoj`) | Covered; §32(5)(c)–(n) applies as relevant. No separate §131o research-document list is stated. |
| (f) | Family reunification (`zlúčenie rodiny`) | Covered; §32(5)(c)–(n) applies as relevant. Ordinary sponsor, relationship and family-reunification conditions remain relevant unless a §131o provision displaces them. |

**Six-month condition:** for a pre-end §131o(14) filing, the activity/purpose itself must have been performed for at least six months immediately before the filing date. A new purpose begun fewer than six months before the application does not satisfy this express text. For the post-end §131o(4) route, the applicant must have performed the purpose for at least six months immediately before the general termination date and continue it without interruption through application. The six months need not be conflated with the date evidence is issued. The purpose must be carried out in Slovakia under §21(3), subject to that subsection's transport exception.

### Documents and decision mechanics under §131o(4)–(13)

- **Required at initial application:** a valid travel document; purpose evidence (with the narrow business-register exception); evidence of good character; and accommodation evidence (with §32(2)(e) exception). If any listed required item is missing, Police does not accept the application and gives written information about what to add. This is a completeness/acceptance rule, not approval.
- **Purpose evidence:** for employment, Social Insurance certificate described above, issued no earlier than 30 days before filing. For purposes (c)–(f), §131o(6)(b) directs to §32(5)(c)–(n), selected to match the purpose. For registered business, §131o(5)(a) waives that purpose document only in the stated cases.
- **Good character:** Ukraine criminal-record extract no older than 90 days; §131o(5)(b) removes the requirement for an applicant younger than 14. Unlike the general residence process, §131o explicitly names Ukraine's extract. The reviewed special text does not specify translations, apostille/legalisation, or a broader country-of-origin list; do not add those as §131o requirements without further current authority.
- **Accommodation:** required unless the applicant falls within §32(2)(e); the evidence form/conditions are subject mutatis mutandis to §32(8) and §122. No special §131o financial-means evidence is listed. The special list also does not require the ordinary §32 public-health medical certificate. Do not copy ordinary §32 financial, medical or document-age requirements into this pathway unless another cited provision expressly applies.
- **Police timelines:** within **60 days of receipt** of a §131o(4) application, Police sends paper or electronic notice that the conditions are met and no §131o(12) refusal ground applies. It then issues the residence document within **30 days after sending that notice**. These are statutory periods; they are not a promise of favorable qualification or appointment availability. If the purpose document has a fixed validity, permit validity follows it; if it has unlimited validity, the permit is for two years. Issuance of the document is the act that grants temporary residence.
- **Refusal:** Police may refuse for failure to meet the purpose/continuity condition or specified grounds in §33(6)(a), (b), (d), (e), (f), or (h); it does not issue a formal written refusal decision but sends a paper/electronic notice with reasons. The reviewed special provisions do not state that refusal itself terminates an otherwise valid temporary-refuge grant; no adverse status consequence should be inferred from this silence. General law or the individual's separate status may still matter.
- **Public-interest assessment:** §131o(13) applies §125(6)–(9), including consultation/assessment rules, with a 30-day period for a negative opinion. Do not turn the 60/30 processing periods into service guarantees.

## One-year post-end bridge and effect of a granted permit

Section 131o(1) applies to a person holding Slovak temporary refuge on the day immediately before its termination. For one year after that termination the person remains treated as `odídenec` automatically; no separate application is prescribed for this statutory treatment. The bridge does not apply if the person has applied for international protection or is granted Slovak temporary/permanent residence; another EU Member State grants asylum/subsidiary protection; another country grants unlimited residence; or another state grants temporary refuge. Under §131o(2), the §73(13) residence document's validity is extended one year from termination for a person in the §131o(1) cohort. Under subsection (3), the person must leave by the last day of the year unless another lawful basis allows stay.

For current holders, Act 69/2026 §64 (cross-referenced in Slovakia's TP research package) states that Slovak temporary refuge ends when Slovak temporary residence is granted. Under §131o(11), the temporary residence is granted upon issuance of the residence document; the earlier 60-day notification is not itself the grant. Therefore the best-supported sequence is: temporary refuge remains the person's current status while the §131o process is pending; when the document is issued, temporary refuge ends and the new temporary residence begins. Section 131o(14)'s availability before the general TP endpoint would otherwise be unworkable if filing itself ended TP; neither the special text nor the TP statute lists filing as a termination event. Individual document-validity handling during pendency is not specified by the sources reviewed.

Ordinary in-country filing is made personally at a competent Foreign Police department under §31(3), not at an embassy. The general default is filing abroad at the accredited Slovak mission under §31(1), unless an in-country exception applies. Section 31(4) allows a person who filed a **complete** ordinary temporary-residence application at Police to remain in Slovakia until decision. Section 32(1) ordinarily requires passport and all statutory attachments at filing, with a distinct relaxed intake rule for specified employment filing. This permission to remain is not a work authorisation.

## Work during pending applications

- **While TP is still granted:** Act 5/2004 §23a(1)(k) expressly permits an employer to employ a person granted temporary refuge without an employment permit, subject to the employment relationship and employer reporting duties. That independent TP-based work exemption remains the evidence for working during a pending residence application while TP itself continues; filing is not its source.
- **Pending ordinary residence:** Act 404/2011 §31(4) gives stay pending decision after a complete in-country application. It does not generally confer a right to work. Narrow express work-pending exceptions exist, including §32(18) employment application rules and specified purposes under §31(10), but they are route-specific and should not be generalized to every applicant.
- **After TP ends:** Act 5/2004 §72bc supplies three separate employer exemptions: (a) the one-year post-end extension for a person already employed in Slovakia for at least six months immediately before TP ends and continuously employed; (b) holders of an issued employment-purpose residence document; and (c) holders of issued family-reunification residence. The first is limited to at most one year after TP termination. This does not establish that all applicants can continue working while a §131o application is pending after TP ends.
- **While a pre-end §131o application is pending:** if temporary refuge has not terminated and remains granted, work may continue under §23a(1)(k). If the general TP endpoint arrives during a pending application, the six-month/continuous-employment test in §72bc(1)(a) may independently preserve employment, but only for qualifying workers. For any person outside that cohort, post-end pending work permission is **not established** by the reviewed official sources. Do not say “filing means you can keep working.”

## Blue Card

The Blue Card is a separate high-skilled employment residence under §§21(2), 37–39, not one of the six lettered §21(1)(a)–(f) §131o purposes. Current §38(1) allows a third-country national lawfully staying in Slovakia to apply at Foreign Police; §37(3) sets a high-qualified job and salary threshold of normally 1.2 times the preceding year's average monthly wage, or that year's average for qualifying recent graduates (subject to the renewal caveat in the Act); higher qualification must be evidenced under §37(5), and ordinary Blue Card documentation and vacancy confirmation apply. A current TP beneficiary is therefore not barred from filing an ordinary Blue Card application in Slovakia solely because of place of application, but eligibility remains route-specific. Section 131o does not establish a direct TP-to-Blue-Card transition or shortcut. This boundary is “not established by the special rule,” not “prohibited.” The ordinary complete-filing pending-stay rule may apply where its conditions are met; no general work right during that pending Blue Card application is inferred here.

## Long-term residence and TP time

Slovak §52(1)(a) generally uses five years of lawful, continuous stay immediately before filing, with alternate categories in §52(1)(b)–(c). Section 52(2)(e) expressly excludes a person who is an `odídenec` from the grant rule. Under §52(3), periods in specified categories are not counted; §52(5) expressly half-counts study/specified activity periods and counts specified absences and international-protection application periods. Temporary refuge is not enumerated as a countable category in §52(5). **Do not state that TP time counts.**

The statutory text therefore establishes that a current TP beneficiary (`odídenec`) cannot obtain §52 long-term residence while that exclusion applies. Prior TP time's treatment after moving to a qualifying status is **Not established** by the reviewed national text: §52 lists the excluded category and specific additions, but does not expressly say whether earlier TP is wholly excluded or how it is treated after a change of status. The one-year §131o post-end bridge continues `odídenec` treatment subject to exclusions, which appears to engage the §52(2)(e) exclusion during that bridge; whether any bridge period or prior TP period can later count is **Not established**. Separately, Directive 2003/109 excludes current TP from its scope. Do not infer national long-term eligibility from lawful presence or the bridge alone.

## Evidence boundaries and follow-up at drafting

1. **§131o(14) cross-reference:** current pre-end availability is resolved affirmatively, but the exact effect of “subsections 4 through 13 apply” on subsection (4)'s continuity-to-end wording is unresolved. Ask Foreign Police for operational interpretation if publishing detailed pre-end checklist language; avoid promising eligibility from six-month duration alone.
2. **Grant/status sequence:** statute supports that filing is not itself residence grant; issuance of the §131o document grants residence. Temporary refuge continues while pending on the reviewed rules, then ends on Slovak temporary-residence grant. No universal confirmation about what a Police office does with each underlying card during processing was found.
3. **Work pending:** current TP-based work exemption is direct. After the scheme ends, only the express Employment Services Act §72bc groups are established; work for other pending applicants is not established. An ordinary lawful-stay rule is not work permission.
4. **Refusal and appeal:** §131o provides reasons by notice, without a formal written refusal decision. The special rule's complete remedy/appeal path was not independently established here; do not invent one or imply rejection itself ends remaining TP status.
5. **Documents:** §131o's special required documents are clear. The reviewed official sources do not settle special translation/certification/legalisation instructions, document-submission language, exact accommodation proof accepted operationally, reservation slot supply or all department locations. Refresh before advising on logistics.
6. **Ordinary family route:** §27's sponsor and relative rules must be applied to facts; TP status alone does not satisfy the listed sponsor temporary/permanent residence condition. A family member's independent qualifying sponsor basis may support an ordinary route.
7. **Blue Card:** lawful in-country filing and substantive requirements exist separately; the special §131o route does not enumerate Blue Card. Do not claim the TP-linked employment exemption converts into a Blue Card or bypasses it.
8. **Long-term residence:** current `odídenec` status is excluded from grant. Prior TP time and the post-end bridge period's eventual counting after another status begins are not established. Preserve that uncertainty.
