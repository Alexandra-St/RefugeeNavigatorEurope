# Slovakia — Temporary Protection: Applying and Maintaining Your Status

**Research checked:** 2026-11-06  
**Scope:** focused evidence review for `protection-scheme`, `protection-application`, and `protection-dates`. This is a research package, not an article, legal opinion, individual eligibility decision, or final comparison answer.

## Research verdict

**READY FOR CONTENT DRAFTING.** Current Ministry instructions establish mandatory reservation for ordinary temporary-refuge registration and registration with a residence document, under `DOČASNÉ ÚTOČISKO`. The OCP page lists a TP-specific 21-day booking horizon; its separate general rules say 40 days for unauthorised/first-time users and 60 days for authorised users, so do not apply those general windows to TP bookings without a change in the live interface. The registration page lists Thursday 07:30–14:00 for TP registration/document requests; the live system determines which offices and slots are offered, and no claim is made that every OCP accepts every service. The linked electronic form is active, is not the appointment, and is not stated as mandatory. Act 69/2026 supports a qualified explanation of the statutory declaration route, including when a person lacks standard identity documents, without promising acceptance or grant. The EU military-obligation rule is established, but Slovakia-specific proof/checking instructions were not found and must be named as an open operational detail. The EU and Slovak extension mechanism support the scheme endpoint through 4 March 2028, while an official MV SR validation service allows holders to verify and print their own confirmation; this does not establish a uniform printed expiry for every individual document. These limits can be stated safely and do not prevent a bounded draft.

## Termination, travel, and transfer boundaries

- **Current statute:** Act 69/2026 §64 states that temporary refuge ends on the applicable EU/Government time limit; death; applying for international protection in Slovakia; grant of Slovak temporary or permanent residence; written renunciation (effective when received by the Ministry of Interior); grant of international protection in another EU Member State; grant of residence without time limit in another country; temporary refuge granted by another EU Member State; Slovak/other EU citizenship; or cancellation. It does not list simply leaving Slovakia or a temporary visit to Ukraine as an automatic termination event.
- **Family:** under §64(3), where the beneficiary’s status ends under §64(1)(c)–(j), family-reunification beneficiaries’ temporary refuge also ends.
- **Transfer:** §64(4) provides for a Ministry-issued transfer card where a beneficiary is relocated to another EU Member State in which temporary refuge will be granted. This is not evidence of automatic recognition, simultaneous grants, or the complete transfer workflow.
- **Address reporting:** current operational detail for address changes should be checked against the currently applicable residence-document instructions. The legacy Ministry page says to report address/accommodation and describes return travel, but the reviewed sources do not settle all long-absence, re-entry, or document-presentation consequences. Do not promise that travel has no effect in every case.
- **Pending application:** §62(b), (d), and (e) list departure from Slovakia, applying for international protection in Slovakia, or Slovak residence grant as possible grounds to stop a pending proceeding. Do not confuse this procedural rule for a pending applicant with termination rules for an existing beneficiary.
- No legal prohibition should be inferred from an evidence gap; refer individual travel/transfer cases to the competent Ministry or Foreign Police.

## Evidence boundaries

- **Universal processing time:** not established as a service promise. The statute has a 30-day decision deadline with repeatable justified extensions; it does not guarantee a favorable decision or immediate card.
- **Universal complete checklist:** not established. Do not convert the legacy examples into a complete list for every applicant.
- **Appointment availability:** reservation is required for ordinary registration and registration with a residence document. Use category `DOČASNÉ ÚTOČISKO` and the matching service in the live system; the first-registration service is `registrácia dočasného útočiska`. For document issuance, the current official pages display slightly different label variants; match the live service shown. The §131o transition service is distinct and should not be used for first registration. A booked slot is not guaranteed until confirmed.
- **Booking horizon and account:** the OCP page lists temporary-refuge services with a 21-day booking horizon. It also states general 40-day unauthorised/first-time and 60-day authorised-user horizons; do not apply that general split to the TP category absent a current interface change. First-time users enter name, surname, date of birth, mobile and email; authorised users sign in with a valid residence document/BOK.
- **Current office list/hours:** the current MV SR registration page lists TP registration/document requests on Thursday 07:30–14:00, replacing the stale Tuesday/Thursday 07:30–13:30 line. It does not establish that every OCP takes every TP service. The reservation system shows the nearest OCP slot for the selected service and lets the user choose from offered offices/times; follow that live result.
- **Pre-registration:** the current MV SR page links an active temporary-refuge electronic declaration form; it is not an appointment and may speed on-site processing. The separate appointment is mandatory. The Ministry does not say completing the electronic form is mandatory, so don't present it as a prerequisite.
- **Same-day decision/document:** not established universally; old page statement is not carried forward.
- **Military-rule operationalization:** the EU rule and correction are clear; no Slovak official accepted-proof list or screening procedure was located. State the rule “where applicable,” preserve the continuity exception, and direct users to Foreign Police for current accepted evidence; no national checklist is invented. This is a qualification, not a readiness blocker.
- **Prior protection in another Member State:** not automatic recognition. Current Slovak Act 69/2026 §60(2)(b) addresses a person relocated from another Member State who had protection there, but this is a specified route and does not establish general automatic transfer or eligibility after prior protection elsewhere.
- **Travel/re-entry:** no universal guarantee about prolonged absence, return to Ukraine, or re-entry documents is supported by a current Ministry page. Temporary travel is not listed in §64 as an automatic termination event, but do not extrapolate further.
- **2028 individual documents:** no confirmed universal printed expiry, card replacement deadline, or renewal process. The official MV SR QR/ID validation service can display and print an individual confirmation, but the EU endpoint and Slovak status extension remain distinct from that document’s own record.
- **Fees:** no universal fee conclusion for application and later residence-document issuance was verified.
- **Current Slovakia-specific categories:** eligibility is bounded by Resolution 185 and EU law; exact additional national category scope must be checked against the adopted resolution attachment before publication.
