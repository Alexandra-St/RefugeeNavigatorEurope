# Slovakia — Temporary Protection: Applying and Maintaining Your Status

**Research checked:** 2026-11-06  
**Scope:** focused evidence review for `protection-scheme`, `protection-application`, and `protection-dates`. This is a research package, not an article, legal opinion, individual eligibility decision, or final comparison answer.

## Research verdict

**Not Ready for Content Drafting.** The statute and official materials support a bounded account of the separate Slovak eligibility cohorts, the EU temporary-protection endpoint, core application rules, and statutory termination events. But the current Ministry operational page is materially stale in its stated office hours and potentially in the displayed intake sites; no current official Ministry source reviewed here confirms the ordinary reservation/pre-registration workflow, live office list, or the new military-obligation assessment in Slovak practice. The 2026 statutory application procedure also does not support the old Ministry page’s blanket statements that all applicants with documents receive same-day status or that people without documents simply use a roughly 30-day route. Those evidence gaps block an operationally useful application aspect and a fully qualified account of the new military rule.

## Termination, travel, and transfer boundaries

- **Current statute:** Act 69/2026 §64 states that temporary refuge ends on the applicable EU/Government time limit; death; applying for international protection in Slovakia; grant of Slovak temporary or permanent residence; written renunciation (effective when received by the Ministry of Interior); grant of international protection in another EU Member State; grant of residence without time limit in another country; temporary refuge granted by another EU Member State; Slovak/other EU citizenship; or cancellation. It does not list simply leaving Slovakia or a temporary visit to Ukraine as an automatic termination event.
- **Family:** under §64(3), where the beneficiary’s status ends under §64(1)(c)–(j), family-reunification beneficiaries’ temporary refuge also ends.
- **Transfer:** §64(4) provides for a Ministry-issued transfer card where a beneficiary is relocated to another EU Member State in which temporary refuge will be granted. This is not evidence of automatic recognition, simultaneous grants, or the complete transfer workflow.
- **Address reporting:** current operational detail for address changes should be checked against the currently applicable residence-document instructions. The legacy Ministry page says to report address/accommodation and describes return travel, but the reviewed sources do not settle all long-absence, re-entry, or document-presentation consequences. Do not promise that travel has no effect in every case.
- **Pending application:** §62(b), (d), and (e) list departure from Slovakia, applying for international protection in Slovakia, or Slovak residence grant as possible grounds to stop a pending proceeding. Do not confuse this procedural rule for a pending applicant with termination rules for an existing beneficiary.
- No legal prohibition should be inferred from an evidence gap; refer individual travel/transfer cases to the competent Ministry or Foreign Police.

## Evidence boundaries

- **Universal processing time:** not established as a service promise. The statute has a 30-day decision deadline with repeatable justified extensions; it does not guarantee a favorable decision or immediate card.
- **Universal complete checklist:** not established. Do not convert the legacy examples into a complete list for every applicant.
- **Guaranteed appointments or appointment availability:** not established. The ordinary registration page is stale; no current ordinary registration booking rules were verified.
- **Current office list/hours:** not established from current official guidance. The old Ministry page’s Tuesday/Thursday hours and displayed offices are explicitly excluded from current claims.
- **Same-day decision/document:** not established universally; old page statement is not carried forward.
- **Military-rule operationalization:** the EU rule and correction are clear; exact Slovak proof documents, screening process, and guidance remain unpublished/not located in this review.
- **Prior protection in another Member State:** not automatic recognition. Current Slovak Act 69/2026 §60(2)(b) addresses a person relocated from another Member State who had protection there, but this is a specified route and does not establish general automatic transfer or eligibility after prior protection elsewhere.
- **Travel/re-entry:** no universal guarantee about prolonged absence, return to Ukraine, or re-entry documents is supported by a current Ministry page. Temporary travel is not listed in §64 as an automatic termination event, but do not extrapolate further.
- **2028 individual documents:** no confirmed universal printed expiry, card replacement deadline, or renewal process. EU endpoint and Slovak status extension are separate from individual document validity.
- **Fees:** no universal fee conclusion for application and later residence-document issuance was verified.
- **Current Slovakia-specific categories:** eligibility is bounded by Resolution 185 and EU law; exact additional national category scope must be checked against the adopted resolution attachment before publication.

## Final topic verdict

**NOT READY FOR CONTENT DRAFTING**

The blocking gap is the current, ordinary Slovakia registration workflow: current reservation requirement/pre-registration, competent intake offices and live office-hour restrictions, plus how the Ministry applies Act 69/2026 to applicants without standard identity documents. Also unresolved is Slovak operational guidance for proof and assessment under the 5 August 2026 EU military rule. Do not fill these gaps from legacy pages or by inference. The legal findings above can guide targeted follow-up but do not support publication-ready advice on how or when a person should file.
