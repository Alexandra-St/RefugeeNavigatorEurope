# Slovakia — Work Authorization and Employee Rights

## Verdict

**READY FOR CONTENT DRAFTING**, with the qualifications below preserved. A current Slovak temporary-refuge beneficiary (`odídenec`) has an express employer-side work exemption under Act No. 5/2004 Coll. §23a(1)(k), read with §23a(2): no work permit, vacancy confirmation, or labour-market test is required for that employment. This is an employee/employment exemption, not established authority for self-employment. Employers still have reporting and document duties. Former beneficiaries have the separate §72bc transition, with different conditions and a one-year cap only for its six-month pre-termination employment branch. Residence filing alone does not grant a general work right.

The 2026 pay figures are established. The six difficulty-grade minimum wage claims apply only where the employer has not agreed employees' wage conditions in a collective agreement; the employer grades each job by its most demanding activity. Collective-agreement coverage is not universal. Draft all three aspects with these boundaries and the date qualification.

## Findings for safe drafting

### Current work under temporary refuge

- **Controlling provisions:** Act 5/2004 §23a(1)(k) covers a third-country national who has been granted temporary refuge (`ktorému sa poskytlo dočasné útočisko`). Under §23a(2), a person in §23a(1) may be employed without a high-skilled vacancy confirmation, ordinary vacancy confirmation, or work permit. The clause does not impose an employer/job restriction for this current TP exemption.
- **Labour-market test:** no confirmation is required under §23a(2), so an ordinary labour-market test is not required for that employment. This is distinct from the narrower §21b(7)(e) no-market-review case used for an employment-purpose residence application: that route specifically requires at least six months in an employment relationship and the same job.
- **Relationship type:** the §23a text does not expressly say in item (k) “contract only.” ÚPSVaR's Ukraine guidance says TP employment may be either a written employment contract or an agreement for work outside an employment relationship (`dohoda`). The page is legacy and has an obsolete TP end date, so use it only for the form-of-work point, in conjunction with the current statute. Treat this claim as qualified and recheck operational guidance at publication.
- **Business/self-employment:** the §23a(1)(k) exception is framed as an employer employing a third-country national and supports employee work. Act 404/2011 §126(2) lists specific residence categories that may conduct business. No reviewed current source establishes that temporary refuge alone or §23a(1)(k) authorizes self-employment/business. Do not infer prohibition from this research gap; assess the separate business/residence rules.
- **Current period:** the exemption attaches to the granted TP status. Act 69/2026 §64(1)(d) says that Slovak TP ends if temporary or permanent residence is granted in Slovakia. An application by itself is not the grant event.

### Employer's information-card and document duties

- **Deadline and channel:** Act 5/2004 §23b(2) requires reporting the start and end of employment of a third-country national within seven working days of each event. For a §23a employee, §23b(5) requires data electronically on the prescribed form with a qualified electronic signature, also within seven working days after start and termination. ÚPSVaR calls the current form an `Informačná karta` and gives the electronic public-service route.
- **Competent office:** submit to the locally competent Office of Labour, Social Affairs and Family. Current ÚPSVaR service guidance describes local competence; older TP-specific wording ties the office to the workplace. Confirm the current portal's competent-office selection for an individual case.
- **Attachments:** §23b(2) requires the employer's notice concerning a third-country national to include a copy of the employment relationship (for TP this can be the contract or applicable agreement). Section 23b(5) additionally requires a copy of the document proving the condition in §23a(1), i.e. the status/eligibility evidence for temporary refuge. Thus both the work-relationship copy and the status evidence are supported by current statute, even though the legacy guidance is not a current workflow source. Keep copies during employment as required by §23b(1)(b).
- **Other foreign-worker duties:** before hiring, the employer must obtain a copy of the person's valid residence document or other lawful residence/work entitlement under §23b(1)(a), retain the specified eligibility documents during employment under §23b(1)(b), and give the employee a copy of any relevant vacancy confirmation where that type of confirmation is required. Under §23b(3), the employer must separately notify the office within seven working days if a third-country national whose job rests on a Blue Card, vacancy confirmation, or permit does not actually start. That non-start notice is not stated as a TP-§23a duty.
- **After a §131o employment residence document is issued:** ÚPSVaR's §72bc instructions direct a continuously employing employer to file an employer-data-change notice and attach a copy of the residence card to update the legal basis from §23a(1)(k) to §72bc. Follow the current portal/form when that transition occurs.
- **Date caveat:** a current ÚPSVaR page specifically bearing an update date of **7 July 2026** was not identified in the reviewed official pages. The available 15 July service and legislative-change pages and the current consolidated statute were checked instead. Do not cite the old Ukraine page's attachment instructions as though its workflow were updated in July 2026.

### Separate post-TP employment bridge: §72bc

Section 72bc took effect on 15 July 2026 and is not the ordinary current-TP permission. Its three cases are:

1. **§72bc(1)(a), up to one year after TP ends:** a former beneficiary was employed in Slovakia for at least six months immediately before the general termination date and remains continuously employed. The statute does not expressly require the same employer or same job. ÚPSVaR states that duration is assessed by the length of employment and that employer company ID and SK ISCO occupation are not decisive. Do not infer that any unexplained gap is allowed: “continuously employed” remains an express condition, and the precise administrative treatment of breaks is not elaborated.
2. **§72bc(1)(b):** a person has been issued an employment-purpose temporary residence document.
3. **§72bc(1)(c):** a person has been issued a family-reunification temporary residence document.

Under §72bc(2), employment in each case is treated as employment under §23a, so §23a's no-permit/no-confirmation rule and its related employer reporting framework apply. The one-year cap is written only into case (a); do not extend it to the separate issued-document cases (b) and (c). These are issued residence documents, not merely pending applications.

For a pre-end §131o employment residence application, §23a(1)(k) continues to support employment while TP itself remains granted. If TP ends while the application is pending, §72bc(1)(a) may cover a person who meets its six-month/continuous-employment test. For everyone else, §131o filing alone does not establish continuing work permission. The Phase 20.3 residence research separately records that temporary residence is granted on issuance of its residence document; Act 69/2026 §64(1)(d) then ends TP.

### Other pending residence applications

- **Ordinary employment residence:** Act 5/2004 §21(1)(h) allows employment while a complete employment-residence application is pending only from the start date specified by the valid vacancy confirmation that contains consent, and until the proceeding is finally concluded. Employer notice and prescribed copies remain due. A pending filing without the required consent confirmation does not qualify through this paragraph.
- **Blue Card:** §21(1)(g) provides a parallel narrow work-pending route where the high-skilled vacancy confirmation contains consent and the complete Blue Card application (or qualifying renewal) has been accepted; it runs from the confirmation's effective date through final decision. Residence Act §§37–39 govern Blue Card requirements. A Blue Card filing or lawful stay alone is not sufficient.
- **§131o:** this is a residence-transition route, not a free-standing work-pending exemption. While TP is current, the existing §23a(1)(k) right applies. After TP ends, apply §72bc only if its specific conditions are met, or rely on another expressly applicable work route.
- **Other statuses:** Act 5/2004 §23a contains other specified categories (for example certain students, graduates of Slovak schools, or asylum applicants where the asylum law permits market access). These are status- and condition-specific; they do not make every pending residence application work-authorizing. Do not list broader eligibility without separately checking that category's current statutory conditions.
- **Key distinction:** Act 404/2011 §31(4) can allow a person with a complete in-country residence application to remain pending decision. That is a stay rule; it is not, by itself, permission to work.

### Employee rights baseline

- **Written employment contract:** Labour Code §42(1) establishes the employment relationship by written contract and requires the employer to give the employee one written counterpart. Section 43 requires employer/employee identity, work type, place of work, starting date, and wage conditions; wage conditions may be referenced through an applicable collective agreement or statute. The relationship begins on the agreed start date (§46). An employer must provide any missing listed working-condition information in writing under §47a, generally within seven days for schedule/pay-related items and four weeks for leave, termination and training information.
- **Language:** no reviewed current provision imposes a universal rule that an employment contract be translated into the foreign worker's preferred language. Do not invent such a requirement. Separately, OSH Act §7 requires workplace safety instruction to be understandable and demonstrable; it does not name a required language.
- **Working time:** ordinary weekly maximum is 40 hours; statutory maxima are 38¾ hours in qualifying two-shift work, 37½ in three-shift/continuous operations and 33½ for specified carcinogenic/radiation work (§85). Average weekly work including overtime ordinarily may not exceed 48 hours (§85(9)), with a narrowly consent-based health-worker exception. Daily rest is normally 12 consecutive hours; weekly rest is normally two consecutive days with statutory operational exceptions (§§92–93).
- **Overtime:** overtime is ordered or agreed only for temporary and urgent increased need or public interest; a part-time worker cannot be ordered to work overtime (§97). Ordered overtime is ordinarily capped at 150 hours/year; total employee overtime is ordinarily capped at 400 hours/year, and the overtime average is generally at most eight hours/week over the statutory reference period. Overtime is paid at the earned wage plus at least 25% of average earnings (35% for risk work), unless compensatory leave is agreed under §121. Statutory and role-based exceptions exist; avoid presenting this as an unconditional entitlement structure for every senior employee.
- **Annual leave:** at least four weeks; at least five weeks for a worker who reaches age 33 by year-end or permanently cares for a child (§103). Calendar-year leave normally requires at least 60 days' work in the continuous employment relationship; otherwise statutory leave-for-days/proportionate rules may apply (§§101–102). Do not imply four weeks are immediately available on hiring.
- **Safety:** employers must assess and prevent risks, maintain safe workplaces and equipment, train employees understandably on risks and safe procedures, and provide required effective personal protective equipment free of charge (§§6–7 Act 124/2006). Safety obligations and costs stay with the employer.
- **Equal treatment and complaints:** Labour Code §13 and Act 365/2004 prohibit discrimination on covered grounds in employment and provide complaint/protection routes. A worker may first submit a complaint to the employer under §13(7); official inspection submissions go to the competent regional Labour Inspectorate or National Labour Inspectorate portal. The inspectorate can inspect and require correction; it is not a court or a guarantee of compensation.

## Evidence boundaries and drafting checklist

1. **Self-employment/business:** the §23a employee exception is not evidence of business permission; a separate status/residence route must be established. Whether a specific temporary-refuge holder may perform particular business activity is not established here.
2. **Work forms:** current statute contains no item-(k) form limitation; the official TP help page identifies both contracts and outside-employment agreements but has other stale information. Keep this as qualified; no different self-employed arrangement is inferred.
3. **Current notice source date:** no relevant official ÚPSVaR page specifically identified as updated 7 July 2026 was located. The legal seven-working-day rule is directly verified in the effective statute and current July 15 service pages; the old TP guide's TP end date is stale.
4. **Documents with information card:** current §23b supports attaching a copy of the work relationship and proof of §23a eligibility. State each separately. The legacy page is not relied upon for current form mechanics.
5. **§72bc continuity:** the Act requires continuous employment in (a); ÚPSVaR says employer company ID and occupation code do not control the six-month duration. The detailed rule for interruptions/brief gaps is not explained and should not be guessed.
6. **Same employer/job:** not textually required by §72bc(1)(a); current ÚPSVaR says company ID/SK ISCO do not govern duration. Do not confuse this with the separate §21b(7)(e) same-job condition for a market-review exemption in an employment-residence process.
7. **Pending application:** only the qualified §21(1)(g)/(h), §23a, and §72bc cases above establish work in pending/status-transition situations. Lawful stay pending decision does not fill gaps in work permission.
8. **Contract language:** no universal translated-contract or foreign-worker-language rule was located in the reviewed provisions. OSH briefing must be understandable; this is not a universal contract translation rule.
9. **Collective agreements:** may improve wage and other conditions; do not claim every employer or worker is covered, or that a collective agreement removes the general minimum-wage floor.
10. **Wage figures:** valid for 1 January–31 December 2026 only. Do not publish net amounts. Recheck annual notices before any 2027 claim.
11. **Grade boundary:** §120 grade claims are based on the job's most demanding activity and only apply where wage terms are not collectively agreed; retain general minimum wage as distinct base protection.
12. **Employee protections:** state core limits as baseline rules with statutory schedule, occupation and agreement exceptions; the Labour Code is too detailed to reduce every overtime, leave, supplement or shift exception to a universal promise.
