# SE-HEALTH-01 — Sources and Claims

Research date: **28 October 2026 (UTC)**. Focused refresh completed at **00:15 UTC**. Research only; not article copy, individual healthcare/insurance advice, legal certification, or publication approval. Healthcare access, population registration and social-insurance status are distinct legal questions.

## Scope

Working title: **Accessing Healthcare in Sweden**. Category: healthcare. Focus: healthcare access for temporary-protection permit holders, the distinction between registered and unregistered people, service navigation, urgent care and the boundary between healthcare and social-insurance benefits. Fees and service arrangements may vary by region.

## Conflicts / source-resolution note

The Försäkringskassan English summary (last updated 16 March 2026) still says that a person who has lived in Sweden for less than two years cannot be “registered as a resident” in a section about social-insurance benefits. Read literally as folkbokföring, this does not match the current Act 2024:691 §5 and Skatteverket guidance: the latter use at least 12 months of combined permit validity plus an expected further six-month stay, with individual decisions and a date-specific 5 September 2026 cutoff. However, the Försäkringskassan page is discussing benefit residence, not clearly defining folkbokföring. The current Social Insurance Code chapter 5 §3 separately says covered TPD permit holders are generally not considered bosatt for residence-based benefits, subject to its express exception; work-based benefits follow a separate framework. Therefore record the English summary as ambiguous and potentially stale when read as population-registration guidance, not as a controlling rule or definitive statement about benefit eligibility. Healthcare access is governed separately and is not inferred from social-insurance residence. The Skatteverket four-week window is the deadline to submit the notification after meeting the criteria. Its separate general processing page currently estimates about six weeks for a moving-to-Sweden case to be assigned to an administrator; that is not a TPD-specific decision-time guarantee.

## Article-safety boundary

Do not state that all healthcare is free, that all adults must pay for all care, that every unregistered adult receives only emergency care, or that folkbokföring automatically makes a TPD holder eligible for residence-based social-insurance benefits. Recheck cohort-specific registration guidance before publication because the 5 September 2026 cutoff is tied to the current EU protection endpoint.
