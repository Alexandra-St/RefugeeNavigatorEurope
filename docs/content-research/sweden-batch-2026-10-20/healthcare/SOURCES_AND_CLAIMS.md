# SE-HEALTH-01 — Sources and Claims

Research date: **20 October 2026 (UTC)**. Research only; not article copy, individual healthcare/insurance advice, legal certification, or publication approval. Sources were opened and relevant passages reviewed on the research date; exact check times were not recorded. Healthcare access, population registration and social-insurance status are distinct legal questions.

## Scope

Working title: **Accessing Healthcare in Sweden**. Category: healthcare. Focus: healthcare access for temporary-protection permit holders, the distinction between registered and unregistered people, service navigation, urgent care and the boundary between healthcare and social-insurance benefits. Fees and service arrangements may vary by region.

## Conflicts / source-resolution note

Försäkringskassan’s English summary contains a conflicting “less than two years” statement about being registered as a resident. It is not used to establish the folkbokföring threshold. Current Act 2024:691 §5 and Skatteverket’s operational guidance establish the 12-month combined-permit period plus expected further six-month stay test. Skatteverket determines individual cases. Folkbokföring and “bosatt” for residence-based Social Insurance Code benefits are legally distinct. A person can be population-registered and still fall under the Code’s special exclusion for covered TPD permit holders. Healthcare access is assessed under its separate healthcare rules, not inferred from the social-insurance residence rule.

## Article-safety boundary

Do not state that all healthcare is free, that all adults must pay for all care, that every unregistered adult receives only emergency care, or that folkbokföring automatically makes a TPD holder “bosatt” for social-insurance benefits.
