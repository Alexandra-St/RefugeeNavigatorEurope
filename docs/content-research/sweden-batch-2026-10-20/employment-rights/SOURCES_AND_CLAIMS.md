## Atomic claims

| Claim ID | Proposition and sources | Status | Editorial boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SE-EMP02-01 | A person with a granted Swedish residence permit under the Temporary Protection Directive has the right to work during the permit’s validity, subject to the permit’s scope and conditions (WR-S01, WR-S02). | Supported | A pending application or receipt is not a granted permit; check the individual decision/card. |
| SE-EMP02-02 | Employers have tax-agency notification duties when employing certain non-EU/EEA/Swiss nationals; those administrative duties are separate from the worker’s immigration authorization (WR-S02, WR-S03). | Conditional | Duties depend on the hiring situation; do not present notification as a work permit or a complete checklist. |
| SE-EMP02-03 | Swedish law requires employers to provide written information about material employment terms, including job, employment form, pay and working-time information; different items have different statutory deadlines (WR-S04, §6c). | Supported | This is a statutory information duty, not a claim that every employment agreement must be made in a signed written contract. Exceptions and collective agreements must be checked. |
| SE-EMP02-04 | The ordinary-hours baseline in the Working Hours Act is at most 40 hours per week, subject to statutory scope, exceptions and qualifying collective-agreement deviations (WR-S05, §§2–5). | Conditional | Do not present 40 hours as an unconditional rule for every occupation or work arrangement. |
| SE-EMP02-05 | The Annual Leave Act provides 25 vacation days per leave year, but paid leave depends on accrual and the Act contains exceptions, including for certain short employment (WR-S06, §§4–7). | Conditional | Do not equate 25 days with 25 paid days in every first year or contract. |
| SE-EMP02-06 | Sweden has no general statutory national minimum-wage amount; wages are commonly set through collective bargaining, so check the applicable agreement and terms for the occupation (WR-S07, WR-S09). | Supported | No wage amount is supplied; do not imply every worker is covered by a collective agreement. |
| SE-EMP02-07 | Employment-protection rules address ending employment and written information must include applicable termination conditions (WR-S04, §§4–6c). | Conditional | Outcomes depend on employment type, length, reason and applicable rules; this is not individual dismissal advice. |
| SE-EMP02-08 | Employers are responsible for the work environment; Arbetsmiljöverket provides official information on workplace-safety responsibilities (WR-S08). | Supported | Do not claim the authority resolves every employment dispute or that all benefits follow automatically from work. |

## Conflicts / source limitations

A general EURES page simplifies employment-contract wording. The current Swedish Employment Protection Act requires written information about material terms; it does not support a blanket statement that every agreement must be a signed written contract. Immigration work authorization, tax reporting, and workplace rights are separate questions. No national wage amount is established by the sources.

## Publication blockers / boundaries

Before publication, recheck permit-specific work wording and the current statutory texts. Do not give individual immigration, tax, termination or benefit advice. Do not state a minimum-wage amount or promise universal collective-agreement coverage.

## Article-safety boundary

Do not say that temporary protection automatically grants a Swedish work permit before a decision, that employer notifications confer work rights, that all employment contracts require a signed writing, that every hour/leave rule is identical, or that Sweden has a statutory national minimum wage.
