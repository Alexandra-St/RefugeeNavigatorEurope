## A. Product objective and editorial baseline

Design a non-personalized navigation aid for applying under Denmark’s Ukraine Special Act, waiting for a decision and checking an existing permit. Selecting a situation selects explanatory content; it does not classify eligibility, recommend which application an individual must file or validate a permit.

Primary article **P**: `afc14824-4035-4ca7-9662-6d5352ba63cb`, “Temporary protection in Denmark: how to apply and extend your permit”. All three current production translations are **Verified**. The research matrix’s earlier draft/publication-gate statements are historical; this proposal does not revert article approval. Article approval is not guide approval or a new source check.

Evidence baseline: `docs/content-research/denmark-temporary-protection-application/SOURCES_AND_CLAIMS.md`, including the later C15 update and C20 supplement. No new legal research or source reopening was performed in Phase 11.1. Source-check dates remain those already recorded (15 October 2026); no freshness claim is made for 18 October. Source support below is traced through the approved article and recorded research, not inferred from source titles.

Optional onward article **R**: `2d106fd3-e981-4ef9-9733-568b654dfca5`, “Residence options after temporary protection in Denmark”. Use only as navigation; do not copy work/study-route advice into this guide. Its opening states: “A residence permit under Denmark’s Special Act for people displaced from Ukraine is temporary. It does not automatically establish which other permits a person may qualify for.”

Proposed guide identity (design metadata only): ID `denmark-temporary-protection-v1`; slug `temporary-protection`; country `10000000-0000-0000-0000-000000000001`. Proposed route `/guides/denmark/temporary-protection`. Initial implementation must be **Draft**, hidden in Production until separate human acceptance.

## B. Four situations

| Scenario / proposed stable ID | Practical purpose | Steps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A — First section 1 application / `first-section-one-application` | Understand the national route and current process without deciding personal eligibility. | A1–A4 |
| B — Family section 2 application / `family-section-two-application` | Navigate a separate and limited family route; assess each person separately with the authority. | B1–B4 |
| C — Application submitted; decision pending / `application-pending` | Keep records, complete instructions and understand conditional waiting-stage positions. | C1–C4 |
| D — Existing Special Act permit / `existing-special-act-permit` | Read the actual decision, separate municipal/CPR matters and clarify extension information. | D1–D4 |

**Total: 16 proposed steps (4 + 4 + 4 + 4).** Order is a reading/navigation sequence, not a universal statutory sequence. All steps are labelled **Recommendation**: the action is to check/follow case-specific instructions, not a guide-generated legal obligation. Source-required conditions are retained in the explanation/warning.

## C. Proposed steps

All steps use supporting article **P** and the Danish Immigration Service as the competent decision authority unless another service is named. Document examples are potentially relevant preparation, never a universal mandatory checklist. Each step’s source labels resolve to exact existing GUIDs and URLs in section E; that crosswalk is part of each step’s evidence specification.

### A1 — Check the national route and current criteria

- **Stable proposed step ID:** `section-one-check-framework`; **kind:** Recommendation.
- **One practical action:** Read the current section 1 criteria on New to Denmark; ask the Danish Immigration Service about uncertain facts.
- **Explanation:** Denmark uses its own national Special Act. Review the date, nationality, residence and other-status conditions rather than treating another EU country’s protection document as a Danish eligibility decision.
- **Potentially relevant documents:** Potentially relevant identity, nationality, residence/displacement timeline, Ukrainian refugee-status and other residence-permission records. These are examples, not a mandatory universal checklist.
- **Responsible authority:** Danish Immigration Service (New to Denmark); follow the current case/appointment channel.
- **Supporting article:** P (`afc14824-4035-4ca7-9662-6d5352ba63cb`).
- **Exact English article passage:** “Denmark’s Ukraine Special Act is a national scheme. Denmark does not participate in the EU Temporary Protection Directive. A protection permit or status from another EU country does not by itself establish eligibility under Danish law.”
- **Research ClaimIds:** C01 C02 C03 C19; **research sources:** S01 S03 S15 S16. Existing OfficialSource GUIDs are mapped in section E.
- **Conditions / limitation:** No eligibility verdict. Preserve the article’s alternative section 1 categories and its other-citizenship/residence caveat; do not reduce them to one simplified qualifying test.
- **Optional next link:** None beyond the full primary article and official sources.

### A2 — Prepare potentially relevant records

- **Stable proposed step ID:** `section-one-prepare-case-records`; **kind:** Recommendation.
- **One practical action:** Keep the documents relevant to your case and check whether the limited military-status rule needs clarification.
- **Explanation:** Identity and status records may be requested. The special military-status provision is limited to the affected group and application dates; ask the service how its scope and transition apply, rather than self-classifying.
- **Potentially relevant documents:** Identity records; Ukrainian refugee-status evidence or children’s/family records where relevant; application dates and military exemption/obligation records only where the supported rule applies.
- **Responsible authority:** Danish Immigration Service (New to Denmark); follow the current case/appointment channel.
- **Supporting article:** P (`afc14824-4035-4ca7-9662-6d5352ba63cb`).
- **Exact English article passage:** “Applicants are asked for identity documents; depending on the case, evidence of refugee status in Ukraine, children’s documents, or a family relationship may also be needed.”
- **Research ClaimIds:** C05 C07; **research sources:** S01 S02 S07 S14. Existing OfficialSource GUIDs are mapped in section E.
- **Conditions / limitation:** The military warning must carry the full scope in section H below, including 25 June / 3 September 2026 and transition safeguards. Neither a universal checklist nor a demand for military records from every reader.
- **Optional next link:** None beyond the full primary article and official sources.

### A3 — Follow the current SL1 instructions

- **Stable proposed step ID:** `section-one-follow-sl1-route`; **kind:** Recommendation.
- **One practical action:** Use the current section 1 application instructions, including the presence requirement and SL1 route.
- **Explanation:** The main section 1 route requires presence in Denmark. The current page offers digital and paper SL1 applications. Follow the live form instructions; this guide does not choose an application for you.
- **Potentially relevant documents:** Current SL1 form, relevant identity/supporting records and a copy of the submission if made; ask the service which evidence it needs.
- **Responsible authority:** Danish Immigration Service (New to Denmark); follow the current case/appointment channel.
- **Supporting article:** P (`afc14824-4035-4ca7-9662-6d5352ba63cb`).
- **Exact English article passage:** “For a section 1 application, the Immigration Service says you must be in Denmark. A qualifying section 2 family application may be submitted in Denmark or abroad.”
- **Research ClaimIds:** C06 C07; **research sources:** S01 S02. Existing OfficialSource GUIDs are mapped in section E.
- **Conditions / limitation:** SL1 and SL2 are different routes. Forms can change. This step does not direct a person outside the criteria to submit.
- **Optional next link:** None beyond the full primary article and official sources.

### A4 — Follow identity and biometric instructions

- **Stable proposed step ID:** `section-one-follow-identity-instructions`; **kind:** Recommendation.
- **One practical action:** After submission, follow the current appointment instructions for identity checks and biometrics.
- **Explanation:** The reviewed page states four weeks after submission, with an exception to repeating biometrics for some asylum/accommodation-card holders. Follow instructions about children’s attendance.
- **Potentially relevant documents:** Appointment instructions, application record and identity papers; any existing asylum/accommodation card where relevant. Confirm the required records with the service.
- **Responsible authority:** Danish Immigration Service (New to Denmark); follow the current case/appointment channel.
- **Supporting article:** P (`afc14824-4035-4ca7-9662-6d5352ba63cb`).
- **Exact English article passage:** “After submitting, book the required appointment for an identity check and biometrics. The current page gives a four-week deadline. It describes an exception to repeating biometrics for some people who already hold an asylum or accommodation card.”
- **Research ClaimIds:** C08; **research sources:** S01 S03. Existing OfficialSource GUIDs are mapped in section E.
- **Conditions / limitation:** Recheck the live deadline and exceptions. Do not require every applicant to repeat biometrics or assume a child need not attend.
- **Optional next link:** None beyond the full primary article and official sources.

### B1 — Check the separate family-member criteria

- **Stable proposed step ID:** `family-check-section-two-route`; **kind:** Recommendation.
- **One practical action:** Read the section 2 family page and ask the service to assess any unclear relationship conditions.
- **Explanation:** This route covers specified relationships, not every relative. The reviewed guidance requires the relevant family life to have existed on 24 February 2022. Each family member’s position must be checked.
- **Potentially relevant documents:** Potentially relevant relationship and family-life records, identity papers, children’s records and the principal person’s case/permit records; not a universal checklist.
- **Responsible authority:** Danish Immigration Service (New to Denmark); follow the current case/appointment channel.
- **Supporting article:** P (`afc14824-4035-4ca7-9662-6d5352ba63cb`).
- **Exact English article passage:** “Section 2 may cover specified close family members of a person with a section 1 permit, including a cohabiting spouse or partner, an unmarried child under 18, and certain dependent or otherwise qualifying close relatives. The agency says the relevant family life must have existed on 24 February 2022.”
- **Research ClaimIds:** C03 C04 C19; **research sources:** S01 S03 S15. Existing OfficialSource GUIDs are mapped in section E.
- **Conditions / limitation:** Preserve limited relationship categories and any child/concurrent-application instructions. A relationship or one relative’s approval does not itself decide another application.
- **Optional next link:** None beyond the full primary article and official sources.

### B2 — Follow the current family application route

- **Stable proposed step ID:** `family-follow-sl2-instructions`; **kind:** Recommendation.
- **One practical action:** Check the SL2 instructions and the location rules for your family application.
- **Explanation:** A qualifying section 2 application may be submitted in Denmark or abroad. Some children may apply with a parent; other family members may need SL2. Follow the current family page.
- **Potentially relevant documents:** Current SL2 instructions, identity and relationship records requested for the particular case; retain submission records if applicable.
- **Responsible authority:** Danish Immigration Service (New to Denmark); follow the current case/appointment channel.
- **Supporting article:** P (`afc14824-4035-4ca7-9662-6d5352ba63cb`).
- **Exact English article passage:** “Some children may apply with a parent; other family members may need their own SL2 application. Each relationship and set of documents is assessed under its own conditions.”
- **Research ClaimIds:** C04 C06 C07; **research sources:** S01 S03. Existing OfficialSource GUIDs are mapped in section E.
- **Conditions / limitation:** Do not import the section 1 presence rule into every family application or imply identical procedures for all family members.
- **Optional next link:** None beyond the full primary article and official sources.

### B3 — Clarify case-specific evidence

- **Stable proposed step ID:** `family-check-case-specific-evidence`; **kind:** Recommendation.
- **One practical action:** Ask the service what further evidence or special conditions apply to the family case.
- **Explanation:** Other nationality or residence permission can affect assessment. Where the supported military-status rule may apply, retain all its scope and transition caveats; do not infer an exemption simply because this is a family route.
- **Potentially relevant documents:** Relevant identity, other residence-permission, relationship and date records; military-status evidence only for the supported affected group and as instructed.
- **Responsible authority:** Danish Immigration Service (New to Denmark); follow the current case/appointment channel.
- **Supporting article:** P (`afc14824-4035-4ca7-9662-6d5352ba63cb`).
- **Exact English article passage:** “Other citizenship or a residence permit in another country can affect the assessment. These rules are fact-specific; the list is not a decision about any reader’s eligibility.”
- **Research ClaimIds:** C03 C05 C07 C19; **research sources:** S01 S03 S07 S14. Existing OfficialSource GUIDs are mapped in section E.
- **Conditions / limitation:** No claim that every family applicant is subject to military restrictions. The complete warning in section H applies only within its stated scope.
- **Optional next link:** None beyond the full primary article and official sources.

### C1 — Keep proof of submission

- **Stable proposed step ID:** `pending-keep-submission-records`; **kind:** Recommendation.
- **One practical action:** Retain your application receipt and follow up on any incomplete identity/biometric instructions.
- **Explanation:** The application is received when it reaches the service. Its reviewed 35-day figure is an agency average maximum processing-time target, not a guarantee; some cases take longer.
- **Potentially relevant documents:** Application receipt, case reference, appointment/biometric record and correspondence, if issued; these are practical record-keeping examples.
- **Responsible authority:** Danish Immigration Service (New to Denmark); follow the current case/appointment channel.
- **Supporting article:** P (`afc14824-4035-4ca7-9662-6d5352ba63cb`).
- **Exact English article passage:** “The agency says an application is received when it reaches the Immigration Service. It currently gives an average maximum processing-time target of 35 days; this is not a promise, and some cases take longer.”
- **Research ClaimIds:** C08 C09; **research sources:** S01 S03 S04. Existing OfficialSource GUIDs are mapped in section E.
- **Conditions / limitation:** No guaranteed decision date and no assumption that pressing submit completes identity requirements.
- **Optional next link:** None beyond the full primary article and official sources.

### C2 — Check the conditions before starting work

- **Stable proposed step ID:** `pending-clarify-work-conditions`; **kind:** Recommendation.
- **One practical action:** Check the waiting-stage work conditions; if their application is uncertain, follow the service’s caution not to start before a decision.
- **Explanation:** The reviewed guidance requires recorded fingerprints and facial image and excludes applicants who obviously cannot qualify under section 1 or 2. Submission alone is insufficient.
- **Potentially relevant documents:** Application/biometric records and any written case guidance; no guide-generated work authorization certificate.
- **Responsible authority:** Danish Immigration Service (New to Denmark); follow the current case/appointment channel.
- **Supporting article:** P (`afc14824-4035-4ca7-9662-6d5352ba63cb`).
- **Exact English article passage:** “The Immigration Service says it begins after your fingerprints and facial image have been recorded, and does not apply if you obviously cannot qualify under section 1 or 2. If you or an employer is unsure whether this exclusion applies, the agency says not to start work before a decision.”
- **Research ClaimIds:** C11; **research sources:** S04. Existing OfficialSource GUIDs are mapped in section E.
- **Conditions / limitation:** Preserve both conditions and the uncertainty caution. Do not describe pending applicants as having unrestricted work rights.
- **Optional next link:** None beyond the full primary article and official sources.

### C3 — Check the limits of the pending position

- **Stable proposed step ID:** `pending-check-stay-travel-health`; **kind:** Recommendation.
- **One practical action:** Ask about re-entry before travelling and use the separate healthcare guidance for processing-stage access.
- **Explanation:** A pending application is not an approved permit or a general re-entry right. The reviewed guidance describes only certain healthcare services on proof of application; this is not full approved-resident coverage.
- **Potentially relevant documents:** Application proof and any written travel/health instructions; the guide does not request uploads or collect medical/travel details.
- **Responsible authority:** Danish Immigration Service (New to Denmark); follow the current case/appointment channel.
- **Supporting article:** P (`afc14824-4035-4ca7-9662-6d5352ba63cb`).
- **Exact English article passage:** “Do not assume that an application gives a right to re-enter Denmark after travel. Check the current travel and re-entry rules before leaving.”
- **Research ClaimIds:** C10 C12; **research sources:** S01 S04. Existing OfficialSource GUIDs are mapped in section E.
- **Conditions / limitation:** Pending-stay guidance is not a promise of entry from abroad. C12 remains a limited signpost, not a list of healthcare entitlements.
- **Optional next link:** Denmark Healthcare article `b1e4aed1-bbaf-4f09-9468-74000bf96430`, `/countries/denmark/categories/healthcare#entry-b1e4aed1bbaf4f09946874000bf96430`; navigation only, not incorporated healthcare claims.

### C4 — Respond through the instructed channel

- **Stable proposed step ID:** `pending-respond-to-case-messages`; **kind:** Recommendation.
- **One practical action:** Read case messages and provide requested information through the service’s instructed contact channel.
- **Explanation:** Use the “Displaced from Ukraine” contact channel when directed. If a refusal arrives, read it immediately, follow its specific appeal instructions and seek current qualified guidance promptly.
- **Potentially relevant documents:** Request letter, case reference and evidence actually requested; retain the decision if one arrives. This is not a universal evidence list.
- **Responsible authority:** Danish Immigration Service (New to Denmark); follow the current case/appointment channel.
- **Supporting article:** P (`afc14824-4035-4ca7-9662-6d5352ba63cb`).
- **Exact English article passage:** “Tell the Immigration Service if it requests more information, and use the contact channel for “Displaced from Ukraine.””
- **Research ClaimIds:** C09 C15 C19; **research sources:** S01 S04 S06 S17. Existing OfficialSource GUIDs are mapped in section E.
- **Conditions / limitation:** No separate appeal journey in V1. Appeal procedures are decision-specific: no universal numeric deadline, automatic suspensive effect or promise of permission to remain.
- **Optional next link:** None beyond the full primary article and official sources.

### D1 — Read your actual decision and conditions

- **Stable proposed step ID:** `permit-read-individual-decision`; **kind:** Recommendation.
- **One practical action:** Check the decision and documents issued to you, including the permit’s work conditions.
- **Explanation:** The reviewed guidance says a Special Act permit allows work subject to its conditions. A general page does not decide your personal validity or travel position.
- **Potentially relevant documents:** Individual decision, permit/residence card if issued and latest written case communication; practical records only.
- **Responsible authority:** Danish Immigration Service (New to Denmark); follow the current case/appointment channel.
- **Supporting article:** P (`afc14824-4035-4ca7-9662-6d5352ba63cb`).
- **Exact English article passage:** “If your application is approved, read the decision and the documents issued to you. The Immigration Service says a Special Act permit allows work in Denmark, subject to the conditions of your permit.”
- **Research ClaimIds:** C13 C19; **research sources:** S01 S05. Existing OfficialSource GUIDs are mapped in section E.
- **Conditions / limitation:** No individualized expiry or work determination; the older post-approval page is not a basis for new procedural detail.
- **Optional next link:** None beyond the full primary article and official sources.

### D2 — Ask the municipality about separate next steps

- **Stable proposed step ID:** `permit-separate-municipality-cpr-housing`; **kind:** Recommendation.
- **One practical action:** Ask the assigned municipality about address/CPR registration and, separately, accommodation arrangements.
- **Explanation:** Municipal assignment is not housing entitlement. Under Integration Act section 57(5), ordinary section 12 placement does not apply to the specified Special Act section 2(1) basis, subject to the service’s very-special-personal-circumstances discretion. Other municipal responsibilities are not automatically removed.
- **Potentially relevant documents:** Decision, assignment letter, address and identity records as requested locally; no universal document list or CPR timing promise.
- **Responsible authority:** Assigned municipality / Citizen Service or relevant International Citizen Service for address and CPR; Danish Immigration Service for permit-basis or section 57(5) questions.
- **Supporting article:** P (`afc14824-4035-4ca7-9662-6d5352ba63cb`).
- **Exact English article passage:** “Under section 57(5) of the Integration Act, ordinary section 12 accommodation placement does not apply to a permit under section 2(1) of the Ukraine Special Act, although the Danish Immigration Service may decide otherwise in very special personal circumstances. This is an accommodation rule only; it does not by itself remove other municipal assignment or integration responsibilities.”
- **Research ClaimIds:** C13 C14 C20; **research sources:** S01 S05 S11 S18. Existing OfficialSource GUIDs are mapped in section E.
- **Conditions / limitation:** No preferred municipality or particular home is guaranteed. Do not generalize the exception to every section 2/family case. Permit, residence card and CPR are distinct.
- **Optional next link:** Denmark Housing article `19afdd19-e2cc-4da6-bde3-6dc3d64b7503`, `/countries/denmark/categories/housing#entry-19afdd19e2cc4da6bde36dc3d64b7503`.

### D3 — Check extension information against your own case

- **Stable proposed step ID:** `permit-check-extension-communication`; **kind:** Recommendation.
- **One practical action:** Check your decision and latest written Immigration Service communication; ask which date applies to your case.
- **Explanation:** BEK 828/2026 provides a conditional framework through 17 March 2028, whereas the reviewed service application/family pages still say 17 March 2027. The reviewed sources do not reconcile them. Normally the authority handles extension on its own initiative; neither date is your guaranteed expiry.
- **Potentially relevant documents:** Your decision, current permit and latest written extension/case messages; no personal details are entered into this guide.
- **Responsible authority:** Danish Immigration Service (New to Denmark); follow the current case/appointment channel.
- **Supporting article:** P (`afc14824-4035-4ca7-9662-6d5352ba63cb`).
- **Exact English article passage:** “The regulation states 17 March 2028, while the Immigration Service’s application and family pages still state 17 March 2027.”
- **Research ClaimIds:** C05 C16 C17; **research sources:** S01 S03 S07 S08 S09 S14. Existing OfficialSource GUIDs are mapped in section E.
- **Conditions / limitation:** Keep the discrepancy visible, attributed and unresolved. Preserve section 10 revocation grounds and military-status/individual caveats; do not promise automatic extension.
- **Optional next link:** None beyond the full primary article and official sources.

### D4 — Check family decisions and further questions separately

- **Stable proposed step ID:** `permit-check-family-and-further-questions`; **kind:** Recommendation.
- **One practical action:** Check each family member’s own decision and ask the service about unclear next steps or reporting requirements.
- **Explanation:** One person’s approval does not automatically give relatives a permit or identical rights. For longer-term residence questions, use the separate approved residence-options article; this step does not repeat its work/study conditions.
- **Potentially relevant documents:** Each relevant decision and correspondence, and records requested by the competent authority. Do not upload these documents.
- **Responsible authority:** Danish Immigration Service (New to Denmark); follow the current case/appointment channel.
- **Supporting article:** P (`afc14824-4035-4ca7-9662-6d5352ba63cb`).
- **Exact English article passage:** “If a family member has their own application or permit, check their decision and conditions separately. Do not assume that one person’s approval automatically grants a permit or identical rights to relatives.”
- **Research ClaimIds:** C04 C19; **research sources:** S01 S03 S05. Existing OfficialSource GUIDs are mapped in section E.
- **Conditions / limitation:** No guaranteed switch to another residence route; no eligibility decision or expanded work/study guidance.
- **Optional next link:** Article R, `/countries/denmark/categories/temporary-protection#entry-2d106fd3e9814ef99733568b654dfca5`; no work/study-route content copied.

> The Danish Immigration Service says a person may qualify under section 1 if, for example, they are a Ukrainian citizen who left Ukraine on or after 1 February 2022 and had residence there on or after that date; a person recognised as a refugee in Ukraine on 24 February 2022 who left Ukraine on or after 1 February 2022; or a Ukrainian citizen or such a recognised refugee who was in Denmark or held Danish residence permission on 24 February 2022. ## F. Legal distinctions and safe scope

- Denmark’s Special Act is national; Denmark does not participate in Directive 2001/55/EC under its relevant opt-out. Another EU country’s status/document does not by itself establish Danish eligibility. A1 attributes this to the approved article and S15/S16, not to an individual assessment.
- Keep section 1, section 2, pending application, approved permit, residence card, municipal assignment, accommodation entitlement and address/CPR registration separate. A person may choose another situation without the guide deciding which legal route fits them.
- C10 stay guidance does not create an approved permit or general re-entry right. Exact primary passage: “A submitted application is not an approved residence permit.” Travel must be checked before departure; no safe re-entry promise.
- C11 retains both recorded fingerprints/facial image and the exclusion where the applicant obviously cannot qualify under section 1 or 2. If uncertain, preserve the agency’s caution not to start work before a decision.
- C12 is only a healthcare pointer. Exact primary passage: “The scope is separate from healthcare access after approval; see the Denmark Healthcare guide and current official health guidance for details.” Do not promise full healthcare equivalence or enumerate benefits.
- C20 is specific to Special Act section 2(1): Integration Act section 57(5) excludes ordinary section 12 accommodation placement, with Immigration Service discretion in very special personal circumstances. Do not generalize to every family permit or erase other assignment/integration responsibilities. No preferred municipality, dwelling, guaranteed CPR timing or universal documents.
- C15 is a conditional branch within C4, not a fifth scenario: “Appeal procedures depend on the type of decision and applicable exceptions.” Read the decision immediately, follow its instructions and seek guidance promptly; no universal numerical deadline, automatic suspensive effect or right-to-remain promise.
