# Germany Temporary Protection Step-by-Step Guide — V1 Proposal

**Status: AWAITING HUMAN EDITORIAL ACCEPTANCE**  
**Phase:** 10.1 — design only  
**Canonical draft language:** English  
**Editorial baseline:** approved article `1a6b97c4-eb9e-5590-8715-c95f5f95677e`, “Applying for Temporary Protection and Checking Residence Dates”  
**Article status checked:** all three article translations are `Verified` in the current catalog. The research matrix's historical draft-status note is not the current status.

This proposal describes a non-personalized navigation aid. It does not determine eligibility, validate an individual permit, or recommend which application a person should submit. Every user-facing string requires Ukrainian, English, and Russian localization and editorial review before publication.

## A. Product objective

Help a person identify a practical next step for Germany temporary protection and residence-permission questions, using the existing approved article and its linked official sources. The guide should clarify which authority to ask and which distinctions to keep in mind; it must not produce an “eligible / not eligible” result.

## B. Four user scenarios

1. **New arrival seeking temporary protection** — find the responsible local process and prepare for contact with the competent authority.
2. **Application submitted, decision pending** — keep application records and clarify the effect of any provisional certificate with the competent authority.
3. **Existing §24 residence permit** — check the actual document and distinguish its validity from the EU temporary-protection period.
4. **Previous temporary protection in another EU Member State** — ask how the same-state continuity rule and the different German title exemption apply to the person's facts.

The choice of scenario only selects explanatory steps. It does not collect facts to classify the user.

## C–D. Proposed step sequences and evidence traceability

### Scenario A — New arrival seeking temporary protection

| Step | Action and concise explanation | Documents / institution | Evidence and limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | **Check the local arrival instructions.** Registration, allocation where applicable, address registration, and an application for a residence title are separate steps; local authorities may combine appointments. | Follow the instructions of the local authority / competent `Ausländerbehörde`. | Article passage: “Follow the local instructions for initial registration, allocation, address registration and the residence-title application. They are separate steps even where a municipality combines appointments.” Claim **TP03**; sources **S02**, **S03**. Local process and appointment requirements vary. |
| A2 | **Gather potentially relevant records.** Keep identity and status documents, first German entry date, and any earlier protection decision. The authority may request other evidence; there is no universal checklist in the cited legislation. | Passport or other identity papers; evidence of nationality and residence/protection in Ukraine; family-link records or prior protection decision where relevant. Ask the competent authority what it needs. | Article passage: “The authority may ask for other evidence; the legislation does not prescribe one universal checklist.” Claim **TP02**, **TP03**; sources **S02**, **S03**, **S09**. These are potentially relevant examples, not a mandatory document list. |
| A3 | **Ask which application route and appointment process applies locally.** Do not assume an online route is available in every municipality. | Competent `Ausländerbehörde`; use an online service only if that authority's instructions direct the user to it. | Article passage: “This does not establish that it serves every municipality. Use it only if the instructions from the authority responsible for you point to that route.” Claim **TP03**; sources **S02**, **S03**, **S11**. The linked online service identifies a Brandenburg ministry as provider and is not evidence of nationwide availability. |
| A4 | **Keep the application receipt and written decisions.** If a `Fiktionsbescheinigung` is issued, read its legal basis and work entry; ask the authority if unclear. | Competent `Ausländerbehörde`; application receipt and any certificate or decision. | Article passage: “If the authority issues a Fiktionsbescheinigung … read its legal basis and work entry.” Claim **TP04**; sources **S03**, **S08**. A pending application or certificate does not establish identical effects or work rights for everyone. |
| A5 | **Treat the 90-day title exemption as a separate, conditional rule.** Ask the authority whether it applies before relying on it; it is not a §24 permit or a grant of EU protection. | Competent `Ausländerbehörde`; first-entry date and relevant status records may help the authority assess the conditions. | Article passage: “This title exemption is not ordinary visa-free travel … a §24 permit or a decision granting protection.” Claim **TP03**, **TP07**; source **S09**, with **S03** for the described route. The exemption has category, date, and military-duty conditions; it must not be presented as universal. |

### Scenario B — Application submitted, decision pending

| Step | Action and concise explanation | Documents / institution | Evidence and limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B1 | **Keep proof of submission and all correspondence.** Use the contact details and instructions supplied for the pending case. | Application receipt, case correspondence, and decisions; competent `Ausländerbehörde`. | Article passage: “Keep the application receipt and every written decision.” Claim **TP04**; sources **S03**, **S08**. This is record-keeping guidance, not a promise about processing time. |
| B2 | **Check what document, if any, was issued while the case is pending.** Read its legal basis and any work notation instead of assuming that all pending applications have the same effect. | `Fiktionsbescheinigung` or other written document; ask the issuing `Ausländerbehörde` about unclear wording. | Article passage: “Under §81 AufenthG, the effect while a case is pending depends on whether the person was lawfully present and whether an existing title was renewed or changed before expiry.” Claim **TP04**; source **S08**, supported by **S03**. No general work authorization conclusion is made. |
| B3 | **Ask the competent authority to clarify the case-specific next step.** | Competent `Ausländerbehörde`; provide case references through its instructed channel. | Article passage: “Check your own document and ask the competent Ausländerbehörde for written clarification if the applicable date is unclear.” Claim **TP04**, **TP05**; sources **S03**, **S08**. The guide does not decide status or the outcome of a pending application. |

### Scenario C — Existing §24 residence permit; checking validity and next steps

| Step | Action and concise explanation | Documents / institution | Evidence and limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | **Read the validity date on the individual residence document.** Do not substitute the EU protection end date for the date on an individual German permit. | The actual residence card/document; competent `Ausländerbehörde` for clarification. | Article passage: “The EU protection period does not itself determine the validity of an individual German residence permit or work authorization.” Claim **TP05**; sources **S04**, **S05**. |
| C2 | **Check whether the continuation rule covers the permit.** The cited ordinance concerns specified §24 permits valid on 1 February 2026 and is subject to its conditions. | Actual permit and any written authority record; competent `Ausländerbehörde` if the cohort or conditions are unclear. | Article passage: “UkraineAufenthFGV §2 continues specified §24 residence permits valid on 1 February 2026 through 4 March 2027, subject to the regulation’s conditions.” Claim **TP05**; source **S04**. Do not infer that every §24 document is extended. |
| C3 | **Keep the EU and German permit dates separate.** The EU temporary-protection period runs through 4 March 2028, but that does not by itself establish the validity of a person's permit. | EU decision and German permit are different records; ask the competent `Ausländerbehörde` about the individual document. | Article passage: “The EU extended temporary protection through 4 March 2028. Separately…” Claim **TP05**, **TP06**; sources **S04**, **S05**, **S06**. No individual validity date or work right is inferred. |
| C4 | **If the document's date or next step is unclear, request written clarification.** | Competent `Ausländerbehörde`; keep its written response with the permit records. | Article passage: “Check your own document and ask the competent Ausländerbehörde for written clarification if the applicable date is unclear.” Claim **TP05**; sources **S03**, **S04**. |

### Scenario D — Previous temporary protection in another EU Member State

| Step | Action and concise explanation | Documents / institution | Evidence and limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D1 | **Record where and when protection was granted and whether it remained continuous there.** Do not treat physical presence somewhere in the EU as equivalent to continuous protection in one Member State. | Earlier protection decision and dates or other records the authority requests; competent German `Ausländerbehörde`. | Article passage: “The EU exception covers people who held protection in the same Member State by 4 August and continuously retain it there.” Claim **TP06**; sources **S05**, **S06**. Whether particular evidence satisfies the rule is not determined by the guide. |
| D2 | **Ask the competent German authority how the rule applies before relying on an exception.** The Germany4Ukraine notice is worded more broadly than the corrected EU Decision; the guide displays both statements without resolving an individual's case. | Competent `Ausländerbehörde`; bring or retain the prior decision and relevant timeline if requested. | Article passage: “A Germany4Ukraine notice describes the exception more broadly … that wording is not the same as the corrected Decision.” Claim **TP08**; sources **S05**, **S06**, **S10**. The official-source discrepancy and uncertainty around moves between states remain explicit. |
| D3 | **Ask separately whether the German 90-day title exemption applies.** Prior protection in another Member State is relevant to this distinct exemption; it is not the EU grant test. | Competent `Ausländerbehörde`; provide the relevant prior decision and entry information if requested. | Article passage: “Conditions include entry by 4 December 2027 and not already having received temporary protection in another EU Member State.” Claim **TP03**, **TP07**; source **S09**, with **S03** for application routing. This proposal does not determine whether the person qualifies. |
| D4 | **Follow the local application instructions and ask for a case-specific explanation if the routes or dates remain unclear.** | Competent `Ausländerbehörde`; local process may vary. | Article passage: “Follow the local instructions for initial registration, allocation, address registration and the residence-title application.” Claim **TP03**, **TP08**; sources **S02**, **S03**, **S09**, **S10**. No unsupported individual conclusion is offered. |

## E–F. Proposed interaction and layout

### Screen 1 — “What do you need help with?”

Show four selectable situation cards using the scenario names above. Selecting a card displays its guide. Do not ask users to enter nationality, dates, permit details, military status, or prior-protection facts to generate a legal classification.

### Screen 2 — Selected guide

Show a clear scenario heading, a short uncertainty notice where relevant, and ordered steps. Each step contains:

1. Step number and short title.
2. One practical action and concise explanation.
3. “Documents to have ready” only where the article supports the examples; label them “potentially relevant” and say the authority may ask for other evidence.
4. “Where to go” with the relevant authority.
5. Official source links associated with that step.
6. An optional link to the existing detailed article.

Provide **Back**, **Change situation**, a step-progress indicator, and **Read full article**. Clearly distinguish required actions stated by a source from recommendations and optional preparation. Never turn recommendations into legal requirements.

### Mobile and desktop concept

- **Mobile-first:** one-column cards and steps, large tap targets, visible progress, source links wrapping within the viewport, and no horizontal scrolling.
- **Desktop:** readable centered content column; scenario selection may use a two-column card grid, while the guide remains a single ordered reading column. Keep source details adjacent to the step they support.
- Use existing site typography, colors, cards, and focus styles. No account, upload, saved profile, or persistence is needed. Do not persist personal answers; the V1 flow need not ask for any.

Use the existing `uk`, `en`, and `ru` language codes on each scenario and step. ## I. Legal and editorial limitations

- EU temporary protection and an individual German §24 residence permit are distinct. EU protection through 4 March 2028 does not establish an individual's German permit validity or work authorization.
- The 90-day title exemption in UkraineAufenthÜV is separate and conditional. Its category, prior-protection, entry-date, and §2(3) military-duty rules must not be collapsed into a general §24 eligibility rule.
- A `Fiktionsbescheinigung` does not by itself establish work rights; the legal basis and document entry matter.
- Local procedures, appointments, and online routes vary. The linked Brandenburg online service is not evidence of nationwide availability.
- The EU corrected decision's same-Member-State continuous-protection exception and the broader Germany4Ukraine notice remain discrepant. The guide must attribute both accurately, avoid resolving the conflict by inference, and send individual questions to the competent `Ausländerbehörde`.
- The research matrix says the accepted evidence does not settle whether a move between Member States or particular evidence affects an individual case. Do not invent a universal evidence list.
- No new legal research was conducted for this proposal. The listed source IDs are from the approved article's existing source relationships. All linked sources and local procedures need a publication-time freshness review.
- The guide is not legal advice, does not collect sensitive facts, and must not issue or imply an eligibility decision.
