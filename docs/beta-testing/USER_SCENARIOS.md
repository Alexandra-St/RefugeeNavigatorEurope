# Private Beta user scenarios

Use hypothetical situations for interactive guides. Do not ask testers to disclose real immigration histories or personal identifiers. Let the tester attempt each task without coaching; record completion and points of confusion separately from your interpretation.

1. **Explore Poland:** Find Poland and describe which kinds of information are available there.
2. **Find PESEL UKR information:** Use search to locate information about PESEL UKR. Note whether the result and its country/category context are clear.
3. **Narrow a search:** Find a topic using a search term, then narrow results to a country and category. Explain what changed in the result list.
4. **Explore Netherlands content:** Open Netherlands, use search to find an accepted Netherlands article, then open Netherlands in Country Comparison V2. Is the available content easy to find? If an answer says “information unavailable,” does it read as an evidence gap rather than proof that a right or service does not exist?
5. **Check an article’s evidence:** Open an article about healthcare or housing in Denmark or Poland. Find its official sources and explain how you would open one.
6. **Compare temporary protection:** Compare Germany and Poland in Country Comparison V2. Are the questions understandable? Are the country columns distinct? Are source links clear? Can you discover the collapsed full-article comparison? Does the page feel like a ranking or recommendation? Does “information unavailable” read as an evidence gap rather than proof that a service or right does not exist?
7. **Use the Germany guide:** Choose a hypothetical situation and navigate several steps in the Germany Temporary Protection guide. What do the progress indicator and suggested next steps mean to you?
8. **Use the Denmark guide:** With a hypothetical application, pending, or permit situation, navigate the Denmark guide. Can you tell what it does and does not decide?
9. **Use the Poland guide:** Find information about PESEL UKR or CUKR in the Poland guide without entering real personal data. Are the relevant terms and source links understandable?
10. **Switch language in context:** While viewing an article or guide, switch among Ukrainian, English, and Russian. Is the language change clear, and is useful page context retained?
11. **Regain orientation:** After visiting several pages, return to country/category navigation without using browser history if possible. Is it clear where you are and what you can do next?

## Guide-specific observations

- Is “What do you need help with?” understandable, and can the tester distinguish the situations?
- Does progress look like navigation through content rather than an eligibility score?
- Are Previous, Next, and Change situation clear?
- Are warnings, source links, and links to full articles discoverable?
- Does the tester think the guide made a legal decision about them? Record any such interpretation as a **Major** issue at minimum, and **Critical** if it creates a serious legal-understanding risk.

## Comparison observations

Check whether users understand that the comparison presents factual differences rather than ranking countries; structured source-linked facts appear first; full articles remain available; and an unavailable answer reports an evidence gap rather than necessarily indicating absence of a service or right.
