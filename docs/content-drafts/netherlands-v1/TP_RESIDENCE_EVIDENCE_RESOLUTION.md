## 3. EU Decision and IND exceptions

### E1 — What are the scheme horizon and corrected effective dates? RESOLVED

**Authority/evidence:** Council of the EU, [Decision (EU) 2026/1912](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32026D1912), Articles 1–3 and recitals 15, 17–20; [corrigendum](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32026D1912R%2801%29), replacements for Articles 2 and 3; [consolidated text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A02026D1912-20260804), C1 markings. All directly read.

Adopted 30 July, published 4 August, corrected 5 August 2026. Article 1 extends protection for the Decision 2022/382 categories through **4 March 2028**. Entry into force is the day after publication; general application begins **5 March 2027**, but Article 2 applies from **5 August 2026**. Its corrected exception covers protection held in a given Member State before/on **4 August 2026** and continuously retained in that same State afterwards. Article 2 requires satisfaction of applicable Ukrainian military obligations and proof where applicable; it does not prescribe Dutch age bands.

**Reader implication:** a scheme horizon is not an individual document-expiry decision. The [IND extension page](https://ind.nl/en/ukraine/extension-sticker-or-card-temporary-protection-ukraine), “Sticker without expiration date,” “Extension … for Ukrainians” and “Extension … for non-Ukrainians,” directly confirms separate document/letter cohorts and the 2028 horizon; no update date is displayed. Preserve those cohort distinctions.

### E2 — Do IND exceptions have precisely the same scope? CONFLICTING

**Authority/evidence:** [IND news](https://ind.nl/en/news/temporary-protection-directive-for-ukraine-extended-with-new-condition-for-new-applications), “New condition for new applications,” updated 31 August 2026, directly read. It describes applications from 5 August, an exception for prior protection anywhere in the EU before that date, and a separate exception for joining already protected family members. That is not the wording of E1's same-State continuous-holder carve-out.

The [IND municipal handbook](https://open.overheid.nl/documenten/ce194a40-fe32-404c-a51b-4b8cdf1bed8e/file), 1 October 2026, Annexes 1 and 3, also describes family-based consideration under WI 2025/6. This corroborates a Dutch administrative family pathway, not a universal exemption from every condition. Decision recital 15 refers to compliance with Directive Article 15 where applicable; it does not by itself resolve each Dutch case.

**Limitation/reader implication:** source wording is verified; its exact reconciliation is not. Do not replace the EU exception with an EU-wide one, present the family pathway as unconditional, or conclude that Dutch guidance is unlawful. Attribute each authority and retain the unresolved scope issue. This is not evidence for an individual's eligibility.

## 4. Military screening

### M1 — What evidence/cohort can actually be described? PARTIALLY RESOLVED

**Authority/evidence:** [IND TPD page](https://ind.nl/en/ukraine/temporary-protection-directive-ukraine), “Requirements … / You have Ukrainian nationality,” directly read. It specifies liable persons or registered reservists aged **23–60**, with examples including recent exit-stamp, official Reserv+, military-registration, exemption/deferral and demobilisation evidence. It accepts paper/electronic documents but excludes screenshots/photos. These are examples, not an exhaustive checklist. The page displays 6 August 2026 despite containing an October ruling update; its metadata is incomplete.

**Municipal layer:** the 1 October handbook, Annex 3, covers BRP registration/triage requests from 5 August plus suspected non-compliance, mainly the stated 23–60 group and others with unfulfilled voluntary military service. It points to further instructions rather than defining every evidence outcome. Military triage requests became possible from 17 September under the handbook's introduction.

**Limits/reader implication:** E1 governs the EU condition; IND supplies Dutch public examples; municipal instructions govern referral. None should be merged into “all Ukrainian men.” No Netherlands-specific 18–22 evidence rule, precise endpoint interpretation, exhaustive checklist or every outside-band case was established. Do not transplant another country's age/document rules.

## 5. Negative municipal triage

### T1 — What happens, and what remedy is established? PARTIALLY RESOLVED

**Authority/evidence:** the directly read 1 October handbook, chapter 4, pp. 7–9, describes physical Utrecht triage, exceptional digital triage and an aim to schedule within a week. Negative physical advice entails handing over a refusal decision and no BRP registration. For negative digital triage, IND sends the decision securely to municipal staff for printing, signature and delivery. The handbook does not give a complete remedy/deadline route.

**New legal evidence:** [Directive 2001/55/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32001L0055), Article 29, directly read in EUR-Lex, entitles people excluded from temporary protection or family reunification to a legal challenge in the Member State. Directive dated 20 July 2001, published 7 August 2001. This establishes a general safeguard, not which Dutch procedure applies to a particular triage decision.

**Additional official guidance:** [IND — objection or appeal](https://ind.nl/nl/na-uw-aanvraag/bezwaar-maken-of-in-beroep-gaan), updated 10 August 2026, explains different procedures and directs readers to their decision for relevant instructions, deadlines and whether they may await proceedings in-country. It does not identify a universal negative-triage pathway.

**Unresolved/reader implication:** competent forum, objection versus direct appeal, deadline and suspensive/continued-reception effects remain unestablished for the relevant refusal. Do not infer these from generic IND deadlines or unrelated third-country appeals. Do not say no remedy exists. Bounded intake navigation can exclude remedy instructions and direct readers to their decision and qualified assistance.

## 7. Asylum-processing conflict

### A1 — May processing automatically wait until TPD ends? CONFLICTING public guidance; RESOLVED general EU principle

**New primary-text verification:** [CJEU, C-249/25 Jilin, 1 October 2026](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A62025CJ0249), directly read, paragraphs 39–53, 60–64 and operative ruling. The judgment precludes general automatic suspension of examination/time limits throughout temporary protection. Exceptional, necessary temporary deferral of some cases may address objective impossibility; it is not a blanket TPD suspension. It does not decide an individual's asylum outcome or processing date.

The [Court's explanatory release](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2026-10/cp260137en.pdf), 1 October, pp. 1–2, corroborates this but is explicitly non-binding. The [Raad van State explanation](https://www.raadvanstate.nl/actueel/nieuws/september/europees-hof-beantwoordt-prejudiciele/), published 1 October, “Arrest” and “Voortzetting,” says national proceedings will continue; it is not their final disposition.

**Current conflicting text:** IND's “European Court ruling…” section acknowledges the ruling and says implementation is under examination. On that same page, “Decision on your application” still says asylum decisions wait while TPD applies. The Dutch government policy page also retains that general suspension rationale and a future withdrawal condition. This is both an internal IND inconsistency and a cross-page conflict, not two equally authoritative legal alternatives.

**Reader implication:** do not repeat blanket suspension as current law or advise withdrawal. The ruling does not itself invalidate or activate every proposed transition condition, establish a transition permit or guarantee immediate examination. No later final national disposition or complete IND operational implementation was established in this scoped check. Existing NL-TP-02 gives neither suspension nor withdrawal instructions; that exclusion remains essential.
