# Netherlands V1 Editorial Review

Review date: 21 October 2026. This is an editorial review of drafts against the approved research matrices, not legal certification, publication approval, or a fresh source check.

## Executive summary

All eight logical articles and 24 EN/UK/RU versions were reviewed against their corresponding research matrices and the Netherlands research-readiness documents. Five findings remain: **1 BLOCKER, 4 CORRECTION, 0 POLISH**. No draft was edited in this phase.

The drafts generally preserve the approved evidence limits, cohorts, conditional language, dates, and unresolved issues. The main publication gate is the future host-household rent-benefit rule in Housing: it is directly attributed to an official source, but has no corresponding atomic claim and is mapped to a different claim. The military-obligation section in TP-01 also needs a more explicit account of the separate IND prior-protection exception. Source-set provenance needs clarification in TP-02, Housing, and Social Assistance.

## Findings

### NL-TP01-F01 — Clarify the separate IND prior-protection exception

- **Article:** NL-TP-01
- **Languages:** EN / UK / RU
- **Severity:** CORRECTION
- **Exact section/text:** “Military-obligation screening has limited, evolving guidance.” The three versions state that IND’s public wording “describes the scope differently” / equivalent, but only spell out IND’s exception for joining a family member who already has temporary protection.
- **Observed problem:** The text contrasts the corrected EU Decision’s same-Member-State continuing-holder exception with IND guidance without identifying the separate IND wording for people who already had temporary protection in another EU country. Readers cannot tell what difference the article is attributing to IND. The three translations share the same omission.
- **Evidence boundary:** Matrix claim NL-TP01-04 and sources TP01-S01, TP01-S04 and TP01-S08 distinguish IND’s broader “already had TPD in the EU” wording and its separate family-joining exception from the Decision’s corrected same-Member-State continuing-holder exception. The matrix says exact reconciliation is unresolved.
- **Required correction:** Attribute and state the distinct IND prior-TPD-in-the-EU wording separately from its family-joining exception; retain the Decision’s narrower wording and say the discrepancy is unresolved. Do not infer an unrecorded age band, additional eligibility rule, or individual outcome.
- **Source/claim:** [TP-01 matrix](../../content-research/netherlands-batch-2026-10-20/temporary-protection-application/SOURCES_AND_CLAIMS.md), NL-TP01-04; TP01-S01 / TP01-S04 / TP01-S08.

- **Source/claim:** [TP-02 matrix](../../content-research/netherlands-batch-2026-10-20/after-temporary-protection/SOURCES_AND_CLAIMS.md), NL-TP02-03 / NL-TP02-04; TP02-S09 / TP02-S10 / TP02-S11.

### NL-HOUSING-F01 — Add a traceable claim for the 2027 host-household rule or remove the statement

- **Languages:** EN / UK / RU
- **Severity:** BLOCKER
- **Exact section/text:** “Check whether huurtoeslag may apply”: “from 1 January 2027 … they are counted” / equivalent Ukrainian and Russian statements.

It is attributed to Dienst Toeslagen and appears consistently in all three drafts, but the approved matrix has no atomic claim for it. NL-HOUSING01-03 maps only the conditional rent-benefit route and general criteria from HOUSING-S07/S08; the paragraph’s separate rule comes from HOUSING-S09. The section mapping therefore overstates what NL-HOUSING01-03 covers.

This supports checking the statement, but does not supply a mapped claim in the approved matrix.
- **Required correction:** Before integration, add and review a separate atomic claim and map this passage to it, preserving the host-household and effective-date limits; alternatively remove the passage. Recheck the official page near 1 January 2027. Do not broaden it to all refugees, tenants, or benefit calculations.

## Cross-language consistency

- All 24 language versions preserve the same article structures, material dates, amounts, cohorts, conditions, modal strength, and uncertainty boundaries found in the EN semantic reference and matrices.
- The TP-01 prior-protection exception omission is shared by EN, UK, and RU; it is not a translation-only divergence and must be corrected consistently in all three.
- The Housing 1 January 2027 host-household statement appears in all three languages with equivalent scope and attribution. Its blocker is claim traceability, not a translation mismatch.
- Employment minimum-wage figures are explicitly tied to 1 July 2026 in each version; they are not generalized to a later year.
- Healthcare keeps RMO, ordinary insurance, SOV/CAK, OVV and RMA distinct; it does not claim all care is free or advise a reader which insurance route to take.
- Education maintains the distinction between compulsory schooling, local language support, adult municipal courses and conditional DUO MBO fee remission. Social Assistance keeps leefgeld, reception contributions, child-related schemes and ordinary bijstand/MOP distinct.
- No other material UK/RU divergence that broadens entitlement or certainty was identified in this review.

## Source integrity

- Each draft’s claim-coverage section was compared with its matrix. The material Housing 2027 statement is the identified claim-mapping gap.
- TP01-S09 is correctly described in NL-TP-01 as a public metadata record only, not evidence of the inaccessible IB 2026/34 text.
- TP02-S09 and TP02-S11 likewise establish record or attachment metadata only. The readable TP02-S10 memorandum supports the draft’s description of a proposal; the unread advice attachment is not treated as substantive evidence in the article prose.
- HOUSING-S05 is stale and not part of the current mapped benefit claim; SOC-S03 is listed in the source set but is not part of the mapped contribution claim. See findings NL-HOUSING-F02 and NL-SOC-F01.
- Source records and source matrices were not changed. Inventory records should continue to distinguish sources directly used for a claim from records consulted only for metadata or historical context.

## Unresolved legal boundaries

- **Temporary protection and military screening:** The corrected EU Decision uses a same-Member-State continuing-holder exception. IND’s public wording describes a broader prior-TPD-in-the-EU exception and separately addresses family joining. The exact reconciliation remains unresolved. The 18–22 evidence detail is not established by the approved matrix, and the text of IND bulletin IB 2026/34 was inaccessible. Do not infer individual eligibility, evidence requirements, or an appeal route.
- **Residence beyond TPD:** The transition document remains a policy proposal, not an enacted or operational route. In-country filing and the counting of TPD time toward permanent or EU long-term residence remain unestablished. No application recommendation or asylum-withdrawal advice is supported.
- **Healthcare:** The Zvw/RMO relationship remains conflicting in official explanations. The draft properly limits itself to RMO/urgent-care navigation and does not give individual insurance instructions. It is not a complete guide to Dutch healthcare coverage.
- **Housing:** Municipal reception depends on route, municipal decision, and capacity; historical occupancy is not current availability. No right to a chosen place, duration, private rental, or rent reimbursement is established. The 2027 hosted-household benefit rule needs a claim entry and publication-time recheck.
- **Social assistance:** The research does not establish universal eligibility for ordinary bijstand or every MOP category. Leefgeld and personal reception contributions remain separate, conditional systems.
- **Education:** Individual school exceptions, placement, course availability, municipal funding, admission, and diploma recognition are not determined by these drafts.
