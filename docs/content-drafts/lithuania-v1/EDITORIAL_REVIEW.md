### LT-TP-01 — Temporary protection in Lithuania

- The three canonical aspects are substantively present: `protection-scheme`, `protection-application`, and `protection-dates`.
- The draft does not say that all Ukrainians qualify. It distinguishes status from the digital document and does not invent a complete initial checklist, biometrics, fee, or processing time.
- The national / EU end date of 4 March 2028 is distinguished from an individual's document expiry. The 5 August 2026 military-condition start is correctly qualified, and the EU and national transition rules are not treated as identical.
- Two details still need correction: the distinct pre-24-February-2022 conditions for third-country groups are too compressed, and the national continuous-TP transition exception does not say that the continuous status may be in any EU Member State. See LT-REV-01 and LT-REV-02.

### LT-TP-02 — Residence options during or after temporary protection

- TP-only time toward long-term residence, TP's effect after another permit is granted, and TP-only family sponsorship remain explicitly not established. The 1 November 2026 filing rule is limited to the described categories.
- UK/RU preserve the same qualifications as EN. All eight source-set entries are used and trace to this package.

## Findings

### LT-REV-01 — LT-TP-01 — CORRECTION

- **Article / language(s):** LT-TP-01, EN / UK / RU.
- **Current wording / meaning:** EN groups third-country residents as “narrowly defined stateless or other third-country residents with qualifying protection or permanent residence in Ukraine” and says that “for some third-country residents, inability to return safely and durably is part of the test.” UK and RU convey the same summary.
- **Research boundary:** The package separates (1) stateless / third-country nationals who had international or equivalent national protection in Ukraine before 24 February 2022, from (2) stateless / third-country nationals who held a valid Ukrainian permanent-residence permit and were lawfully permanently resident there before that date and cannot safely and durably return to their origin country or another receiving state. Family coverage is also limited to the specified family definition and conditions.
- **Why correction is required:** “Qualifying protection or permanent residence” does not state the pre-date condition; “permanent residence” does not state the valid-permit and lawful-permanent-residence requirements; and the return test is not explicitly attached to the permanent-residence cohort. The word “narrowly” does not preserve these operative eligibility boundaries.
- **Exact correction required:** In EN/UK/RU, distinguish the two third-country cohorts. For the permanent-residence cohort, state the valid Ukrainian permit and lawful permanent residence before 24 February 2022, plus inability to return safely and durably to the origin country or another receiving state. Keep the protection cohort's pre-date condition and the specified family scope. Do not broaden coverage to other former Ukrainian residents.

### LT-REV-02 — LT-TP-01 — CORRECTION

- **Article / language(s):** LT-TP-01, EN / UK / RU.
- **Current wording / meaning:** The text says national transition rules include “defined cases of continuous TP across 4 August 2026” and a Lithuanian application already pending on that date; UK/RU mirror this.
- **Research boundary:** Resolution No. 742 describes the national exception for continuous temporary protection in **any EU Member State** across the cutoff and for a Lithuanian TP application pending at the cutoff. The corrected EU rule separately refers to continuous protection in the same Member State.
- **Why correction is required:** The national continuous-status sentence omits the geographic scope “in any EU Member State.” Without it, the transition category is underspecified and could be read more broadly than the researched national rule.
- **Exact correction required:** In EN/UK/RU, specify continuous TP in any EU Member State across the 4 August 2026 cutoff, retain the separate pending-Lithuanian-application exception, and continue to distinguish the narrower EU same-Member-State wording.

## Cross-article consistency

No blocking contradiction between articles was found.

- **TP application and work trigger:** LT-TP-01's application/status boundary is consistent with LT-EMP-02's work right after a qualifying TP application; the latter does not apply this right to unrelated residence applications.
- **TP date and other programme dates:** the 4 March 2028 national/EU TP endpoint is kept separate from the 1 January 2027 wage change, the 2026 UŽT project intake snapshot, June 2026 school-programme snapshot, and time-limited housing/social measures.
- **TP and ordinary residence:** LT-TP-02 keeps national permits application-based and separate; no automatic transition or general pending-application work right appears elsewhere.
- **UŽT and Sodra:** LT-EMP-01 separates vacancy browsing/registration and assessed assistance from Sodra unemployment insurance; LT-SOC-01 separately distinguishes Sodra insurance benefits from municipal assistance.
- **TP healthcare and CHI:** LT-HEALTH-01 does not convert TP emergency/necessary-care entitlement into full CHI; TP documents are not treated as proof of active insurance.
- **Housing and social assistance:** LT-HOUSING-01 and LT-SOC-01 separate accommodation, tenancy, housing compensation, social housing and municipal assistance; application is not approval.
- **Education:** compulsory schooling, local placement and conditional language support do not conflict with the statement that Ukrainian online schooling alone is not established as a substitute. The eight-school figure is dated.
- **Benefit applications:** LT-SOC-01 distinguishes application channels from entitlement and award, and municipal decisions from Sodra insurance decisions.
