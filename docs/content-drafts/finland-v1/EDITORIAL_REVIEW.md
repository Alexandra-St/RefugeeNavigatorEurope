## Required corrections

### 1. FI-TP-01 — state the Finnish eligibility boundary

- **Issue:** The opening describes people fleeing Russia’s war against Ukraine, then groups eligible family members and other cohorts together as categories “described by Migri.” This does not tell readers the operative Finnish eligibility boundary and can be read to include Ukrainian residence alone or all relatives/third-country residents.
- **Why unsafe/incomplete:** The focused package distinguishes Ukrainians unable to return because of Russia’s attack; specified non-EU/non-Schengen family members of Ukrainians or people with international/equivalent national protection in Ukraine, with family ties established there before 24 February 2022; non-EU/non-Schengen third-country nationals/stateless people holding Ukrainian international-protection residence; and those with permanent Ukrainian residence who cannot return safely and durably. It expressly warns against importing broad EU categories or treating any Ukrainian residence as sufficient.
- **Supported correction boundary:** Replace the vague cohort summary with a concise, Finland-specific summary of those categories and their key conditions. Keep the case-specific nature of assessment and relevant exclusions. Do not imply all Ukrainian residents, all family members, or all third-country nationals qualify.
- **Languages:** EN, UK and RU all require the same boundary.

### 2. FI-TP-02 — align study-permit information across languages

- **Issue:** UK and RU say a study permit requires admission, sufficient funds and acceptable insurance/health coverage. EN only says study permits have separate conditions.
- **Why unsafe/incomplete:** The UK/RU addition is supported, but the asymmetry makes the applicant guidance materially different by language. The research states that a study-based extended-permit route requires acceptance to qualifying studies, sufficient funds (generally €800/month), and prescribed insurance or accepted health-cover evidence; admission alone does not create a permit.
- **Supported correction boundary:** Give EN/UK/RU the same concise account of the study-permit conditions, or remove the extra detail from UK/RU. Keep the amount qualified as general and current-source dependent, and distinguish admission from the permit decision. No blanket entitlement follows from TP status.
- **Languages:** Align all three versions; the present expansion is in UK and RU.

### 3. FI-HEALTH-01, FI-HOUSING-01 and FI-SOC-01 — state the capped transition and its exceptions consistently

- **Issue:** Health and Housing say services may end “three months after” eligibility, which can read as exactly three months. Social says “at most three months” but all three articles omit the researched exception for unaccompanied minors and the reception-centre director’s limited extension power. In EN Social, “After reception ends with `kotikunta`” is ambiguous about whether actual registration triggers the end.
- **Why unsafe/incomplete:** The 1 September 2026 rule starts from eligibility to apply for `kotikunta`, not actual application or registration. For the covered clients it caps the transition at three months. Unaccompanied minors are excepted, and a director may extend services for exceptionally weighty grounds for a reasonable time. These details materially qualify a blanket end-date statement.
- **Supported correction boundary:** Harmonize all three articles and all languages: identify eligibility as the clock trigger; state the maximum, not an exact end date; retain the unaccompanied-minor exception and limited extension; and direct readers to the reception centre for their individual end date. Describe Kela/housing/urgent fallback routes as separately assessed, never as an automatic replacement.
- **Languages:** EN, UK and RU in all three articles.

### 4. FI-EDU-01 — distinguish school access from compulsory-education scope

- **Issue:** The draft states the general compulsory-education age boundary immediately before saying a TP child can access pre-primary/basic education without `kotikunta`, but does not distinguish access from the legal compulsory-education duty.
- **Why unsafe/incomplete:** The research says the ordinary compulsory-education duty applies to children permanently resident for the Act (including the relevant `kotikunta` scope); a TP child without `kotikunta` is not automatically subject to that same duty. The municipality’s arranging duty/access to pre-primary/basic education for a child living in its area is a separate point and remains supported.
- **Supported correction boundary:** Explain the distinction without weakening the child’s supported access or implying that lack of `kotikunta` prevents schooling. Preserve the normal pre-primary age-6 and compulsory-education school-year-7-to-18/qualifying-completion boundaries for the population to which the statutory duty applies.
- **Languages:** EN, UK and RU.

### 5. FI-EDU-01 — make the higher-education fee tests usable

- **Issue:** “The application fee is separate and has its own TP timing condition” flags a distinction but does not state the condition. “Otherwise it may be €100 in 2026” does not explain that this is a scoped statutory application fee with exceptions. The current wording therefore cannot guide the reader on which date matters.
- **Why unsafe/incomplete:** The research gives two different tests. A qualifying granted TP permit can exempt covered foreign-language Bachelor’s/Master’s degree tuition; a pending application is not a granted permit. Separately, the €100 application-fee exemption requires that the applicant held a Finnish TP permit when studies began and that it was not later revoked under Aliens Act §58(4). The fee applies only to the covered application categories and has statutory exceptions.
- **Supported correction boundary:** State the two distinct tests in concise language and retain the pending-versus-granted distinction. Qualify €100 to covered 2026 applications and statutory scope/exceptions. Do not imply admission, scholarship or Kela student aid follows from either exemption.
- **Languages:** EN, UK and RU.

## Cross-article and blocked-area audit

- **`protection-dates` leakage: no.** FI-TP-01 states 4 March 2028 only as the EU scheme endpoint, discloses the conflicting Migri 2027/2028 pages, does not assert universal individual permit/card validity or universal card action/no-action, and directs readers to their own current record/card and Migri. The 5 August 2026 EU/Migri military-condition date and 13 August Finnish Decision 751/2026 date both remain visible and unreconciled; no universal proof checklist is claimed.
- **`kotikunta` / reception consistency: yes, correction needed.** All three transition articles identify eligibility to apply as the trigger and do not make actual registration the trigger. However, Health/Housing can imply an exact three-month duration while Social says maximum, and the ordinary-rule exceptions are missing. Education correctly preserves child access without `kotikunta`; no article says that absence of `kotikunta` removes school access or urgent healthcare.
- Healthcare correctly retains the statutory basis for granted TP without `kotikunta` while stating that the reception centre’s operational service ends and the practical provider/referral/proof/billing handoff is not nationally uniform. Pending adult care, broader child scope, Kela coverage/card/identity code, 112, geographically qualified 116117 and charges are distinguished.
- Housing correctly treats reception accommodation as capacity-based, private rent/deposit/utilities as unreimbursed by reception, emergency county accommodation as assessed and temporary, Kela support as conditional, subsidised applications as no allocation guarantee, and Varke as the current authority. It does not overstate a universal three-month deposit cap.
- **Work-right / jobseeker / benefit inconsistency: no.** Registration starts TP work rights; the current pending certificate is evidence and a residence card is not required first. Pending TP work rights do not automatically establish jobseeker-registration eligibility. Employer notification is separate from jobseeker registration. Registration/authority statement is separate from Kela or fund benefit decisions; `yleistuki` is current from 1 May 2026.
- Employment pay is safely grounded: no national statutory minimum wage is implied; applicable collective-agreement terms and the statutory usual/reasonable-remuneration fallback are distinguished. No gross/net or euro-amount issue arises because the articles give no pay figure.
- **Ordinary residence-transition overclaim: no.** FI-TP-02 presents a separate, conditional ordinary extended-permit application, distinguishes first-permit filing, says a job/study/family basis alone is insufficient, and does not promise continued TP work rights after expiry, Blue Card availability, or an automatic post-2028 Finnish conversion.
- Social assistance does not promise automatic Kela handoff, recurring no-`kotikunta` support, or guaranteed urgent cash; reception allowance, `yleistuki`, child benefits and other Kela benefits remain conditional and distinct. The one-year TP child-benefit route, child-abroad uncertainty and future 2027 home-care rule are qualified.
