## Correction verification

### 1. FI-TP-01 — Finnish temporary-protection eligibility — Resolved

EN / UK / RU now identify the bounded Finnish cohorts: Ukrainians unable to return because of Russia's attack; specified non-EU/non-Schengen family members whose ties were established in Ukraine before 24 February 2022; non-EU/non-Schengen third-country nationals or stateless people with Ukrainian residence based on international protection; and those with qualifying permanent Ukrainian residence who cannot return safely and durably. The text says eligibility is assessed case by case and that residence in Ukraine, family relationship or third-country nationality alone is insufficient.

The military-obligation qualification preserves the EU/Migri date of 5 August 2026, Finnish Decision 751/2026's effective date of 13 August 2026, the unreconciled date difference and the absence of a complete nationwide proof checklist. No broader cohort is introduced.

### 2. FI-TP-02 — Study-permit parity — Resolved

EN / UK / RU use the same concise boundary: study, family and entrepreneur permits have distinct conditions, and admission alone does not grant a residence permit. The prior extra UK/RU detail about funds and insurance was removed rather than made universal without a selected source. No €800/month amount remains. The rest of the residence guidance still separates extended permits from first permits, rejects automatic conversion, and leaves Blue Card interaction, work rights while awaiting a decision after TP expiry, and any automatic post-2028 transition unresolved.

### 3. FI-HEALTH-01, FI-HOUSING-01 and FI-SOC-01 — Reception transition — Resolved

All three articles and all three languages state that the rule takes effect on 1 September 2026 and that the ordinary transition is capped at a maximum of three months from eligibility to apply for `kotikunta`. They state that actual registration is not the trigger and that three months is not necessarily the duration for every person. Each preserves the unaccompanied-minor exception, the reception-centre director's limited power to extend services for a reasonable period on exceptionally weighty grounds, the absence of a guaranteed extension and the instruction to confirm the individual end date with the reception centre.

- **Healthcare:** The statutory basis for a TP beneficiary's healthcare does not simply disappear when reception services end. The lack of a uniform nationwide provider, referral, proof and billing handoff after exit without `kotikunta` remains explicit.
- **Housing:** Reception accommodation ends with entitlement to reception services. No universal additional move-out period or guaranteed replacement housing is asserted. Emergency county accommodation is temporary and individually assessed, not guaranteed permanent housing.
- **Social assistance:** Reception-service termination is described under the eligibility-based rule. With `kotikunta`, a person may apply to Kela for basic social assistance subject to a separate decision. Without `kotikunta`, ordinary recurring support remains unestablished; urgent Kela/county routes are assessed fallback routes, not guaranteed recurring payments. The wording does not make actual `kotikunta` registration the reception-service end trigger.

### 4. FI-EDU-01 — School access and compulsory-education duty — Resolved

EN / UK / RU distinguish access from statutory duty. A TP child living in a municipality can access pre-primary/basic education without `kotikunta`; lack of `kotikunta` does not mean no school. The text also avoids assigning every TP child without `kotikunta` the same compulsory-education duty. It preserves the qualified general timing: pre-primary normally in the year the child turns six; compulsory education begins in the school year the child turns seven and ends at 18 or earlier after qualifying completion.

### 5. FI-EDU-01 — Tuition and application-fee tests — Resolved

The higher-education section separates the tests in all three languages. A qualifying granted Finnish TP permit may exempt its holder from tuition fees for covered foreign-language Bachelor’s/Master’s degree education, subject to statutory validity/non-revocation conditions; a pending TP application is not a granted permit. Separately, the €100 application fee is limited to covered 2026 categories and statutory scope/exceptions, excludes postgraduate applications, and has its own TP timing requirement: holding the Finnish TP permit when studies began, subject to the stated later-revocation qualification. The text does not equate the tuition and application-fee exemptions, admission and scholarship, or admission and Kela student aid.
