# Collective Protection in Norway: Eligibility, Application and Status

- **Article ID:** NO-TP-01
- **Research date:** 18 November 2026
- **Editorial status:** DRAFT
- **Compare aspects:** `protection-scheme`, `protection-application`, `protection-dates`
- **Last verified:** 18 November 2026

Norway has a national collective protection scheme for people displaced from Ukraine. It is based on section 34 of Norway’s Immigration Act and is separate from the EU Temporary Protection Directive. Under collective protection, applications can be assessed as a group rather than by a full individual assessment of each person’s need for protection.

## Who may qualify

Eligibility depends on the rules in force and your circumstances. Ukrainian citizenship or previous residence in Ukraine alone does not guarantee a permit. UDI’s current criteria include requirements about where you lived, your status in Ukraine and other residence or citizenship circumstances. Check UDI’s eligibility list before applying.

Men aged 18–60 who apply from 5 May 2026 are generally not eligible for collective protection, subject to the limited exceptions listed by UDI. These include certain sole-care situations involving a child, qualifying permanent exemption or inability to serve in Ukraine, and a medical evacuation category. The exception is evidence-specific; accompanying a child or having medical documents alone does not establish eligibility. The amendment entered into force on 4 May 2026 and applies to applications submitted after that date; UDI describes the operational cutoff as applications from 5 May 2026. The change applies to new applications, not existing permits or their renewals under the transition described by UDI.

## Applying and waiting

If you seek protection in Norway, follow UDI’s current instructions for registration at the National Arrival Centre. The police register applicants and collect identity information. Do not treat registration as approval of collective protection.

You cannot work or apply for jobs while UDI is processing an application for collective protection. If collective protection is granted, you have the right to work and receive healthcare through the public system. If you are not eligible for collective protection, UDI may assess your need for protection individually under the ordinary rules. Collective protection is not the same as individual refugee status.

## Scheme continuation, permit limit and proposal

The Norwegian Government has said the national scheme will not be discontinued in 2027 and has announced that it will continue the scheme to March 2028. This is a decision about the scheme’s continuation, not an extension of each person’s permit. Individual permits remain one year at a time and the current statutory maximum remains five years from the first permit. The first permits granted in March 2022 reach that limit in spring 2027.

On 14 October 2026, the Justice Ministry proposed increasing the maximum from five to seven years. The consultation closed on 10 November 2026; the proposal remained under consideration on 18 November 2026 and had not become law. Do not describe a sixth or seventh year as currently available.

## Safe-area rule

For new applicants, collective protection is generally unavailable where the applicant’s last permanent residence was in an area UDI considers safe. The restriction took effect on 28 September 2024. UDI’s current list, effective 13 January 2025, contains 14 areas: Cherkasy, Chernivtsi, Ivano-Frankivsk, Khmelnytskyi, Kirovohrad, Kyiv oblast (not Kyiv city), Lviv, Poltava, Rivne, Ternopil, Vinnytsia, Volyn, Zakarpattia and Zhytomyr. UDI lists exceptions, including close family in Norway with collective protection and people covered by medical evacuation. The rule does not automatically end an existing permit: UDI says existing holders may still qualify for extension even if their home area is now considered safe.

## Dual citizenship and extension decisions

UDI generally excludes Ukrainian applicants who also hold citizenship in a country on its safe-country list. UDI says these applications are normally handled under a 48-hour procedure; the applicant is registered by police, receives information from Caritas, may have a short interview, and is assisted by a lawyer who can help submit a complaint. UDI separately says that, from 1 July 2026, a refusal to extend collective protection cannot be appealed, including certain earlier decisions that had stated an appeal right. Do not confuse the initial-application complaint process with the extension rule.

## Permit period

The permit is granted for one year at a time. Current UDI guidance says it may be extended, if the requirements continue to be met, up to five years from the first permit. A proposed longer period is not a current individual entitlement. Do not assume that reaching five years automatically gives you another permit or permanent residence.
