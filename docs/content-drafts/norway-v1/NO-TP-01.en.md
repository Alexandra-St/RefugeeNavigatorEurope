# Collective Protection in Norway: Eligibility, Application and Status

- **Article ID:** NO-TP-01
- **Research date:** 14 November 2026
- **Editorial status:** DRAFT
- **Compare aspects:** `protection-scheme`, `protection-application`, `protection-dates`
- **Last verified:** 14 November 2026

Norway has a national collective protection scheme for people displaced from Ukraine. It is based on section 34 of Norway’s Immigration Act and is separate from the EU Temporary Protection Directive. Under collective protection, applications can be assessed as a group rather than by a full individual assessment of each person’s need for protection.

## Who may qualify

Eligibility depends on the rules in force and your circumstances. Ukrainian citizenship or previous residence in Ukraine alone does not guarantee a permit. UDI’s current criteria include requirements about where you lived, your status in Ukraine and other residence or citizenship circumstances. Check UDI’s eligibility list before applying.

Men aged 18–60 who apply from 5 May 2026 are generally not eligible for collective protection, subject to the limited exceptions listed by UDI. These include certain sole-care situations involving a child, qualifying permanent exemption or inability to serve in Ukraine, and a medical evacuation category. The exception is evidence-specific; accompanying a child or having medical documents alone does not establish eligibility. The change applies to new applications, not existing permits or their renewals under the transition described by UDI.

## Applying and waiting

If you seek protection in Norway, follow UDI’s current instructions for registration at the National Arrival Centre. The police register applicants and collect identity information. Do not treat registration as approval of collective protection.

You cannot work or apply for jobs while UDI is processing an application for collective protection. If collective protection is granted, you have the right to work and receive healthcare through the public system. If you are not eligible for collective protection, UDI may assess your need for protection individually under the ordinary rules. Collective protection is not the same as individual refugee status.

## Permit period

The permit is granted for one year at a time. Current UDI guidance says it may be extended, if the requirements continue to be met, up to five years from the first permit. A proposed longer period is not a current individual entitlement. Do not assume that reaching five years automatically gives you another permit or permanent residence.
