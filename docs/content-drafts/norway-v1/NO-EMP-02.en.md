# Work Authorization and Employee Rights

- **Article ID:** NO-EMP-02
- **Research date:** 14 November 2026
- **Editorial status:** DRAFT
- **Compare aspects:** `work-permission`, `work-employer-duties`, `work-minimum-pay`
- **Last verified:** 14 November 2026

Your right to work depends on your current immigration status. Norway also sets basic employment protections, but pay rules differ across sectors.

## When you may work

If you have applied for collective protection and UDI is still processing your application, you cannot work or apply for jobs. If collective protection is granted, UDI says you have the right to work in Norway. If your case is assessed as an individual asylum application, separate rules apply; check UDI before accepting work.

## Your employment contract

All employees must have a written employment contract, and the employer is responsible for preparing it. For a job lasting more than one month, it must be ready as soon as possible and no later than seven days after work starts. For a job lasting one month or less, it must be ready on the first day. The contract must state important terms, including pay, payment intervals, hours and the duration or type of employment.

## Minimum pay

Norway does not have one national minimum wage that applies to every job. Mandatory minimum rates apply in certain sectors. In other sectors, pay is agreed individually or under a collective agreement. Check the current rate for your sector with the Norwegian Labour Inspection Authority (Arbeidstilsynet); rates can change.
