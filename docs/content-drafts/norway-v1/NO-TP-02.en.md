# Residence Options Beyond Collective Protection

- **Article ID:** NO-TP-02
- **Research date:** 14 November 2026
- **Editorial status:** DRAFT
- **Compare aspects:** `residence-routes`, `residence-conditions`, `residence-filing`
- **Last verified:** 14 November 2026

Collective protection is a temporary national permit. It does not itself provide a basis for permanent residence. If you are considering another residence route, check the conditions for that specific route with the Norwegian Directorate of Immigration (UDI) before applying.

## Possible routes

UDI identifies family immigration and work immigration as examples of other routes that may be relevant. Each has separate eligibility rules. For some applications from Norway, the general rule requires that the applicant is a skilled worker or has lived in Norway on a valid residence permit for the previous nine months. Family immigration has different rules, and residence under EEA rules is a separate system. These are not universal conditions: use UDI’s guide for the exact category and your circumstances.

UDI says a person who has been granted collective protection may apply for another type of residence permit if they meet its requirements. UDI’s instructions also say that when another residence permit is granted, the collective protection permit ceases. A person who loses, lets expire, or is not granted an extension of collective protection cannot return to that scheme later under the current rules.

## Before you apply

1. Choose the specific route you want to explore, such as work or family immigration.
2. Check UDI’s current eligibility conditions and whether that application can be filed from Norway.
3. Read the consequences of changing permits carefully. Do not assume that submitting an application automatically changes your current status or preserves every right.

This article does not provide a universal filing address, document list, or step-by-step procedure: these differ by route. UDI also states that a proposed special work-based permit for some people with collective protection is not yet an available application route. A proposal is not a current right.

## Permanent residence

Collective protection itself does not form the basis for permanent residence. Do not assume that time under this permit automatically counts toward permanent residence or that a person receives permanent residence after five years. Any future permit or rule must be confirmed by UDI when it is adopted and implemented.
