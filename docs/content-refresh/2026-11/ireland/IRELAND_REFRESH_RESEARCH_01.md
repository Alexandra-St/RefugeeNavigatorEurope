## H. EU Temporary Protection framework

Directive 2001/55/EC establishes temporary protection; Council Implementing Decision (EU) 2022/382 activated it for the defined displaced-from-Ukraine categories. Ireland’s older grant basis was section 60 of the International Protection Act 2015. New grants/permissions since 12 June 2026 are under section 237 of the International Protection Act 2026, which sets Irish eligibility/exclusion, registration and entitlement rules. The EU protection period, Ireland’s permission and documents (Temporary Protection Certificate and IRP), and registration renewal are separate questions.

## I. EU horizon and Irish document horizon

Decision (EU) 2026/1912 extends EU Temporary Protection to **4 March 2028**, applying the extension from **5 March 2027**. Its Article 2 military-obligation rule applies from **5 August 2026**. A corrigendum sets the continuity carve-out for people enjoying TP in a given Member State before/on **4 August 2026** and continuously retaining it in that same State thereafter.

ISD’s latest accessible Irish renewal page is narrower: it extends the Irish permission for beneficiaries residing with a yellow Temporary Protection Certificate valid through **4 March 2026** until **4 March 2027**; no replacement certificate is needed, an expired covered certificate remains proof of TP/service entitlement to that date, and the person must renew the IRP card. The general ISD registration FAQs say IRP renewal can be made up to 12 weeks before expiry and that TP beneficiaries can renew online from March 2026. **No newer Irish notice extending the covered permission, certificate or IRP instruction through 4 March 2028 was identified by 18 November 2026.** EU extension does not automatically rewrite those Irish document instructions.

## J.–K. 5 August change, existing holders and other EU grants

From **5 August 2026**, ISD says new applicants must demonstrate that they were authorised under Ukrainian law to leave Ukraine and provide evidence acceptable to Irish authorities. ISD does not publish an exhaustive evidence list. ISD says people granted TP before the new rule are unaffected and need not provide the additional evidence. The EU rule’s continuity language is narrower and tied to continuous protection in the same Member State as of 4 August; Ireland’s public wording is preserved as Irish implementation, not replaced with another State’s process.

ISD also states Ireland will not grant TP where the person has already been granted TP in another EU country. This does not establish automatic transfer, withdrawal, or a travel-based change of status.

## L. Eligibility and family reunification

ISD eligibility guidance covers Ukrainian nationals resident in Ukraine before 24 February 2022; qualifying third-country nationals/stateless people who benefited from international/equivalent protection in Ukraine; their qualifying family members where the family existed in Ukraine before 24 February 2022; and permanent Ukrainian residence holders unable to return safely and durably. Nationality alone is not an entitlement; statutory exclusions and individual facts remain relevant.

Family reunification follows Article 15 TPD and Irish law. Article 15 distinguishes the spouse/qualifying stable partner and unmarried minor children from other close relatives who lived as the family unit and were wholly/mainly dependent; the latter is discretionary/case-by-case and includes hardship considerations. Article 15 governs transfer/reunification under temporary protection; it is not ordinary Irish family sponsorship. From 12 June 2026 the revised Non-EEA family policy excludes section 60 TP beneficiaries as sponsors under that policy, while Article 15 TP reunification remains a separate legal route. Exact TPTS family-unit rules are subject to the unresolved portal Policy/FAQ check below.

## M.–N. Temporary Protection vs International Protection; TPC, IRP and PPSN

ISD says the two processes cannot be availed of concurrently: a person can apply for international protection, but one application must be withdrawn before TP can be granted or vice versa. The International Protection Act 2026 came into operation on **12 June 2026** (S.I. 244/2026); section 237 excludes a person with an active international-protection application in the statutory process. This creates a relevant update to older route explanations but does not turn these articles into an asylum guide.

The Temporary Protection Certificate/“yellow paper” evidences permission/entitlement; it is not the IRP card. For the covered 2027 cohort, certificate replacement is not required but IRP renewal is. ISD requires a current/extended certificate copy and proof of address for online renewal, and allows applications in the 12-week pre-expiry window. TPTS approval requires a separate registered Stamp 4. PPSN identifies a person for tax/services and some applications; it is not TP permission and is obtained through a process. Recent Ukraine arrivals follow the special PPSN allocation route rather than online application; children also need their own number.

## O. TPTS — Task 109 record (superseded by Task 110 amendment below)

The TPTS is current and open; the ISD announcement says applications opened **30 September 2026** through its online portal. Government’s published outline and Dáil answers confirm the main public criteria: one year resident in Ireland as a BOTP before opening; at least six months employed or self-employed immediately before opening; average gross earnings **€29,432 per year**; continue employment at application and decision; and independent accommodation at application. A person unable to show a full year of earnings may show that their earnings rate annualised would meet the threshold. It is a specific option for eligible current TP beneficiaries, not a replacement for TP or automatic conversion. Government describes Stamp 4 for up to two years, renewable for further two-year periods, with time on TPTS counting towards naturalisation; TPTS Stamp 4 cannot be held at the same time as TP or another registered immigration permission.

## P.–R. Employment, self-employment, wage, tax and PRSI

A valid TP permission gives a right to seek/enter employment and engage in a business, trade or profession on Irish-citizen terms while valid; a separate employment permit is not normally required for TP work. An arrival or pending first application is not work permission; ISD’s renewal receipt is evidence only in the specified renewal situation. Employer duties include core written terms by day five, full terms within one month, payslips, payroll reporting and PAYE/USC/PRSI as applicable. PPSN absence is handled through Revenue’s alternative details; it does not eliminate payroll obligations. Self-employment rights do not substitute for Revenue, business-name/company, tax, VAT (if relevant), PRSI or professional-registration steps.

WRC/eISB rates effective **1 January 2026**: age 20+ **€14.15**; age 19 **€12.74**; age 18 **€11.32**; under 18 **€9.91**, subject to statutory exclusions and reckonable pay. Higher Sectoral Employment Orders/Employment Regulation Orders may apply. Budget 2027 announced **€14.94** for 20+ from **1 January 2027**; no implementing 2027 NMW Order was found in the reviewed official statutory sources by the cutoff. Do not state it as enacted law before verification. Income-tax residence is fact-based; avoid a single generic Irish tax rate. Tax/PRSI liabilities follow Revenue/DSP rules and do not arise as automatic welfare entitlement from TP alone.

## S. Healthcare and medical cards

Section 60/237 TP entitles a valid permission-holder to medical care on Irish-citizen conditions, subject to scheme conditions; this does not make every service free or register a GP automatically. The UKR Medical Card is a separate HSE application for eligible people from Ukraine with TP arriving less than one year ago; it needs a PPSN and form, and HSE can assign a GP when the applicant has none. After one year, HSE directs applicants to the ordinary Medical Card route. HSE says care can still be provided without PPSN. HSE’s July 2025 circular says the generic Ukrainian medical-card numbers were expired and the interim arrangement stood down. The evidence does not establish an exhaustive national document list for the UKR card.

## T.–W. Accommodation, DAC, ARP, BOTP weekly payment

For eligible new arrivals registering from **10 November 2025** who seek state accommodation, guidance provides for a maximum **30-day** stay in a Designated Accommodation Centre (DAC), with food, services and integration support, subject to capacity and policy; a chosen centre, minimum stay or placement is not guaranteed. People granted TP before 10 November 2025 who seek state accommodation remain under the prior maximum of **90 days**. These are time-limited arrival arrangements, not indefinite housing rights.

At a DAC, eligible BOTPs receive the income-tested Beneficiary of Temporary Protection Weekly Payment: net **€38.80/adult** and **€29.80/child** per week. Meal contributions may adjust amounts. This is different from IPAS Daily Expenses Allowance, even where overlapping numbers appear. Residents may be excluded from certain ordinary payments while full-board DAC residents; outside DAC, standard welfare can be applied for under its separate conditions.

Government announced withdrawal of state-contracted commercial accommodation from **August 2026**, targeted for completion by **March 2027**, with resident notice/support and no less than three months’ notice for later phases; March is a programme target, not a hard individual legal status expiry. Residents requiring ongoing accommodation may apply through UCTAT with reasons/evidence; medical evidence and specified DSP payment statuses are examples, not automatic approvals. A different location is likely and not guaranteed; DAC placement may change payment eligibility.

ARP is host support, not BOTP cash support. DOJ guidance sets **€400/month from October 2026** and extends the scheme to **31 March 2027**. No further continuation after that date was identified; watch the scheme before expiry.

## X.–Y. Social welfare, HRC and housing support

The January 2026 DSP HRC operational guideline says valid section 60 TP status establishes the right-to-reside limb; the separate centre-of-interest/five-factor HRC and each payment’s rules remain. Do not state a universal HRC waiver. The page has not been updated for new section 237 permissions, so that precise interaction remains an open qualification requiring a current DSP interpretation. DAC weekly support is distinct from ordinary welfare and IPAS support. Jobseeker’s Allowance, Basic SWA, Additional Needs Payment, rent support and other payments have their own means, residence, employment, HRC and other criteria. HAP requires local-authority social-housing approval and eligible tenancy; June 2026 Government policy says temporary protection is not eligible for social-housing support under existing policy (a proposed bill is not law). Rent Supplement and Additional Needs Payment are assessed separately. Child Benefit is non-means-tested but has residence/HRC and other conditions; official service rate reviewed is **€140/month** per qualifying child. WFP has its separate insured employment, hours, child and income rules; self-employed hours are not counted as employee hours. PPSN identifies a claimant; it is not an award.

## Z. Education and higher/further study

Children with valid TP can apply to local primary/post-primary schools; local capacity/admission is not guaranteed. Compulsory education begins at **6** and runs until **16 or completion of three post-primary years, whichever is later**; ordinary school starting age is not the same as school-leaving age. REALT/TESS can help with school search/admission issues; do not guarantee a preferred school or universal transport. For **2026/27**, EAL allocation is school-level and needs/resource dependent, not a fixed one-to-one entitlement. The reviewed temporary higher-education tuition support is a defined continuing section 60 cohort progressing from an approved 2025/26 course; new 2026/27 entrants are excluded and new section 237 coverage is not established. CAO processes participating first-year undergraduate applications but does not decide admission; institutions decide admission and recognition; SUSI separately assesses criteria. Do not promise free fees, a bursary or an apprenticeship place.
