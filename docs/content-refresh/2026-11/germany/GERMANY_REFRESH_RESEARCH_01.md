### DE-HEALTH-01 — Coverage routes: COMPLETE

There is no single healthcare route created by §24 status alone. Employment can create statutory or private insurance depending on the job. Qualifying SGB II/Grundsicherungsgeld recipients are generally insured in GKV for the period of benefit receipt (with statutory exceptions); qualifying SGB XII recipients obtain treatment through the Sozialamt, often administered by a Krankenkasse under SGB V §264, without automatically becoming insured members. AsylbLG §4 covers necessary acute illness/pain treatment, medicines, vaccinations/prevention and pregnancy/maternity care; §6 supports necessary case-specific health needs, including additional protection for specified vulnerable §24 holders. Local authority/card/treatment implementation varies. Eligible §24 holders not needy under SGB II or SGB XII may have a voluntary GKV entry right under §417 SGB V within six months, subject to qualifying residence-document and identity requirements.

### Leistungsrechtsanpassungsgesetz — healthcare impact

**CURRENT STATUS:** Referred government bill; not promulgated, not in force, no effective date. It proposes moving specified people who first receive/apply for §24 after 31 March 2025 into AsylbLG. Existing SGB II/XII awards would continue to their individual end date, no later than three months after the proposed law takes effect. The bill also proposes completion of treatment begun with SGB II/XII GKV-equivalent coverage and a clarification that an AsylbLG §4 health claim counts as other health coverage for certain GKV continuity rules, preventing some unintended continuation memberships/debt. These provisions are not operative. The separate SGB II reform took effect mainly 1 July 2026; it is not the LRA bill. [BMAS bill page](https://www.bmas.de/DE/Service/Gesetze-und-Gesetzesvorhaben/leistungsrechtsanpassungsgesetz.html), [Bundestag bill BT-Drs. 21/3539](https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/21/035/2103539.pdf), [DIP bill status](https://dip.bundestag.de/vorgang/_/329468).

### DE-HOUSING-01 — Reception, renting and cost support: COMPLETE

Asylum applicants follow the Asylum Act reception/distribution framework. Temporary protection under §24 AufenthG has a separate route: §24(3) provides distribution among Länder, §24(4) permits Land assignment to a place, and §24(5) denies an entitlement to choose a Land/place and requires residence at the assigned place. That assignment ends upon issuance of the §24 residence permit. Follow any decision received and check with the immigration authority before moving while it applies. Operational routing and shelter capacity remain local; no particular dwelling is guaranteed. Private renting is separate.

Housing costs are scheme-specific. SGB II §22 and SGB XII §35 assess actual reasonable accommodation/heating costs under local standards; AsylbLG §3 has its own necessary-cost route and may provide cash or in-kind support depending on accommodation/authority. A material change took effect 1 July 2026: SGB II §22 and SGB XII §35 cap accommodation costs during the one-year waiting period at 1.5 times the local abstract reasonableness limit, subject to hardship exceptions; local square-metre ceilings and rent-control rules can further affect recognition. Heating is assessed separately. There is no nationwide euro threshold. Under SGB II, request the new-housing cost assurance before signing; housing-search/moving costs may require prior assurance from the current authority, and a deposit from the authority for the new place (generally as a loan). SGB XII also requires advance notice/consent for certain costs of a move, deposit or costs above the reasonable limit. AsylbLG follows its own local assessment.

**Housing / local availability — FINAL RESULT:** The AsylG reception route and §24 temporary-protection allocation route must be distinguished. Under §24(3)–(5), federal distribution and possible Land assignment can constrain place of residence until the assignment ends on permit issuance. Local authorities administer reception and shelter availability; no particular dwelling is guaranteed. Confirm any individual assignment and local availability with the responsible authority.

### DE-EDU-01 — Education and German-language learning: COMPLETE

School-age rules, compulsory attendance, entry ages, newcomer/preparatory classes and German-language support are set or implemented by the Länder and local schools; there is no Germany-wide age, deadline or application form. Families should contact the education ministry, Schulamt or school for their Land and ask what to do if residence documents, prior records or translations are missing. SGB VIII childcare rights are distinct from school enrolment and remain conditional by age/status; Jugendamt allocation, provider and vacancy are local.

A §24 holder may apply to BAMF for admission to an integration course under §44(4) AufenthG; this is not a guaranteed statutory course place. BAMF guidance says admitted Ukrainian participants are exempt from the course fee. Berufssprachkurse have separate eligibility and may require a prior integration course or B1-level German. Higher-education admission depends on the applicant’s Hochschulzugangsberechtigung and programme requirements; the institution decides, sometimes using uni-assist. For Ukrainian school-leaving certificates issued from 2026, uni-assist asks for a school statement about the Ukraine-focused curriculum. Public-university tuition is not universally zero: semester contributions apply and Land/institution exceptions exist. §24 alone does not guarantee admission or student finance; qualifying holders may apply for BAföG under §61 subject to its conditions.

### DE-SOC-01 — Financial and social assistance: COMPLETE

The current route depends on the person’s document, actual status, age, ability to work, need, household and income/assets.
