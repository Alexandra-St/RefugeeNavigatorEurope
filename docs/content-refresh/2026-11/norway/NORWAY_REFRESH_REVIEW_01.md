## E. National collective-protection framework

**ACCEPT.** Norway's collective-protection regime is national, grounded in Immigration Act §34 and Immigration Regulations §7-5a. It is not an application of the EU Temporary Protection Directive. The relevant official legal text is [Immigration Regulations §7-5a](https://lovdata.no/dokument/SF/forskrift/2009-10-15-1286/KAPITTEL_7-3); UDI's collective-protection guidance describes the §34 basis. The article and Compare copy preserve this distinction.

## F. Current five-year cap

**ACCEPT.** Section 7-5a allows continued stay for one year from permit expiry and permits that extension up to four times, subject to the scheme continuing and the individual remaining within the eligible group. That creates the current maximum of five years from first permit. UDI's current [grant guidance](https://www.udi.no/en/information-ukraine-and-russia/ukraine/i-have-received-an-answer-to-my-application/my-application-for-collective-protection-has-been-granted/) gives the same public-facing one-year-at-a-time and up-to-five-year rule. The source plan now links both operational guidance and the rule itself.

## G. Seven-year proposal and post-deadline search

**ACCEPT — PROPOSAL ONLY.** The Justice Ministry consultation opened 14 October 2026, closed 10 November, and proposed extending the maximum from five to seven years. The [consultation page](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/dokumenter/horing-forslag-til-endringer-i-utlendingsloven-34-utvidelse-av-antall-ar-det-kan-gis-midlertidig-kollektiv-beskyttelse/id3177598/) still identifies its status as under consideration. A targeted official search through 18 November found no enacted amendment. No staged wording grants a sixth or seventh year as current law.

## H. Scheme continuation toward March 2028

**ACCEPT — government policy/announced continuation, not a change to individual permits.** The Government announced that it would not discontinue the national scheme in 2027 and stated that the scheme was to continue through March 2028 ([17 September notice](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/aktuelt/ordningen-med-midlertidig-kollektiv-beskyttelse-for-fordrevne-fra-ukraina-avvikles-ikke-i-2027/id3173049/); [9 October update](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/aktuelt/regjeringen-foreslar-a-utvide-varigheten-for-ordningen-med-midlertidig-kollektiv-beskyttelse/id3176866/)). This is scheme-level policy/announcement; it does not override the five-year individual statutory maximum. The staging copy keeps those concepts separate.

## I. First five-year cases — spring 2027

**ACCEPT.** Official Government/UDI material says first arrivals reach five years in spring 2027. This is season-level timing; the drafts and watch do not invent an exact expiry date. UDI says affected people will receive more information well before permit expiry.

## J. Safe-area restriction and list

**ACCEPT.** The restriction applies to qualifying new protection applications from 28 September 2024. UDI's current [published list](https://www.udi.no/en/important-messages/more-areas-excluded-from-the-right-to-collective-protection/) remains 14 areas, effective 13 January 2025: Cherkasy, Chernivtsi, Ivano-Frankivsk, Khmelnytskyi, Kirovohrad, Kyiv oblast (not Kyiv city), Lviv, Poltava, Rivne, Ternopil, Vinnytsia, Volyn, Zakarpattia, and Zhytomyr. No later list change was found by cutoff. UDI identifies exceptions including relevant close family in Norway and medical evacuation.

## K. Safe-area extension distinction

**ACCEPT.** The new-area restriction does not automatically terminate an existing permit. UDI's safe-area guidance says the change does not apply to holders due for extension; extension eligibility is assessed separately under current extension rules. Coverage mapping has been corrected to cite the source page that states this directly.

## L. Men aged 18–60; 4 May / 5 May; exceptions and extensions

**ACCEPT.** The amendment took effect on 4 May 2026 and applies to protection applications made after that date; UDI operationally describes applications from 5 May. Men aged 18–60 are generally outside new collective-protection grants, subject to tightly framed exceptions. These include the prescribed sole-care/parental-responsibility/guardianship or close-family child circumstances, documented military-service exemption or clear inability to serve, UCPM medical evacuation, and qualifying accompanying relatives. Evidence and the exact family/care circumstances matter; the drafts do not broaden these exceptions.

The new-application restriction is not an across-the-board extension ban. Pre-cutoff applications and grants, permanent military-exemption cases, and the specifically described post-cutoff sole-care grants are treated separately in UDI extension guidance. No article implies automatic loss of an existing permit. Individual protection remains a separate assessment where collective protection is unavailable.

## M. Eligibility, application, registration, and pending rights

**ACCEPT.** Citizenship alone is not described as automatic eligibility. The copy accounts for residence/status on 24 February 2022, prior protection/residence elsewhere, family, dual nationality, safe-country citizenship, and medical evacuation. It distinguishes the 48-hour initial dual-nationality process and lawyer-assisted complaint from the separate rule that certain extension refusals are not appealable from 1 July 2026.

National Arrival Centre attendance, police registration and identity collection, application, UDI decision, and residence card remain distinct steps. Registration is not approval. Pending applicants are not given post-grant work rights. Pending healthcare rights use separate UDI/Helsenorge material; no work right is inferred from a pending collective-protection application.

## N. Extension, absence, and permanent residence

**ACCEPT.** Many renewals are automatic, with current address/contact responsibility stated. The drafts do not use obsolete universal renewal steps. More than six months abroad is described only as a potential case-specific non-extension/revocation issue, not automatic loss for ordinary short travel. Collective protection itself is not a basis for permanent residence; any later qualifying permit is a distinct route. The appeal restriction for extension refusal is kept separate from the initial complaint process.

The draft invents no effective date and does not present an application route as available.

## P. Employment and self-employment

**ACCEPT.** Employment access for collective-protection holders begins after grant, not while pending. Employer statements are limited to ordinary employment-contract/payroll duties; no unsupported Ukraine-specific notification duty is asserted. Self-employment is separated from immigration authorization and business registration, tax, VAT, National Insurance, and profession-specific requirements. Registration alone is not represented as permission to work as self-employed.

## Q. Minimum wage and rates

**ACCEPT.** Norway has no general nationwide statutory minimum wage. The Labour Inspection Authority currently lists ten sectors with statutory minimum rates and says no set minimum applies in other sectors ([official rate page](https://www.arbeidstilsynet.no/en/pay-and-engagement-of-employees/pay-and-minimum-rates-of-pay/minimum-wage/)). Automotive-sector coverage began 15 June 2026; current rate tables were revised 6 November 2026. Retained amounts are sector/category-specific. There is no fabricated nationwide NOK/hour amount and the Compare row states the same.

## R. Tax and National Insurance

**ACCEPT.** Tax-card, PAYE, ordinary taxation, tax residence, D-number/identity-number, tax-return, and National Insurance statements remain qualified by individual status and applicable scheme. No flat tax rate, tax residence conclusion, or automatic benefit membership is inferred solely from collective protection.

## S. Healthcare and pending healthcare

**ACCEPT.** Granted-holder entitlements are distinguished from pending-applicant healthcare rules. Public access is not described as making every service, medicine, or dental treatment free; user fees and exceptions are qualified. GP access and practical availability are not promised. Emergency contact numbers are consistent with the staged official guidance.

## T. Reception, accommodation, housing, settlement, and support

**ACCEPT.** Reception accommodation is separated from municipal settlement and private renting. IMDi-assisted municipal settlement depends on the applicable process, matching, and municipal capacity; preferred municipality is not guaranteed. Housing allowance, NAV rent/deposit help, and other housing support are individual and conditional. Deposit protections are grounded in the Tenancy Act; no universal free/permanent housing promise appears. Private-rental search and read-before-signing statements are now treated as practical advice rather than material externally sourced facts.

## U. Introduction programme and social benefits

**ACCEPT.** Municipal introduction programmes are distinguished from automatic entitlement to benefits. The 1 January 2026 transition between right-and-duty and right-only categories is retained with its permit-date condition. The 18–55 right and over-55 case-by-case/discretionary offer language is cautious; it does not turn a discretionary 55–67 offer into a right. Introduction allowance is conditional/taxable. NAV social assistance is individually assessed and locally administered; child benefit and cash-for-care have separate residence, child-age, National Insurance, and application conditions.

## V. Education and 1 July 2028 change

**ACCEPT.** The current rule is instruction as soon as possible and a full offer no later than three months for a child likely to stay more than three months. The three-month text was continued effective 1 July 2026. Act 12 June 2026 No. 22 provides that Parts I–X are repealed on 1 July 2028; the current text also flags a §2-1 change effective that date ([Udir §2-1](https://www.udir.no/regelverkstolkninger/opplaring/lov-om-grunnskoleopplaringa-og-den-vidaregaande-opplaringa-opplaringslova/andre-del--grunnskoleopplaring/kapittel-2-rett-og-plikt-til-grunnskoleopplaring/-2-1-rett-til-grunnskoleopplaring/); [Act 2026 No. 22](https://lovdata.no/dokument/LTI/lov/2026-06-12-22)). At sunset, the temporary three-month amendment and its related extension authority cease unless amended or replaced; the consolidated §2-1 and transition rules must be checked before asserting the post-sunset deadline. The staging copy does not claim a definitive replacement rule or cite the expired 2022–mid-2026 emergency provision as current.
