## National collective-protection framework

Norway is not an EU Member State. Its temporary collective-protection scheme is a national regime under Immigration Act §34 and Immigration Regulations §7-5a for group assessment during mass displacement. It is not EU temporary-protection status and is not governed directly by Directive 2001/55/EC or the EU Council decision of 4 March 2028. Individual protection under Immigration Act §28 remains a separate assessment; exclusion from collective protection does not itself require refusal of an individual claim.

## Scheme continuation, individual permits and duration

The Government announced on 17 September 2026 that it would not discontinue the scheme in 2027 and stated on 9 October that the scheme is intended to continue to March 2028. Scheme-level continuation does not alter each holder’s statutory maximum. Under current §34 an individual permit is granted for one year at a time and can be renewed or extended for up to five years from the first permit. First permits granted in March 2022 begin to reach five years in spring 2027.

The Justice Ministry’s consultation to amend §34 opened 14 October and closed 10 November 2026. It proposes increasing the maximum from five to seven years. On 18 November 2026 the consultation page still showed “under consideration”; no enactment was found. **The seven-year period is a proposal, not law.** No sixth or seventh year is currently available under this proposal.

## Safe-area rule and list

The new-applicant restriction took effect 28 September 2024. The current UDI list is the 14-area list effective 13 January 2025: Cherkasy, Chernivtsi, Ivano-Frankivsk, Khmelnytskyi, Kirovohrad, Kyiv oblast (not Kyiv city), Lviv, Poltava, Rivne, Ternopil, Vinnytsia, Volyn, Zakarpattia and Zhytomyr. UDI says the assessment may change. The 13 January 2025 list remains the current published list; no later list change was found. Exceptions include relevant close family ties in Norway and medical evacuation. This is not an automatic loss rule for existing holders: UDI expressly allows possible extensions even where the home area is now considered safe. Application date matters; applications from 28 September 2024 are assessed under the restriction, with earlier pending applications assessed under rules at application time.

## Men aged 18–60

The amendment entered into force 4 May 2026 and applies to protection applications after that date; UDI describes the operational cutoff as applications from 5 May 2026. Men 18–60 are generally excluded from new collective-protection grants and assessed individually, subject to evidence-based exceptions: sole day-to-day care/parental responsibility or guardianship for a child in the specified situations; documented permanent exemption from all forms of Ukrainian military service or clear inability to serve; and UCPM medical evacuation or accompanying relatives. Existing permit holders and renewals are not automatically excluded. UDI’s extension guidance separately preserves renewal for those who applied before 5 May, those with permanent military-service exemption, and specified post-cutoff sole-care grantees who continue to have sole day-to-day care in Norway.

## Eligibility, application, pending rights and extensions

Eligibility is not determined by Ukrainian citizenship alone. UDI assesses residence/status on 24 February 2022, prior protection or residence elsewhere, dual nationality and other criteria, with an expedited 48-hour procedure for certain safe-country dual citizens; UDI says a lawyer assists with an initial-case complaint. Since 1 July 2026, refusal to extend collective protection cannot be appealed, a separate process rule. Registration at the National Arrival Centre is a police/identity process; it is not the UDI decision or permit grant. Pending collective-protection applicants cannot work or apply for jobs under the current UDI guidance. Post-grant rights must not be back-projected to pending applicants. Many permit renewals occur automatically; the holder must keep UDI contact details/address current and check whether an application is required. UDI identifies more than six months abroad in a permit period and travel/residence circumstances as possible non-extension or revocation grounds; these are case-specific, not automatic universal rules. Since 1 July 2026, refusal to extend collective protection cannot be appealed; this is distinct from the initial dual-nationality 48-hour complaint procedure.

## Individual protection, permanent residence and proposed work route

A person not eligible for collective protection may still be assessed individually under ordinary asylum rules, with higher evidentiary thresholds and internal-relocation/safe-area considerations. Collective protection itself does not form the basis for permanent residence.

The proposed special work-based route is not current law. The Labour Ministry consultation was published 17 September 2026; the deadline was extended from 30 October to 5 November. The proposal concerns persons already in Norway with collective protection who meet employment/income/self-support conditions, with possible derivative residence for qualifying family. The proposal contemplated permanent-residence qualifying status after three years if enacted and requirements are met. No rules had been introduced on 18 November.

## Employment, wages, tax and National Insurance

Work rights for collective-protection holders begin after grant; registration alone is not a grant. Employee duties include written employment terms and normal payroll/reporting obligations. The article should not imply that the collective-protection work right independently establishes a self-employment right; business registration, taxes, VAT and regulated-profession rules are separate.

There is no nationwide statutory minimum wage. Arbeidstilsynet currently lists ten sectors with legal minimum rates. Automotive-sector rates began 15 June 2026; the published rate schedule was revised 6 November 2026. Rates are sector/category-specific and must not be turned into a single national NOK amount. Tax-card/PAYE versus ordinary-tax treatment and National Insurance membership are individual and scheme-specific; no blanket tax rate or benefit entitlement follows solely from collective protection.

## Healthcare

UDI confirms healthcare rights for people granted collective protection. Pending-applicant rules are separate. Public-system access does not mean all services, medicines or dental care are free; user fees and exemptions depend on the service/status. GP registration and practical access may depend on residence/registration and local capacity. Emergency numbers in production Compare were retained.

## Reception, settlement and housing

Reception accommodation is a temporary process-stage service, distinct from municipal settlement and ordinary rental housing. IMDi-assisted municipal settlement depends on matching, municipal capacity and process rules; independent self-settlement can affect support. Housing allowance, deposit or rent help from NAV/Husbanken is not guaranteed; NAV decisions are individual. Deposit rules are general tenancy law. No universal free permanent housing statement is supported.

## Introduction programme and social support

Municipalities administer introduction programmes. Current rules distinguish first grants from 1 January 2026 (generally right and duty) from earlier grants (right-only under the transition). UDI says people aged 18–55 may have a right to participate; people over 55 may still qualify depending on circumstances, and municipalities may offer places to some older participants. Age, settlement and the applicable individual programme remain material; the benefit is conditional and taxable, not guaranteed net cash. NAV social assistance is individually assessed, means-tested and locally administered, with no universal fixed amount. Child benefit and cash-for-care are separate benefits with their own residence, National Insurance, child age and application rules; collective protection alone does not make them automatic.

## Education and 1 July 2026 change

The production school-enrolment wording “no later than one month” is stale. Current Opplæringslova §2-1 provides a three-month full-offer deadline while requiring education to start as soon as possible. The Ukraine-related three-month rule was continued by Act 12 June 2026 no. 22, effective 1 July 2026, and is scheduled to sunset 1 July 2028. The one-month claim should be updated across EN/UK/RU and Compare. Compulsory-school age, language-support rights and upper-secondary rules are separate.

- Permit/cap: Immigration Regulations §7-5a provides one-year continued stays up to four times (five years total); UDI’s current grant page explains the same rule. The permit itself is not a basis for permanent residence.
- Seven-year proposal: the Justice Ministry consultation remains “under consideration” after the 10 Nov 2026 deadline; official search through 18 Nov found no enactment. Proposal only; no current six/seven-year entitlement.
- Scheme continuation: government announcement that the scheme will not be discontinued in 2027 and is intended to continue toward March 2028 is government policy/announced intention, not alteration of the individual five-year cap.
- First five-year cohort: UDI says people who arrived first reach five years in spring 2027. This is a season-level official statement; no exact date is inferred.
- Safe areas: UDI’s live page still lists Cherkasy, Chernivtsi, Ivano-Frankivsk, Khmelnytskyi, Kirovohrad, Kyiv oblast (not Kyiv city), Lviv, Poltava, Rivne, Ternopil, Vinnytsia, Volyn, Zakarpattia and Zhytomyr, effective 13 Jan 2025. Rule concerns qualifying new applications; existing holders may still be extended under extension rules.

Exceptions cover sole-care/guardian/close-family care for children, documented military exemption or clear inability to serve, UCPM medical evacuation and accompanying relatives.
