### IT-TP-01 — Temporary protection

**Eligibility.** EU categories and Italy’s national application mechanism are distinct. The EU framework includes displaced Ukrainian nationals and specified family members; stateless people and third-country nationals who had international or equivalent national protection in Ukraine; and stateless people or third-country nationals with permanent residence in Ukraine who cannot safely return to their origin country/region. Prior status and the EU category conditions matter. A person already protected in another Member State does not automatically acquire Italian protection; individual eligibility and the competent Questura decision remain case-specific. No special Italian rule for prior Italian permits was found in the reviewed sources.

**Military rule — FINAL RESULT:** The corrected Decision 2026/1912 applies Article 2 from 5 August 2026. New grants, where the condition is applicable, require satisfaction of Ukrainian military obligations and proof where applicable. The corrigendum exempts people who enjoyed protection in a given Member State before or on 4 August 2026 and retain that status continuously in that same State. No public, nationwide Italian evidence list, age threshold or Questura procedure for this rule was verified. Eligibility is therefore confirmed at EU level but Italian implementation details require qualification.

**Application — FINAL RESULT:** The DPCM says to apply directly to the Questura; the Questore responsible for the person’s domicile issues the electronic permit free of charge. It does not prescribe a single current booking or in-person process, uniform checklist, processing time or detailed approach to missing identity/family documents. A Civil Protection circular of 26 September 2022 states that the applicant and accompanying minor children receive a paper filing receipt with codice fiscale when the request is presented. This is dated operational guidance, not a current national appointment protocol. Local Questura instructions must not be generalized.

**Permit/document chain — FINAL RESULT:** National legal sequence established: request presented at Questura → filing receipt (the 2022 national circular describes it as paper and says it includes codice fiscale for applicant/minors) → electronic `permesso di soggiorno per protezione temporanea` issued by the Questore. The DPCM states free issuance. The reviewed sources do not establish current uniform timing, card collection/reissue steps, whether every existing card is physically replaced, or a nationwide residence-registration (`anagrafe`) process. The Ministry booklet’s fingerprint details are legacy operational guidance.

**TP/document validity — FINAL RESULT:** EU protection runs through 4 March 2028. Separately, Italy’s consolidated Decree-Law 201/2025, converted by Law 27/2026, states that the relevant existing TP permits can be further renewed at the holder’s request through 4 March 2027. No Italian national measure extending permit renewal/reissue to 4 March 2028 was found. Do not describe the EU date as a printed-card expiry, automatic physical renewal, or established national renewal process. No evidence of an Italian enactment after 8 November 2026 extending the national measure to 2028 was found.

### IT-TP-02 — Residence options

**Pending rights — FINAL RESULT:** TP-specific rules give beneficiaries access to work and healthcare from the request/permit stage, subject to the specific TP provisions and document used; Civil Protection Ordinance 872/2022 expressly allows work on the basis of a TP-permit application submitted to the competent Questura. Separately, TUI Article 5(9-bis), as updated by Legislative Decree 83/2026, permits lawful stay and temporary work during an ordinary permit’s issuance, renewal or conversion where the competent office has issued the receipt and other legal requirements are met, until any notice identifying grounds preventing the permit. Do not generalize this to unrelated benefits, every receipt, or a right to submit an otherwise unavailable application from Italy.

**TP conversion — FINAL RESULT:** No automatic conversion. Law 213/2023 paragraph 396 provides a work-permit conversion route for the covered TP permits, for the work actually performed, on request and subject to the requirements. UNHCR Italy’s current guidance continues to state that a TP permit can be converted into a work permit when legal requirements are met. DL 202/2024’s renewal deadline of 4 March 2026 has expired; it does not establish the end of the separate conversion route. Evidence does not establish TP-to-study, TP-to-ordinary-family or TP-to-Blue-Card conversion, nor a uniform nationwide filing sequence for each.

**Employment route — FINAL RESULT:** Existing TP work authorization is separate from an ordinary employment permit. A work conversion route exists for covered TP permit holders and actual work; the sources do not establish that the ordinary Decreto Flussi entry procedure applies to that TP-specific conversion. For an ordinary employment route, the Ministry’s generic instructions describe route-specific Sportello Unico/Questura steps and applicable quotas or exemptions. Employer, job and permit requirements still apply.

**Study/family route — FINAL RESULT:** TP includes access to education; this is not an ordinary study permit. Ordinary study, family, self-employment and Blue Card permits have their own conditions. The reviewed sources establish no general TP-to-study or TP-to-ordinary-family permit conversion right. The TP regime includes separate family-member eligibility/reunification provisions. A TP holder may file for international protection under DPCM Article 3, but examination is deferred until TP ends. A route’s availability and filing location remain case-specific.

**Long-term residence credit — FINAL RESULT:** A person residing on TP cannot apply for the EU long-term resident permit on that TP basis under TUI Article 9(8)(e). Specialist legal reporting indicates that TP residence time may be counted if a different eligible permit is later issued, but this downstream credit was not confirmed in current official public guidance during this batch. Treat any claimed credit as requiring individual legal review; do not imply that TP alone qualifies for long-term residence.

**Post-TP transition — FINAL RESULT:** NO NATIONAL MECHANISM FOUND as of 16 November 2026. EU Decision 2026/1912 extends the scheme through 4 March 2028. Council Recommendation 2025/5129 encourages Member States to plan coordinated transition and independent lawful residence options, but is non-binding. No Italian enacted, adopted-future, proposed or formally announced general post-TP residence mechanism was located in the reviewed official sources. This does not rule out a later measure.

### Final results

**IT-EMP-01 — COMPLETE.** Jobseekers may use SIISL, EURES, regional portals, CPI and authorized private agencies. A DID is the formal unemployment declaration for access to reintegration services, not a prerequisite to simply search or apply and not work authorization. It can be filed at a CPI, on a regional portal where available, or on the national employment-services portal. That national online route requires Italian codice fiscale plus SPID or CIE; identity is checked by CPI at first contact. CPI support applies in the region where the person has at least a domicile. Local appointment/document practice and access for a particular TP holder should be confirmed with that CPI; evidence does not establish one uniform TP-specific registration procedure. Training, assessment and GOL participation depend on regional/programme criteria and available places. NASpI has separate insurance eligibility; no automatic credit of Ukrainian contribution periods was established.

**IT-EMP-02 — COMPLETE.** Draft distinguishes TP work authorization, ordinary employer duties, and pay standards. Content synchronized EN/UK/RU.

**Work permission — FINAL RESULT:** A person covered by Italy’s TP measures may work as an employee (including seasonal work) or self-employed under the TP-specific rule. Work is authorized on the basis of the TP permit request filed with the competent Questura; official guidance says the receipt for that exact TP request permits work before the card is issued. It is not a blanket rule for all Ukrainian nationals or all immigration receipts. Licensing, tax, registration and profession-specific requirements continue to apply. No ordinary work quota is required for this TP-specific right.

**TP receipt — FINAL RESULT:** It is more than generic proof that papers were filed: for a TP request to the competent Questura, the official rule/guidance makes it a basis for work while the TP card is pending. Do not conflate this with Article 5(9-bis) or other receipts.

**Ordinary-permit receipt rights — FINAL RESULT:** Separately, TUI Article 5(9-bis), as amended in 2026, permits lawful stay and temporary work during a valid ordinary permit issuance, renewal or conversion procedure once the competent authority has issued the receipt and the other legal requirements are met, subject to any subsequent notice barring the permit. It does not create eligibility to file an otherwise unavailable application in Italy.

**TP-to-work conversion — FINAL RESULT:** The route is scoped to the TP permit groups and actual work described by Law 213/2023, paragraph 396, on request and subject to legal requirements. It is not automatic. Do not revive expired filing/renewal windows or assume ordinary Decreto Flussi applies to this specific route. The reviewed sources do not establish general TP-to-study, ordinary-family or Blue Card conversion. Filing and post-conversion effects remain individual-case matters.

**Employment services — FINAL RESULT:** National and EU vacancy channels coexist with regional CPI/portals; none is a complete list. Current Ministry guidance directs jobseekers to a CPI near where they live, while access to regional support depends on having at least a domicile in that region. DID can be submitted in person or digitally; online identity requirements and first-contact validation are stated above. Training/funding and exact documents, appointments and TP-specific acceptance vary locally. Registration neither grants work permission nor guarantees a service, course or benefit.

**Minimum pay / CCNL — FINAL RESULT:** No statutory universal hourly or monthly amount for every job. Article 36 guarantees proportionate and sufficient remuneration. The 2026 private-sector fair-pay framework relies on the relevant representative CCNL’s overall economic treatment, selected by sector and the employer’s actual activity; if the sector lacks coverage it refers to the most connected covered sector. It also links some contribution incentives to that pay treatment and requires SIISL vacancy listings to disclose the applicable CCNL and pay level. This does not make one CCNL binding in the same way for every worker or create a single national rate. Check sector, employer activity, classification/level, hours, contract and full pay components; the private framework does not automatically apply to public or self-employed work.

**Employer duties — FINAL RESULT:** For subordinate employment, the employer generally files the mandatory hiring communication before work begins and provides the written employment information required by law; the communication or written information may be supplied using the legally permitted means, so do not claim every job requires one specific written contract format. Applicable duties also include payroll/tax withholding, INPS contributions, INAIL insurance where the work is covered, and workplace safety. From 1 April 2026, authorized employers/intermediaries can use SIISL as an alternative channel for mandatory communications while regional systems remain available. Vacancy publication is distinct: the October 2026 decree sets an experimental period through 30 November 2026 and, from 1 December 2026, prior or concurrent publication and a vacancy identifier for certain private-employer contribution benefits; agency publication rules also apply. No Ukraine-only process.

**Self-employment — FINAL RESULT:** TP-specific work access includes self-employment; that does not bypass business/tax/social-security or licensing steps. Depending on the activity, a worker may need a partita IVA, registration with the Chamber of Commerce/Registro Imprese and applicable INPS management, or registration with a professional order and its pension fund. For a sole proprietorship, the official Registro Imprese ComUnica process can cover VAT attribution, business registration, INPS/INAIL steps and any required SCIA. Which steps apply depends on whether the activity is a profession, a business, regulated, or insured; no universal checklist or TP-specific exemption from these requirements was found. This is distinct from the ordinary self-employment residence-permit route.

**Qualification recognition — FINAL RESULT:** Work permission does not recognize a foreign qualification. Regulated occupations require the applicable recognition, authorization and/or professional registration. The Ministry of Health specifically requires recognition for foreign health-profession qualifications, with the procedure depending on the profession and qualification origin. No single recognition process for all professions was established.
