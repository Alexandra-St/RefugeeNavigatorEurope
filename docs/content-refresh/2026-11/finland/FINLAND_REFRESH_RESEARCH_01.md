### Required results

**Military rule — FINAL RESULT:** Council Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/1912 extends the EU scheme to 4 March 2028. Corrected Article 2 applies from 5 August 2026; its continuity carve-out is for a person protected in the same Member State on or before 4 August who continuously retains that status there. Migri applies the rule to relevant new Ukrainian applicants and gives evidence examples (passport exit stamp; otherwise an official document confirming military compliance or exemption, including a document in Reserv+). Government Decision 751/2026 took effect on 13 August; the public Finnish materials reviewed do not fully reconcile its national transitional wording with Migri’s 5 August operational guidance. There is no exhaustive national proof checklist. **Reader-facing result: follow the current Migri rule, with a qualification on national transition/evidence limits.**

**Application — FINAL RESULT:** Apply in person by notifying the police or border-control authority after arrival. That authority registers the application and captures identity/family information, photo, fingerprints and signature; Migri decides. Identity documents and children’s birth certificates are requested examples; if identity papers are unavailable, current Migri guidance points to obtaining a Ukrainian identity document, but a complete no-document alternative checklist was not found. Intake is locally arranged; check the local authority before travelling. No initial Enter Finland route is established. Application is free.

**Decision/document chain — FINAL RESULT:** Police/Border Guard registration → Migri decision → after a positive decision Migri orders a residence-permit card for collection. Police/Border Guard may issue the pending-application certificate at registration. It proves the pending application and, under current Migri work guidance, the right to work to an employer; it is not a residence-permit card. The right to work and study starts at registration. Approximate processing and card delivery times are estimates only.

**TP/card validity — FINAL RESULT:** EU scheme through 4 March 2028; Migri’s central temporary-protection and card pages currently say individual permits/cards through 4 March 2027, including Finland-only recognition of 2023–2026 printed card dates while the underlying permit remains valid. A separate Migri Information Bank page says the permit will be automatically extended through 2028 without an application, while the central/extension pages say future rollout instructions will follow. The discrepancy remains unresolved as of 17 November. Do not infer an individual permit/card date or required action from the EU end date; follow the notice/record for the holder. No new 2028 rollout clarification was found after the 11 November baseline.

**Personal identity code — FINAL RESULT:** Migri says it issues a Finnish personal identity code with a positive TP decision and includes it on the permit card. If registration in the Population Information System cannot be completed at that stage, visit DVV; a replacement card carrying the code is not issued automatically. The code does not itself grant a kotikunta.

**Kotikunta — FINAL RESULT:** Migri says a TP permit holder may apply to DVV after having lived in Finland for a year and holding the TP permit for at least a year. DVV’s official application flow also requires an identity code and intent to stay at least a year, and requires a personal DVV visit after online submission. Kotikunta is not automatic. On gaining it, a person ceases to be a reception-system client and becomes a resident/client of the wellbeing services county for public health and social services. Before kotikunta, reception-centre responsibility remains applicable; it is not a blanket transfer on a single date for every service.

**Pending rights — FINAL RESULT:** Work and study rights start when Police/Border Guard registers the TP application. Applicants use reception-system services while covered by that system; adults awaiting a TP decision are entitled to urgent and essential healthcare, while children under 18 have healthcare on the same grounds as children with kotikunta. After a positive TP decision, healthcare access expands to the same level as permanent residents, with the reception centre arranging care until kotikunta. Reception allowance and other assistance are conditional, not automatic. The article confines itself to work/study at registration and the legal decision/document chain; Batch 3 covers healthcare/housing details.

**Residence filing — FINAL RESULT:** Current TP holders may apply from Finland for an extended permit on another qualifying ground, such as work, study or family. Each route’s criteria remain. Ordinary first permits generally must be filed abroad; listed first-permit exceptions are limited (including Finnish-citizen family member, a child born in Finland, asylum or temporary protection application, and specified protection/humanitarian exceptions). TP is not a general in-country first-permit exception. Extended permits must be filed in Finland before the current permit expires to retain the general right to reside while pending; Migri’s general work-pending guidance does not fully reconcile work after a TP permit itself expires.

**Employment/study/family filing — FINAL RESULT:** An existing TP holder considering a new work, study or family ground can file for an extended permit in Finland, subject to each route’s conditions and fees. A first permit for an ordinary work, specialist/Blue Card, study or ordinary family route is normally filed abroad, subject to the route-specific exceptions. An offer, admission or family tie does not itself grant a permit. TP work authorization is not an ordinary work permit. Finnish guidance does not settle current TP-holder Blue Card eligibility.

**Permanent/long-term residence — FINAL RESULT:** Since 8 January 2026, permanent-residence applications have multiple requirement paths; those paths require qualifying time under specified continuous A permits or Brexit permits. P-EU is separate and requires five years under listed qualifying statuses. A person holding only TP (a temporary B permit) cannot count that period toward the P-EU qualifying time and does not meet a qualifying A-permit residence period for P solely through TP. Apply only after checking the current Migri path and personal permit history.

**Post-TP transition — FINAL RESULT:** No enacted Finnish automatic TP-to-ordinary conversion or special post-4-March-2028 national programme was found in the reviewed official material. Current Migri advice is to use an ordinary extended-permit route for a qualifying ground. EU transition recommendations are not themselves Finnish permits. This remains a review-bounded finding; check for later legislation/instructions.
